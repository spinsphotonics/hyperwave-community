## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Called "the New College" from its first years, though its first classes met in temporary rooms in George Street, Edinburgh, before the college building was erected; also referred to as "the Free Church College" and, generically, "a Divinity Hall for the Free Church." [new-college-edinburgh-q001, new-college-edinburgh-q002]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): Immediately after the Disruption of 1843 it was "at once resolved to open a Divinity Hall for the Free Church," with a Committee empowered to appoint professors and prepare "for beginning at the usual time in November." The College was opened at Edinburgh, with an inaugural address by Dr. Chalmers delivered in the Brick Church, Castle Terrace, within a week of the close of the (Glasgow) Assembly at which Candlish made his appeal for ministers — i.e., November 1843. A permanent college building was separately funded from 1844-45 (see L6) and its foundation-stone was laid by Chalmers "at the close of the Assembly of 1846." [new-college-edinburgh-q001, new-college-edinburgh-q002, new-college-edinburgh-q005, new-college-edinburgh-q018]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): Thomas Chalmers and David Welsh had resigned their professorships at the University of Edinburgh shortly before the Disruption and became the Free Church College's first teachers; they "were to be joined by Dr. Duncan as Professor of Hebrew and Dr. Cunningham in the Chair of Church History." After Welsh's death (1845), Chalmers "accepted the convenership of the College Committee." [new-college-edinburgh-q001, new-college-edinburgh-q003, new-college-edinburgh-q017 — the convenership detail is drawn from the same passage as q014/q017, read in text/hanna-memoirs-v4.txt around p. 419]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): Edinburgh; first classes met in rooms in George Street; the 1843 inaugural address was delivered in the Brick Church, Castle Terrace; the permanent building was later erected at the head of the Mound (per the discussion of its site and cost — see L6). [new-college-edinburgh-q001, new-college-edinburgh-q005]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): Chalmers's inaugural address at the opening of the Free Church College, delivered in the Brick Church, Castle Terrace, Edinburgh, in November 1843. Only a short fragment of this address survives in the material fetched this session, quoted in Brown's Annals of the Disruption (document_id `brown-annals-1893`), which cites it to "Assembly Proceedings, 1843, Glasgow, p. 170." The address's own full printed text (if a separate pamphlet exists) was NOT located this session. [new-college-edinburgh-q005, new-college-edinburgh-q007]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): No single formal purpose-clause or constitution was located this session (see discrepancies.md). Chalmers's own words at the 1843 opening frame the purpose practically, as meeting an urgent shortage of ministers: men "abandoning secular employments and professions... to devote themselves to the Christian ministry." [new-college-edinburgh-q007]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Candlish, in an appeal to the outgoing Assembly just before the College opened, said the Church could "expect no miracle" and must rely on ordinary means — chiefly "the piety of Christian parents and the early devotion of Christian youth" — to supply ministers; the Committee that resolved to open the Hall acted because "there was, indeed, no time to be lost in bringing forward young men for the ministry," since "the fields were white to the harvest, the labourers were few." [new-college-edinburgh-q002, new-college-edinburgh-q004]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: brown-annals-1893, hanna-memoirs-v4) — no passage locating a specific subscription requirement (e.g., to the Westminster Confession) for New College's own students or professors was found in the material read this session. The Free Church's general doctrinal continuity with the pre-Disruption Church of Scotland's Confession and Standards is implied by Wingate's remark about the missionaries (see S1/M1) but that remark concerns the wider Church, not a College subscription clause specifically.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): Chalmers "had the satisfaction of seeing the scheme for theological education (first broached by him in 1828) adopted by the Free Church" — i.e., the College's staffing and course design drew on a plan Chalmers himself had proposed to the Royal Commissioners on the Scottish Universities in 1828, years before the Disruption. [new-college-edinburgh-q014]

By the time of Brown's 1893 edition, "upwards of 1300 students of divinity, intended for the ministry of the Free Church," had attended the Hall, plus 41 other Scottish students, 120 from Ireland, 14 from England, 14 from Wales, and 145 from abroad (Canada, the United States, and elsewhere). ## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): First staff (1843): Chalmers and Welsh (resigned Edinburgh University professors), Duncan (Professor of Hebrew), Cunningham (Chair of Church History). By 1852 (Hanna's footnote), the College had "a staff of five Theological Professors." Chalmers became convener of the College Committee after Welsh's death in 1845; Cunningham later delivered "the Inaugural Address... at the opening of the New College" building, implying he succeeded Chalmers as Principal after Chalmers's 1847 death, though this succession itself is not directly stated in the material read this session (see discrepancies.md). [new-college-edinburgh-q003, new-college-edinburgh-q015, new-college-edinburgh-q017]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): NOT FOUND — no constitutional/charter-style statement of professorial duties was located this session (searched: brown-annals-1893, hanna-memoirs-v4).

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): At the College's building-fund foundation-stone ceremony (1846), Chalmers said the walls would teach not "the passions and politics of this world" but "that higher wisdom which is often hid from the wise and the prudent," and above all "one equality between man and man which will strenuously be taught — the essential equality of human souls," concluding that "the Christian minister is a man of no rank, because a man of all ranks," whose "peculiar glory" is "to be a frequent visitant of the poor man's humble cottage, and to pray by the poor man's dying bed." [new-college-edinburgh-q019, new-college-edinburgh-q020, new-college-edinburgh-q021]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): The 1846 foundation-stone address stresses pastoral humility and the "essential equality of human souls" ahead of "general science" as such; the College is framed as forming ministers who will serve rich and poor alike rather than acquiring social rank. [new-college-edinburgh-q019, new-college-edinburgh-q021]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Chalmers explicitly disclaimed any intention of teaching "the passions and politics of this world" or anything with "the remotest tendency to disturb the existing order of things, or to confound the ranks and distinctions which at present obtain in society." [new-college-edinburgh-q021]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): No New College-specific licensing or sending clause was located this session; sending of ministers and missionaries is documented at the level of the Free Church as a whole rather than the College. At the Disruption, the whole existing missionary staff of the pre-1843 Church of Scotland (about twenty missionaries "among Jews and Gentiles") had to individually decide which church to adhere to; those to the Jews adhered "cordially and unanimously" to the Free Church, and the senior India missionaries (Duff at Calcutta, Wilson at Bombay, and named colleagues) likewise declared for the Free Church, at the cost of their mission property and buildings in India. [new-college-edinburgh-q010, new-college-edinburgh-q011, new-college-edinburgh-q012]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): India (Calcutta, Bombay, Poonah, Madras) and, for the Jewish mission, unspecified overseas fields; also noted (L7) large numbers of foreign students who themselves came to study at New College, from Ireland, England, Wales, Canada, the United States, the Cape of Good Hope, Hungary, Bohemia, Italy, France, Switzerland, and Belgium — implying an outward reach in the other direction as well, though this reflects the whole span of the Hall's history to 1893, not the founding years alone. [new-college-edinburgh-q009, list of missionary stations read in text/brown-annals-1893.txt, p. 504, not separately quote-recorded]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): Twenty missionaries were in the field at the Disruption (1843); no separate count is given for New College's own graduates sent out in its first 25 years (searched: brown-annals-1893, hanna-memoirs-v4). [new-college-edinburgh-q010]
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: brown-annals-1893, hanna-memoirs-v4)
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): No deaths are recorded in the material read this session; instead, Duff and his Calcutta colleagues were expelled from their mission institution by the rival Established-Church committee on 9 March 1844, losing "library, apparatus — everything down to the minutest atom," though not their lives. ## R — Fruit
- **R1 Thomas Chalmers (1780-1847)** (PS): First Principal and Professor of Systematic Theology at the Free Church College from its opening in November 1843 until his death in 1847; delivered the College's 1843 opening address and, in 1846, its introductory address and the foundation-stone address for its permanent building. [new-college-edinburgh-q001, new-college-edinburgh-q005, new-college-edinburgh-q016, new-college-edinburgh-q018]
- **R2 David Welsh (d. 1845)** (PS): Co-founder with Chalmers, resigning his University of Edinburgh professorship to teach at the new College from 1843; personally solicited and helped raise the £20,000-plus building fund from twenty individual donors before his death in 1845, memorialized by Chalmers as one "of which he was so distinguished a member." [new-college-edinburgh-q001, new-college-edinburgh-q008, new-college-edinburgh-q017]
- **R3 William Cunningham** (PS): Appointed to the Chair of Church History on the College's founding staff in 1843; later delivered the published "Inaugural Address... at the opening of the New College" building, described by Hanna as available for "full information" on the College's five-professor course of theological education. [new-college-edinburgh-q003, new-college-edinburgh-q015]
- **R4 Alexander Duff, D.D.** (PS): The Free Church's senior missionary in Calcutta at the Disruption; declared for the Free Church, was expelled from his mission institution's buildings by the rival Established-Church committee on 9 March 1844, and proposed unoccupied ground at Agra and Delhi as an alternative field rather than contest Calcutta. [new-college-edinburgh-q011, new-college-edinburgh-q013]
