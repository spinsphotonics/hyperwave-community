# Dossier — Wheaton College (`wheaton-college`)

Coverage note: this session could not reach wheaton.edu itself (Cloudflare "Just a moment..." JS
challenge on every page tried, including the Statement of Faith and History pages) or
archives.wheaton.edu (a Cloudflare Turnstile human-verification gate on every finding-aid URL
tried), and a2z.my.wheaton.edu (Wheaton's own history wiki) could not be reached at all (TLS
connection failure through the proxy). Six documents were successfully fetched and read in full
instead: a public-domain 1904 Wheaton College catalog bulletin (Internet Archive); two posts/tag
pages from Wheaton College's own Archives & Special Collections blog, *From the Vault*; a short
devotional excerpt from *The Journals of Jim Elliot* (1978) published on the official Elisabeth
Elliot Foundation website; and the Foundation's own biographical timeline. No 1926 (or any other
year's) Statement of Faith, no Foreign Missions Fellowship constitution, and no 1940s catalog
matching the Elliots' own attendance years was located and read this session (see sources.csv and
discrepancies.md). Facts specific to the 1940s Wheaton the Elliots actually knew rest on the
`elisabethelliot.org` and `fromthevault.wheaton.edu` sources; facts about Wheaton's general
institutional character and admission/curriculum machinery rest on the 1904 catalog and are
explicitly dated and flagged as pre-Elliot-era throughout.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Illinois Institute (chartered
  as a college by the State of Illinois, 1855) was renamed Wheaton College in January 1860, when
  Jonathan Blanchard took up its presidency, with the new charter confirmed by amendment of the
  Illinois Legislature of 1861. [wheaton-college-q001, wheaton-college-q009]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): The Illinois Institute's
  first classes were held 14 December 1853. [wheaton-college-q011] Blanchard proposed the new name
  "Wheaton College" in a letter to the donor Warren L. Wheaton dated 5 December 1859.
  [wheaton-college-q008] Blanchard "officially entered upon the duties of his office" as president
  in January 1860, "the name of the institution was changed to Wheaton College," and "the charter
  was amended by the Illinois Legislature of 1861." [wheaton-college-q009] The first class of
  seven men graduated 4 July 1860. [wheaton-college-q010]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Jonathan Blanchard, first president of
  Wheaton College under that name (previously president of Knox College), proposed the renaming in
  his 1859 letter to Warren L. Wheaton, a major landowner and benefactor who had also served as
  secretary of the Illinois Institute's board of trustees. [wheaton-college-q008,
  wheaton-college-q001] Blanchard's own 1904-era catalog names him as having "served until 1882,
  when his son Charles. A. Blanchard succeeded him" — the second president. [wheaton-college-q001]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Wheaton, Illinois (a town in DuPage County, on the Illinois prairie west
  of Chicago); the Illinois Institute's original 1853 building stood on a hilltop in Section 16,
  Township 39, DuPage County. [wheaton-college-q011]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No 1860/1861
  legislative charter text and no historical Statement of Faith was located and fetched this
  session (see coverage note and discrepancies.md). In their place, this chapter's abridged
  "Charter" section uses the *Bulletin of Wheaton College, Special Catalog Number* (May 1904),
  document_id `wheaton-catalog-1904`, as a general (and explicitly pre-Elliot-era) description of
  the kind of institution Wheaton described itself as being; the 1859 Blanchard letter
  (document_id `fromthevault-humble-beginnings`) is quoted separately in the Founding section as
  the closest thing to primary founding-document text located this session.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The 1904 catalog states of the college:
  "Its origin and its growth have marked it as a center... of active and vital Christianity...
  Its objective aim is the fully developed man." [wheaton-college-q001] Blanchard's own 1859 letter
  is narrower and practical — proposing the name and the terms of a land gift — rather than a
  statement of educational purpose as such. [wheaton-college-q008]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The Illinois Institute reached
  a financial crisis under President Lucius C. Matlack, who resigned in 1859 stating "I have no
  salary, no revenue, and am in debt." [wheaton-college-q024] This prompted the trustees to recruit
  Blanchard, described on Wheaton's own archives blog as "known as a strong leader and fundraiser"
  for his earlier work at Knox College [narrative sentence, fromthevault-humble-beginnings; not
  independently formalized as its own quote_id this session]. The Wesleyan Methodist founders' own stated purpose in 1853, per an 1882 county
  history quoted on Wheaton's own archives blog, was explicitly religious: the founders are
  described as having "dedicated the hill and all that should be upon it to that God in whom
  trusting they had boldly gone into the thickest of the fight, not only for the freedom of human
  bodies, but of human souls as well." [wheaton-college-q012] This 1853 statement predates
  Blanchard's 1860 Wheaton College by seven years and describes the Illinois Institute, its
  Wesleyan Methodist antecedent, rather than Wheaton College as such.
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND this session for
  any specific text of a Statement of Faith at any date (see discrepancies.md, item 2). The 1904
  catalog states in general terms only that "Christian sentiment pervades the atmosphere of the
  College, and the spiritual culture of all its members receives intelligent consideration."
  [wheaton-college-q003] The Elisabeth Elliot Foundation's own devotional page states Wheaton's
  motto as "For Christ and His Kingdom." [wheaton-college-q013]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): The
  1904 catalog states plainly that the college began "by the transfer of Illinois Institute from
  the Wesleyan Methodist denomination to a board of trustees affiliated with the
  Congregationalists" — i.e., Wheaton College's own account of its founding names the Illinois
  Institute (1853) as its direct institutional antecedent, not an outside model imitated.
  [wheaton-college-q001, wheaton-college-q011]

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: wheaton-catalog-1904 and all other
  documents fetched this session)
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): Per the 1904 catalog, candidates for the Freshman class
  needed "the equivalent of forty-eight admission credits," with twenty-four of these fixed
  (language, history, English, algebra, geometry, laboratory science) and twenty-four elective.
  [wheaton-college-q004] For Jim Elliot and Elisabeth Howard specifically (1940s, not 1904): Jim
  went to Wheaton in the fall of 1945 "with the object of preparing himself for the Lord's
  service" [wheaton-college-q014]; Elisabeth "enters Wheaton College desiring to become a Bible
  translator and studies Classical Greek" in 1944. [wheaton-college-q017] No document read this
  session states what specific secondary-school credits either of them presented for admission.
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): NOT FOUND as a
  stated admission requirement in the 1904 catalog (which describes general "Christian sentiment"
  but not an entrance testimony); NOT FOUND in the 1940s-era sources for the Elliots' own
  admission specifically.
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): The 1904 catalog states that accredited high
  school graduates could enter "without examination," while others sat examinations "held on the
  Monday preceding the opening day of each term, and on opening day." [wheaton-college-q004; see
  also the general admission paragraph in charter_abridged.md]
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PS): Yes, as full students alongside men, on the
  evidence of Elisabeth Howard's own 1944 entry to study Classical Greek. [wheaton-college-q017]
  The 1904 catalog independently confirms co-education by that date: the campus included a
  separate "Woman's Building," "40 x 140 feet, three stories high," with "living rooms, parlors,
  and the halls of the young ladies' societies." [wheaton-college-q025]
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): Four years, on the evidence of Jim Elliot's own timeline: he
  entered fall 1945 and kept a college journal beginning in his "Junior Year" in January 1948
  [wheaton-college-q013, wheaton-college-q014; and the elisabethelliot-journals-part1 document's
  own title], consistent with a 1949 graduation. The 1904 catalog's own graduation rule required
  "thirty-six college credits, in addition to those presented for admission to Freshman Class."
  [wheaton-college-q005]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): Per the 1904 catalog, eleven of the thirty-six
  graduation credits were fixed for all students: "Mathematics 3; English 3; Political Science 1;
  Psychology 1; Ethics 1; Philosophy of Theism 1; Evidences of Christianity 1." [wheaton-college-q006]
  Elisabeth Howard's own course of study (1940s) is stated only in general terms: she "studies
  Classical Greek." [wheaton-college-q017] No document read this session gives a full 1940s-era
  course list.
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): The 1904 catalog organizes the year into Fall,
  Winter, and Spring terms (per its printed calendar; not independently formalized as a quote_id
  this session, visible in the raw catalog text). NOT FOUND for the specific 1940s term structure.
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Latin, Greek, German, or French required
  among the fixed Freshman admission credits (six credits in one of the four). [wheaton-college-q004]
  Elisabeth Howard specifically studied Classical Greek at Wheaton. [wheaton-college-q017]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND in the 1904 catalog's abridged sections (the
  catalog's full course-by-course syllabi, not reproduced in the abridged charter, would likely
  answer this in more detail than this session's abridgement covers; flagged as a follow-up).
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): The 1904 catalog describes a
  "Student Missionary Department," a scholarship arrangement with the American Sunday School Union
  under which a student split each year between college work and six months of actual "Sunday
  School missionary work," examined annually on both his "school and his missionary work."
  [wheaton-college-q007] This is a 1904-era program; no equivalent 1940s practice was found for
  the Foreign Missions Fellowship named in the roster (see discrepancies.md).
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): Annual promotion/entrance examinations described under
  A4; the Student Missionary Department scholarship was itself renewed or discontinued based on an
  annual examination result (90%, 80%, or below 70%). [wheaton-college-q007]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): The A.B. degree, on
  completion of thirty-six college credits beyond admission requirements, per the 1904 catalog.
  [wheaton-college-q005] Jim Elliot's Wheaton degree year (1949) is corroborated by his four-year
  span (entered 1945) but no document read this session states his specific degree letters (A.B.
  or other) or field of study.
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a full timetable; the 1904
  catalog gives only isolated time points (10:30 a.m. daily chapel; Sunday services at 10:30 a.m.
  and 7:30 p.m.; Tuesday evening prayer meeting; Sunday 6:30 p.m. Christian Endeavor).
  [wheaton-college-q002]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): The 1904 catalog describes a daily
  10:30 a.m. chapel exercise, "primarily a devotional half hour for all members of the College,"
  with attendance expected of all absent good reason. [wheaton-college-q002] No fuller daily
  schedule (rising, meals, study hours, bed) was located this session.
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): NOT FOUND for the 1940s. The 1904
  catalog describes a separate Woman's Building with "living rooms, parlors, and the halls of the
  young ladies' societies" [wheaton-college-q025]; the Main Building's own "living rooms for young
  men" is stated in the fuller catalog text but not independently formalized as its own quote_id
  this session.
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): Daily chapel (10:30 a.m.); Sunday College Church
  services (10:30 a.m. and 7:30 p.m.); Sunday Bible School (noon); Sunday evening Christian
  Endeavor (6:30 p.m.); a weekly Tuesday evening Students' Prayer Meeting described as "a historic
  feature of the College." [wheaton-college-q002] All 1904-era; not confirmed unchanged for the
  1940s.
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): NOT FOUND as formal rules. The 1904 catalog
  describes weekly "Division Meetings," small-group sessions under a faculty member, as a setting
  for "the discussion of personal religion, habits, dress, etiquette and various other subjects in
  a freer and more confidential way than would be possible in a general gathering." This is the
  closest the sources read this session come to a description of discipline as ongoing pastoral
  oversight rather than a penal code. [wheaton-college-q002 covers Religious Life;
  Division Meetings passage is in charter_abridged.md, not independently issued its own quote_id
  beyond what is verified there via check_quotes.py --charter]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): "Dress, etiquette" are named among the topics
  taken up in Division Meetings (see L4), but no specific rule is stated. NOT FOUND further.
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): NOT FOUND for general tuition/board
  rates. The Student Missionary Department scholarship (C6/C7) gives one specific 1904-era figure:
  an applicant deposited "$100.00" for his first six months, then received $33.33/month in his
  first year of field work (of which the Union credited $17.00 toward his college account), rising
  to $21.33/month in years two and three. [wheaton-college-q007]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): NOT FOUND for any specific year, 1904 or 1940s.
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): NOT FOUND as a general student
  body record; on Jim Elliot's own death, see S5/R1 below (this occurred after his Wheaton years,
  in Ecuador, not at the college).

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The 1904 catalog: the college is
  "a center... of active and vital Christianity," with "its objective aim... the fully developed
  man." [wheaton-college-q001] The Elisabeth Elliot Foundation's own note states Wheaton's motto,
  "For Christ and His Kingdom," and that Jim Elliot went to Wheaton "with the object of preparing
  himself for the Lord's service," eliminating "all that he felt would distract him from this
  objective, dating being one example." [wheaton-college-q013, wheaton-college-q014]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): Piety integrated with ordinary academic and social life — daily
  chapel, Sunday worship, weekly prayer meeting, and small-group "Division" pastoral oversight, all
  described together with the ordinary curriculum in the same catalog. [wheaton-college-q002, and
  Division Meetings in charter_abridged.md] A concrete missionary-preparation stress is also
  present institutionally as early as 1904 (the Student Missionary Department). [wheaton-college-q007]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT FOUND as an explicit
  institutional rejection statement in the 1904 catalog. On Jim Elliot's own, personal practice
  (not the college's stated policy): he "eliminated all that he felt would distract him" from
  preparing for the Lord's service, "dating being one example of such a distraction."
  [wheaton-college-q014] This describes Elliot's individual discipline, not a Wheaton College rule,
  and is labeled accordingly.
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): "For Christ and His Kingdom," per the
  Elisabeth Elliot Foundation's introductory note to the Journals excerpt; no earlier or
  independent primary source for the motto's adoption date was located this session.
  [wheaton-college-q013]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND this session.

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)**
  (PO): Not a formal institutional mechanism on the evidence read this session; Wheaton's own 1904
  Student Missionary Department (C6) is a college-run scholarship/assignment arrangement with an
  outside body (the American Sunday School Union), not a general "sending" clause for graduates.
  [wheaton-college-q007] For Jim Elliot specifically, sending ran instead through the Plymouth
  Brethren commendation system after he left Wheaton: Elisabeth Elliot's own Foundation states that
  "Jim sets sail for Quito, Ecuador, for mission work" in February 1952 [wheaton-college-q020], and
  Wheaton's own archives describe Elisabeth as "a Plymouth Brethren missionary to Ecuador" at the
  time of Jim's death. [wheaton-college-q023] See the `cmml-brethren` dossier for the commendation
  mechanism itself.
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): Ecuador, for Jim Elliot (February 1952) and
  Elisabeth Howard (April 1952, per the roster's known-facts sheet; this session's own fetched
  timeline source gives Jim's February 1952 sailing directly [wheaton-college-q020] but the exact
  April 1952 Elisabeth sailing date, while present in the fetched timeline raw text, was not
  independently issued its own quote_id this session).
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND for any general Wheaton-wide count
  of graduates entering missionary service, in any period.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND from any
  Wheaton document for graduates generally. For Jim Elliot specifically, support ran through the
  Brethren faith-principle system described in the `cmml-brethren` dossier, not through Wheaton
  College.
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): Jim Elliot, killed in
  Ecuador; Wheaton's own archives blog states the Archives "marked the 65th anniversary of the
  death of Wheaton alumnus Jim Elliot and four other American missionaries in Ecuador at the hands
  of Waorani tribe members in January 1956." [wheaton-college-q022] Elisabeth Elliot's own
  Foundation timeline does not give the day of death directly in the material fetched this session
  beyond confirming "October 8, 1953" (marriage) and other dates; the 8 January 1956 date is
  independently attested in the known-facts sheet [HIGH] and is consistent with, though not
  independently re-derived from, wheaton-college-q022's "January 1956."
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): NOT FOUND.

## R — Fruit
- **R1** Philip James "Jim" Elliot (1927-1956): born 8 October 1927 in Portland, Oregon
  [wheaton-college-q016]; entered Wheaton fall 1945 "with the object of preparing himself for the
  Lord's service" [wheaton-college-q014]; kept a college journal from his junior year (January
  1948) [elisabethelliot-journals-part1 document title and content]; his Wheaton-student-era papers
  were donated to the Wheaton College Archives in 2019 [wheaton-college-q021]; killed in Ecuador,
  January 1956, one of five missionaries killed by Waorani warriors. [wheaton-college-q022]
- **R2** Elisabeth Howard Elliot (1926-2015): entered Wheaton College in 1944 "desiring to become
  a Bible translator" and "studies Classical Greek" [wheaton-college-q017]; met Jim Elliot, her
  brother David's roommate, at Wheaton on 23 March 1947 [wheaton-college-q018]; spent 1948 at
  Prairie Bible Institute in Alberta, Canada [wheaton-college-q019]; described by Wheaton's own
  archives as "a Plymouth Brethren missionary to Ecuador" at the time of her husband's death.
  [wheaton-college-q023]
