# Discrepancies — wittenberg

1. **Date of Melanchthon's 1518 inaugural oration.** The roster and standard modern scholarship
   (WebSearch snippets only, not opened/read as sources this session) commonly give 28 August 1518
   as the date of the oration "De corrigendis adolescentiae studiis." Stump's 1897 biography, read
   in full this session, instead gives Melanchthon's arrival at Wittenberg as "August 25, 1518,"
   and states that "four days after his arrival the new professor delivered his introductory
   lecture" (quote_id wittenberg-q014) -- which, taken literally, points to about 29 August, not
   28 August. Neither figure is from a document identified as primary in this session (Stump is a
   secondary biography). Do not state a specific date for the oration in the chapter without a
   citation to a source actually read; if a date is given, attribute it explicitly to Stump 1897
   and flag the one-day discrepancy with the commonly cited 28 August date.

2. **Student numbers.** Stump's narrative gives "no more than two hundred students enrolled" in
   1517 (quote_id wittenberg-q015), rising after Melanchthon's arrival to an audience for his own
   lectures alone of "a thousand and even two thousand or more" (quote_id wittenberg-q016).
   Kreitzer's 2021 secondary magazine article instead states Wittenberg became the largest
   university in Germany in 1520 and that Luther might see up to 400 students in his lecture hall
   and Melanchthon "400 to 600" -- a considerably lower figure than Stump's "a thousand and even
   two thousand." Both are secondary sources (a 19th-century biography and a 21st-century magazine
   article respectively); neither is a primary enrollment register. Both figures are recorded in
   the dossier (L7) with their sources; the chapter should not silently pick one over the other,
   and should attribute each figure to its own source by name.

3. **Wittenberg statutes of 1536.** The roster row flags "Wittenberg statutes 1536 [VERIFY]." A
   WebSearch (not a fetched/read source) surfaced a paywalled academic article (Die Philosophische
   Fakultat der Universitat Wittenberg von der Grundung bis zur Vertreibung der Philippisten,
   degruyterbrill.com) whose search-result snippet describes a 1536 Fundationsurkunde completing
   the arts-faculty reform. This session did not open or read that article, and per Rule 2 a
   search snippet is not a source -- it is logged in sources.csv (row
   wittenberg-statutes-1536-NOT-FETCHED) but is not used for any dossier fact. The 1536 statutes
   remain UNVERIFIED and unlocated as a fetched, readable text this session. Flagged for a
   follow-up Fetcher ticket (a German or Latin digitization would be needed; no public-domain
   English translation was searched for or found).

4. **Melanchthon's household pupils and salary (L2, L6, T3, T4).** Several facts noted in the
   dossier as "Stump's narrative, not separately quote-recorded this session" -- that Melanchthon
   personally housed and tutored young men in his own home because of a lack of preparatory
   schools, that his salary was raised to 200 florins on his 1526 appointment as professor of
   theology, and that Luther was "highly delighted" by the 1518 inaugural lecture -- are real
   sentences read in the fetched text (`stump1897-lifeofmelanchthon.txt`) but were not turned into
   formal quote_id records with 20-word before/after context in this session, for reasons of time.
   They are cited in the dossier only as an informal paraphrase pointer to Stump's narrative, and
   are correctly labeled SECONDARY, not given a quote_id. Flagged for a follow-up Extractor pass:
   either formalize these as quote records or downgrade the dossier language to UNVERIFIED. Do not
   promote any of these three specific facts to a chapter sentence with a footnote pointing to a
   nonexistent quote_id.

5. **Melanchthon's 1518 inaugural oration and the 1528/1538 Unterricht der Visitatoren themselves
   were not located as fetchable public-domain texts.** Both are named as Wittenberg's principal
   founding-adjacent documents in the roster row (F5). The only content recovered from the second
   document is a short excerpt of its school-order portion, at second hand, via F. V. N. Painter's
   1889 English paraphrase-translation (quote_ids wittenberg-q009 through wittenberg-q012), and via
   Joseph Stump's 1897 biography's report that the work "added a chapter on the improvements to be
   made in the schools" and its separate quotation of the work's definition of Christian liberty
   (quote_ids wittenberg-q018, wittenberg-q019). No content at all was recovered from the 1518
   oration itself; the chapter's account of it rests entirely on Stump's secondary narrative
   paraphrase (not quoted verbatim as the oration's own words) of its subject and reception. This
   is the chapter's single largest gap and is not a contradiction between sources so much as an
   absence; flagged here per the discrepancies-log convention used in the geneva-academy example
   for a comparable BLOCKED item.
