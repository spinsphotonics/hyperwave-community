# Dossier — New Tribes Mission (`new-tribes-mission`)

**Coverage note:** No digitized 1940s NTM "boot camp" training manual, Fundamental Principles
document, or founding-era doctrinal statement was located and fetched this session. The
closest surviving founding-era text is a 1 August 1942 "Pledge and Covenant," signed by Cecil
Dye, Paul Fleming, and Bob Williams, quoted only in part, at second/third hand, via a staff
member's personal ministry blog that itself cites Ken Johnston's *The Story of New Tribes
Mission* (not independently located this session). A single sentence of the founding-era
periodical *Brown Gold* (Issue 1, May 1943) likewise survives only at second hand, though
corroborated identically by two independent later NTM-family websites. The roster's claim of a
Fredonia, Wisconsin boot camp could not be corroborated this session — see discrepancies.md
#2. The Charter section reproduces the Pledge and Covenant excerpt alongside Ethnos360's
*current* Purpose statement and Statement of Faith, both explicitly flagged as present-day
substitutes where the founding-era text itself could not be located in full.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Founded in 1942 as "New
  Tribes Mission"; renamed "Ethnos360" on 28 April 2017, on the occasion of the organization's
  75th anniversary. [quote_ids: ntm-q013, ntm-q022, ntm-q049]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): "In 1942, the two men
  felt led to form a committee... and in the spring of the same year, New Tribes Mission was
  founded." The organization "sent out its first group in November 1942 to Bolivia." Canada's
  branch was separately incorporated in Vancouver in May 1951, and NTM Canada's own charter was
  formed 9 April 1969. [quote_ids: ntm-q007, ntm-q014, ntm-q020, ntm-q024]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Paul Fleming and Cecil A. Dye are
  named as the two men who formed the founding committee in 1942; a formal 2017 press release
  additionally names Lance B. Latham and M. Robert Williams as co-founders, and the 1 August
  1942 Pledge and Covenant is signed by Dye, Fleming, and (Bob) Williams — see discrepancies.md
  #3 for the unresolved question of how many founders should be named. [quote_ids: ntm-q006,
  ntm-q007, ntm-q020, ntm-q032, ntm-q049]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): NTM was "initially... based in a former nightclub in Chicago"; the
  organization "moved headquarters to Chico, California" in 1944/45 and "shortly thereafter...
  established a 'boot camp'... at Fouts Springs, California." No source located this session
  corroborates the roster's claim of a Fredonia, Wisconsin boot camp — see discrepancies.md #2.
  The present Sanford, Florida headquarters building was acquired in 1977, decades after the
  founding. [quote_ids: ntm-q021, ntm-q023, ntm-q054]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND as a
  complete text (searched: ntm-principles-boot-camp-manual-1940s [no hit], brown-gold-magazine-
  1943-issue1 [no hit, single sentence only], ntm-pledge-and-covenant-1942 [partial excerpt
  only]). The closest attested founding document is the 1 August 1942 Pledge and Covenant,
  quoted in part via document_id `housley-founding-fathers`; `charter_abridged.md` reproduces
  this excerpt alongside Ethnos360's current Purpose and Statement of Faith as explicitly
  flagged substitutes for the missing full text.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The 1942 Pledge and Covenant states: "We
  hereby pledge ourselves to work for the completion of the Bride of Christ until death... namely,
  reaching the last evangelized tribe in our generation." Paul Fleming is separately quoted as
  saying "New Tribes Mission's efforts shall be directed... where no witness of the gospel has
  yet reached." [quote_ids: ntm-q016, ntm-q032]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Paul Fleming's own account
  of his personal turning point — resolving, after an evening spent observing an old,
  purposeless man, "that very night... to dedicate his life to missions and to the preaching of
  the gospel to a needy world" — is the closest founder's-own-words statement of a crisis found
  this session; it is a personal call narrative rather than an institutional diagnosis of need.
  [quote_id: ntm-q003]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No 1942-era doctrinal
  statement was located. Ethnos360's current Statement of Faith affirms, among its ten core
  points, belief "In the word-by-word inspiration, inerrancy, and final authority of the Holy
  Scriptures," and among its stated values, "Readiness to sacrifice for Christ and His Church" —
  reproduced in full in `charter_abridged.md` and flagged there as a present-day document only.
  The 1942 Pledge and Covenant itself functions as a founding-era commitment document, though it
  is a pledge of purpose rather than a doctrinal confession in the ordinary sense. [quote_ids:
  ntm-q032, ntm-q051, ntm-q052]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO):
  Paul Fleming's own missionary service preceded the founding: he served in British Malaya,
  partnering with "fellow missionary Dr. Robert Jaffray," and sought unsuccessfully to reach the
  Arang-Asli people of the Malay Peninsula's interior before returning to the United States
  through illness. No source read this session states that the founders modeled New Tribes
  Mission on a named earlier institution or document. [quote_ids: ntm-q004, ntm-q005]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Paul Fleming: "We had no
  funds, no organization behind us; we were just a group of fellows who desired honestly to
  give our lives for Jesus Christ... the Lord brought it into existence in spite of us." Cecil
  Dye, explaining the 1943 Bolivia expedition: "It is because the glorious name of Jesus is not
  known here, and must be made known at any cost... that we are going... we want God to get the
  most possible glory from everything that happens." [quote_ids: ntm-q008, ntm-q036]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): The distinctive stress across every source read this session is
  reaching peoples with, in the present-day organization's own words, no access to the gospel:
  "In more than 6,000 people groups around the world, there is no church, nor is there any work
  being done to establish a church. These are the people among whom Ethnos360 works." A stated
  founding-era value, from the 1942 Pledge and Covenant, is single-minded focus: "to refrain
  from doing even the good things if they do not contribute to the most important things." The
  current Statement of Faith separately lists as a value "Excellence and urgency in all we do to
  finish the task." [quote_ids: ntm-q032, ntm-q044, ntm-q053]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The 1942 Pledge and
  Covenant states the signers would "do nothing that would hinder or detract or render us
  powerless in the accomplishment of this goal" — a rejection of any distraction from the
  mission's central aim, rather than a named doctrinal error. Cecil Dye's letter to his men
  invokes, without rejecting, the possibility of martyrdom itself: "I shall not be surprised if
  some will share with Stephen a martyr's crown." [quote_ids: ntm-q031, ntm-q032]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): The earliest dated motto-like phrase
  located this session is from *Brown Gold* magazine, Issue 1, May 1943: "By unflinching
  determination we hazard our lives and gamble all for Christ until we have reached the last
  tribe regardless of where that tribe might be." [quote_ids: ntm-q018, ntm-q050]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): At the 2017 name-change
  event marking the organization's 75th anniversary, professor George Walker restated the
  founding vision: "The founders of New Tribes Mission were captured and driven by the love of
  the Lord Jesus Christ," and the organization's own blog account states, "We remain just as
  committed to the mission here in the spring of 2017 as our founders were in 1942." [quote_ids:
  ntm-q011, ntm-q012]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)**
  (PO): New Tribes Mission was itself the sending body from its founding: "In 1943, NTM's first
  team of missionaries went to Bolivia to seek out people groups who had never heard the gospel
  and to establish a church among them." The organization "sent out its first group in November
  1942 to Bolivia." [quote_ids: ntm-q024, ntm-q034]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): Bolivia (1942/1943, the first field); Canada
  (from 1947 conference, 1951 incorporation); by the present day, "more than a dozen countries"
  host training programs and missionaries serve in, per the organization's own current
  description, "more than 6,000 people groups around the world." [quote_ids: ntm-q017, ntm-q044,
  ntm-q048]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): No founding-decade (1940s) sending count is
  given. By the time of the sources fetched this session (current, undated pages), "more than
  2,000" (per ethnosca-heritage) or "more than 3,000" (per ntmuk-heritage and ethnos360-about)
  missionaries serve worldwide — see discrepancies.md #1.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): "Paul Fleming and
  five others had no funds or organization behind them when they dared to trust God to
  establish NTM" is the clearest statement of a faith-support principle, applied to the
  founders themselves rather than stated as a formal policy document for missionaries generally.
  [quote_id: ntm-q015]
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): Five NTM missionaries —
  Dave Bacon, Bob Dye, Cecil Dye, George Hosbach, and Eldon Hunter — were killed by Ayoré people
  in Bolivia in 1943, their fate unconfirmed until "the startling news came back" on 12 January
  1944 that the men were missing, and not fully explained until a friendly contact in 1950
  revealed "one of the warriors got upset, believing he deserved a bigger gift. And out of that
  greed, the five men were killed." In June 1950, "the first plane bought by NTM crashed in
  Venezuela, killing all 15 people on board"; the second NTM plane crashed that November at
  Mount Moran, Wyoming, "killing all 21 aboard, including spouses, several children and founder
  Paul Fleming." In July 1953, "14 NTM members serving as volunteer firefighters died" in the
  Rattlesnake Fire near Fouts Springs, California. [quote_ids: ntm-q025, ntm-q026, ntm-q027,
  ntm-q028, ntm-q029, ntm-q040]
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): The 1942 Pledge and
  Covenant functions as the closest attested instruction to those the founders sent (and were
  themselves bound by): "We hereby pledge ourselves to work for the completion of the Bride of
  Christ until death." [quote_id: ntm-q032]

## R — Fruit
- **R1-R4 Up to six named alumni or missionaries: name, dates, field, one-sentence outcome,
  source** (PS):
  1. **Paul Fleming**, co-founder, served earlier as a missionary in British Malaya, founded
     New Tribes Mission in 1942, and was killed with his family in the November 1950 NTM plane
     crash at Mount Moran, Wyoming. [quote_ids: ntm-q004, ntm-q007, ntm-q028]
  2. **Cecil Dye**, co-founder and a pastor turned missionary, led the first team to Bolivia in
     1943 and was killed there with four companions; his final written words to two team members
     who had turned back were, "If you don't hear anything inside a month, you can come and make
     a search for us." [quote_ids: ntm-q006, ntm-q037]
  3. **Jean Dye Johnson**, wife of Bob Dye, endured the years-long uncertainty over her
     husband's fate and remained connected to the mission's subsequent work. [quote_id:
     ntm-q038]
  4. **Audrey Bacon**, wife of Dave Bacon, later told the Ayoré family responsible for her
     husband's death, "It was worth my husband's death to see you come to know Jesus Christ."
     [quote_id: ntm-q042]

No other named New Tribes Mission figure from the 1940s founding decade is independently
sourced in the material fetched this session; the sources are silent on any further name this
chapter could responsibly add.
