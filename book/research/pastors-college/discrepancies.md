# Discrepancies — pastors-college

1. **Exact issue date of the Sword and Trowel "College" notes quoted (q022-q024).** The Google-digitized
   file at document_id `sword-trowel-bound` (archive.org identifier `swordandtrowela00spurgoog`) is a large
   bound composite whose topical index at the front spans years 1865-1882; it is not a clean single-year
   run. The specific "Notes" page quoted (running page 48 of the OCR) mentions, in the same section, the
   recent death of "Mr. Bost" (almost certainly John Bost of La Force, France, who died 28 January 1881)
   and a Sabbath service by "Mr. Moody" at the Metropolitan Tabernacle, consistent with the Moody-Sankey
   London campaign of 1881-1884. On this internal evidence the issue is most likely March 1881, but no
   title page or masthead giving the month and year was located and read this session. Do not state a
   specific month/year for these three quotations in the chapter beyond "Spurgeon's magazine, early
   1880s" without further verification. The College's own formal ANNUAL REPORT set-piece (the roster's
   specific target for this institution, per `05_repositories_and_search_strings.md`) was searched for
   inside this same file (string "annual report of the Pastors' College") and not found in the portion
   read; a dedicated search for individual dated issues (1865-1892) is a follow-up Scout task.

2. **Date the Pastors' College Conference began.** The roster's known-facts sheet marks "The College's
   annual Conference began 1865 [VERIFY]." The material fetched this session does not confirm 1865 as the
   Conference's start date; it does establish (autobio-v3, quote pastors-college-q017) that "until the
   year 1865, no statistics were collected from the brethren educated in the College," which is a related
   but distinct fact (statistics collection, not the Conference's founding). Do not conflate the two in
   the chapter. The Conference's founding date remains UNVERIFIED by a source read this session.

3. **"Some seven years ago" vs. 1856 founding.** Spurgeon's letter reprinted in autobio-v3 (quote
   pastors-college-q010) opens "Some seven years ago, one youthful brother was blessed of God in
   street-preaching," and the letter is presented in the chapter "The Pastors' College 1861-1878" without
   an exact date attached to the letter itself in the passage read. Counting back seven years from a date
   in the early-to-mid 1860s is consistent with the roster's 1855-1856 founding narrative, but this
   session did not locate an exact date for this particular letter. Not a contradiction, but the Writer
   should not silently assign the letter a specific year not given in the source.

4. **Number of men trained / sent, later than 1878.** The only cumulative statistics located and read this
   session are the 1878 figures in autobio-v3 (quote pastors-college-q017: over 500 students trained by
   the end of 1878, 25 "called home," 450 then serving as pastors/evangelists/missionaries, 3,600 baptisms
   in the year, a net increase of 33,319 church members over the preceding fourteen years). Spurgeon died
   in 1892 and the College continued to grow after 1878; widely cited totals for "students trained under
   Spurgeon" (commonly given elsewhere as high as ~900) were NOT located in a source read this session and
   must not be used in the chapter. Only the 1878 figures, cited to autobio-v3, may be stated.

5. **"School of the prophets" chapter title (OCR-garbled).** The chapter heading at the start of Chapter
   XLVI of autobio-v2 is badly OCR-garbled ("fMu Scijool of tljt"). Cross-referenced against the roster's
   own search-string hint ("Spurgeon 'school of the prophets'"), the title is almost certainly "The School
   of the Prophets," and the chapter's content (Medhurst's and Spurgeon's founding narrative) is fully
   consistent with that title, but the exact wording was not confirmed against a clean scan or the printed
   table of contents this session. Treat the title as UNVERIFIED-exact-wording, though the chapter's
   content and page range are not in doubt.
