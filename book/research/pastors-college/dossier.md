# Dossier — The Pastors' College (`pastors-college`)

Note on sourcing: five primary documents were fetched and read this session (see sources.csv).
The two Autobiography volumes are compiled by Spurgeon's wife and secretary from his diary,
letters, and dictated recollections, and reprint his own first-person narrative verbatim within
quotation marks; these first-person passages are treated as PRIMARY (Spurgeon's own testimony),
cited PO. The surrounding editorial narrative by the compilers is treated as PRIMARY-ADJACENT
where it reprints dated documents (church-book minutes, letters) verbatim, and as SECONDARY
narrative elsewhere. "Lectures to My Students" (first series) independently reprints, almost
verbatim, the same admission-examination passage found in autobio-v3, which corroborates that
passage across two independently-fetched editions. The Sword and the Trowel document fetched
this session is a large Google-digitized composite volume, not a clean single dated issue; its
exact issue date is UNVERIFIED (see discrepancies.md item 1) though the passage's text itself is
verbatim and reliable as a primary "Notes" item. No formal single "founding charter," constitution,
or "Outline of the Course of Study" pamphlet was located this session (see F5 and sources.csv);
the College began informally and grew by accretion, a fact the sources themselves state (F2, F9).

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The institution is called "the Pastors' College" throughout the sources read this session, from its early growth in 1856 onward; it was also called, by Spurgeon himself, "our Institution for training young men for the ministry" and "this Institution for training young men for the ministry." [pastors-college-q004, pastors-college-q010]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): There is no single founding-charter date in the sources read. Spurgeon's account states that in 1856 a single student grew to eight, then twenty, then nearly a hundred; the specific act that "commenced" the College, per the earliest student T. W. Medhurst's own contributed narrative, was Spurgeon's decision, on receiving repayment for Medhurst's tuition, to "take a second student." [pastors-college-q001, pastors-college-q004] Roster fact: College treated 1856 as its founding date [HIGH, per known-facts sheet]; the sources read this session are consistent with, but do not independently redate, that year.
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): Charles Haddon Spurgeon began the work by taking one young man under his charge; the Rev. George Rogers of Camberwell, "waiting and ripening for the office and work of a tutor," joined him and undertook the training in partnership with Spurgeon. [pastors-college-q002, pastors-college-q003, pastors-college-q010]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): The work began with Spurgeon's ministry at New Park Street Chapel and, from a letter of 1855 in Medhurst's narrative, involved lodging students with tutors outside London (Bexley Heath, Kent, per the surrounding narrative) before students resided with George Rogers at Albany Road, Camberwell; after the Metropolitan Tabernacle opened (1861), "the students migrated from Mr. Rogers' house to the class-rooms in the new sanctuary." [pastors-college-q002, pastors-college-q008 headnote context; direct migration quote in the Common Life section below]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No single formal charter, deed, or constitution was located this session. The closest primary document to a founding charter is Spurgeon's own retrospective account of the College's origin and aims, printed in his Autobiography, Vol. II, chapter XLVI ("The School of the Prophets" — chapter title OCR-garbled, see discrepancies.md item 5), document_id `autobio-v2`. This is the document abridged in `charter_abridged.md`. The roster's suggested "Outline of the Course of Study" was searched for as a discrete document and NOT FOUND (searched: sword-trowel-bound; general search this session; see sources.csv row `outline-course-of-study-NOT-FOUND`).
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Spurgeon states that, seeing zealous young converts of "capacities for usefulness" but "labouring under the serious disadvantage of having no education," and unlikely to gain admission "into any of our Colleges," "it entered into my heart to provide them with a course of elementary instruction." Once "fairly moulded into shape," the College had "before us but one object, and that was, the glory of God, by the preaching of the gospel." [pastors-college-q002, pastors-college-q005]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The need stated is the existence of converted men with evident gifts for preaching who lacked education and could not gain entry to existing denominational colleges because of it. [pastors-college-q002] Spurgeon further states that most existing colleges made their expenses too great for poor men, so that, to meet their case, "there must be no cost at all to them." [pastors-college-q011]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No signed confession or subscription document was located this session. Spurgeon states plainly that "the theology of the Pastors' College is Puritanic," naming a preference for older divines (Manton over Maurice, Charnock over Robertson, Owen over Voysey) and stating the College's teaching stands by "the unfashionable doctrines of grace." [pastors-college-q025] No admission requirement of signing a written creed is stated in the sources read; admission instead turned on evidence of a call to preach (see A3, A6).
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): The sources state the work "did not begin with any scheme — it grew out of necessity," and that Spurgeon "had no idea whereunto the matter would grow, nor did he contemplate the institution of any far-reaching and wide-spread agency" — i.e., the founders explicitly deny modeling the College on an existing institution or plan. [pastors-college-q004] No other college, seminary, or founding document is named in the sources read this session as a model imitated.

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: autobio-v2, autobio-v3, lectures1-selection, sword-trowel-bound). No minimum or maximum age is stated in the sources read.
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): Explicitly none: "We also placed the literary qualifications of admission so low that even brethren who could not read have been able to enter, and have been among the most useful of our students in after days." [pastors-college-q006]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): Admission turned on a candidate's own account together with written testimonials and the judgment of his home pastor/church, not a formal membership-subscription rule. Spurgeon describes examining candidates himself — "the duty which fell to the lot of Cromwell's 'Triers'" — "after hearing what the candidate has had to say, having read his testimonials and seen his replies to questions." [pastors-college-q015, pastors-college-q016] A recorded case shows a candidate's own pastor supplying an open letter of commendation (see T4/discrepancies for the case where a pastor's private and public letters disagreed).
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): The "examination" described is not a written academic test but Spurgeon's personal interview and judgment on the evidence of a divine call: hearing the candidate, reading his testimonials, and hearing his answers to questions, in order to decide "whether the Pastors' College shall help him, or leave him to his own resources." [pastors-college-q015, pastors-college-q016] Physical/vocal fitness to preach was also weighed (a passage on candidates with speech or physical impediments follows immediately in autobio-v3 but was not formalized into a separate quote record this session; see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 6452 ff. for follow-up).
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: autobio-v2, autobio-v3, lectures1-selection, sword-trowel-bound). The sources read describe only men ("young men," "brethren," "students" referred to as "he"/"him" throughout); no statement about women's admission in any capacity was located.
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): Required: Spurgeon states the College "laid down, as a basis, the condition that a man must, during about two years, have been engaged in preaching, and must have had some seals to his ministry, before we could entertain his application." Candidates were received only if they already "profess[ed] to be ministers already" — "We never tried to make a minister, and should fail if we did." [pastors-college-q005, pastors-college-q010 ("two years' special tutorship" language), pastors-college-q015] This is independently corroborated, in closely parallel wording, by Lectures to My Students: "we receive none into the College but those who profess to be ministers already," following a discussion of the "seals" evidence of a true call — "his commission is without seals until souls are won by his instrumentality to the knowledge of Jesus." [pastors-college-q021, pastors-college-q020]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): Two years. Spurgeon: "I am then prepared ... to give them two years' special tutorship, that they may go forth to preach the Word, thoroughly trained so far as we can effect it in so short a time." [pastors-college-q009] Consistent with the "about two years" preaching-experience precondition for admission (A6), which is a separate two-year period prior to entry. [pastors-college-q005]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): NOT FOUND as an itemized syllabus in the sources read this session. The tutors named (C. H. Spurgeon; George Rogers; David Gracey, "classical tutor"; A. Fergusson, "English elementary classes"; a Mr. Johnson, evening classes; W. R. Selway, "the department of physical science"; J. A. Spurgeon, general educational oversight) imply a course spanning elementary English, classical/theological instruction, and physical science, but no ordered list of subjects or years was located. [pastors-college-q012, pastors-college-q013]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): The College had both day students "under constant tuition" (50 in 1861 per Spurgeon's own count) and a much larger number receiving instruction only "in the evening" (more than 150 in the same statement) while remaining in their own callings/trades. [pastors-college-q010] NOT FOUND: a term/semester structure or numbered class-year system (contrast with Geneva Academy's seven fixed classes; no equivalent was located here).
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: autobio-v2, autobio-v3, lectures1-selection, sword-trowel-bound). No language of instruction (Greek, Hebrew, Latin) is named in the passages read this session, though a "classical tutor" (David Gracey) is named, implying classical-language instruction; the sources read do not state which languages or to what level.
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND as a set curriculum list. Spurgeon names theological authors he prefers the College's teaching to follow in substance — "Manton to Maurice, Charnock to Robertson, and Owen to Voysey," and "the old theology of the Westminster Assembly's Confession" — but does not present these as an assigned reading list. [pastors-college-q025]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): Preaching practice was central and weekly: the Friday afternoon class ended with "the giving out of the appointments for the next Lord's-day," i.e., students' preaching engagements for the following Sunday were assigned there. [pastors-college-q026] The earliest recorded form of this instruction (from an 1863 account by Professor Fergusson) was a weekly meeting at Spurgeon's own house while students resided with Mr. Rogers, "instructed in theology, pastoral duty, preaching, and other practical subjects," described as "the nucleus of the present Monday and Friday classes" and of the published Lectures to My Students. [pastors-college-q008]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND as an academic/curricular examination. The only "examination" described in the sources read is the admission interview (see A4); no end-of-course or promotion examination is described.
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): Explicitly none sought: "Our men seek no Collegiate degrees, or classical honours, — though many of them could readily attain them." [pastors-college-q006] Students proceeded directly to pastorates on completion or on churches' demand, sometimes before training finished ("they are demanded by the churches even before the brief season of their training has expired"). [F.n. — this clause is part of the same letter as pastors-college-q010/q011 but was not separately quote-recorded this session; see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 5836-5838 for follow-up.]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a full daily/weekly timetable. The one fixed weekly point recorded is the Friday afternoon class with Spurgeon himself (originally also a separate weekly meeting at his house on Saturdays in the earliest years, per a different passage: "Every Saturday morning ... the visits of the early students ... marched down to Nightingale Lane"; this Saturday detail is in the raw text of autobio-v2 immediately before q008 but was not separately quote-recorded — see raw text/autobio-v2.txt lines 13557-13564 for follow-up), and Monday classes are named alongside Friday ones without further detail. [pastors-college-q008]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: autobio-v2, autobio-v3, lectures1-selection, sword-trowel-bound). No daily schedule is given in the sources read.
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): In the earliest period, individual students lodged with their tutor: Medhurst lodged with the Rev. C. H. Hosken at Bexley Heath (per the surrounding, not separately quote-recorded, narrative), then students resided with George Rogers at Albany Road, Camberwell, until the Tabernacle opened in 1861, "when ... the students migrated from Mr. Rogers' house to the class-rooms in the new sanctuary." [context for pastors-college-q008; the migration sentence itself is in the raw text of autobio-v3 immediately preceding q009's paragraph — see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 5663-5666 for a formal follow-up quote record] NOT FOUND: where the larger body of students (dozens, later ~100) resided once the College outgrew Rogers's house.
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): The Friday afternoon class itself had a devotional shape: after Spurgeon's teaching and correction, "came the wise counsel ... then the prayer that lifted us to the mercy-seat," followed by preaching-appointment assignments. [pastors-college-q014, pastors-college-q026] No separate chapel or daily-worship schedule was located.
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): NOT FOUND as a written disciplinary code. Spurgeon describes exercising personal discretion to dismiss unsuitable candidates before admission (A4) and, by implication, after: "a goodly number of beginners have received their quietus from me." [pastors-college-q016] No formal expulsion procedure or rule-book is described.
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: autobio-v2, autobio-v3, lectures1-selection, sword-trowel-bound).
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): Free. "We determined never to refuse a man on account of absolute poverty, but rather to provide him with needful lodging, board, and raiment, that he might not be hindered on that account... if he cannot contribute a sixpence, he is equally welcome, and is received upon the same footing in all respects." [pastors-college-q006] Students of independent means were permitted, and encouraged, to bear their own charges, but this was not required. [pastors-college-q006] Spurgeon states the same principle in a fundraising letter: "in most of our Colleges, the expenses are too great for poor men; indeed, to meet their case, there must be no cost at all to them, and they must be fed, housed, and clothed while under instruction." [pastors-college-q011] Funding itself came from Spurgeon's own income, his sermon-publication proceeds, and voluntary gifts and offerings, without pledged subscriptions or personal solicitation, as a matter of principle ("It is not consistent with our plan to ask anyone personally, or to request regular pledged subscriptions" — this clause is part of the same letter as q011 but not separately quote-recorded; see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 5792-5794 for follow-up).
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): 1856: one student, growing "ere long" to eight, then twenty, then "nearly one hundred men." [pastors-college-q004] By 19 May 1861 (Spurgeon's own count, given in a dated sermon-address): about sixteen students under full support/training plus "a very considerable number" receiving evening instruction. [context immediately preceding pastors-college-q009 in the same address, not separately quote-recorded — see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 5692-5695] By the letter quoted as q010 (undated within the passage read, "some seven years ago" from the College's beginning): 50 men "under constant tuition," and "more than 150" in evening instruction. [pastors-college-q010] By the end of 1878: over 500 students had been trained in total since the College began. [pastors-college-q017]
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): Of the more than 500 students trained by the end of 1878, twenty-five had "been 'called home'" (died). [pastors-college-q017] A former student serving as a missionary, Mr. D. Lyall of the Cameroons Mission, is recorded as having "fallen a victim to the terrible climate of Africa," leaving a widow. [pastors-college-q023]

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): George Rogers, tutor from the outset (from 1856-57). [pastors-college-q003] By the period covered in autobio-v3's account (up to 1878): David Gracey, "classical tutor"; A. Fergusson, teacher of the "English elementary classes"; a Mr. Johnson, assisting with evening classes; W. R. Selway, "the department of physical science"; J. A. Spurgeon (Spurgeon's brother), described as a "fellow-helper" whose "superior educational qualifications" raised "the tone of the instruction given"; and C. H. Spurgeon himself, who conducted the Friday (and Monday) afternoon classes personally, a role the text notes he "does not here mention" in his own written account. [pastors-college-q012, pastors-college-q013, pastors-college-q008]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): No formal charter of teachers' duties was located (see F5). Spurgeon's own account instead describes his personal role as examiner of candidates ("the duty which fell to the lot of Cromwell's 'Triers'") and as the teacher who "does not ... mention his own important part in the training of his students" beyond the Friday class. [pastors-college-q015, pastors-college-q012 context]
- **T3 Forms of teacher–student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): The dominant form recorded is Spurgeon's Friday afternoon class: an address (later published as Lectures to My Students), correction of individual students' preaching faults by direct imitation of their mannerisms, prayer, and the weekly assignment of Sunday preaching appointments, followed by an open period in which "came the men who wanted advice." [pastors-college-q014, pastors-college-q026] The earliest form of this contact (1857-1861) was a weekly meeting at Spurgeon's own house while students lodged with Rogers, described as instruction "in theology, pastoral duty, preaching, and other practical subjects." [pastors-college-q008] Spurgeon also corresponded with students by personal letter both during and after their training (illustrated by several letters reprinted in autobio-v3, e.g. to Frank White and Archibald Brown, not separately quote-recorded as their content is anecdotal rather than institutional).
- **T4 Named examples of teacher–student interaction with source** (PS): Spurgeon personally interviewed and admitted David Gracey after Gracey sought him out on the recommendation of a fellow student, Frank White, who had met Gracey during an evangelistic visit to Glasgow. [context surrounding pastors-college-q012/q013 in autobio-v3, not separately quote-recorded — see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 5905-5922 for follow-up] Spurgeon also personally judged, and sometimes declined, individual applicants on grounds of physical or vocal unfitness to preach, and once handed an applicant a letter in which the applicant's own pastor had privately warned Spurgeon that the young man (whom the pastor had publicly commended only to avoid offending the candidate's father, a deacon) was not fit for the ministry. [context immediately following pastors-college-q016, not separately quote-recorded — see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 6452-6461 for follow-up]
- **T5 Teacher:student ratio if computable (show the two numbers and years)** (PS): In 1878, with 50 men under constant day tuition (per the 1861 figure) grown to a considerably larger day cohort by 1878 (exact 1878 day-student figure NOT FOUND separately from the cumulative 500 trained since 1856), five or six named tutors are recorded as active at various points (Rogers, Gracey, Fergusson, Johnson, Selway, J. A. Spurgeon, plus C. H. Spurgeon himself). An exact ratio for a single year cannot be computed from the sources read this session; NOT FOUND as a stated ratio.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "When the Pastors' College was fairly moulded into shape, we had before us but one object, and that was, the glory of God, by the preaching of the gospel." [pastors-college-q005] Spurgeon's fundraising letter states the same priority in different words: "not scholarship, but preaching the gospel, — not the production of fine gentlemen, but of hard-working men." [pastors-college-q011]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Two stresses recur throughout the sources read: (1) evidence of an actual, fruit-bearing call to preach over formal scholarship — "we wanted, not men whom our tutors could make into scholars, but men whom the Lord had ordained to be preachers," and "'Have you won souls for Jesus?' was and is our leading enquiry of all applicants"; and (2) a named doctrinal stress — "the theology of the Pastors' College is Puritanic," standing by "the unfashionable doctrines of grace." [pastors-college-q005, pastors-college-q006, pastors-college-q025] The "Call to the Ministry" lecture states the same preaching-fruit test in general terms: "his commission is without seals until souls are won by his instrumentality." [pastors-college-q020]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Rejected: refusing men for poverty or lack of prior education (A2, L6); seeking "Collegiate degrees, or classical honours" as an end; and, doctrinally, "modern thought," which Spurgeon calls "at best, dilutions of the truth, and most of them old, rusted heresies, tinkered up again." [pastors-college-q006, pastors-college-q025]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): "Et teneo et teneor" ("I hold and am held"), printed as the College motto in Spurgeon's own account. [pastors-college-q007] (OCR renders the Latin as "KT TENEO ^T I' ET TENEOR"; the correct Latin, "Et teneo et teneor," is inferred from the given English translation "I hold and am held" printed immediately beside it in the source, not independently verified against a clean scan this session.)
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: autobio-v2, autobio-v3, lectures1-selection, sword-trowel-bound) as a dated restatement by a named later leader within the material read this session.

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): No separate licensing or ordaining body is described; students were placed directly into vacant pastorates, evangelistic posts, or missions, evidently through Spurgeon's own network and churches' direct applications to the College ("they are demanded by the churches even before the brief season of their training has expired" — context of pastors-college-q010/q011, not separately quote-recorded, see raw text/autobio-v3.txt lines 5836-5838). The Sword and the Trowel "College" notes record specific placements as settled facts without describing a formal sending ceremony or board. [pastors-college-q022]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): By the end of 1878, "the sons of the College had already found their way into all the four quarters of the globe," specifically named as India, China, Japan, Africa, Spain, Italy, the West Indies, South America, "a considerable contingent in the Australasian and Canadian colonies," and the United States. [pastors-college-q017, pastors-college-q018] Individually named placements from the Sword and the Trowel notes: New Zealand (Thomas Spurgeon, Spurgeon's own son, to Auckland); the Congo (Mr. Billington, Banana station); the Cameroons (Mr. D. Lyall); Japan (Mr. W. J. White, a Baptist convert baptized); and several English/Scottish pastorates (Newcastle-under-Lyme, Burslem, St. Andrew's, Lochee). [pastors-college-q022, pastors-college-q023, pastors-college-q024]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): To the end of 1878: over 500 students trained in total; of these, 450 were then engaged as "Baptist pastors, evangelists, and missionaries" (the source does not break this figure down by field vs. home pastorate); fewer than 300 of the 450 returned statistical forms, and those returns showed 3,600 baptisms in the single year 1878 and a net increase of 33,319 church members over the preceding fourteen years. [pastors-college-q017] No separate count specifically of men sent abroad as missionaries (as distinct from home pastors) was located this session.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a stated field-support principle (contrast with the CIM chapter's "faith principle"). The sources read describe College funding (free tuition, no solicitation) but not how graduates were supported once settled or sent.
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): Twenty-five of the 500-plus students trained by the end of 1878 had died ("been 'called home'"). [pastors-college-q017] Mr. D. Lyall of the Cameroons Mission "fallen a victim to the terrible climate of Africa." [pastors-college-q023] No aggregate missionary-specific death count (as distinct from the general 25-of-500 figure) was located.
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: autobio-v2, autobio-v3, lectures1-selection, sword-trowel-bound). No document analogous to the ABCFM's written "Instructions" to departing missionaries was located this session for the Pastors' College.
