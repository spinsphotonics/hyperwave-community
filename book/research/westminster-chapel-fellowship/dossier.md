# Dossier — Westminster Chapel (Lloyd-Jones ministry) and the Westminster Fellowship (`westminster-chapel-fellowship`)

Note on sourcing: this is not a chartered school, and no founding charter, constitution, or
rules document for either Westminster Chapel's pulpit ministry or the Westminster Fellowship
(the ministers' fraternal) was located as a digitized primary text this session. The roster
itself frames this institution as informal ("a congregation and ministers' fraternal used as an
informal school"), and the dossier below reflects that: most facts come from secondary
institutional pages (MLJ Trust, itself a body devoted to Lloyd-Jones's legacy) and from one
substantial secondary memoir (Powell 2007, Eusebeia) that quotes Lloyd-Jones's own published
words at second hand, with page citations. No Westminster Fellowship rules/aims document and no
Friday-night discussion transcript was located; both are marked NOT FOUND below. Two genuine
primary-adjacent short quotations (Campbell Morgan's spoken invitation, and Lloyd-Jones's own
words as reported by Fred Catherwood) were recovered at second hand from copyrighted sources and
are flagged `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` per the task's rights instructions;
see `book/rights/permissions_log.csv`.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The pulpit ministry is called Westminster Chapel (a Congregational, later Free Evangelical, church in the City of Westminster, London). The ministers' fraternal Lloyd-Jones led there is called the Westminster Fellowship (also "Westminster Ministers Fraternal" / "Westminster Ministers' Fraternal"), and, separately, the annual Puritan Conference he co-founded there was later renamed the Westminster Conference. [wcf-q001, wcf-q014] [SECONDARY: evangelical-times-westminster-fellowship-2014; eusebeia-powell-2007]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): Lloyd-Jones's own pastorate at Westminster Chapel began in September 1938, as associate minister to G. Campbell Morgan; he became sole minister on Morgan's retirement in 1943; he retired from the pastorate in 1968. The Westminster Fellowship was started in 1942. The Puritan Conference began in 1950. Discrepancy: one MLJ Trust page gives 1939, not 1938, for Lloyd-Jones's arrival at the Chapel; see discrepancies.md. [wcf-q002, wcf-q003, wcf-q004, wcf-q006, wcf-q007, wcf-q008, wcf-q015, wcf-q018, wcf-q019] [SECONDARY: evangelical-times-westminster-fellowship-2014; mljtrust-meet-mlj; mljtrust-faqs; mljtrust-puritan-conferences; eusebeia-powell-2007]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): D. Martyn Lloyd-Jones founded and led the Westminster Fellowship; he co-founded the Puritan Conference with J. I. Packer and O. Raymond Johnston, and chaired it. G. Campbell Morgan, minister of Westminster Chapel since before Lloyd-Jones's arrival, invited Lloyd-Jones to "come and join me" and "share the ministry" in September 1938, a call Lloyd-Jones later recounted in his own words. [wcf-q002, wcf-q004, wcf-q009, wcf-q015, wcf-q018, wcf-q019] [SECONDARY: evangelical-times-westminster-fellowship-2014; mljtrust-meet-mlj; mljtrust-puritan-conferences; eusebeia-powell-2007]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): Westminster Chapel, in the City of Westminster, London (Buckingham Gate, near Buckingham Palace and the Houses of Parliament, per background reading of eusebeia-powell-2007, not separately quoted here). The Fellowship met at Westminster Chapel itself for most of Lloyd-Jones's lifetime; a 2014 Evangelical Times article (outside this dossier's period, describing the Fellowship 46 years after Lloyd-Jones's retirement) records the Fellowship moving to Westminster Baptist Church, Horseferry Road, "after 70 years at Westminster Chapel." [SECONDARY: evangelical-times-westminster-fellowship-2014] NOT independently quoted for this dossier field beyond the meta-description excerpt already used in F1/F2.
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all documents in sources.csv). No constitution, rules, or founding minute for the Westminster Fellowship, and no charter for Westminster Chapel's pastorate, was located as a digitized text this session. See discrepancies.md and sources.csv row `westminster-fellowship-founding-rules-document`.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): No founding statement in Lloyd-Jones's own words was located for the Fellowship's original 1942 founding. A 2014 secondary description states the Fellowship is "a ministers' fraternal, open 'to men exercising ministry in a local church or among the churches' who give wholehearted agreement to its principles" -- itself already a later (2014) restatement, not the 1942 founding text, and its inner quotation marks ('to men exercising...') are not further sourced by this session to an original document. Powell (2007) states in his own words that the Fraternal "did much to open men's eyes and minds to the dynamic power of Reformation truth." [wcf-q001, wcf-q014] [SECONDARY: evangelical-times-westminster-fellowship-2014; eusebeia-powell-2007]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all documents in sources.csv). No source fetched this session records, in Lloyd-Jones's own words, why he began the Fellowship in 1942.
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all documents in sources.csv). The 2014 description's phrase "give wholehearted agreement to its principles" implies a doctrinal basis existed, but the principles themselves were not located in a fetched document.

A different, MLJ-Trust-described "Friday night discussion" format at the Institute Hall is also recorded, in which Lloyd-Jones made the audience work out a scriptural answer to questions they themselves raised. These two accounts (Sunday-afternoon vs Friday-night discussion meetings, both located "at the institute hall") were not reconciled by any single source fetched this session; see discrepancies.md.
