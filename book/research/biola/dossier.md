# Dossier — Bible Institute of Los Angeles (`biola`)

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): "The Bible Institute of Los Angeles," founded under that name 25 February 1908; "later called Biola" [SECONDARY: biola-hope-street-1985, timeline]. No primary source read this session gives an earlier working name distinct from this; the "Log Cabin Institute," an 1898-1901 Stewart-Prichard publishing venture named in the secondary source, is recorded as an antecedent (F9) rather than an earlier name of BIOLA itself, since the secondary text describes it as folding by 1903, before BIOLA's 1908 founding.
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): "The Bible Institute of Los Angeles was formally founded on February 25, 1908" [biola-q003]. First classes held 10 March 1908 at 260-264 South Main Street, above a pool hall [SECONDARY: biola-hope-street-1985]. No primary Articles of Incorporation text was located this session to confirm the incorporation date independently (see discrepancies.md item 2); this founding date is therefore PS rather than PO.
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): "Lyman Stewart was president, A.B. Prichard vice president, T.C. Horton superintendent, and Dr. W.E. Blackstone... served as dean" [biola-q003, context]. R. A. Torrey succeeded as dean in 1912 [SECONDARY: biola-hope-street-1985, timeline; see discrepancies.md item 1].
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Los Angeles, California; first classes downtown at 260-264 South Main Street; the Institute's permanent building on South Hope Street, cornerstone laid 31 May 1913 [SECONDARY: biola-hope-street-1985, quote_id biola-q005 context].
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No 1908 Articles of Incorporation or founding constitution text was located and fetched this session (see discrepancies.md item 2). The nearest primary self-description located and fetched is the back-cover advertisement of `biola-hunan-1916` (1916), naming the Institute "(EVANGELICAL AND EVANGELISTIC)" and stating it "Trains Men and Women, without Charge, for all Kinds of Christian Work" [biola-q001]. This is used in the chapter's Charter section in place of the unlocated founding document, with that substitution stated plainly.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO/PS): Primary (1916): "Trains Men and Women, without Charge, for all Kinds of Christian Work" [biola-q001]. Secondary, quoting the Institute's own 1908 stated purpose: "to 'train accredited men and women, free of cost, in the knowledge and use of the Bible.' The Institute was to be 'interdenominational' and have the Bible as its 'chief textbook'" [biola-q004].
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO/PS): Secondary, quoting Horton's own words: the confirmed need "in the minds of both men" was that "the greatest drawback was 'the fact that our leaders are not qualified by practical equipment'" [biola-q006]. The same secondary narrative states both founders were "concerned about the liberal tenets of modernism which were continuing to creep into the churches," but this sentence is the historian's own summary, not a quoted utterance, and is marked [SECONDARY: biola-hope-street-1985] rather than treated as the founders' own words.
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: biola-hunan-1916, biola-inspiring-story-1920). No text of BIOLA's own doctrinal statement was located this session. `biola-hope-street-1985` refers to "the Bible Institute Statement of Doctrine" as an item placed in the 1913 cornerstone time capsule, and to faculty "sign[ing] its doctrinal statement" during a 1928 controversy, confirming such a document existed and required faculty subscription, but does not quote its text [SECONDARY: biola-hope-street-1985].
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO/PS): R. A. Torrey, invited as dean, had earlier "designed the curriculum for the... Bible Institute" at Chicago (Moody Bible Institute) "which became the pattern for many all over the world" [SECONDARY: biola-hope-street-1985]. No primary source read this session states this imitation in BIOLA's own words; the roster's lineage map places BIOLA downstream of Moody Bible Institute (1886), consistent with but not independently confirmed by this session's sources beyond Torrey's personal move from Chicago to Los Angeles.
