# Discrepancies — Evangelical Library / IFES / Banner of Truth Trust (`evangelical-library-ifes-banner`)

## 1. IFES founding location: roster says "Lausanne"; every fetched source says Harvard/Boston, USA

The roster table's "Place" cell for this row reads "London; **Lausanne** (IFES
founding); London/Edinburgh." The task brief that commissioned this chapter
flagged this as needing verification rather than assumption. Every source
actually fetched this session gives a different location for the 1947
founding meeting:

- IFES's own site: "representatives from ten national student movements came
  together at **Harvard University, USA**" [elib-q013, document_id
  `ifes-together`].
- InterVarsity/USA's own site: "leaders... gathered at **Harvard University**"
  [elib-q016, document_id `iv-ifes-history`].
- Wikipedia's IFES article: delegates "came together in **Boston (USA)**"
  [elib-q021, document_id `wp-ifes`].

No source fetched this session names Lausanne as the founding meeting's
location. Lausanne, Switzerland is where IFES is legally registered as an
organization today (the footer of `ifes-history.html`, a 404 page, reads
"IFES, une organisation déclarée à Lausanne, Suisse" — not separately quoted
here as it came from a page-not-found page, not usable content). This
chapter states the founding location as Harvard University, Cambridge,
Massachusetts (near Boston), per the three sources above, and does not use
"Lausanne" for the 1947 meeting. The roster's "Lausanne" entry for the
founding is not supported by any source located and is presumed to be a
conflation with IFES's present-day legal domicile. Per Rule 9, this is
recorded rather than silently corrected; the Coordinator/human reviewer
should update the roster row.

## 2. Evangelical Library founding date: roster says 1945; fetched sources give 1938 and 1944, not 1945

The roster gives 1945 [HIGH] as the Evangelical Library's founding year. No
source fetched this session states "1945" for the Library. The dates that
were found are:

- 1938: "much encouraged in 1938 by the then associate minister of
  Westminster Chapel, Dr Martyn Lloyd-Jones" [elib-q010, document_id
  `wp-evangelical-library`]; Wikipedia's infobox separately gives
  "Established| 1938" [elib-q012].
- 1944: "Before the move in 1944 to its Chiltern Street location... it was
  known as the Beddington Free Grace Library" [elib-q011, document_id
  `wp-evangelical-library`], corroborated by the Evangelical Times/Wanderer
  account that the library "found its way to Chiltern Street... and became
  the EL" [elib-q007, document_id `et-el-appreciation`].

The Evangelical Library's own current "History" page (`el-history-page`,
elib-q001-q004) gives no exact founding year at all -- only "in the 1930s"
for when Geoffrey Williams began collecting. No source fetched this session
independently corroborates 1945 specifically. This chapter therefore states
1938 (Lloyd-Jones's encouragement / Wikipedia's "Established" date) and 1944
(the move to Chiltern Street and adoption of the name "Evangelical Library")
as the two dated milestones actually found, flags the roster's 1945 as
UNVERIFIED against fetched sources, and does not assert 1945 as fact.

## 3. Lloyd-Jones's IFES role: "chairman," "treasurer," and "president" all appear in different sources

The roster states Lloyd-Jones was "first IFES chairman" [HIGH]. One fetched
primary-quality source directly supports this: IFES's own tribute page for
his daughter Elizabeth Catherwood quotes the sitting IFES General Secretary,
Tim Adams, saying "her father was chairman of the meeting when IFES was
founded in 1947" [elib-q027, document_id `ifes-elizabeth-catherwood`]. A
search-engine-generated summary (not a page this session could fetch and
verify) separately asserted Lloyd-Jones was "the first treasurer of IFES" --
this claim is not used anywhere in this chapter or dossier because it could
not be traced to any fetched, quotable source; it is recorded here only so
the discrepancy is not silently lost. No fetched source used the word
"treasurer." A different fetched source, an obituary tribute (`csl-mlj-tribute`,
1981), does not mention IFES by name at all but says Lloyd-Jones was
"frequently of the Intervarsity Christian Fellowship" [elib-q046] --
ambiguous as between the British Inter-Varsity Fellowship (of which
Wikipedia separately confirms he became president in 1939, a different and
earlier body, elib-q050) and IFES itself. This chapter follows the
Elizabeth-Catherwood-tribute wording (chairman of the 1947 founding meeting)
as the only claim traced to an actual fetched quotation naming IFES
specifically, and does not repeat the unsourced "treasurer" claim.

## 4. Banner of Truth Trust founders: two vs. three named

Wikipedia and the Gospel Coalition's reproduction of Banner's own site both
name three founders -- Iain Murray, Jack Cullum, and Sidney Norton
[elib-q038, elib-q044, document_ids `tgc-banner-60`, `wp-banner`]. Iain
Murray's own first-person account (`et-banner-part2`) never mentions Sidney
Norton in the passage fetched, and says "Jack Cullum and I were the only
trustees" at the outset [elib-q035] -- with Sidney Norton entirely absent
from Murray's own telling of the founding. This chapter names Murray and
Cullum as the two founders documented by Murray's own first-person account,
and separately notes Norton as a third co-founder per Wikipedia and the
Gospel Coalition's quotation of Banner's own site, rather than silently
resolving the discrepancy.
