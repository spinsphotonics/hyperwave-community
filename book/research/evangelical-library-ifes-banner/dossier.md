# Dossier — Evangelical Library, IFES and Banner of Truth Trust (`evangelical-library-ifes-banner`)

**Note on scope.** This is a Tier B combined chapter covering three related
but administratively separate 1940s-50s organizations that grew out of D.
Martyn Lloyd-Jones's circle at Westminster Chapel: the Evangelical Library
(vision from the 1930s; dated 1938 and 1944 in fetched sources, see
`discrepancies.md` item 2), the International Fellowship of Evangelical
Students (IFES, founded 1947), and the Banner of Truth Trust (founded 1957).
Each has its own founders, founding date, and (where located) founding
document; fields below are answered per institution where they differ, and
jointly where a single answer covers all three. Westminster Chapel, the
Westminster Fellowship, the Puritan Conference and London Theological
Seminary are covered in companion chapters (VI-01, VI-03) and are not
repeated here except where a source names an overlap.

## F — Founding

- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Three
  institutions. (1) The Evangelical Library, earlier known as the Beddington
  Free Grace Library before it moved to Chiltern Street, London [elib-q007,
  elib-q009]. (2) The International Fellowship of Evangelical Students
  (IFES), founded under that name in 1947; a predecessor body, the
  International Conference of Evangelical Students, is attested by a
  surviving 1935 constitution held in the Wheaton archives [elib-q024]. (3)
  The Banner of Truth Trust, named "in continuity with the name of" its own
  magazine, The Banner of Truth, which had begun publishing in September
  1955, two years before the Trust itself was formed [elib-q041, elib-q045].
  [PRIMARY/SECONDARY, various]

- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): EL:
  Geoffrey Williams began collecting "in the 1930s" [elib-q001]; Lloyd-Jones
  encouraged the project "in 1938" and Wikipedia's infobox separately dates
  the Library's establishment to 1938 [elib-q010, elib-q012]; the library
  moved to Chiltern Street, central London, and took the name "Evangelical
  Library" in 1944 [elib-q011, elib-q007]. No source fetched this session
  states "1945," the year given by the roster; see `discrepancies.md` item
  2. IFES: founding meeting in 1947 [elib-q013, elib-q016, elib-q019,
  elib-q020, elib-q026]. Banner of Truth Trust: the trust deed was executed
  "on 22 July1957" [sic, exact wording as printed] [elib-q041]; Wikipedia's
  infobox agrees, "Founded| 1957 (1957)" [elib-q043]. [PRIMARY-quality
  institutional sources and SECONDARY Wikipedia, various]

- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): EL: Geoffrey
  Williams supplied "the vision," with Lloyd-Jones's encouragement and,
  later, "long" service as the Library's president [elib-q001, elib-q010,
  elib-q046]. IFES: representatives of ten national student movements
  founded IFES at the 1947 meeting; Lloyd-Jones "was chairman of the meeting
  when IFES was founded in 1947," per IFES's own General Secretary Tim
  Adams [elib-q027]; Stacey Woods served as first General Secretary,
  1947-1972 [elib-q022]. Banner of Truth Trust: Iain Murray and Jack Cullum
  are named by Murray's own first-person account as the founding trustees,
  "Originally, Jack Cullum and I were the only trustees" [elib-q035];
  Wikipedia and a secondary reproduction of the Trust's own site add Sidney
  Norton as a third founder [elib-q038, elib-q044] (see `discrepancies.md`
  item 4). Professor John Murray and the Rev. W. J. Grier were added as
  trustees soon after, on Iain Murray's own account [elib-q036]. Lloyd-Jones
  himself served the Trust as "helper and adviser" [elib-q047]. [PRIMARY:
  et-banner-part2; SECONDARY: others]

- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): EL: Beddington, Surrey, moving to Chiltern Street,
  central London, in 1944 [elib-q007, elib-q011]. IFES: founded at Harvard
  University, USA (Wikipedia: "Boston (USA)") [elib-q013, elib-q016,
  elib-q021] -- NOT Lausanne, Switzerland, despite the roster; see
  `discrepancies.md` item 1. Banner of Truth Trust: founded in London, "out
  of Westminster Chapel" [elib-q038]; the operation "was moved from London
  to Edinburgh" soon after Jack Cullum's death in 1971 [not separately
  quoted; summarized from et-banner-part2's narrative, see F2 and Sources].
  [PRIMARY/SECONDARY, various]

- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id**
  (PO): No full founding charter, constitution, or deed was located in full
  text for any of the three institutions. The single exception is a short
  clause reproduced (not the full document) from the Banner of Truth
  Trust's own trust deed of 22 July 1957: "The object of the Charity is to
  promote in such parts of the world as the Trustees may decide the better
  knowledge and understanding of the doctrines of the Christian faith as
  taught by the Protestant Reformers and English Puritans" [elib-q042,
  document_id `tgc-banner-60`, English, reproduced by a secondary source
  quoting banneroftruth.org's own page, which this session could not fetch
  directly -- see sources.csv row `banneroftruth-story`]. For IFES, a 1947
  constitution and basis of faith are attested to exist [elib-q014] but
  their text was not located; the Wheaton archives hold a 118-cubic-foot
  physical collection including "constitutions, incorporation documents"
  from the founding era, not digitized [elib-q023]. For the Evangelical
  Library, no constitution, rules, or deed of any kind was located.
  [PRIMARY: tgc-banner-60 (partial, quoted at second hand); NOT FOUND
  (searched: el-history-page, et-el-appreciation, wp-evangelical-library,
  ifes-together, iv-ifes-history, wp-ifes, wheaton-ifes-vault) for the rest]

- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): EL: "the Restoration
  of the Word of God at the heart of the Christian community, the
  continuing necessity of Reforming the church which teaches that Word --
  and, ultimately, the Revival of the people brought about by a personal
  relationship with Jesus Christ" [elib-q004]. IFES: the 1947 meeting's own
  stated purpose was "to bring together the existing free movements which
  possess the same doctrinal basis and evangelical outlook and to give the
  utmost possible assistance to evangelical students who desire to take the
  gospel of Christ to the universities of the world" [elib-q017]. Banner of
  Truth Trust: the trust deed's object clause, quoted in full at F5
  [elib-q042]. [PRIMARY-quality institutional sources, various]

- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): EL:
  Geoffrey Williams "recognised the anomaly of a Protestant nation which
  possessed a variety of specialist information libraries but lacked a
  national repository of the best in Protestant evangelical literature,"
  and that "many excellent evangelical works were fast disappearing from
  the public domain" [elib-q002, elib-q003]. IFES: an "unexpected telegram
  from the rapidly-growing movement in China, suggesting the formation of a
  global movement of students witnessing for Jesus" precipitated the 1947
  meeting [elib-q015]. Banner of Truth Trust: Iain Murray's Wednesday-night
  addresses on church history, given at Lloyd-Jones's direction, exposed
  Jack Cullum to "the way that God had worked in times past" and to books
  that were, in Murray's own account, "almost all long out-of-print,"
  regarded by contemporary publishers as commercially "unsaleable"
  [elib-q039, elib-q040; the "almost all long out-of-print" and
  "unsaleable" wording is narrative summary of the tgc-banner-60 passage
  rather than a separately logged quote_id]. [PRIMARY/SECONDARY, various]

- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): EL: not
  addressed in any source fetched this session -- NOT FOUND (searched:
  el-history-page, et-el-appreciation, wp-evangelical-library). IFES: the
  1947 founders "agreed on a basis of faith, a constitution and the
  autonomy of every national movement member" [elib-q014]; IFES's
  current site states, "The doctrinal basis of IFES shall be the
  fundamental truths of Christianity," including "The unity of the Father,
  Son and Holy Spirit in the Godhead" and "The divine inspiration and
  entire trustworthiness of Holy Scripture, as originally given, and its
  supreme authority in all matters of faith and conduct" [elib-q030,
  elib-q031, elib-q032] -- this is the doctrinal basis as currently posted,
  not confirmed as verbatim unchanged since 1947. Banner of Truth Trust: no
  separate doctrinal basis distinct from the object clause at F5/F6 was
  located; the Trust's own object clause commits it to "the doctrines of
  the Christian faith as taught by the Protestant Reformers and English
  Puritans" [elib-q042]. [PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY, various]

- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents,
  persons)** (PO): EL: none named in the sources fetched -- NOT FOUND
  (searched: el-history-page, et-el-appreciation, wp-evangelical-library).
  IFES: a predecessor "International Conference of Evangelical Students"
  is attested by a surviving 1935 constitution in the Wheaton archives
  [elib-q024]; the (British) Inter-Varsity Fellowship of Students, of
  which Lloyd-Jones became president in 1939 -- eight years before IFES's
  founding and a distinct body from it -- is also relevant antecedent
  context [elib-q050]. Banner of Truth Trust: the Trust's own monthly
  magazine, The Banner of Truth, "first appeared in September 1955," two
  years before the Trust that took its name was formed [elib-q045].
  [PRIMARY/SECONDARY, various]

## M — Emphasis

- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): EL: "the
  Restoration... Reforming... Revival" (see F6) [elib-q004]. IFES: Elizabeth
  Catherwood, Lloyd-Jones's daughter, recalled "IFES flowed through my
  father's living room," adding "I care passionately about IFES... I think
  our children regarded IFES as part of their DNA" -- her own later
  testimony, not a founding-era document [elib-q028]. Banner of Truth
  Trust: Iain Murray's own retrospective states plainly, "The ministry of
  Dr Lloyd-Jones was fundamental to everything," and that it was Murray's
  own church-history addresses, given "under [Lloyd-Jones's] direction,"
  that first drew Jack Cullum's interest [elib-q034, elib-q040]. [PRIMARY:
  et-banner-part2; SECONDARY: ifes-elizabeth-catherwood]

- **M2 Distinctive stress** (PO): Recovery and republication/reading of
  Reformed and Puritan literature (EL and Banner of Truth); international
  federation of existing national evangelical student movements without
  absorbing their autonomy (IFES, "the autonomy of every national movement
  member," elib-q014).

- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT
  FOUND for EL and IFES in sources fetched this session. Banner of Truth
  Trust: contemporary publishers' view that Puritan-era reprints were
  commercially "unsaleable" is the position the Trust's founding rejected
  in practice, per Murray's own retrospective narrative (see F7); not a
  separately logged verbatim quote_id.

- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND for EL and
  Banner of Truth in sources fetched this session. IFES's stated 1947
  purpose (F6, elib-q017) functions as the closest equivalent found.

- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS):
  Lloyd-Jones gave three addresses at the 1971 IFES Conference at Schloss
  Mitersill, Austria, later published by the Banner of Truth Trust as the
  pamphlet What is an evangelical? -- a direct, dated link among all three
  institutions covered by this chapter [elib-q048, elib-q049]. [SECONDARY:
  et-defenders-mlj-part2]

## R — Fruit

- **R1 Iain Murray** (1931- ; dates not independently confirmed this
  session): served three years as Lloyd-Jones's assistant at Westminster
  Chapel and, on his own account, co-founded the Banner of Truth Trust with
  Jack Cullum in 1957 [elib-q035, elib-q038, elib-q039]. [PRIMARY:
  et-banner-part2]
- **R2 Jack Cullum** (d. 1971): businessman who, persuaded by Murray's
  church-history addresses, financed the Trust "largely" from 1957 until
  his death in 1971 [elib-q033]. [PRIMARY: et-banner-part2]
- **R3 Stacey Woods**: first General Secretary of IFES, 1947-1972
  [elib-q022]. [SECONDARY: wp-ifes]
- **R4 Elizabeth Catherwood** (1927-2026), Lloyd-Jones's daughter: recalled
  IFES's founding at first hand through her father and remained involved
  with IFES for the rest of her life, latterly as Honorary IFES
  Vice-President [elib-q027, elib-q028]. [SECONDARY: ifes-elizabeth-catherwood]
