The **Banner of Truth Trust** is an
[Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical "Evangelical") and
[Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinist "Calvinist") non-profit[1]
publishing house, structured as a charitable trust[2] and founded in
[London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") in 1957[3] by [Iain
Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iain_Murray_\(author\) "Iain Murray
\(author\)"), Sidney Norton and Jack Cullum.[1] Its offices are now in
[Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh"), Scotland
with a key branch office and distribution point in [Carlisle,
Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle,_Pennsylvania "Carlisle,
Pennsylvania").[1] It positions itself within the [conservative
evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_evangelicalism_in_the_United_Kingdom
"Conservative evangelicalism in the United Kingdom") wing of the church, and
has been described as "an extremely powerful organization within British
[nonconformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconformist_\(Protestantism\)
"Nonconformist \(Protestantism\)") evangelicalism."[4]

The trust publishes a monthly magazine called _The Banner of Truth_
([ISSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISSN_\(identifier\) "ISSN
\(identifier\)") [0408-4748](https://search.worldcat.org/issn/0408-4748))
which normally appears eleven times per year, with there being a single issue
for August and September.[5] The magazine first appeared in September 1955[6]
and as of December 2010 had reached issue number 566.[7]

The Banner of Truth Trust also holds conferences in three countries: UK
(annual youth conference and annual ministers' conference[8]), United States
(annual conference[9]), and Australia (every two years[10]).

The trust has been connected with the revival of interest in
[evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical "Evangelical")
[Calvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism "Calvinism") in 20th
century England.[11] It has promoted Puritan theology[4] and helped resurrect
the ideas of [Jonathan
Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Edwards_\(theologian\)
"Jonathan Edwards \(theologian\)").[3][12][13] [Alister
McGrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alister_McGrath "Alister McGrath")
refers to the "revival in [Puritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritan
"Puritan") spirituality that had been borne aloft on the wings of Banner of
Truth's inexpensive paperbacks."[14]

The Banner of Truth Trust's logo depicts [George
Whitefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Whitefield "George
Whitefield") preaching.[15]
