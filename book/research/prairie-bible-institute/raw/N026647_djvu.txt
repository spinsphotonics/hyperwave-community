Presidents 


The expansion of an ever-growing organization such 
As Prairie Bible Institute sometimes calls for changes 
in the general administrative structure. To this end 
the creation of additional offices was conceived in 
order that executive responsibilities might be more 
evenly distributed. 

After much consideration and prayer, the Lord's 
guidance was discerned. Consequently, at a meet¬ 
ing of the Board of Incorporators, three new ap¬ 
pointments were made. Mr. A. H. Muddle was 
voted in as President, and Messrs. D. E. Crites and 
G. R. Imbach were elected to serve as Vice- 
Presidents. 

For Mr. Muddle this was the culmination of twenty 
years of loyal service on the Board of Directors. 
In addition, he has filled fhe position of Secretary- 
Treasurer of the Institute since 1950. 

Mr. Crites, who will be in charge of all personnel 
matters, joined Prairie staff ten years ago as man¬ 
ager of the Print Shop. Elected as a member of 
the Board of Incorporators in 1953 and as a Direc¬ 
tor two years later, he became chairman of the 
Board of Directors in 1956, a position which he 
still holds today. 

Mr. Imbach, elected to the Board of Incorporators 
in 1954 and the Board of Directors a year later, 
became Dean of Bible School men in 1956. He 
will supervise the business affairs of the Institute. 

These godly and well-qualified men realize their 
need of wisdom from God, and they covet the pray¬ 
ers of God's people on their behalf.

President Em. 


Celebrating their fortieth anniversary on the staff this year 
are Mr. and Mrs. J. Fergus Kirk. The pioneer of Prairie 
Bible Institute, Mr. Kirk was recently elected President 
Emeritus by a grateful Board of Incorporators. 

It was through his original vision that Mr. Maxwell came 
to Three Hills to teach the Bible. It was also largely on 
account of his willingness to sacrifice and refusal to sub¬ 
mit to adverse circumstances that Prairie was able to begin 
and to grow. 


Throughout the years he has tenaciously held to his original 
aim of training young people for missionary service, and it 
is partly due to his influence that Prairie has been able 
to keep this principle foremost. 

Today Mr. Kirk continues to serve as a member of the 
Board of Directors, and he remains an example of Christian 
living in social service and prayer.

Extension 



Throughout the winter months the extension ministry of the Institute is carried on 
largely by Rev. J. M. Murray and Rev. Ken Buchanan. These two men travel 
widely, preaching the gospel of Christ to church congregations throughout Canada 
and the United States. 



Director of the extension department at Prairie is Mr. Ernie James, a recent and 
welcome addition to the staff. He is responsible for the arrangement of winter 
preaching appointments and summer itineraries for the gospel teams.

CARMEN FIDEI 

(A Song of Faith) 

The sound of music . . . 

Across the plain a thousand knees went down 
Before an image on whose face 

Almost was cast reflection of the monarch's smile . 
and frown . . . 

For in the palace news had reached the king 
That there were three who dared to disobey 
The royal order — dared to stand — 

Though death be only one command away. 

The men were ushered in before the throne 
To face the image's reality. 

The king was gracious — "life is yours! 

If you will just kneel down and worship me." 

"If not," a sudden frown enforced the threat, 

"There is a furnace hungry for such fools. 

And where's the God who can deliver you? 

I'll prove to any gods there be — who rules!" 

But God had given a backbone to these three; 

The kind of courage that refused to tire 
Or compromise with any kind of wrong, 

Though threatened now to lose their lives — by fire. 

"Be sure of this, O King, the God we serve 
Is able to deliver us!" Bold claim 
To make within that hostile hall. 

Bold men — to dare to brave a martyr's shame. 

To dare to brave a tyrant's fiery rage — 

To dare be true when other men be wrong — 

To dare believe that God had power enough 
To change a martyr's shame to victor's song.

As the day of the big event drew near, curious questions were heard 
in the dormitories. "Anybody have a pair of cowboy boots?" "How 
can I make a Roman Toga?" "How did the Elizabethans dress?" 
"Where can I get some feathers?" 


At 6:00 p.m. on November 25, 1961, a procession of cowboys. In 
dians, Romans, and Elizabethans filed into the girls' gym, accom 
panied by other sundry creations. 


The evening passed swiftly as the seniors pondered over the identity 
of one another, forfeited beans, enjoyed a light lunch, and roared 
with laughter at the antics of their usually more subdued classmates.
