# Dossier — Prairie Bible Institute (`prairie-bible-institute`)

**Coverage note.** No constitution, incorporating statute, or founding-era catalog/prospectus
was located and fetched this session (see `discrepancies.md` and `sources.csv` for the BLOCKED
and NOT-FOUND search rows). In their place, this dossier rests on two Prairie Press pamphlets —
*Hoping For Nothing: The Miracle of the Prairie Bible Institute* (document_id
`pbi-hoping-nothing-1950`, reprinting a 1945 magazine article plus L. E. Maxwell's own historical
sketch, and itself carrying a "SCHOOL CALENDAR—1948-49" section) and *First Things First:
Beginnings back of "Hope for Nothing"* (document_id `pbi-first-things-first-1947`, by Hector Kirk,
brother of the Institute's co-founder J. Fergus Kirk) — both close to the founding generation and
containing Maxwell's own words in direct quotation, plus three Institute yearbooks (1954, 1962,
1975) and the school's own present-day retrospective website. These are treated as PO (primary,
or primary-adjacent institutional publications quoting the founders directly) except where noted
PS. *Born Crucified* (1945) could not be obtained in readable text form this session (see
`sources.csv`, rows `pbi-born-crucified-1945` and `pbi-born-crucified-lending`). Fields below are
filled as far as the fetched material allows; many PO fields are `NOT FOUND` because no true
governing document was located.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The school has been called the Prairie Bible Institute since its formal beginning in 1922 and, since a later era outside this session's fetched material, "Prairie College"; no change-of-name date beyond what Wikipedia states in passing was located and read this session. [prairie-bible-institute-q024, prairie-bible-institute-q025 — both refer to it as "Prairie College" (modern) narrating the 1922 "Prairie Bible Institute" founding]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO/PS): Bible-teaching classes for young people began in an empty farmhouse near Three Hills in the winter of 1921-22; a letter of appeal went to Dr. W. C. Stevens of the Midland School (Bible Institute), Kansas City, for a teacher; L. E. Maxwell "responded and came to Three Hills in the fall of 1922." [prairie-bible-institute-q011, prairie-bible-institute-q012] Prairie's own current website states the first class met 9 October 1922. [prairie-bible-institute-q025, PS] No incorporation or provincial-charter date was located and read this session; Wikipedia's Prairie College article mentions in passing that a 2026 legislative amendment "updates the institute's original 1946 incorporating statute," which would place formal incorporation in or before 1946, but no 1946 document itself was found or read. [SECONDARY: wikipedia-prairie-college, narrative paragraph on the 2026 amendment — not independently formalized as a quote_id this session; see discrepancies.md]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO/PS): J. Fergus Kirk, a Presbyterian farmer near Three Hills, Alberta, gathered his family for Bible study and, with his family, wrote to Dr. W. C. Stevens of the Midland Bible Institute, Kansas City, asking for a teacher; L. E. Maxwell, a graduate of Midland, answered the call and arrived in the fall of 1922. [prairie-bible-institute-q011, prairie-bible-institute-q023, prairie-bible-institute-q026] A 1962 Institute yearbook, marking the Kirks' fortieth anniversary on the staff, calls Mr. Kirk "the pioneer of Prairie Bible Institute" and states "it was through his original vision that Mr. Maxwell came to Three Hills to teach the Bible." [prairie-bible-institute-q018] Maxwell served as principal and later president for fifty-eight years, retiring in the spring of 1980. [prairie-bible-institute-q027, PS]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): Three Hills, Alberta, Canada; the first classes were held "in a fair-sized empty house on my brother-in-law's farm" before the Institute built its own campus in the village. [prairie-bible-institute-q012 (surrounding text), prairie-bible-institute-q011]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No constitution, statute, or founding prospectus was located and fetched this session. The closest available document is *Hoping For Nothing: The Miracle of the Prairie Bible Institute* (Prairie Press, ca. 1948-1950, document_id `pbi-hoping-nothing-1950`), which contains L. E. Maxwell's own "Further Sketch of History" of the school's beginning, principles, and course of study, together with a companion pamphlet, *First Things First* (1947, document_id `pbi-first-things-first-1947`), giving the Kirk family's own account of the 1921-22 origin. Neither is a governing charter; both are promotional/historical pamphlets published by the Institute itself and quoting the founders directly. This gap is recorded, not papered over — see `charter_abridged.md` headnote and `discrepancies.md`.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Maxwell's own words state the Institute's task as "to prepare trained, schooled, disciplined, and fit young people, to 'make up the hedge and to stand in the gap' on the far-fung [sic, OCR for 'far-flung'] missionary fronts." [prairie-bible-institute-q001]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): A Bible-study group under Kirk's leadership felt "the need of definite Bible teaching" for the young people of their farming families during the long prairie winters, and had no qualified teacher of their own; the group wrote away for one. [prairie-bible-institute-q012 (surrounding text at document_id pbi-first-things-first-1947, "the need of defi- nite Bible teaching, together with the oppor- tunity afforded by the long winter months when there is so little to do on the average prairie farm" — quoted in full in the text file; not separately broken into its own quote record beyond q012's context)] Prairie's own current retrospective agrees that "It was this goal that in 1921 caused local farmer J. Fergus Kirk to write Midland Bible Institute in Kansas City for a Bible teacher." [prairie-bible-institute-q023, PS]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No subscription document (a signed creed, a doctrinal statement students or faculty had to sign) was located and read this session. The nearest primary-adjacent statement is descriptive rather than a subscription clause: the Institute "stands firmly for the old faith, [be]lieving the whole Bible from cover to cover, and proclaiming salvation by faith through the blood of Christ." [prairie-bible-institute-q002]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): "Several of our family had studied at the Missionary Training Institute, Nyack, N.Y." before writing to Kansas City for a teacher, and the teacher who came, L. E. Maxwell, was himself "a graduate of the Midland Bible Institute, a short lived school of the Christian and Missionary Alliance in Kansas City." [prairie-bible-institute-q011, prairie-bible-institute-q026 (PS)] This places Prairie's own immediate lineage inside the same A. B. Simpson/Christian and Missionary Alliance Bible-institute network (Nyack, Midland) that this book's roster traces back through Moody and the East London Institute.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): The Bible School's "school year" ran six months, October to April; the High School term ran eight months. [prairie-bible-institute-q007] The pamphlets do not state how many such six-month years made up the full Bible-theological-missionary course; the subject list in C2 below (up to four years of a given subject) implies a multi-year course, but no single stated total (e.g., "a three-year course" or "a four-year course") was located. NOT FOUND (searched: pbi-hoping-nothing-1950, pbi-first-things-first-1947) for an explicit total.
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): Bible teaching itself used the Institute's "Guide Questions" method, described as directing "the pupil into his own findings in the Scriptures," of which "the late Dr. C. I. Scofield said, 'It works.'" [prairie-bible-institute-q005] Beyond Bible study, the course included "four years each of History (Ancient, Medieval, Church, Missions) and of English; three years each of Personal Evangelism and of Doctrine; two years each of Homiletics and of Bible Geography; and one year each of Public Speaking, General Biblical Introduction, Christian Evidences, Scriptural Refutation of Modernism and False Cults, Sunday School Teaching, and Church Supervision," plus music instruction. [prairie-bible-institute-q006] No order of subjects (which year each was taken) is given beyond the year-counts themselves.
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): Three departments: Grade School, High School, and Bible School, each a separate department of the one Institute. [surrounding text at prairie-bible-institute-q014 in pbi-first-things-first-1947.txt: "700 in the Bible School, 340 in the High School, and 90 in the Grade School"] Each Bible School year was a six-month term (C1).
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: pbi-hoping-nothing-1950, pbi-first-things-first-1947). No Greek, Hebrew, or other language-study requirement for the Bible School is stated in the fetched material (contrast the roster's note on Wheaton College, where Greek is independently attested for Elisabeth Howard).
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND beyond the Scripture-centered "Guide Questions" method itself (C2); no textbook list was located.
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): Homiletics (two years) and Public Speaking (one year) were part of the course of study. [prairie-bible-institute-q006] Practical, hands-on instruction also took the form of daily "gratis" work: "Each student has his part in the scheduled life of the institute by doing what is known to us as 'gratis' work for one and one-half hours each day. Whether milking cows or giving music lessons, he has opportunity to make practical use of what he learns in the classroom." [prairie-bible-institute-q019]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: pbi-hoping-nothing-1950, pbi-first-things-first-1947, pbi-servants-1954).
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): NOT FOUND for the founding-era course (searched: pbi-hoping-nothing-1950, pbi-first-things-first-1947). Wikipedia states, of a much later period, that the Institute "conferred associate's degrees and Bachelor degrees to its graduates since 1980" and became accredited by the Association for Biblical Higher Education in 1997 — both long after the Institute's founding generation and outside this chapter's period; not used as a founding-era fact. [SECONDARY: wikipedia-prairie-college, narrative paragraph — not independently formalized as its own quote_id this session]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): No full timetable with clock times was located. What is attested is the shape of the day: from "the rising-bell until the 'lights-out' knock," and 1.5 hours of daily "gratis" (practical work) duty. [prairie-bible-institute-q020, prairie-bible-institute-q019] No exact rising or lights-out time is stated in the fetched yearbook text.

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO/PS): The day ran "from the rising-bell until the 'lights-out' knock." [prairie-bible-institute-q020] A 1975 yearbook account, recalling a graduate's own mission-field adjustment, states that "Prairie's discipline did soften the blow of rising with the chickens. Sunset was our 'curfew siren' and meant we had only one more hour by candlelight" — this passage describes the effect of Prairie's training as later felt on the mission field, not a verbatim campus rule, and is used here only for that qualified purpose. [prairie-bible-institute-q022]
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): The campus included "more than a dozen large buildings as well as more than a dozen smaller ones, including five dormitories, high school, grade school, a small and a large tabernacle, powerhouse, laundry, barns, infirmary, a dozen houses for the faculty" and further classroom, dining, and printing buildings. [prairie-bible-institute-q009] No rule on which students lived in which dormitory, or with whom, was located.
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): NOT FOUND as a stated rule (searched: pbi-hoping-nothing-1950, pbi-first-things-first-1947, pbi-servants-1954); the 1947 pamphlet does mention "a gospel broadcast from the school each Sunday morning" [context surrounding prairie-bible-institute-q014b in pbi-first-things-first-1947.txt] but no chapel schedule.
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): No dormitory rule-book or list of penalties was located and read this session. The only discipline-adjacent language found is the retrospective 1975 alumna's phrase "Prairie's discipline did soften the blow of rising with the chickens." [prairie-bible-institute-q022] NOT FOUND for any specific rule or penalty text.
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: pbi-hoping-nothing-1950, pbi-first-things-first-1947, pbi-servants-1954, pbi-prairian-1962, pbi-prairian-1975).
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): Board and room cost "$5.00 per week"; a Bible School student needed "about $160.00 Canadian to cover the entire cost of board and room, laundry, and registration fees for the school year, six months in length, beginning in October and ending in April," and a High School student's costs for the eight-month term "would not exceed $220.00 Canadian." [prairie-bible-institute-q007] The school itself operated on the same faith/no-salary principle described for staff below (T2, S4): "we refuse to go into debt." [prairie-bible-institute-q010]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS/PO): 1946-47: "700 in the Bible School, 340 in the High School, and 90 in the Grade School" (total 1,130). [prairie-bible-institute-q014] Total attendance "about 1140 students in the three departments" as of the ca. 1948-1950 pamphlet. [prairie-bible-institute-q008] Wikipedia separately states the school's enrolment passed 900 students by 1948. [prairie-bible-institute-q028, PS] These figures are broadly consistent (900+ by 1948, ~1,130-1,140 by the later 1940s) rather than contradictory, but are recorded as given rather than reconciled into one number; see discrepancies.md.
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): No death or casualty figure was located. The 1950 pamphlet does describe winter conditions as a form of training hardship: "The simple fare and hardy [life] during the cold winters at Three Hills, when the thermometer sometimes goes to [fifty] below zero, give the students a fine preparation for the roughest kind of missionary labour" (paraphrased from surrounding text near prairie-bible-institute-q003 in pbi-hoping-nothing-1950.txt; passage not separately formalized as its own quote_id this session because of OCR corruption of the exact wording — the sense is accurately reported but the precise sentence was not clean enough to quote verbatim).

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): As of the mid-1940s (the "twenty-third year," i.e., ca. 1945): "total number of the faculty is sixty, including twenty-four teachers and thirty-six other workers." [prairie-bible-institute-q004] As of 1946-47: "Staff members now number 85, all of whom work voluntarily without salary." [prairie-bible-institute-q014b] These are different years and not a contradiction, but the two counts (60 total/24 teachers in ca. 1945; 85 total staff in 1946-47) should not be conflated; see discrepancies.md.
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): No formal charter of teachers' duties was located. Maxwell's own account states the faith/no-salary principle he set as a precedent for all staff from his own first months: "No one serving in any capacity receives any salary—only board and room and medical attention." [prairie-bible-institute-q013]
- **T3 Forms of teacher–student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): Classroom teaching using the Guide Questions method (C2); and shared "gratis" work duty, in which a student might, for instance, receive "music lessons" from a staff member as part of the gratis-work system. [prairie-bible-institute-q019, prairie-bible-institute-q005]
- **T4 Named examples of teacher–student interaction with source** (PS): A 1954 yearbook names the six senior-year Bible School teachers and their subjects: "L. E. Maxwell — Bible; J. M. Murray — Psalms; Ruth Thompson — Theme Writing; P. L. Bromley — Synoptics, Homiletics, Doctrine; D. E. Enarson — Acts; N. A. Charter — Missions." [prairie-bible-institute-q021]
- **T5 Teacher:student ratio if computable (show the two numbers and years)** (PS): Ca. 1945: 24 teachers (plus 36 other, non-teaching staff) to a total enrolment (all three departments) that the same source elsewhere puts near 1,000-1,100 (L7); a precise ratio is not independently computed here because the ca. 1945 enrolment figure by department was not separately given alongside the ca. 1945 staff count. ## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "to prepare trained, schooled, disciplined, and fit young people, to 'make up the hedge and to stand in the gap' on the far-fung missionary fronts." [prairie-bible-institute-q001] Maxwell's account of his own decision to take no fixed salary is framed the same way, as a matter of the "give" of divine nature over against the "get" of fallen human nature (paraphrased from the surrounding text near prairie-bible-institute-q013 and q010; the phrase "give or get" occurs in the pamphlet's later "Way of Growth" section but was not separately extracted as its own quote_id this session).
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): The "faith principle" applied to staff support (no guaranteed salary; T2, S4) and to institutional finance (no debt; M3), combined with a stress on missionary discipline. A later alumna's account of her own missionary field service explicitly credits "Prairie's discipline" for having "soften[ed] the blow" of hardship abroad. [prairie-bible-institute-q022]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Institutional debt: "we refuse to go into debt." [prairie-bible-institute-q010] Fixed salary for staff was declined as a matter of principle by Maxwell from his first months onward (T2, S4).
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): Two distinct mottoes are attested in fetched sources, from two different eras and (in the first case) a specific department, not the whole Institute: (1) a 1962 Institute yearbook states, of the High School specifically, "High school activities are planned in accordance with the school's motto, 'Deo Supremo'" ("God supreme"). [prairie-bible-institute-q017] (2) Prairie's own present-day (2022 centennial) website states, of the College as a whole, "Through the years, Prairie's mission has remained the same: To know Christ and make Him known." [prairie-bible-institute-q024, PS] Neither of these matches the phrase "training disciplined soldiers for Christ" that appears in the roster and in the title of Tim W. Callaway's 2010 secondary history of the Institute; no primary source located this session uses that exact phrase as the Institute's own motto, and it is not asserted as one in this dossier or the chapter. See discrepancies.md.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): At the College's 2022 centennial, President Mark Maxwell (L. E. Maxwell's grandson) said: "As I reflect on Prairie's early days, I am repeatedly impressed with the fact that L.E. and the team around him did a very good job. We look toward the next century and will continue to build on Prairie's solid foundation..." (paraphrased/partially quoted from prairie-edu-100years.txt surrounding the "Words fail to adequately reflect..." passage; not separately formalized as its own quote_id because the passage runs long and mixes reported and directly quoted speech in the source HTML in a way that made isolating a single clean verbatim sentence unreliable this session — flagged as a follow-up quote-record task rather than risking an inexact quotation).
