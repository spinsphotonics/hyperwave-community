# Discrepancies — prairie-bible-institute

1. **No true founding document (constitution, statute, or founding-era catalog/prospectus) was
   located this session.** The roster's "Key primary documents" cell names a "Prairie catalog /
   prospectus (1920s-1940s)," a "Prairie *Constitution* [VERIFY]," and Maxwell's *Born Crucified*
   (1945). None of the three was obtained as readable text this session: no catalog/prospectus
   digitized copy was found by search; no constitution or incorporating statute text was found
   (Wikipedia mentions in passing "the institute's original 1946 incorporating statute" without
   quoting it or citing where to find it — see item 2); and *Born Crucified* is present on
   Internet Archive only as a `.mobi` ebook file at one identifier
   (`BornCrucifiedByL.e.Maxwell`, no OCR text derivative) and as a controlled-digital-lending
   scan at a second identifier (`borncrucified0000maxw`) whose text and PDF derivatives return
   HTTP 403. `charter_abridged.md` uses two Prairie Press pamphlets instead (documented there and
   in the dossier's coverage note) and says plainly that they are not a charter.

2. **Incorporation date: 1946 (Wikipedia, in passing) vs. no primary confirmation.** The English
   Wikipedia article "Prairie College" states, describing a 2026 Alberta legislative amendment,
   that "The amendment updates the institute's original 1946 incorporating statute..." This is
   the only source read this session that gives any incorporation year at all. No 1946 statute
   text, and no independent secondary confirmation of 1946 specifically (as opposed to some other
   year), was located or read. Do not state "incorporated in 1946" in the chapter as an
   established fact; if used, it must be attributed explicitly to this single passing Wikipedia
   mention.

3. **Two different mottoes, from two different eras/departments, neither matching the roster's
   expected phrase.** A 1962 Prairie Bible Institute yearbook states the motto of the High School
   specifically as "Deo Supremo" ("God supreme") [quote_id prairie-bible-institute-q017]. Prairie
   College's own 2022 centennial website gives the mission of the whole College, "through the
   years," as "To know Christ and make Him known" [quote_id prairie-bible-institute-q024]. The
   roster row for this institution also names the phrase "Training disciplined soldiers for
   Christ," which matches the title of Tim W. Callaway's 2010 book about the Institute
   (*Training Disciplined Soldiers for Christ: The Influence of American Fundamentalism on
   Prairie Bible Institute (1922-1980)*) but was not found, in any source read this session, used
   by the Institute itself as its own motto. Do not state in the chapter that "Training
   disciplined soldiers for Christ" was Prairie's own motto; it is at most a later scholarly
   characterization/book title. All three phrases are recorded here without being resolved into
   one "the" motto.

4. **Sending and enrollment figures vary between the two pamphlets and Wikipedia, without being
   contradictory outright.** (a) Numbers sent to foreign mission fields: "over 275 young people"
   since 1922, per the 1947 pamphlet [quote_id prairie-bible-institute-q015], vs. "about
   one-third of the graduates" (a fraction, not a headcount), per the ca. 1948-1950 pamphlet
   [quote_id prairie-bible-institute-q003]. (b) Total enrollment: 1,130 across three departments
   in 1946-47 (700 + 340 + 90) [quote_id prairie-bible-institute-q014]; "about 1140 students" in
   the ca. 1948-1950 pamphlet [quote_id prairie-bible-institute-q008]; "over 900 students by
   1948" per Wikipedia [quote_id prairie-bible-institute-q028, SECONDARY]. (c) Total staff/faculty
   counts: 60 total (24 teachers, 36 other workers) in ca. 1945 [quote_id
   prairie-bible-institute-q004] vs. 85 total staff in 1946-47 [quote_id
   prairie-bible-institute-q014b]. None of these pairs is a strict contradiction (different years,
   or a headcount vs. a fraction), but the chapter must cite each figure to its own year and
   source rather than picking one as "the" number.

5. **Elisabeth Howard (Elliot)'s attendance at Prairie: year only, not independently confirmed by
   any Prairie-side document.** The Elisabeth Elliot Foundation's own timeline states only, under
   "1948": "Elisabeth spends the year at Prairie Bible Institute in Alberta, Canada" [quote_id
   prairie-bible-institute-q030]. This is the same fact, and the same single sentence, already
   used with the same caution in `research/wheaton-college/dossier.md` and its discrepancies file
   (which likewise records "1948" rather than "1948-49"). No document actually published by
   Prairie Bible Institute — no yearbook, class list, or catalog — naming Elisabeth Howard was
   located or read this session, despite three Institute yearbooks (1954, 1962, 1975) being
   fetched and searched; none falls in the 1948-49 window, and none happens to mention her by
   name. Wikipedia's "Prairie College" article lists "Elisabeth Elliot" under "Notable alumni,"
   but that whole list carries Wikipedia's own unsourced-names warning banner [quote_id
   prairie-bible-institute-q029], so it is not treated here as independent confirmation. The
   chapter should state the Prairie/Elisabeth Howard connection exactly as narrowly as the dossier
   does: attested by Elliot-side sources for the single year 1948, not by any Prairie institutional
   record read this session.
