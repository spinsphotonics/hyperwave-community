# Dossier — London Missionary Society (`london-missionary-society`)

Note on sourcing: the LMS is a missionary sending society, not a school, so several template
fields designed for an educational institution (Admission, Curriculum, most of Common Life
and Teachers) do not apply to it as such; those fields are answered honestly as NOT
APPLICABLE rather than stretched to fit. This chapter's two charter documents, per the
roster, are the 1795 *Plan of the Society* and the 9 May 1796 *Fundamental Principle*
minute, both quoted in full from Lovett's centenary history (1899), which reproduces them
directly from the Society's own minute-book.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The 1795 Plan of the Society names it, simply, "The MISSIONARY Society" (Article I); it is known to later scholarship, and in this book's roster, as the London Missionary Society (LMS). [lms-q002]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): A preparatory meeting on the evening of 21 September 1795, "intended to be merely preparatory," was, per Lovett, the meeting "at which the Society was actually founded"; at it, the assembled ministers and laymen resolved "with perfect unanimity, that it is the opinion of this meeting, that the establishment of a society for sending Missionaries to the heathen and unenlightened countries is highly desirable." [lms-q001] The Plan of the Society, prepared by a committee, was read and approved the following day, 22 September 1795. The Fundamental Principle was separately adopted at "the meeting of Directors held at the Castle and Falcon on May 9, 1796." [lms-q004, context]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): The committee appointed on 21 September 1795 "to bring forward the plan of the Society" comprised Thomas Haweis, Rowland Hill, John Eyre, Samuel Greatheed, John Hey, George Lambert, David Bogue, James Steven, Alexander Waugh, John Reynolds, John Love, John Saltern, and Thomas Kingsbury. The Directors present when the Fundamental Principle was adopted (9 May 1796) were "Messrs. Waugh, Sir Egerton Leigh, Neale, Robert Steven, Hardcastle, Wilks, Eyre, Piatt, Cowie, Joseph Wilson, Alday, Haweis, Burder, Brooksbank, Campbell, Hill," with Thomas Wilson in the chair.
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): London (the Castle and Falcon, Aldersgate Street, for the preparatory and 9 May 1796 meetings; Spa Fields Chapel, Haberdashers' Hall, and Surrey Chapel for the September 1795 public services).
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): The *Plan of the Society*, adopted 22-24 September 1795, and the *Fundamental Principle* minute of 9 May 1796, both document_id `lovett1` (reprinted within Lovett's 1899 history from the Society's own minute-book). [lms-q002, lms-q004]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Plan of the Society, Article II: "The Object -- The sole object is to spread the knowledge of Christ among heathen and other unenlightened nations." [lms-q002] The Fundamental Principle states the Society's non-denominational basis: "As the union of God's People of various Denominations, in carrying on this great Work, is a most desirable Object, so, to prevent, if possible, any cause of future dissention, it is declared to be a fundamental principle of the Missionary Society, that our design is not to send Presbyterianism, Independency, Episcopacy, or any other form of Church Order and Government." [lms-q004]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PS): [SECONDARY: lovett1, narrative framing] Lovett's narrative frames the Society's origin in a series of 1794-95 pulpit appeals (by Bogue, Burder, and others) calling for cooperative, cross-denominational missionary action; the meeting itself resolved, without dissent, "that the establishment of a society for sending Missionaries to the heathen and unenlightened countries is highly desirable." [lms-q001]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): The Fundamental Principle itself functions as the Society's doctrinal-basis clause by explicitly declining to require subscription to any particular church polity: the choice of "such form of Church Government, as to them shall appear most agreeable to the Word of God" is left "to the minds of the Persons whom God may call into the fellowship of His Son" among the converts, not fixed in advance by the Society. [lms-q004, lms-q005, lms-q006]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): NOT FOUND as an explicit citation of an earlier school or document in the passages read this session (searched: lovett1); Dr. Haweis's inaugural sermon (22 Sept. 1795) explicitly sets the new Society apart from precedent by rejecting "schemes of worldly advantage," "projects of vain ambition," and the ordinary marks of institutional founding, and by preferring a "plain man... from the forge or the shop" to "all the learning of the schools." [lms-q007, lms-q009]

## A — Admission
Not applicable in the school sense: the Society appointed and sent missionaries; it did not
admit a student body. The Plan of the Society does, however, define who could become a
*member* of the Society itself (a subscriber or benefactor, not a missionary candidate):
"The Members -- Persons subscribing one guinea or more annually; every benefactor making a
donation of ten pounds." [lms-q003] On missionary candidates specifically, Dr. Haweis's
1795 sermon states a qualification in principle rather than a formal rule: missionaries
should be "such as the Lord hath prepared and qualified for the arduous task... really
moved by the Holy Ghost to devote themselves to the work." [lms-q008]

## C — Curriculum
Not applicable: the Society ran no course of study of its own at its founding. Haweis's 1795
sermon addresses the question of missionary education directly, preferring practical piety
to formal learning: "A plain man, -- with a good natural understanding, -- well read in the
Bible, -- full of faith, and of the Holy Ghost, -- though he comes from the forge or the
shop, would, I own, in my view, as a missionary to the heathen, be infinitely preferable to
all the learning of the schools." [lms-q009] (Bogue's later Gosport Academy, named in the
roster as the Society's training arrangement, was not reached in the chapters read this
session; see discrepancies.md item 2.)

## T — Teachers
Not applicable in the school sense. The Society's governing body was its Board of Directors
(twenty-five elected at the first meeting, per Plan of the Society Article V) and its paid
Secretary; see F3 for named Directors present at the 9 May 1796 meeting.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Non-denominational union in the missionary work itself is named as the Society's fundamental principle, "declared" specifically "to prevent... any cause of future dissention." [lms-q004] Haweis's sermon opens by disclaiming worldly motive: "No schemes of worldly advantage -- no projects of vain ambition -- no selfish ends or aims -- contaminate our views." [lms-q007]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Interdenominational cooperation without imposing church polity on converts (F6/F8, M1); a preference for spiritual qualification over formal education in choosing missionaries: "A plain man... full of faith, and of the Holy Ghost... would... be infinitely preferable to all the learning of the schools." [lms-q009]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Denominational imposition: the Society's design is explicitly "not to send Presbyterianism, Independency, Episcopacy, or any other form of Church Order and Government." [lms-q004]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): "The Fundamental Principle" is itself treated, by Lovett's own chapter heading and narrative, as the Society's defining watchword from 1796 onward. [lms-q004]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The Society's Board of Directors, per the Plan of the Society, examines and directs missionaries through its own or subdivided committees: Article V empowers the Directors to subdivide "into committees, for managing the funds, conducting the correspondence, making reports, examining missionaries, directing the missions, &c.," though "no act of these committees shall be valid till ratified at a monthly meeting," and the Directors may not "enter upon a new mission till they obtain the general concurrence" of a general meeting of the Society. [lms-q002, lms-q003, context]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PO): At the meeting of ministers on 24 September 1795, "Matthew Wilks proposed that the first attempt of the Society should be to send missionaries to the South Seas," and volunteers were solicited for that field on the spot. [lms-q010]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PO): At that same 24 September 1795 meeting, "Dr. Haweis was asked to speak on this subject... and gave in the name of one volunteer ready to go; Mr. Bogue mentioned another, and seven names in all were registered" -- the first recorded tally of missionary candidates. [lms-q010] No further running total over a longer period was found in the chapters read this session.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a stated field-support principle in the passages read this session (searched: lovett1); the Plan of the Society (Article VII) does specify that "the secretary shall receive such a salary as the directors may appoint: but the directors themselves shall transact the business of the Society without any emolument" -- a rule about the Society's home officers, not its field missionaries.
