[wycliffe-sil-q001, wycliffe-sil-q010, wycliffe-sil-q015, wycliffe-sil-q018, wycliffe-sil-q019, wycliffe-sil-q021, wycliffe-sil-q032, wycliffe-sil-q034] [HIGH for 1934 Camp Wycliffe opening and 1942 WBT founding; see Discrepancies for the SIL incorporation year]

- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Townsend, selling Spanish Bibles in Guatemala from 1917, "soon realized that the indigenous people couldn't use his Bibles – they didn't speak Spanish." A 1933 prayer meeting is recorded (at second hand, via Wikipedia quoting Hartch 2006) as the occasion on which Townsend and Legters received what they described as a call "for the purpose of meeting with the government to get permission for sending men into the Indian tribes to learn the languages and to translate the Bible into those Indian tongues." Wycliffe Bible Translators was created as a separate 1942 organization specifically because "the Mexican government did not allow missionary work through its educational system," and because, per Wycliffe Australia's history page, "friends of the work strongly recommended the formation of a society specifically focused on Bible translation" once Camp Wycliffe's original translators-for-other-agencies model proved insufficient. Historian Mark Noll is quoted (at second hand, via Encyclopedia of Arkansas) calling the founding of what became Wycliffe Bible Translators an event that "may stand symbolically for one of the great Christian events of the age." ## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Townsend is reported to have said "that the Bible in the language of the people was the best missionary of all, because it 'never got sick, never took a furlough, and never sounded like a foreigner'" (quoted without a specific citation in the fetched Wikipedia text). Pike calls the summer course itself "a turning point in my life." Townsend's tombstone at the JAARS center is inscribed: "Dear Ones: by love serve one another. Finish the task. Translate the Scriptures into every language." A third-hand-quoted exchange (via Christianity.com, citing Wallis and Bennett 1959) has an unnamed indigenous chief ask Townsend, "Why haven't you come sooner . . . . We have been wondering what sin we committed against God that kept him from sending you to us," to which Townsend reportedly answered, "The fault is not theirs. God has sent but we have refused to go."
