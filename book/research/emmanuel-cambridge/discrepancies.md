# Discrepancies — emmanuel-cambridge

1. **Numbering of the "seed-ground" statute.** E. S. Shuckburgh (1904, p. 22) calls the founder's
   purpose-statement "his nineteenth statute" [emmanuel-cambridge-q006]. The Victoria County History
   (1959, p. 474 and fn. 11) numbers the same statute "Statute XXI" [emmanuel-cambridge-q032,
   emmanuel-cambridge-q034]. This session located and read the original 1585 Latin statutes
   themselves (commdoc1852v3, the 1852 reprint from the British Museum Sloane MS.): the chapter
   containing the "seed-ground" (*seminarium*) language is there headed "Car. XXI" — i.e. Chapter 21
   — which agrees with the Victoria County History's numbering, not Shuckburgh's. Do not state a
   statute number in the chapter without attributing it, or state only "Statute XXI (so numbered in
   the original; Shuckburgh 1904 calls it the nineteenth)."

2. **Range of annual admissions under Chaderton, 1586-1611.** Shuckburgh (1904, p. 36) states that
   after 1586 "they vary from twenty-seven to forty-two till 1611" [emmanuel-cambridge-q014]. The
   Victoria County History (1959, p. 475) states that "Between 1586 ... and 1611, admissions varied
   between 27 and 44" [emmanuel-cambridge-q037]. The low figure (27) agrees; the high figure differs
   (42 vs. 44). Both sources agree on a peak of 79 admissions in 1622-3. Not resolved; both figures
   are recorded in the dossier field L7 with their sources; the chapter should either give the range
   as "27 to about 40" or cite one source explicitly rather than silently picking a number.

3. **Date of the supplementary statute reserving a chamber for the founder's descendants.**
   Shuckburgh (1904, p. 5) dates this supplementary statute to "December of that year [1587]"
   (in the context of building work "approaching completion by the end of 1587"). The 1852 Commission
   reprint of the statute itself (commdoc1852v3) prints its date as "December, 1578" — five years too
   early to fit either the building chronology (the range it refers to was not yet built in 1578) or
   Shuckburgh's own dating. This is almost certainly an OCR misreading of "1587" as "1578" in the
   1852 print, or an error already present in the 1852 print/its manuscript source; this session
   could not adjudicate which, since only the OCR text (not a page image) was examined. Not used as a
   quoted, dated fact in the dossier or chapter; flagged here for a human to check against a clean
   scan or the original manuscript.

4. **"Acorn" anecdote — earliest source and reliability.** The roster row flags this anecdote itself
   for verification ("[VERIFY wording and source]"). This session traced it to Thomas Fuller,
   *The History of the University of Cambridge* (1655), Book VI, section 17-19
   [emmanuel-cambridge-q001], the earliest printed source located. Fuller wrote 66 years after
   Mildmay's death (1589) and was not an eyewitness to the exchange with Elizabeth I; he introduces it
   only as something reported ("the queen told him..."), with no citation of his own source. No
   16th-century or early-17th-century source for the exchange was searched for or found this session.
   The anecdote is therefore SECONDARY throughout this dossier and chapter, attributed to Fuller by
   name and date, never stated as verified fact of what Mildmay or Elizabeth actually said. See also
   scout_notes.md.

5. **Founding date: charter vs. deed vs. statutes vs. site purchase.** Multiple dates appear across
   the sources for "when Emmanuel College was founded," none contradicting the others but easily
   conflated: the royal charter empowering Mildmay to found a college is dated 11 January 1583/84
   [emmanuel-cambridge-q004]; Mildmay's own deed of foundation is dated 25 May 1584
   [emmanuel-cambridge-q004]; the first admissions are recorded from 1 November 1584
   [emmanuel-cambridge-q005]; and Mildmay's Statutes for governing the College are dated 1 October
   1585 [emmanuel-cambridge-q005, emmanuel-cambridge-q031]. The Victoria County History (1959, p.
   474) additionally states the site itself was purchased and conveyed to Chaderton and Culverwell in
   June and November 1583 — a year earlier than Shuckburgh's account of the same conveyance, which
   Shuckburgh dates to 12 June 1583 (agreeing on the year) conveyed onward to Mildmay on 23 November
   1583 (VCH: "23 November 1583"; Shuckburgh's own text, not separately quoted here, gives the same
   November conveyance without a discrepancy on that specific point). The roster gives "1584 [HIGH]"
   as the founding date, which this session's sources support as the date of the charter and deed of
   foundation; 1585 is the date of the statutes, not of the founding.
