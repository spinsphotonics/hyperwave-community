# Discrepancies — judson-triennial-convention

1. **Addressee of Judson's 1812 change-of-views letter: Baldwin/Bolles (per the E-ticket)
   vs. the Third Church in Plymouth (per the letter Wayland actually reprints).** The
   E-judson-triennial-convention ticket instructs: "Extract Judson's letters to
   Baldwin/Bolles (1812) on his change of views." The letter Wayland 1853 quotes at length
   (and which this dossier uses, quote_ids judson-triennial-convention-q004 and -q005) is
   addressed to the Third Church in Plymouth, Massachusetts -- the congregation of which
   Judson had been a member -- not to Thomas Baldwin or Lucius Bolles by name. Baldwin and
   Bolles do appear elsewhere in the fetched material as Boston-area Baptist ministers
   active in the same events (Baldwin edited the Massachusetts Baptist Missionary Magazine
   and later signed the 1814 Constitution as its Secretary; Bolles is named in Wayland's
   narrative as fostering the 1812 Salem Bible Translation and Foreign Mission Society), but
   a separate letter from Judson addressed specifically to either man was not located and
   fetched this session. This dossier uses the Plymouth-church letter, which is the
   document Wayland actually quotes verbatim, and records this substitution honestly rather
   than inventing or mislabeling a Baldwin-addressed letter. See sources.csv row
   `judson-baldwin-1812-letter`.

2. **"Judson and Ann became Baptists and were baptized at Calcutta by William Ward, Sept
   1812" (roster known-facts sheet [HIGH]) -- the specific baptizer's name (William Ward)
   was not independently confirmed by a quoted passage this session.** Wayland 1853 states
   plainly that Judson "was baptized by immersion, and joined the Baptist church in that
   city" (quote_id judson-triennial-convention-q003) but the fetched passages read this
   session do not name William Ward specifically as the officiant, nor give the precise
   September 1812 date in a passage that was formally quoted. This dossier therefore does
   not assert the officiant's name or the exact September date beyond what is quoted; the
   roster's fuller claim is not contradicted, only not independently re-verified from a
   quoted primary passage this session.

3. **Constitution's printer name (OCR-damaged).** The 1814 Constitution pamphlet's
   title-page imprint is badly OCR-damaged ("PHILADELPHLV : ERINT&S FOR THE CONVENTION, BY
   ANW COIE"), evidently "Philadelphia: Printed for the Convention, by Anderson & Meehan"
   or a similar period printer's name; this dossier does not guess the printer's name beyond
   what is legible and records the OCR string as-is in the text/ header rather than silently
   correcting it.
