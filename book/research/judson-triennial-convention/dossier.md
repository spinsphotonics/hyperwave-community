# Dossier — Adoniram Judson and the Baptist General Convention / "Triennial Convention" (`judson-triennial-convention`)

Note on sourcing: this institution, like the ABCFM, is a missionary sending body rather than
a school, so Admission/Curriculum/Teachers fields are answered honestly as NOT APPLICABLE
where the template does not fit. The chapter's charter document is the 1814 Constitution of
the General Missionary Convention (document_id proceedings1814), quoted in full in
charter_abridged.md. Judson's own account of his change of views on baptism is quoted at
length from Francis Wayland's 1853 memoir (document_id wayland1853v1), which reprints
Judson's 1812 letter to the Third Church in Plymouth verbatim in quotation marks; that
quoted letter, and the quoted 1813 letters of Rev. Daniel Sharp, are treated PRIMARY.
Wayland's own connective narrative is SECONDARY.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PO): "The General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America, for Foreign Missions" (1814), commonly called the "Triennial Convention" because it met every three years; renamed the "American Baptist Missionary Union" in 1845. [judson-triennial-convention-q009, judson-triennial-convention-q011, judson-triennial-convention-q012]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): Delegates assembled on 18 May 1814 in the First Baptist Church, Philadelphia; the Constitution was read, debated, and, after a committee redraft, adopted article by article over several days, with the final unanimous adoption vote recorded in the minutes ("Shall this Constitution as now read be adopted, as the basis of union, and the rule of conduct to be observed by this Convention and its board of Commissioners?" -- "The vote was unanimously passed in the alfirmative"). This followed, by roughly two years, Judson's own change of views: he was ordained at Salem on 6 February 1812 and sailed for the East on 19 February 1812; Wayland dates his conviction on infant baptism to the sea passage itself, and his baptism by immersion to shortly after his arrival at Calcutta. [judson-triennial-convention-q001, judson-triennial-convention-q002, judson-triennial-convention-q003, judson-triennial-convention-q009, judson-triennial-convention-q014]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): The Constitution is signed "RICHARD FURMAN, President" and attested by "THOMAS BALDWIN, Secretary." Luther Rice, who like Judson changed his views on baptism en route to the East and returned to America, is credited by Wayland with traveling the country organizing local societies and promoting the general convention that followed. [judson-triennial-convention-q015, judson-triennial-convention-q009]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): The founding meeting was held in the First Baptist Church, Philadelphia; the Constitution itself was signed there. [judson-triennial-convention-q009]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): The Constitution of "The General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America, for Foreign Missions," adopted May 1814. English. document_id: proceedings1814.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The Constitution's preamble states its purpose as "carrying into effect the benevolent Intentions of our Constituents, by organizing a plan for eliciting, combining, and directing the Energies of the whole Denomination in one sacred effort, for sending the glad tidings of Salvation to the Heathen, and to nations destitute of pure Gospel-light." [judson-triennial-convention-q010]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Both fetched sources present the Convention as a direct response to Judson's (and Rice's) change of views on baptism, which "was just the event which was required to awaken the dormant energies of the Baptists in America, and concentrate them all...upon one object." Rev. Daniel Sharp's 1813 letter to Judson states the Boston Baptists "considered it as the voice of God calling us to the formation of a missionary society" on learning of "your change of views on the subject of Christian baptism." [judson-triennial-convention-q008, judson-triennial-convention-q006, judson-triennial-convention-q007]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): The Constitution does not print a separate doctrinal creed comparable to Andover's Associate Creed; its doctrinal test is implicit in Article V's requirement that missionaries be "in full communion with some regular Church of our Denomination" (i.e., a Baptist church) and furnish "satisfactory evidence of genuine Piety, good Talents, and fervent Zeal for the Redeemer's Cause." [judson-triennial-convention-q013]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PS): Wayland's narrative traces the Convention's antecedents to a proliferation of local Baptist missionary societies formed independently in American cities on news of Judson's and Rice's change of views (including a Boston society, "The Baptist Society for Propagating the Gospel in India and other Foreign Parts"), which lacked "any bond of connection" until Rice, newly returned from India, was asked to travel the country and organize a general convention. [judson-triennial-convention-q008; SECONDARY: wayland1853v1, pp. 121-126, narrative connecting these societies to the 1814 convention]

## A — Admission
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): Under the Constitution, only persons "in full communion with some regular Church of our Denomination, and who furnish satisfactory evidence of genuine Piety, good Talents, and fervent Zeal for the Redeemer's Cause, are to be employed as Missionaries" -- this is a standard for employment as a missionary under the Board, not admission to a school. [judson-triennial-convention-q013]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The record centers everything on Judson's own reasoning from Scripture: on shipboard, reflecting on his coming ministry among converts, he asked himself "how...am I to treat the unconverted children and domestics of the converts?" and concluded, after examining the New Testament evidence for infant baptism, that "in a double sense, I was unbaptized, and I felt the command of Christ press on my conscience." Daniel Sharp's letter frames the American Baptists' response in the same providential key: "We considered it as the voice of God calling us to the formation of a missionary society." [judson-triennial-convention-q004, judson-triennial-convention-q005, judson-triennial-convention-q007]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): A single doctrinal point -- the mode and proper subjects of baptism, examined directly from Scripture rather than inherited practice -- reorganized an entire national body of churches around foreign missions; the Constitution's own machinery (Article V) then makes credible personal piety and church-membership testimony, not formal theological training, the standard for missionary employment. [judson-triennial-convention-q005, judson-triennial-convention-q013]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT FOUND as an explicit warning-clause in the Constitution itself (searched: proceedings1814); Wayland's narrative frames the change of views as producing "momentary irritation" between Baptists and Pedobaptists that "rapidly passed away" -- not a warning issued by the founders but Wayland's own retrospective comment. [SECONDARY: wayland1853v1, narrative]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The Constitution establishes "a Board of twenty-one Commissioners...to be called the 'Baptist Board of Foreign Missions for the United States,'" which employs, fixes the field and compensation of, superintends, and (if necessary) dismisses missionaries; missionaries themselves must be in full communion with a Baptist church furnishing evidence of piety, talent, and zeal. [judson-triennial-convention-q013]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): Judson and Rice were already in the East (India, then Burma) when the American organization formed around them; the Constitution names no specific field but organizes support for missions generally "to the Heathen, and to nations destitute of pure Gospel-light." [judson-triennial-convention-q010]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND as a formally quoted count for the Convention's early years this session (searched: proceedings1814, wayland1853v1); Judson himself is the sole missionary whose support the Convention is shown, in the fetched material, as organizing to take up.

## R — Fruit
- **R1 Adoniram Judson** (PS): Ordained at Salem 6 February 1812, sailed 19 February 1812 under the ABCFM; changed his views on baptism during the voyage and was baptized by immersion at Calcutta; his change of views, publicized in America, directly produced the 1814 Convention that then supported him as its own missionary. [judson-triennial-convention-q001, judson-triennial-convention-q002, judson-triennial-convention-q003, judson-triennial-convention-q004, judson-triennial-convention-q005]
- **R2 Luther Rice** (PS): Ordained with Judson at Salem 6 February 1812; also changed his views on baptism; returned to America and, per Wayland, traveled the country organizing local missionary societies and promoting the general convention, "received almost with acclamation" wherever he went. [judson-triennial-convention-q001; SECONDARY: wayland1853v1, pp. 125-126]
- **R3 Richard Furman** (PS): Signed the 1814 Constitution as its first President. [judson-triennial-convention-q015]
- **R4 Thomas Baldwin** (PS): Signed the 1814 Constitution as its Secretary; per Wayland's narrative, had earlier (from 1803) edited the Massachusetts Baptist Missionary Magazine and corresponded with William Carey, Andrew Fuller, and John Ryland in England. [judson-triennial-convention-q015; SECONDARY: wayland1853v1, p. 46]
