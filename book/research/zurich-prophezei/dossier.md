# Dossier — The Prophezei, Zurich (`zurich-prophezei`)

Note on sourcing: no primary 16th-century document (Zwingli's own ordinance text, a
Zurich Council decree in the original, or Bullinger's *Diarium*/*Reformationsgeschichte*
itself) was successfully fetched and read in usable form this session. The Bullinger
*Reformationsgeschichte* scan located on Internet Archive (`heinrichbulling00bullgoog`)
was fetched but its OCR is so badly garbled (German Fraktur, machine-scanned) that no
fact or quotation could be safely drawn from it; see `sources.csv`/`discrepancies.md`.
Zwingli's own Latin/German works (Corpus Reformatorum) were not located as a free
digitized text this session. What follows is therefore built entirely from six
English-language secondary works: four 19th/early-20th-century scholarly-popular
biographies of Zwingli (Christoffel 1858, Jackson 1901, Simpson 1902, Grob 1883, all
public domain and read in full via Internet Archive OCR) and two contemporary web
sources (a 2025 University of Zurich Faculty of Theology news feature, and a blog post
quoting at length, by page number, from Bruce Gordon's 2021 scholarly biography
*Zwingli: God's Armed Prophet*). Per Rule 3, nothing here is primary; every field below
is labeled `[SECONDARY: document_id]` even where a source itself quotes a primary voice
(e.g. Zwingli's own Latin prayer, or Myconius's assessment) at second hand, because this
session did not verify those inner quotations against Zwingli's or Myconius's own works
directly. This mirrors the geneva-academy dossier's convention but is, if anything,
stricter: geneva-academy's one source reprinted archival documents with citation;
none of this session's sources for zurich-prophezei is itself an edition of a primary
document.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The exercise is called "the prophesying" or "exposition of Scripture" in the 1858 English translation of Christoffel; the footnote there explains Zwingli himself called it "prophesying" with reference to 1 Corinthians 14. The modern German/English form "Prophezei"/"Prophezey" is used throughout the 2025 UZH source and the Gordon-quoting blog post. [zurich-prophezei-q001, zurich-prophezei-q007; SECONDARY: uzh-news-prophezey; SECONDARY: adfontes-prophezei]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): Two English secondary sources fetched this session give the opening date as 19 June 1525: the University of Zurich's own 2025 theology-faculty feature states "Monday, 19 June 1525, 8:00 am" as the day the group first gathered, and a blog post quoting Bruce Gordon's 2021 biography states "The sessions began on 19 June 1525 in the Grossmunster." A third source, the 1858 English translation of Christoffel, instead gives "the 19th July 1525." This is a direct discrepancy between sources actually read this session; see `discrepancies.md` item 1. Two independent sources (one an institutional 2025 account, one quoting a named 2021 academic monograph by page number) agree on 19 June; the single outlying date is in an 1858 translation. [zurich-prophezei-q001, zurich-prophezei-q015, zurich-prophezei-q019; SECONDARY: uzh-news-prophezey; SECONDARY: adfontes-prophezei]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Huldrych Zwingli, "Leutpriester" (people's priest) at the Grossmünster, obtained the Zurich Council's approval for the Prophezei classes. Zwingli himself first expounded the Hebrew and Greek texts; a "special master" was then appointed for Hebrew — first Ceporin, then, after Ceporin's early death, Pellican. Zwingli was separately chosen rector of the Carolinum (the Great Minster school) on 14 April 1525. [zurich-prophezei-q004, zurich-prophezei-q009, zurich-prophezei-q016; SECONDARY: uzh-news-prophezey]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Zurich; the sessions were held in the choir of the Grossmünster (Great Minster) church. [zurich-prophezei-q002, zurich-prophezei-q015, zurich-prophezei-q020]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: christoffel1858, jackson1901, simpson1902, grob1883, uzh-news-prophezey, adfontes-prophezei; also searched raw/heinrichbulling00bullgoog_djvu.txt and raw/translationsrepr01pennuoft_djvu.txt with no usable result). No primary ordinance, council decree text, or Zwingli's own founding statement was located and read in full this session. The closest is Zwingli's 1525 tract "On the Preaching Office" (published, per Jackson, 30 June 1525 — after the Prophezei's own opening — with a dedication to Zwingli's fellow Toggenburgers, directed against the Baptists), named as the source of Zwingli's stated "two roles of the prophet" in the blog post quoting Gordon; that tract's own text was not fetched this session. [zurich-prophezei-q011, zurich-prophezei-q019]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO/PS): No sentence attributed directly to Zwingli's own founding statement was located and read this session (hence PRIMARY-ONLY is NOT FOUND); several secondary sources state the purpose in their own narrative words. Christoffel's translator writes that Zwingli "called into life an altogether new institution of a higher order, specially adapted for advancing the work of education among the candidates for the priesthood, for affording intellectual and spiritual exercise to the canons, and promoting edification also among the people." The 2025 UZH account states the classes' "mission was to teach Hebrew, Greek and Latin, the three languages 'necessary to the proper understanding of the Holy Scriptures.'" Judith Engeler (UZH) is quoted saying Zwingli "wanted first and foremost to 're-educate' the orthodox priests." Simpson's narrative of the 1523 Council decree states "that the Bible should be read and explained daily in three languages — Hebrew, Greek, and Latin," and "that candidates for the ministry should be thoroughly trained." [zurich-prophezei-q008, zurich-prophezei-q012, zurich-prophezei-q016, zurich-prophezei-q017; F5: NOT FOUND for a primary-only citation]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PS): Christoffel's narrative frames the new institution as replacing "the choir-service in the morning, heedlessly mumbled over by canons and chaplains" with the new "prophesying" exercise — i.e., answering a perceived failure of the existing cathedral clergy's Latin office. Simpson's narrative frames the wider 1523 Council reforms (of which the language-exposition rule was part) as following directly from "the advantage gained by the Reformers in the disputation" of 1523. [zurich-prophezei-q001, zurich-prophezei-q012]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: christoffel1858, jackson1901, simpson1902, grob1883, uzh-news-prophezey, adfontes-prophezei). None of the six sources read this session describes a confession or doctrinal-subscription requirement specifically tied to the Prophezei (contrast geneva-academy, where the schola publica required a signed profession of faith).
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO/PS): NOT FOUND as a source's own citation of an antecedent institution. The blog post quoting Gordon does state that Zwingli derived the name "Prophezei" from 1 Corinthians 14 (Paul's discussion of prophesying in the church), which functions as the founders' own cited scriptural warrant for the exercise's name and form, though this is at second hand (the blog's own gloss, following its quotation of Gordon, rather than a quoted primary sentence of Zwingli's). Jackson's narrative separately notes that on 30 June 1525 Zwingli published a tract on the preaching office "with a dedication to his fellow Toggenburgers," directed against the Baptists' claim "that all the Lord's people were prophets" — suggesting the Prophezei's restricted, examined form of "prophesying" was framed partly in contrast to Anabaptist claims, though this is inference from adjacent narrative, not a quoted founding statement. [zurich-prophezei-q011; SECONDARY: adfontes-prophezei, discussing 1 Corinthians 14]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): NOT FOUND as a fixed term; the exercise was an ongoing daily institution (not a fixed-length course of study for an individual student) per all sources read.
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): The exposition proceeded book by book through the Old Testament in the original languages, beginning at Genesis 1. Christoffel's narrative: "the exposition was begun with the first chapter of the First Book of Moses," proceeding — a scholar reads the Vulgate (Latin), the teacher comments, the same section is then read in Hebrew, then in the Greek Septuagint, and explained critically, doctrinally, and practically in Latin. The blog post quoting Gordon states "the plan was simple: to work through all the books of the Old Testament — and when finished, to start again." [zurich-prophezei-q003, zurich-prophezei-q019]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a formal class/year structure; the Prophezei was a daily recurring exercise, not organized into terms or year-classes in any source read this session.
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Latin (Vulgate), Hebrew, and Greek (Septuagint), with a German sermon following for the laity. The 2025 UZH source: "Then they open the Bible, read and translate, into Latin, Hebrew and Greek." Simpson's narrative of the 1523 decree: "the Bible should be read and explained daily in three languages — Hebrew, Greek, and Latin." [zurich-prophezei-q003, zurich-prophezei-q012, zurich-prophezei-q015]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): The Latin Vulgate and the Greek Septuagint, read against the Hebrew text, working through the Old Testament beginning at Genesis. No other set texts or authors (e.g., grammars, commentaries) are named in any source read this session. [zurich-prophezei-q003]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): After the scholarly exposition (lasting about an hour), the congregation assembled to hear a German sermon at nine, based on the material just studied. Christoffel: "the congregation had assembled to hear the sermon at nine. An ecclesiastic mounted the pulpit"; the sermon "was then expounded in a manner level to the capacities of the congregation." The blog post quoting Gordon states it "fell to Leo Jud... to prepare a sermon based on Zwingli's words, to be delivered in German for the faithful." [zurich-prophezei-q005, zurich-prophezei-q021]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six text/ files).
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six text/ files); the Prophezei is described in every source as a recurring daily exercise, not a program conferring a credential.
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): Sessions began at eight o'clock each morning (except, per the sources, Fridays and Sundays), lasted about an hour, and were followed by the German sermon at (or around) nine. See table below.

| Time | Activity | Source |

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): At the outset Zwingli himself expounded the Hebrew and Greek texts; a dedicated Hebrew master was then appointed — Ceporin first (from April 1525 until his death that December), then Pellican (1526–1556, per Christoffel's footnote). Grob's narrative separately lists "Ceporin, Pellican, Myconius, Collin, Megander, and others" as the teachers of what it calls "a prosperous theological seminary for Christian young men." [zurich-prophezei-q004, zurich-prophezei-q014]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): No charter text was located (see F5); Jackson's narrative states that after his April 1525 election as rector of the Carolinum, Zwingli "used his new position to improve the schools and took part himself in the biblical instruction, which he had made part of the curriculum" — describing a duty taken on, not a duty formally prescribed in a document read this session. The 2025 UZH source states lessons were to be given, "according to Zwingli's remit," by "learned, artistic and moral men." [zurich-prophezei-q009, zurich-prophezei-q010, zurich-prophezei-q016]
- **T3 Forms of teacher–student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): The format was a shared public exposition: a scholar read the Vulgate aloud, "the teacher commented upon it," the same passage was then read in Hebrew and Greek and "critically, as well as doctrinally and practically, explained in Latin" by the presiding scholar — a lecture/exposition format performed before an assembled group (canons, clergy, and "more advanced scholars"), rather than one-on-one tutorial contact. [zurich-prophezei-q003, zurich-prophezei-q021]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The University of Zurich's own 2025 account quotes Judith Engeler's summary that Zwingli "wanted first and foremost to 're-educate' the orthodox priests" — i.e., the target was the existing clergy's competence, not lay instruction as such. Myconius, quoted (at second hand) in Christoffel's narrative, said of Zwingli's plan: "Zwingli formed the plan of founding an institution specially intended for the study of profane learning and scientific theology, and I doubt not that, had he survived the full execution of his plan, it would not have found its equal anywhere." [zurich-prophezei-q006, zurich-prophezei-q017]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Original-language exegesis (Hebrew, Greek, Latin) feeding directly into a German sermon for lay hearers — i.e., scholarship placed in the direct service of preaching, performed daily and communally rather than by an individual scholar alone. [zurich-prophezei-q003, zurich-prophezei-q021]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The choir-service previously "mumbled over" by canons and chaplains "heedlessly" is explicitly what the Prophezei replaced, per Christoffel's narrative. [zurich-prophezei-q001]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): The name itself functions as the watchword: Christoffel's footnote states "Zwingli called this scientific and didactic exercise 'prophesying,' in reference to the proceedings alluded to in 1 Corinthians xiv." [zurich-prophezei-q007]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND as a restatement by a named later leader of the Prophezei itself (contrast, e.g., Bullinger, who continued the tradition as Zwingli's successor but whose own restatement was not located and read this session — see discrepancies.md and F5).
