# Discrepancies — saumur-sedan

1. **Saumur founding date: three different years given, for three different acts.** Henri IV's
   letters patent authorizing Duplessis-Mornay to build a college at Saumur are dated March 1593
   [ss-q001]. The academy proper (theological instruction) is stated by Boisson to have begun
   functioning "à partir de 1606" (from 1606) [text/larhra-boisson-saumur.txt, opening
   paragraph]. The Musee protestant page dates "creation of the Academy" to "1599-1600"
   [ss-q033]. Its own Duplessis-Mornay biography page states, in two different sentences on the
   same page, that he "founded the Academy of Protestant Theology in 1604" [ss-q040] and,
   immediately after, that he "founded the Academy of Protestant theology of Saumur in 1599"
   [ss-q041] — an internal contradiction within a single source. The roster gives "1593
   [HIGH]" for Saumur, which matches the letters patent for the college, not the academy's
   theological instruction, which by every source read this session began later, on a date
   nobody read this session states consistently. All figures are reported in the chapter with
   their sources; none is silently preferred.

2. **Sedan founding date: college vs. academy, and two nearby years for the college.** The
   roster gives "1579 [HIGH]" for Sedan. Behr's chapter dates the college's actual functioning
   to "la fin de l'année 1577 et au début de l'année 1578" (the end of 1577 and the start of
   1578) [ss-q019], while stating the college's creation "ne prend place qu'à la toute fin des
   années 1570" (only at the very end of the 1570s) in the same paragraph. The Musee protestant
   page gives 1579 for the college's opening [ss-q030], matching the roster. The academy proper
   (as opposed to the college) is dated by Behr to 1602, when Henri de La Tour d'Auvergne is
   named its official founder [ss-q018], while the Musee protestant page instead dates the
   "turning of the college into an Academy" to the 1601 National Synod of Gergeau [ss-q031].
   These are close but not identical (1601 synodal approval vs. 1602 attested functioning) and
   both are reported.

3. **"Gergeau" vs. "Gap" as the site of the synod approving Sedan's upgrade to an academy.**
   The Musee protestant page names "the National Synod in Gergeau" for the 1601 decision
   [ss-q031]. No corroborating primary-adjacent source read this session confirms or
   contradicts this place name (Boisson's chapter, by contrast, names the 1603 Synod of Gap as
   the occasion when the national synods began setting general regulations for colleges and
   academies — a different synod, a different date, and possibly a different subject). Status:
   UNVERIFIED; both are reported as stated in their respective sources rather than reconciled.

4. **Number of officially recognized Reformed academies at a given date.** Boisson's chapter
   states that the 1612 Synod of Privas fixed the number of academies to be maintained at four:
   Montauban, Nimes, Montpellier, and Saumur [text/larhra-boisson-saumur.txt, paragraph
   beginning "Lors du synode de Privas de 1612"] — Sedan is not in this list of four. The Musee
   protestant page instead states that at the 1620 Synod of Ales, "there were five colleges that
   had been promoted to Academies (besides Sedan)" [ss-q034], implying Sedan was a sixth, already
   established academy alongside five others (Montpellier, Nimes, Saumur, Sedan, and Die) by
   1620. The two figures are not necessarily contradictory (the 1612 list may predate Sedan's or
   Die's formal recognition, or reflect a different funding category), but neither source read
   this session reconciles them, so both are reported as stated.

5. **Roster's secondary source "Maag" not identified or located.** The roster row for
   `saumur-sedan` names only "Maag" in its Secondary column. Karin Maag is a known historian of
   Reformed higher education (her best-documented monograph, used for the `geneva-academy`
   chapter's own roster row, concerns the Genevan Academy specifically). No title by Maag
   specifically on Saumur or Sedan was identified or fetched this session; see sources.csv row
   `maag-karin-not-fetched`. Flagged as a follow-up Scout task.

6. **Primary archival registers not independently located.** Both LARHRA chapters used this
   session (Boisson on Saumur's college/academy relationship; Pittion on student life at Saumur;
   Behr on Sedan) quote extensively and verbatim, with page/folio-level footnotes, from the
   academies' own manuscript council registers (Registre du conseil academique de Saumur,
   1613-1673 and 1683-84; the Sedan Registres du conseil des moderateurs, from March 1602) and
   from the 1620 Synod of Ales' printed General Statutes as transcribed into the Saumur register.
   None of these underlying manuscripts or the printed Loix et Reiglements de l'Academie et
   Collège de Saumur was independently located and read this session; every quotation in this
   chapter that appears to be primary is in fact quoted at second hand, through the cited
   academic chapter, exactly as Borgeaud was used at second hand for `geneva-academy`. This is
   disclosed at the top of dossier.md and is not a silent gap.
