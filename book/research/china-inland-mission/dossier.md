# Dossier — China Inland Mission (`china-inland-mission`)

Note on sourcing: six primary or primary-adjacent full texts were fetched and read this
session (see sources.csv). The chapter's charter document is *The Principles and Practice of
the China Inland Mission* (`principlespracti00chin_0`), an undated pamphlet catalogued by
Columbia University Libraries/Internet Archive as "1900" but carrying no printed date on the
pages themselves (see F5 and discrepancies.md). The 1886 *Arrangements of the China Inland
Mission* named in the roster could not be located as digitized full text (print/microfilm only
at SOAS; see `text/cim-arrangements-1886.REQUEST.md`) and so is not used. Two editions of
*China's Spiritual Need and Claims* were fetched (1868 Third Edition and 1887 Seventh Edition)
to cross-check the "twenty-four workers" appeal against Taylor's own account of its 1865
origin. `jubileechinamis00broouoft` (Broomhall, *The Jubilee Story of the China Inland
Mission*, 1915) and `martyredmission00broogoog` (Broomhall, comp., *Martyred Missionaries of
the China Inland Mission*, 1901) are formally secondary histories, but — following the same
convention used in the geneva-academy dossier for Borgeaud — passages that explicitly quote
Hudson Taylor's own words (his 1865 diary/letter account, the margin-note in his Bible, his
signed 1900 preface) are treated PRIMARY and cited as such; the compilers' own narrative
sentences are cited [SECONDARY].

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The organization is named
  "the China Inland Mission" throughout every document read this session, with no evidence of
  an earlier or different name found. Its own 1895 retrospective states it is "evangelical and
  interdenominational" and "also international," with home departments in London, Dunedin,
  Melbourne, and Toronto. [cim-q002, cim-q003, cim-q018]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): Hudson Taylor dated
  the founding decision to Sunday, 25 June 1865, on the sands at Brighton, in his own account as
  quoted by Broomhall: he there resolved to serve and wrote in the margin of his own Bible,
  "Prayed for twenty-four willing, skilful labourers at Brighton, June 25, 1865." The Mission's
  own rule-document states, "The Mission was founded in 1865 by the Rev. J. Hudson Taylor."
  The first missionary party (the "Lammermuir party") sailed from London on 26 May 1866.
  [cim-q022, cim-q023, cim-q003, cim-q021, cim-q024]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): James Hudson Taylor founded and
  directed the Mission ("carried on under the direction of a General Director"); Broomhall's
  account of the 1865 decision names Taylor's fellow-worker the Rev. F. F. Gough and Mr. and
  Mrs. Berger as those with whom Taylor prayed and conferred before Brighton, and records that
  Mr. Berger undertook direction of the Home work while Taylor went to China. [cim-q003,
  cim-q022; Berger's role as Home Director is stated in Broomhall's narrative prose,
  [SECONDARY: jubileechinamis00broouoft, p. 39], not separately quoted this session]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): The founding decision was made at Brighton, England (25 June 1865); the
  Mission's home base at the time was 30 Coborn Road, Bow, East London (per Broomhall's
  narrative, [SECONDARY: jubileechinamis00broouoft, p. 34], not separately quoted this session);
  the first party sailed from London and the field of work was inland China. [cim-q022]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): *The Principles
  and Practice of the China Inland Mission* (English), document_id `principlespracti00chin_0`.
  This copy is an undated pamphlet (cover title only, "[S.l. : s.n.]"); Internet Archive /
  Columbia University Libraries catalogue it as "1900," but that date is not printed anywhere on
  the fetched pages. Two other catalogued editions of the same title were found this session but
  not fetched as full text: a Wycliffe College Archives item dated 1904 (4 pages, shorter than
  this 18-page/15-article copy) and an HKBU Special Collections item dated 1905. See
  discrepancies.md. *China's Spiritual Need and Claims* (Hudson Taylor, 1865, with the 1868
  Third Edition and 1887 Seventh Edition fetched this session) is the Mission's founding appeal
  pamphlet, not its rule-document; its own 1887 Prefatory Note records: "In the year 1865 I was
  led to write the pamphlet 'China's Spiritual Need and Claims'... A second edition was
  published in 1866; a third in 1868; and a fourth in 1872... A revised and enlarged edition...
  was published in June, 1884... a sixth edition... The present edition of 10,000 is a simple
  reprint of the sixth." [cim-q014, cim-q015, cim-q012]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): "The China Inland Mission was formed under
  a deep sense of China's pressing need, and with an earnest desire, constrained by the love of
  Christ and the hope of His coming, to obey His command to preach the Gospel to every creature.
  Its aim is, by the help of God, to bring the Chinese to a saving knowledge of the love of God
  in Christ, by means of itinerant and localized work throughout the whole of the interior of
  China." [cim-q001] Taylor's own account of the Brighton decision states he asked for "twenty-
  four fellow-workers, two for each of the eleven inland provinces which were without a
  missionary, and two for Mongolia." [cim-q022]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Taylor's 1895 retrospective
  states the Mission was formed because China "had only ninety-seven Protestant Missionaries
  among its hundreds of millions of people." [cim-q017] *China's Spiritual Need and Claims*
  (1868 3rd ed.) records the appeal "to ask the great Lord of the harvest to call forth, to
  thrust forth at least twenty-four European, and twenty-four native evangelists, to plant the
  standard of the cross in the eleven unevangelized provinces of China Proper and in Chinese
  Tartary." [cim-q013] The 1887 Seventh Edition, under the heading "'GOD is Faithful,'" restates
  the same appeal in near-identical words: "twenty-four European, and twenty-four native
  evangelists, to plant the standard of the cross in all the unevangelized districts of China
  Proper and of Chinese Tartary." [cim-q016]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): Candidates must satisfy the
  Directors and Council "as to their soundness in the faith on all fundamental truths, by
  handing in, together with their schedule of application, a written statement of their
  convictions as to the divine inspiration and authority of the Scriptures, the Trinity, the
  fall of man and his consequent moral depravity and need of regeneration, the atonement,
  justification by faith, the resurrection of the body, the eternal life of the saved, and the
  eternal punishment of the lost." Candidates "must be catholic in their views, and able to have
  fellowship with all believers holding these fundamental truths, even if widely differing in
  their judgment as to points of Church government." [cim-q006]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO):
  NOT FOUND (searched: principlespracti00chin_0, b29353518, cu31924023067972, MN41583ucmf_9,
  jubileechinamis00broouoft, martyredmission00broogoog). None of the documents read this session
  contains a passage in which Taylor or the Mission names an earlier missionary society,
  document, or person as a model being imitated. (Broomhall's narrative records that Taylor had
  approached "the leading Missions in England" before Brighton and found them unable to take on
  Inland China, but this is a statement that existing societies could not act, not a claim of
  imitating a model; [SECONDARY: jubileechinamis00broouoft, p. 24], not separately quoted this
  session.)

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: principlespracti00chin_0 and all
  other fetched texts).
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): Not stated as an entrance requirement in the fetched
  rule-document; instead, accepted Probationers "pursue a definite course of study" in China
  itself, are "from time to time examined in the language," and keep a diary, implying training
  begins after acceptance rather than being a prerequisite for it. [cim-q007]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): No narrated
  personal-conversion testimony is described; the documented requirement is a written doctrinal
  statement of "soundness in the faith" (see F8), together with a declaration that candidates
  "cordially approve" the Mission's principles and "heartily desire to carry them out."
  [cim-q005, cim-q006]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): No pre-acceptance entrance examination is
  described; the "examinations in the language" named in the document are administered "from
  time to time" during the two-year probationary period in China, and again before recognition
  as a Senior Missionary after three years' membership or five years' residence. [cim-q007,
  cim-q009]
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): Women were admitted as full members of the
  missionary party from the Mission's first major sending: the Lammermuir party of 1866 included
  nine named women ("Misses Barnes, Bausum, Blatchley, Bell, Bowyer, Desgraz, Faulding, J.
  M'Lean, and Rose") alongside Mrs. Hudson Taylor and Mrs. Nicol, out of a party of 22.
  [cim-q024] The rule-document's marriage clause explicitly addresses "unmarried Candidates of
  either sex," confirming women were accepted as Candidates in their own right, not merely as
  missionaries' wives. [cim-q010]
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): Not stated as a formal
  requirement; instead the rule-document warns candidates to "count the cost" of "privation,"
  "toil," "loneliness," and "danger," including being unable to rely on "the aid of qualified
  physicians." [cim-q005]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): Two years as a Probationer, then recognition as a "member" and
  "Junior Missionary" for three further years; after three years' membership (or five years
  after arrival in China) and passing "all the required examinations in the language," the
  worker is "recognized as Senior Missionaries." [cim-q007, cim-q009]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): The document names only the Chinese language
  specifically ("examined in the language"); no sequence of subjects or set curriculum beyond
  language study is given. [cim-q007]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): Probationer (2 years) -> Member/Junior
  Missionary (3 further years) -> Senior Missionary (after 3 years' membership or 5 years'
  residence, and passing the language examinations). [cim-q007, cim-q009]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Chinese; Probationers are "examined in the
  language" during probation, and passing "all the required examinations in the language" is a
  condition of becoming a Senior Missionary. No other language is named in the fetched
  rule-document. [cim-q007, cim-q009]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: principlespracti00chin_0 and all other
  fetched texts).
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): Probationers "engage in
  missionary work" during their probation, alongside language study and keeping a diary; no
  separate, named preaching-training exercise is described. [cim-q007]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): Language examinations "from time to time" during
  probation, and a further set of "required examinations in the language" before recognition as
  a Senior Missionary; no description of how these examinations were conducted (oral/written,
  by whom) was found. [cim-q007, cim-q009]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): No academic degree or
  certificate is described. Advancement is by recognized status within the Mission itself
  (Probationer -> Member/Junior Missionary -> Senior Missionary). [cim-q007, cim-q009]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  principlespracti00chin_0 and all other fetched texts). No weekly or daily class schedule is
  given in any document fetched this session; the Mission is a sending body governing missionary
  service, not a residential school with a class timetable, and none of the fetched texts
  describes one.

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  principlespracti00chin_0 and all other fetched texts).
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): "Probationers and Junior
  Missionaries, where practicable, will be placed under the superintendence of Senior
  Missionaries in charge of stations, with whom they may reside, and whom they will assist in
  the work as far as they can." [cim-q008]
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): Not described for probationers/candidates as such in
  the rule-document. Broomhall's narrative records that from the end of 1865 the Mission
  observed 31 December and 26 May (the anniversary of the Lammermuir's sailing) "as days of
  prayer and fasting throughout the Mission," and that a regular Saturday prayer meeting was
  held in East London; these are Broomhall's own narrative statements, [SECONDARY:
  jubileechinamis00broouoft, pp. 34, 39], not separately formalized as quote records this
  session.
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): The Directors hold "the power of summarily
  terminating the connection of any worker with the Mission" in any case "of sufficient
  gravity," though they "would, of course, act with all possible consideration." Financial
  penalties (refund of outfit and passage money on a sliding scale by year of service) attach to
  voluntary retirement within five years, and specifically to retirement occasioned by violating
  the marriage-deferral rule. [cim-q010; the summary-dismissal clause itself is in Article 11 of
  principlespracti00chin_0, not separately formalized as a quote record this session — see
  discrepancies.md]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): Probationers are required, alongside language
  study, to "[wear] the native dress" — the rule-document's own OCR text renders this phrase
  corruptly as "woap tho native dregs," which is quoted here exactly as it appears in the
  fetched source (see cim-q007's raw text; the intended reading, confirmed by the surrounding
  sense and by the phrase's wide currency as a hallmark CIM practice, is "wear the native
  dress"). [cim-q007]
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): "The Mission is supported entirely
  by the free-will offerings of the Lord's people. The needs of the work are laid before God in
  prayer, no personal solicitations or collections being authorized. No more is expended than is
  thus received, going into debt being considered inconsistent with the principle of entire
  dependence upon God. The Directors therefore cannot, and do not, promise or guarantee any
  fixed amount of support to the workers." Approved candidates "may be assisted in their
  outfits for the voyage, may have their passage-money paid for them, and may be supported in
  whole or in part by the funds of the Mission," but "their faith must be in God, their
  expectation from Him." [cim-q004]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): By 1895, "the present staff of the Mission
  numbers about 650. Of the total number 121 are associates. The members of the Mission from
  North America number 85. There are also 417 native helpers." [cim-q019]
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): The rule-document notes "the
  frequent failure of health and great mortality which has been found to prevail among ladies
  who arrive in China newly married, or who marry too soon after arrival" — the stated reason
  unmarried candidates were required to defer marriage until recognized as Junior Missionaries.
  [cim-q010] See S5 for mortality/casualty figures among missionaries generally.

  of a Provincial District, and Missionary-in-charge of a station. [cim-q003; the full

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The rule-document's own
  closing words: "In conclusion, the weapons of our warfare must be practically recognized as
  spiritual and not carnal." [cim-q011] Taylor's own account of the founding moment at Brighton:
  having "surrendered myself to God for this service," he recorded, "There and then I asked Him
  for twenty-four fellow-workers... and writing the petition on the margin of the Bible I had
  with me, I returned home with a heart enjoying rest such as it had been a stranger to for
  months." [cim-q022] His signed preface to *Martyred Missionaries of the China Inland Mission*
  (Davos, December 1900), written after the Boxer-crisis losses, closes: "...let us never forget
  that a million a month in China are dying without God. J. Hudson Taylor." [cim-q026]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): The "faith principle" of unsolicited, prayer-based support with
  no guaranteed salary and no going into debt (see L6, cim-q004); an explicitly interdenomina-
  tional and international character embracing "members of all the leading denominations of
  Christians" (cim-q002) and, in the 1895 restatement, likened to "the British and American
  Bible Societies, and the Evangelical Alliance" (cim-q018); and the requirement to "wear the
  native dress" (cim-q007, see L5 on the OCR corruption of this phrase).
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): "No personal
  solicitations or collections being authorized" and "going into debt being considered
  inconsistent with the principle of entire dependence upon God." [cim-q004]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): No single fixed motto or watchword is
  stated as such in any document fetched this session. The phrase "GOD is Faithful" appears as a
  section heading introducing the renewed appeal for workers in the 1887 Seventh Edition of
  *China's Spiritual Need and Claims*, but nothing in the fetched text identifies it as a
  Mission motto rather than simply a chapter title; UNVERIFIED as a motto. [cim-q016]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): Taylor's own 1895
  thirty-year retrospective restates the support principle in almost the same terms as the
  original *Principles and Practice*: "The Missionaries and Native Helpers are supported...by
  contributions sent to the offices of the Mission, without personal solicitation, by those who
  wish to aid in this effort." [cim-q020]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)**
  (PO): The Home Department "receives applications from candidates, accepts as probationers
  those who appear suitable, and facilitates their going to China"; the China Department then
  "receives probationers on arrival in China, admits to membership of the Mission those who
  approve themselves as suitable, locates the workers, distributes the funds, and directs the
  operations of the Mission in the field." [Article 3 of principlespracti00chin_0; not
  separately formalized as a quote record beyond cim-q003 this session — see discrepancies.md]
  The appeal pamphlets themselves functioned as a direct call for volunteers, asking God "to
  call forth, to thrust forth" workers for named unevangelized provinces. [cim-q013, cim-q016]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PO): "The eleven unevangelized provinces of China
  Proper and...Chinese Tartary" (1868 wording); "all the unevangelized districts of China Proper
  and of Chinese Tartary" (1887 wording); Taylor's own account of the Brighton prayer specifies
  "two for each of the eleven inland provinces which were without a missionary, and two for
  Mongolia." [cim-q013, cim-q016, cim-q022]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): The first major party, the "Lammermuir
  party," sailed 26 May 1866. Two fetched primary/primary-adjacent sources disagree on its size:
  Hudson Taylor's own 1895 retrospective states it as "seventeen adults and four children"
  (twenty-one total) [cim-q021], while Broomhall's 1915 *Jubilee Story*, giving a full name
  list, states the "missionary party numbering 22 in all" [cim-q024]. This discrepancy is
  recorded, not resolved — see discrepancies.md. By 1895, Mission staff numbered about 650,
  including 121 associates, 85 members from North America, and 417 native helpers. [cim-q019]
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): See L6/M2: no
  guaranteed salary; support by unsolicited free-will offering; "their faith must be in God,
  their expectation from Him." [cim-q004]
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): Broomhall's 1901 Editor's
  Preface to *Martyred Missionaries of the China Inland Mission* records that in "thirty-three
  years" (i.e., from 1865 up to the 1900 crisis) "no member of the Mission suffered death by
  violence or accident" until "Mr. Fleming, who was murdered in November 1898 in the province of
  Kuei-chau." [cim-q027] It then gives the Mission's own count for the 1900 Boxer crisis: "Of the
  one hundred and twenty-seven adults and the forty-four children known to have been killed
  among the Protestant Missionary Societies, the China Inland Mission has lost fifty-two adults
  and sixteen children, while the worst is feared for six more adults and four children."
  [cim-q028] Separately, Broomhall's 1915 *Jubilee Story* records that of "the hundred who
  sailed in 1887" specifically (a later, distinct cohort from the Lammermuir party), by 1914
  ("after a lapse of twenty-seven years") "seven laid down their lives during the Boxer crisis;
  sixteen others have been called to Higher Service by natural death; twenty-eight have on
  account of health or other causes retired; while forty-nine are still in the Foreign Field."
  [cim-q025] These are two different, non-overlapping figures (Mission-wide Boxer-crisis dead in
  1900 vs. the fate of one specific 1887 sailing-cohort by 1914) and must not be conflated in the
  chapter.
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): *The Principles and
  Practice of the China Inland Mission* itself functions as the instructions given to those
  accepted, ending with a candidate's own signed declaration: "Having carefully and prayerfully
  read the 'Principles and Practice of the China Inland Mission,' I would say that I heartily
  approve of all that is contained in the various articles, and that I desire, if accepted, to
  serve in accordance with them." [text of principlespracti00chin_0, the candidate-signature
  form following Article 15; not separately formalized as a quote record this session, but
  visible in text/principlespracti00chin_0.txt]
