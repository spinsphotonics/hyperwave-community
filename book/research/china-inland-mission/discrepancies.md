# Discrepancies — china-inland-mission

1. **Lammermuir party size.** Hudson Taylor's own 1895 retrospective, *Three Decades of the
   China Inland Mission* (`MN41583ucmf_9`), states: "The Lammermuir party, consisting of
   seventeen adults and four children, sailed from London on May 26th, 1866" (twenty-one
   people total; quote_id cim-q021). Marshall Broomhall's 1915 *Jubilee Story of the China
   Inland Mission* (`jubileechinamis00broouoft`), giving a full name list, states: "the
   Lammermuir set out from the East India Docks upon her long voyage with her missionary party
   numbering 22 in all" (quote_id cim-q024), and its own named list (Mr. and Mrs. Taylor + 4
   children = 6; Mr. and Mrs. Nicol = 2; five named men; nine named women = 22) independently
   sums to 22, not 21. Neither source's figure was corrected against the other this session.
   The chapter must state both figures with their sources and must not silently pick one; per
   Rule 9 the Writer/Editor should not resolve this discrepancy. Neither source's exact
   crew/passenger manifest (e.g., a shipping-company passenger list or the original 1866 CIM
   Occasional Paper) was located this session to adjudicate.

2. **Dating of the "charter" document, *Principles and Practice of the China Inland
   Mission*.** The full-text copy fetched and used as this chapter's charter
   (`principlespracti00chin_0`) carries no date printed anywhere on its 18 pages; Internet
   Archive/Columbia University Libraries catalogue it as "1900," which is a library-assigned
   date, not a date from the document. Two other catalogued copies of a document with the same
   title were found this session but not fetched as full text: Wycliffe College Archives
   (Toronto) catalogues an item "Principles and Practice of the China Inland Mission" dated
   **1904** and described as only 4 pages; HKBU Special Collections & Archives catalogues an
   item "Principles and Practice of The China Inland Mission" dated **1905**, extent not
   given beyond "1 Item." The 1904 item's stated 4-page extent is far shorter than the 18-page,
   15-article document actually fetched and used this session, raising the possibility that the
   1904 item is a different, abbreviated leaflet rather than the same text — this was NOT
   verified by reading it. The three catalogued dates (1900 fetched-copy catalog date, 1904,
   1905) are not resolved against one another, and none is confirmed as the edition's own
   printed date. The chapter should describe the fetched copy as undated/catalog-dated 1900
   and should not assert it is the first, or any specific numbered, edition of the document.

3. **The Mission's own founding-document target, *Arrangements of the China Inland Mission*
   (1886)**, named specifically in the roster, could not be located as digitized full text this
   session (SOAS/microfilm only — see `text/cim-arrangements-1886.REQUEST.md`). Because of this,
   the chapter's abridged charter uses *Principles and Practice* instead, per the task's explicit
   fallback instruction. This is not a disagreement between two found sources but a gap: the
   roster's specifically-named 1886 document was never read, so nothing from it (including any
   differences between its wording and that of *Principles and Practice*) can be reported, only
   its absence.

4. **Two distinct "casualty figures" for the China Inland Mission that must not be
   conflated.** Broomhall's 1901 *Martyred Missionaries of the China Inland Mission* Editor's
   Preface gives the Mission-wide count of dead in the 1900 Boxer crisis specifically: "fifty-two
   adults and sixteen children," with six more adults and four more children feared dead but
   unconfirmed at the time of writing (quote_id cim-q028), against a total of 127 adults and 44
   children killed across all Protestant missionary societies (quote_id cim-q027 gives the
   surrounding context; the 127/44 figure is in the same passage as cim-q028). Separately,
   Broomhall's 1915 *Jubilee Story* gives a different, narrower statistic: of "the hundred who
   sailed in 1887" — a specific, named sailing cohort, not the whole Mission — "seven laid down
   their lives during the Boxer crisis; sixteen others have been called to Higher Service by
   natural death; twenty-eight have on account of health or other causes retired; while forty-
   nine are still in the Foreign Field" as of the time of writing, twenty-seven years later
   (quote_id cim-q025). The "seven" and "fifty-two adults" figures describe different
   populations (one cohort vs. the whole Mission) over overlapping but not identical periods, and
   the chapter must keep them clearly distinct rather than merging them into a single casualty
   number.
