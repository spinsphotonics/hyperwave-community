# Dossier — Strasbourg Gymnasium / Academy (`strasbourg-academy`)

Note on sourcing: two primary documents were fetched and read this session. (1)
`sturm1538-ghi`: an English-language excerpt (about 1,370 words, from pp. 71-72 and 73-77 of
Lewis Spitz and Barbara Sher Tinsley's 1995 translation) of Johann Sturm's own 1538 founding
treatise, *De literarum ludis recte aperiendis*, reproduced with permission on the academic
website "German History Intersections" (German Historical Institute Washington). This is Sturm's
own document in translation, not a secondary paraphrase, and is cited PO (PRIMARY-ONLY fields).
It is only a partial excerpt of a longer work; the full Latin original was located this session
(`sturm1538-latin-archiveorg`, archive.org) but not transcribed or translated, to avoid an
uncontrolled new translation (see sources.csv). (2) `calvin-psalms-preface`: Calvin's own
autobiographical Author's Preface to his Commentary on the Book of Psalms (English translation,
Calvin Translation Society, Edinburgh, 1845-49), used only to document, from Calvin's own words,
that he was drawn to Strasbourg by Bucer and taught in "our small school" there — the primary-
source basis for this book's claim (in the roster and in the Geneva Academy chapter) that Calvin
taught at Strasbourg 1538-41. It is NOT a Strasbourg Academy founding document and supplies no
fact about the Academy's own admission, curriculum, or rules. Two secondary web sources
(`museeprotestant-gymnase`, `museeprotestant-sturm-bio`) were also fetched and are cited
[SECONDARY] for PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY fields only. No timetable, admission rule, examination
rule, or sending record for the Academy itself was located in any source opened this session;
those fields are honestly marked NOT FOUND below rather than filled from general knowledge.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Founded in 1538 as a Latin/vernacular "Gymnase" (grammar school); it "became the first academy to award university degrees" in 1566, and became "a university comprising of four faculties awarding doctorates" in 1621. Separately, it is described as having become "first the Haute Ecole and then in 1566, the Académie de Strasbourg." [strasbourg-academy-q011, strasbourg-academy-q014]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): In March 1538 the Strasbourg "chief town councillor" asked Sturm "to completely reorganize education at a local level"; his plan "was accepted and the 'Gymnase' (secondary school) came into being," with Sturm "shortly after appointed headmaster." A separate secondary source states simply that "this High school was established" in 1538 "in a former Dominican monastery by Jean Sturm." [strasbourg-academy-q012, strasbourg-academy-q013, strasbourg-academy-q008]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Jean (Johannes) Sturm was appointed headmaster and later held the rectorship "until 1581." The same source lists Martin Bucer, Jean Calvin, Wolfgang Capito(n), and Caspar Hédion as having taught at the school (not stated to be founders or officers, only teachers). [strasbourg-academy-q008, strasbourg-academy-q009, strasbourg-academy-q012, strasbourg-academy-q013, strasbourg-academy-q014] See discrepancies.md item 1 on the roster's naming of Bucer as a co-founder.
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Strasbourg. Sturm "decided to go back to Strasbourg, the heart of European humanism, in 1537," the year before the school's founding; a separate source places the school "in a former Dominican monastery." [strasbourg-academy-q015, strasbourg-academy-q008]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): *De literarum ludis recte aperiendis, liber Ioannis Sturmii, ad Prudentissimos Viros, ornatissimos homines, optimos cives, Iacobum Sturmium, Nicolaum Cnipsium, Iacobum Meierum* (Strasbourg, 1538, Latin), dedicated to Jacob Sturm, Nicolaus Cnipsius (Cnipfius), and Jacob Meyer. document_id `sturm1538-ghi` (English excerpt, the text used for this dossier and the abridged charter) and `sturm1538-latin-archiveorg` (the Latin original, located but not used this session — see sources.csv).
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Sturm states it is "absolutely necessary to restore to the states the ancient education" as a remedy for the "large measure of barbarity" and corrupted morals he says all men are born to; and that in a proper school, "piety and religion be set forth ... and let the youthful spirit be trained for this through the cultivation of letters." [strasbourg-academy-q001, strasbourg-academy-q003]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Sturm frames the crisis as moral and civic: "unless a remedy is found for these evils, what, in the future, can be uncorrupted or enduring in the state?" — arguing that his own times show "more traces of foolishness and greed than of prudence and probity" for want of an educated class fit to serve the state "in periods of crisis and danger." [strasbourg-academy-q001]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: sturm1538-ghi). The excerpted portion of the treatise (on the usefulness of letters, the goal of studies, and teachers' stipends) contains no doctrinal-subscription clause; a confession or oath requirement may exist elsewhere in the full 1538 document, which was not transcribed in full this session.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: sturm1538-ghi) for any passage in which Sturm names an earlier school or document as his model. Separately, and in the other direction of the lineage claimed by this book's roster (that Strasbourg fed Geneva through Calvin), Calvin's own primary Preface records that after being "banished from Geneva," Martin Bucer "drew me back to a new station," and that Calvin had "expounded here, in our small school, the Book of Psalms, about three years ago" — Calvin's own word for the institution he taught at is "school" (Latin/French original not consulted; this is the English translation). Calvin's Preface does not name Sturm or the Gymnasium specifically. [strasbourg-academy-q017, strasbourg-academy-q018, strasbourg-academy-q019] A secondary source separately states that "Jean Calvin" was among those who "taught there" at Sturm's gymnasium. [strasbourg-academy-q009]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: sturm1538-ghi). A secondary source states only that Sturm held the rectorship of the later Academy "until 1581," which describes his tenure, not a course length. [strasbourg-academy-q014]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PS): NOT FOUND in the primary excerpt read this session (searched: sturm1538-ghi). A secondary source states in general terms that the school "was marked by humanism and the Protestant faith. It was a Latin secondary school and a primary school where rhetoric, knowledge and divinity were taught," without listing an order or set texts. [strasbourg-academy-q010]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: sturm1538-ghi, sturm1538-latin-archiveorg [located but not read this session]). - **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Sturm's own text states the baseline standard he expects of "every place, especially among Christian communities": that "there should not be any place ... where there is no one who can teach the elements of Latin and Greek," with wealthier states expected to provide further instruction in "the arts of divisions and of speaking" and, at the highest level, professors for "theologians, physicians, and lawyers." ## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): A secondary source names four men, besides headmaster Sturm, as having taught at the school: "Martin Bucer, Jean Calvin, Wolfang Capiton and Caspar Hédion." No date range is given for each. Separately, Sturm's own text states the founding principle that "states that are able should, from the very beginning, provide two teachers in each category" of subject — a stated ideal, not a report of Strasbourg's actual staffing. [strasbourg-academy-q009, strasbourg-academy-q007]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the founding document** (PO): Sturm specifies that in choosing teachers, "three kinds of zeal should be observed: for the humanities, for virtue, and for teaching," and that "the teachers of letters should be not only learned but also endowed with virtue and be most avid for the fatherland and for public service." [strasbourg-academy-q004, strasbourg-academy-q005]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "let piety and religion be set forth in schools and let the youthful spirit be trained for this through the cultivation of letters"; and, of the calling of letters itself, "there is no class of men who should establish and preserve a holier and stricter discipline than the class of men of letters." [strasbourg-academy-q003, strasbourg-academy-q002]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Piety joined to classical letters (Latin and Greek at minimum, per C4) and to a demand that teachers themselves combine learning with virtue and public-spiritedness, not learning alone. [strasbourg-academy-q002, strasbourg-academy-q005, strasbourg-academy-q006] A secondary source states the school "was marked by humanism and the Protestant faith," teaching "rhetoric, knowledge and divinity." [strasbourg-academy-q010]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Sturm warns against teachers motivated by money rather than talent and character, and states flatly that "Avarice and inexhaustible ardor in acquiring riches cannot but be condemned by good men." [strasbourg-academy-q020]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): UNVERIFIED this session. A Latin motto is widely associated with the school in general reference material, but no source opened this session states or cites it; it appeared only in unopened WebSearch summary text, which this project's rules do not treat as a citable source. Not stated in the chapter. See discrepancies.md item 4.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): A secondary source states that "Jean Sturm's educational and teaching theories can be mostly found in *Classicae Epistolae*, published in 1565" — i.e., a later work by Sturm himself restating his educational thinking — but the content of that restatement was not quoted or read this session, only its existence and date. [strasbourg-academy-q016]
