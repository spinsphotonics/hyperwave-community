# Discrepancies — strasbourg-academy

1. **Who is named as founder/initiator.** The roster (`02_institution_roster.md`) names "Johannes
   Sturm, with Bucer and the Strasbourg magistrates" as founders. Of the two secondary sources
   fetched this session, one (`museeprotestant-sturm-bio`) says the initiative came from "the
   Strasbourg chief town councillor," who "asked him [Sturm] to completely reorganize education,"
   with no mention of Bucer in that request; the other (`museeprotestant-gymnase`) says only that
   Sturm was "then in charge of the education policy in Strasbourg," also without mentioning
   Bucer as a founder, and lists Bucer only among those who later "taught there" alongside Calvin,
   Capito, and Hédion. Neither fetched source corroborates Bucer as a co-founder or initiator, as
   opposed to a later teacher at the school. Not resolved; the roster's claim is not contradicted
   outright, but it is not confirmed by anything read this session either. A primary document
   naming Bucer's role (if any) at the 1538 founding was not located.

2. **Precise sequence of the 1538 founding.** `museeprotestant-sturm-bio` frames March 1538 as
   when the town councillor asked Sturm to act, with the "Gymnase" itself coming into being only
   afterward ("shortly after appointed headmaster"). `museeprotestant-gymnase` instead states
   flatly that "this High school was established" in 1538 "in a former Dominican monastery,"
   without the intermediate step. The two are not strictly contradictory (a request in March 1538
   followed by establishment later the same year is consistent with both), but they emphasize
   different moments as "the founding," and neither gives an exact day comparable to Geneva's 5
   June 1559. Do not state a specific day for the Strasbourg founding in the chapter; "1538" is
   as precise as the sources read this session allow.

3. **Number of classes / structure of the course.** General reference material commonly states
   that Sturm's Gymnasium had nine or ten graded classes. This figure did not appear in any source
   actually opened and read this session — it surfaced only in unopened WebSearch AI-generated
   summary text, which this project's rules (Rule 2: quotations and specific facts must be copied
   from an opened source) do not treat as citable. The dossier marks C3 NOT FOUND rather than use
   this number. Flagged as a follow-up: the full Latin text of Sturm's 1538 treatise
   (`sturm1538-latin-archiveorg`, located but not read this session) or Schmidt's 1855 biography
   (`schmidt1855-gallica`, located but not fetched) are the most likely sources to confirm or
   correct this figure with a citation.

4. **Motto "Sapiens atque eloquens pietas."** This Latin motto is widely associated with the
   school in general reference material and appeared in unopened WebSearch summaries this session,
   but no source actually opened and read this session states it or attributes it to a specific
   founding-era document. Per Rule 1, this is recorded as UNVERIFIED in dossier field M4 and is
   not stated in the chapter. A primary or scholarly citation for the motto (which document first
   uses it, and when) is needed before it can be used.
