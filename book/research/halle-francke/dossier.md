## F — Founding

- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The Halle side of this chapter is Francke's charity-school and orphan-house foundation at Glaucha near Halle, begun as an alms-box in 1695 and commonly known in English by the title of Francke's own account, *Pietas Hallensis* (1705, tr.). The mission side is the Danish-Halle Mission (also called the Tranquebar Mission), the enterprise commissioned by King Frederick IV of Denmark and staffed by men trained at Halle, beginning with the two missionaries' departure in November 1705 and arrival in India in July 1706. [halle-francke-q005, halle-francke-q018] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar, quoting the missionaries' own report]

- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): At Halle: the catechetical work with poor children at Francke's door began about the beginning of 1694; an alms-box for poor householders was set up in 1695; and the charity-school itself was founded a quarter-year after that, when Francke resolved to spend an unsolicited gift of 18 shillings sixpence on "the Foundation of a Charity-School." For the mission: the Royal Declaration and Instruction commissioning Ziegenbalg and Plutschau is dated Copenhagen, 17 November 1705; the two missionaries left Copenhagen on (the exact day is illegible in the OCR of the source, see Discrepancies) November 1705; they arrived at Tranquebar in July 1706 (exact day likewise illegible in the source's OCR). [halle-francke-q002, halle-francke-q004, halle-francke-q005, halle-francke-q018, halle-francke-q019, halle-francke-q027] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): August Hermann Francke, pastor at Glaucha and professor of divinity at Halle, is the founder of the Halle charity-school and orphan-house. The mission was set in motion by King Frederick IV of Denmark and Norway, acting through his German court-preacher Dr. Franz Julius Lutkens, who wrote to colleagues in Berlin who in turn consulted Professor Francke at Halle; Francke recommended two men, Bartholomew Ziegenbalg and Heinrich Plutschau, who became the first missionaries. [halle-francke-q005, halle-francke-q015, halle-francke-q016, halle-francke-q018] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar, quoting/narrating the commissioning chain]

- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Glaucha, a suburb of Halle in Saxony, for the Foundations. Tranquebar (Tarangambadi), a Danish trading settlement on the Coromandel Coast of India, for the mission; the mission's first church there, consecrated 4 August 1707, was named "New Jerusalem." [halle-francke-q019, halle-francke-q023] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): The Royal Declaration and Instruction given by Frederick IV to the first Missionaries (Ziegenbalg and Plutschau), Copenhagen, 17 November 1705; English translation printed as Appendix I to Fenger's *History of the Tranquebar Mission* (1863); document_id `fenger1863-tranquebar`. This is the one complete, English-language, primary founding document recovered and verified this session; the Halle side's own school orders (*Ordnung und Lehrart*, 1702) are German-language and were not read this session (see Discrepancies / sources.csv). [halle-francke-q027] [PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Francke states that on receiving an unsolicited gift he resolved on the spot to found a charity-school: "This is a considerable Fund, worthy to be laid out in some important Undertaking, wherefore I'll even take this for the Foundation of a Charity-School." The Royal Instruction states the missionary's purpose as being sent "to instruct the heathens who dwell within Our territory and on the borders thereof, in the holy doctrine ... and to bring them to the knowledge of salvation." [halle-francke-q005, halle-francke-q027] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Francke describes the poor of Glaucha as gathering weekly at his door for alms, "lying generally under gross Ignorance," and the children of the poor as growing up with no schooling and "the least Tincture of good Education," left to "Licentiousness and Irreligion." He describes orphans as otherwise liable to be "drawn away into numberless Disorders and most heinous Sins." [halle-francke-q001, halle-francke-q003, halle-francke-q011] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections]

- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): The Royal Instruction binds the missionary to teach "nothing besides" Scripture "as it is written in God's Word and repeated in the Symbolic Books of this realm after the Augsburg Confession," and requires him to "bind himself by a truthful promise as in the presence of God, to obey this Instruction," subscribing a copy "in his own hand-writing." At Halle, Francke states that "Unfeigned Faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, is laid for a Foundation, and a real Sense of Godliness attended with a conscientious Behaviour, are the most material Points" of the whole undertaking. [halle-francke-q009, halle-francke-q027, halle-francke-q028, halle-francke-q030] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): No passage located this session states that Francke's Halle foundation itself imitated an earlier school or document. What the sources do establish is the reverse direction of influence: Halle's own Orphan-house became a training and recruiting ground for later missionaries — one later missionary, C. W. Gericke, "went to the University of Hallo, where he became a teacher at the Orphan-house and inspector of the girl's school" before going to India in 1765 — and the mission itself was set in motion because Francke, consulted from Copenhagen, personally recommended Ziegenbalg and Plutschau. ## M — Emphasis

- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Francke states of the whole Halle undertaking that "Unfeigned Faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, is laid for a Foundation, and a real Sense of Godliness attended with a conscientious Behaviour, are the most material Points, to the obtaining whereof our earnest Endeavours are constantly directed." Recalling his own decisive conversation with Ziegenbalg, Francke (if he is "the worthy man" of the account) is quoted as saying that winning one soul among the heathen "is as much as winning a hundred in Europe." The missionaries themselves record that "Prayer was our all-important assistance to begin our high and weighty employment with courage." [halle-francke-q009, halle-francke-q017, halle-francke-q020] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): A faith principle is explicit at Halle: the work "hath been begun in Faith and Reliance upon God, and not in any Trust upon a set Stock of Provisions," and continues "under the like Difficulties ... having as yet no other Foundation than it has stood upon ever from the Beginning." The locked book-press for the free charity-school (so that poor children's books were not sold off) "ever since hath been the constant Custom in all Charity-Schools," in Francke's own account -- an early instance of a practice becoming a named institutional model. [halle-francke-q007, halle-francke-q008] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections]

The Royal Instruction implicitly rejects doctrinal innovation, binding the missionary to teach "nothing besides" Scripture and the Augsburg Confession. [halle-francke-q028] [PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

## S — Sending

- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The mission was a royal commission: King Frederick IV, through his court-preacher Lutkens, sought and received a recommendation from Francke, then personally issued a Royal Declaration and Instruction to each missionary, requiring each to subscribe it in his own hand. At Halle itself, Francke describes the Foundation as training "a great many Students" who go on "to a skilful Management of Schools up and down in the Country," and sometimes into "Parsonages, or Parochial Cures" with "the particular Inspection of Schools" -- an informal pipeline into ministry and school oversight, though no separate sending body or commissioning document for that pipeline is attested. [halle-francke-q013, halle-francke-q016, halle-francke-q018, halle-francke-q027] [PRIMARY: pietas-hallensis-1727-selections; PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): Tranquebar, on the Coromandel Coast of the Danish colony in India ("Eastern India" in the Royal Instruction). [halle-francke-q018, halle-francke-q027] [PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): Two missionaries sailed as the founding party (Ziegenbalg and Plutschau, 1705-06). A third, Grundler, is recorded joining before 1719. No comprehensive count of missionaries sent in the mission's first 25 years was located in the passages read this session (a commonly repeated modern figure of "38 missionaries between 1706 and 1818" was seen only in an unverified secondary web summary and is NOT used here -- see Discrepancies). [halle-francke-q018, halle-francke-q026] [PRIMARY: fenger1863-tranquebar]

- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): Each missionary was granted a royal salary and instructed to "be content with what We in Our Royal favour have granted him for his annual pay and support, and not take any money from the people." Their salary was 200 rix-dollars a year each, out of which they funded the mission's first church building "though we did not know how we should bring the work to a conclusion."
