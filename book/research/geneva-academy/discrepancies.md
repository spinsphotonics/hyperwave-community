# Discrepancies — geneva-academy

1. **"School of death" nickname.** WebSearch found the phrase used on two contemporary
   devotional websites (tifwe.org / institutefc.org) claiming Geneva Academy was known as
   "Calvin's school of death" because so many graduates were martyred in France, and citing a
   figure of 88 church planters sent. Neither site's search snippet showed a primary or
   scholarly citation for the phrase itself. A full-text search of Borgeaud's 660-page 1900
   scholarly history (which quotes extensively from primary archival material) for "mort",
   "martyr", "école de la mort", and related terms in connection with the Academy or its
   graduates returned no hits. This does not prove the phrase has no earlier source — only
   that it was not found in the one substantial 19th/20th-century scholarly source read this
   session. Status: UNVERIFIED. Do not attribute the phrase to Calvin, Beza, or any 16th-century
   source in the chapter. Needs a follow-up Scout pass against Kingdon 1956 and a general
   full-text search of Google Books/JSTOR with date filtering (per 05_repositories, the
   "before 1900" filtered search was designed for exactly this and was not completed this
   session).

2. **Number of pastors sent to France.** The figure "~88" is widely repeated in secondary/
   tertiary sources (including the two devotional sites above) and is associated with Kingdon
   1956, "Geneva and the Coming of the Wars of Religion in France 1555-1563". Kingdon's book
   was not located as a digitized full text this session (see sources.csv row
   kingdon-1956-noted-not-fetched). Do not state this number in the chapter without a citation
   to a source actually read.

3. **Building location and completion date.** Borgeaud's narrative (lines ~2210-2225 of the
   fetched text, not yet formalized into a quote record) states the college building, in the
   garden of Bolomier, was only partly finished at the 1559 inauguration and was completed in
   1562. This is consistent with, and adds detail to, the roster's simple "1559" founding date;
   not a contradiction, but flagged so the Writer does not compress "founded 1559" into
   "building completed 1559."
