## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Founded as "l'Université et Collège" of Geneva (also called the Académie de Genève, Académie de Calvin); the Latin foundational statutes call it Academia Genevensis. [geneva-academy-q003, geneva-academy-q010]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): The Genevan Small Council took up the college project by at least 25 and 28 March 1558 (registers quoted by Borgeaud); Calvin brought a detailed proposal on the seven classes to the Council on 24 October 1558; the statutes were solemnly promulgated and the Academy inaugurated at St Pierre on 5 June 1559. [geneva-academy-q010, geneva-academy-q003]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): Jean Calvin proposed the plan to the Council and preached at the inauguration; Theodore Beza was elected the first rector by the ministers and confirmed by the city government (the Seigneurie), and delivered the Latin inaugural oration. Michel Roset, secretary of the Council, read the statutes aloud in French. [geneva-academy-q003, geneva-academy-q004]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): Geneva; the inauguration was held in the temple of St Pierre; the college building itself (in the garden of Bolomier) was only partly finished in 1559 and completed in 1562. [geneva-academy-q003; NOT FOUND for Bolomier detail in a quote record this session — noted from raw text lines 2210-2225, not yet formally quoted]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): Leges Academiae Genevensis (1559, Latin, with a French reading at the inauguration), reprinted in Borgeaud 1900 appendix. document_id: borgeaud1900-v1 (appendix section). NOTE: this session was not able to produce a clean, fully-verified abridgement of the Latin Leges text itself because of OCR quality; the abridged charter for this chapter instead centers on Beza's inaugural oration and the oath formulas, which were legible. This is flagged for a follow-up Fetcher ticket to obtain a cleaner scan or the Robert Fills 1562 English translation.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Beza told the incoming students that they had not come to Geneva "as most of the Greeks once did, who went off to the spectacles of their gymnasia to watch fleeting games" — the implied contrast (completed by the following, unquoted sentence per Borgeaud's paraphrase) being that they came for true religion and good letters. [geneva-academy-q001]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PS): Borgeaud states the town's resources were insufficient to complete the college and Calvin appealed to private giving; over 500 bequests and gifts were received in the college's first sixty years. [SECONDARY: borgeaud1900-v1, narrative p.37 — not yet formally quoted, see discrepancies.md for follow-up]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): At the inauguration, alongside the statutes, "the confession of faith required of students" was read, and an oath was required of the rector "and all those who taught in either section of the school." Signing a profession of faith was the sole obligation imposed on schola publica students beyond giving their name to the rector. [geneva-academy-q004, geneva-academy-q008]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: borgeaud1900-v1) in a directly quotable passage this session; the roster's claim that Calvin drew on Strasbourg (Sturm's Gymnasium, where Calvin taught 1538-41) is standard secondary history but was not located as a quoted primary statement in the material read this session.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): Seven classes in the schola privata (grammar school), progressing from the seventh (lowest) to the first (highest), followed by the schola publica (no fixed class structure, "higher instruction"). Exact year-count NOT FOUND in a quoted passage.
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): 7th class: reading (French and Latin) and writing. Progressing upward to the 4th class: Greek begun. 3rd class: Cicero, Virgil, Caesar. 2nd class: Homer, Xenophon, Polybius. 1st class: dialectic and rhetoric, studying the orations of Cicero and Demosthenes. [geneva-academy-q005, geneva-academy-q006]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): Two sections: schola privata (also called gymnasium; teachers titled praeceptores/hypodidascali) with seven classes, and schola publica (teachers titled publici professores), which had no classes. [geneva-academy-q008]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): French and Latin (from the 7th class); Greek (from the 4th class); Hebrew was taught at the schola publica level by a named chair (Tremellius). [geneva-academy-q005, geneva-academy-q011]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): Cicero, Virgil, Caesar (3rd class); Homer, Xenophon, Polybius (2nd class); Cicero's and Demosthenes' orations (1st class, rhetoric/dialectic). [geneva-academy-q005, geneva-academy-q006]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: borgeaud1900-v1) — the fetched material describes grammar/rhetoric instruction and the schola publica's higher lectures, but no passage specifically on preaching practice was located this session.
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): Annual promotion examinations, conducted publicly, with the public professors (under the rector) setting and correcting compositions and personally interrogating each candidate in the presence of his regent, deciding pass or failure. [geneva-academy-q009]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): No degree is described; entry to the schola publica required only giving one's name to the rector and signing a profession of faith. [geneva-academy-q008]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a timetable; the only schedule-like fact found is the annual ceremony of 1 May (see L3) and the practice of grouping pupils within each class into "decuries" (groups of ten) ranked by achievement, not age or family, with the top pupil of each group seated first and serving as monitor. [geneva-academy-q006]

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): At founding, named chairs included Tremellius for Hebrew, Beza for Greek, and an unnamed Frenchman "to come from Paris" for Latin, each at 280 florins; teachers below the schola publica were called regents (regents of the schola privata), praeceptores/hypodidascali. [geneva-academy-q011, geneva-academy-q008]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): All who taught, in either section of the school, were required to take an oath, read aloud at the inauguration alongside the rector's oath. [geneva-academy-q004]
- **T3 Forms of teacher–student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): Regents taught fixed classes; public professors personally examined each promotion candidate face to face, in the presence of the candidate's own regent. [geneva-academy-q009]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Beza, closing his inaugural oration to the first students, contrasted their purpose with idle spectacle: they had not come "as most of the Greeks once did, who went off to the spectacles of their gymnasia to watch fleeting games." Elsewhere Borgeaud records that the first rector reminded the young men, on 5 June 1559 at St Pierre, that they were a "milice" (a militia/host). [geneva-academy-q001, geneva-academy-q002]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Doctrinal subscription (profession of faith) and the languages (Latin, Greek, Hebrew) needed for direct reading of Scripture and the classical/rhetorical texts used to train public speech. [geneva-academy-q004, geneva-academy-q005, geneva-academy-q011]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Idle spectacle-going, by contrast with the Greek gymnasia image in Beza's address. [geneva-academy-q001]
