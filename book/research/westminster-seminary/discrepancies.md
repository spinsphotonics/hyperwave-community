# Discrepancies — Westminster Theological Seminary (`westminster-seminary`)

Recorded per Rule 9 (`00_README.md`): both/all values are given; none is chosen or corrected.

## 1. Wording of Machen's "though Princeton is dead" sentence

Three sources purport to quote the same sentence from Machen's 25 September 1929 address verbatim, but give three different wordings:

| Source | document_id | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| OPC, "Today in OPC History" (also matching Wikipedia) | machen-opctoday | "[T]hough Princeton is dead, the noble tradition of Princeton is alive." [westminster-seminary-q008] |
| Edwin H. Rian, *The Presbyterian Conflict* (1940), ch. 4 | rian1940-ch4 | "No, my friends, though Princeton Seminary is dead, the noble tradition of Princeton Seminary is alive." [westminster-seminary-q027] |
| D. G. Hart and John R. Muether, *Fighting the Good Fight* (1995) | hart-muether-pt1 | "Princeton Seminary is not dead, the noble tradition of Princeton Seminary is alive." [westminster-seminary-q035] |

The third (Hart & Muether) reads as if it negates the very point the sentence is making ("is not dead" rather than "is dead"), which is difficult to reconcile with the surrounding argument in all three sources (that the *institution* Princeton is gone but its *tradition* continues at Westminster). This session did not locate the original periodical text to adjudicate which wording is correct; possibilities include a transcription error in one or more secondary sources, or genuine variation between different reprintings of the address. Not resolved here.

## 2. Wording of the "We believe, first... second... third" doctrinal platform sentence

| Source | document_id | Wording differences |
|---|---|---|
| OPC, "Today in OPC History" | machen-opctoday | "as set forth in the Confession of Faith"; "...whatever opposes it, whether from within or without the church" [westminster-seminary-q010] |
| Rian, *The Presbyterian Conflict* | rian1940-ch4 | "as it is set forth in the Confession of Faith"; "...whatever opposes it, whether within or without the church" (no "from") [westminster-seminary-q027 continuation, not separately quote-recorded beyond q027] |

Minor wording variants ("as set forth" / "as it is set forth"; presence or absence of "from"). Not resolved here; both are recorded so a future session with access to the original periodical or a book reprint can adjudicate.

## 3. Complete text of the founding address not located

Not strictly a discrepancy between sources but a gap flagged per Rule 6 (BLOCKED): the full text of "Westminster Theological Seminary: Its Purpose and Plan" (*The Presbyterian* 99, October 10, 1929, pp. 6-9; approximately four printed pages) was not located as an open digitized primary source this session. Three secondary web pages each reproduce a different, non-overlapping excerpt (see dossier.md field F5 and `text/machen1929-address.txt`), totaling roughly 1,060 words of the four-page original. Two later book reprints exist (Stonehouse, ed., *What Is Christianity?*, 1951; Hart, ed., *Selected Shorter Writings*, 2004, pp. 187-194) but neither was located as an open digitized full text; the archive.org copy of the Hart volume (`selectedshorterw0000mach`) returned a 401 Authorization Required response when its OCR text endpoint was requested directly, confirming it is lending-restricted. A human researcher with library access to either book, or to a bound run of *The Presbyterian* for 1929, could supply the missing text; a future session should also query Westminster's own Montgomery Library finding aids (wts.edu/library) at the item level for the 1929-30 catalogue and any archival copy of the address.
