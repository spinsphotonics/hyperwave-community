# Dossier — Westminster Theological Seminary (`westminster-seminary`)

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The institution has been called "Westminster Theological Seminary" and "Westminster Seminary" interchangeably from its founding; the sources read this session give no evidence of an earlier or later change of name for the Philadelphia institution itself (a California branch later became the separate "Westminster Seminary California" in 1982, per Wikipedia, but that is a distinct institution, not a renaming of this one). [westminster-seminary-q039] [PRIMARY: rian1940-ch4 (used throughout as "Westminster Theological Seminary" / "Westminster Seminary"); SECONDARY: wikipedia-wts]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): Four dated steps are recorded. Conservative Princeton figures first met to discuss a new seminary on 17 June 1929 at the University Club, New York, days after the General Assembly finalized Princeton's reorganization that June. A temporary executive committee was formed and "the seminary was definitely launched" at a meeting of seventy-eight men at the Philadelphia YMCA on 18 July 1929. The seminary opened for instruction on 25 September 1929, with Machen delivering the opening address that day, and an enrollment of fifty students. The sources read this session do not locate a separate incorporation or state-charter date distinct from these organizational and opening dates; Rian's account describes a state-issued charter only in connection with degree-granting power sought in 1938. [westminster-seminary-q013, westminster-seminary-q014, westminster-seminary-q016, westminster-seminary-q018, westminster-seminary-q012] [PRIMARY: machen-opctoday; PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): The founding executive committee (formed 18 July 1929) consisted of ministers Maitland Alexander, Roy T. Brumbaugh, Walter D. Buchanan, Samuel G. Craig, Charles Schall, and Frank H. Stevenson, and elders Roland K. Armes, Edgar Frutchey, Frederic M. Paist (chairman), James L. Rankin, T. Edward Ross, James F. Schrader, John L. Steele, and Morgan H. Thomas, with Robert Dick Wilson, J. Gresham Machen, and Oswald T. Allis serving in an advisory capacity; Frank H. Stevenson became the first president of the board of trustees. Rev. Charles Schall is credited with first conceiving the idea of a new seminary. The founding faculty -- Wilson, Machen, Allis, and Van Til, all former Princeton professors, joined by R. B. Kuiper and recent Princeton graduates Allan A. MacRae, Ned B. Stonehouse, and Paul Woolley -- are also named as founders in the broader sense used by Wikipedia. [westminster-seminary-q017, westminster-seminary-q020, westminster-seminary-q037, westminster-seminary-q036] [PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4; SECONDARY: wikipedia-wts, hart-muether-pt1]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Philadelphia. The seminary opened in the Witherspoon Building; its early classrooms and student rooms were at 1526 and 1528 Pine Street (the latter donated rent-free by Dr. Allis). In June 1937 a twenty-two acre estate on the edge of Philadelphia, with five buildings, was purchased as a permanent campus, and its main building was dedicated as J. Gresham Machen Memorial Hall that September; the institution's later address is Glenside, Pennsylvania, per Wikipedia's present-day infobox (outside this chapter's founding-era scope). [westminster-seminary-q012, westminster-seminary-q019, westminster-seminary-q029] [PRIMARY: machen-opctoday; PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4; SECONDARY: wikipedia-wts]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): J. Gresham Machen's opening convocation address, "Westminster Theological Seminary: Its Purpose and Plan," delivered 25 September 1929 and published in *The Presbyterian* 99 (October 10, 1929), pp. 6-9. English. The complete original text was NOT located this session (BLOCKED -- see sources.csv rows `presbyterian-periodical-1929`, `stonehouse1951-what-is-christianity`, `hart2004-shorter-writings`); what survives here is three non-contiguous verbatim excerpts recovered from secondary web transcriptions, assembled as document_id `machen1929-address` (composite of `machen-pcahistory`, `machen-wscal`, `machen-opctoday`). [westminster-seminary-q001 through q011] [PRIMARY: machen-pcahistory, machen-wscal, machen-opctoday]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The address frames the new seminary's purpose christologically and biblically before it is institutional: "Our new institution is devoted to an unpopular cause; it is devoted to the service of One who is despised and rejected by the world," and "The Bible, then, which testifies of Christ, is the center and core of that with which Westminster Seminary has to do." Machen states that the school will continue Princeton's tradition "though Princeton is dead," "not on the foundation of equivocation and compromise, but on an honest foundation of devotion to God's Word." The seminary's own later catalogue (quoted secondhand by Rian) states the purpose in institutional terms: "Westminster Theological Seminary was founded in 1929 to carry on and perpetuate the policies and traditions of Princeton Seminary as that institution existed prior to its reorganization." [westminster-seminary-q001, westminster-seminary-q002, westminster-seminary-q008, westminster-seminary-q009, westminster-seminary-q025] [PRIMARY: machen-pcahistory, machen-opctoday; PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4 (catalogue quotation)]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The precipitating crisis was the 1929 reorganization of Princeton Theological Seminary by the General Assembly of the Presbyterian Church in the USA, which the founders regarded as placing Princeton under modernist influence. An initial resolution (17 June 1929) pledged support "toward preventing by legal means the misuse of the Seminary's funds" or "toward the formation of a new seminary if they decide that it is necessary." The Seminary's own catalogue traced the deeper cause further back: "a long process of defection from the Christian faith which has been going on in the Protestant churches of the world during the past one hundred years," with "the special occasion" found "in certain recent events in the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America." [westminster-seminary-q013, westminster-seminary-q015, westminster-seminary-q026] [PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): The seminary is "committed to the Westminster Confession of Faith as the system of doctrine taught in the Bible and to the Presbyterian form of church government," though it is "independent of ecclesiastical control" and not interdenominational; Machen's own address states the school's platform as threefold: the truth of "the Christian religion, as set forth in the Confession of Faith of the Presbyterian Church," its capacity for "scholarly defense," and its proclamation "without fear or favor, and in clear opposition to whatever opposes it, whether from within or without the church, as the only way of salvation for lost mankind." The sources read this session do not specify a separate signed subscription document distinct from this stated platform, nor do they specify whether trustees as well as faculty were bound to it at the outset (Wikipedia's statement that "all trustees and faculty members are required to affirm" the Westminster Standards describes the present-day, not necessarily the 1929, requirement). [westminster-seminary-q010, westminster-seminary-q023] [PRIMARY: machen-opctoday; PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): The single antecedent the founders themselves name is Princeton Theological Seminary before its 1929 reorganization: Machen's address explicitly positions Westminster as continuing "the noble tradition of Princeton," not as a new departure. Several founding faculty (Machen, Wilson, Allis, Van Til) had themselves been Princeton professors, and R. B. Kuiper had studied at Princeton under B. B. Warfield. No document or school outside the Princeton lineage is cited as a model in the sources read this session. ## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "Let it never be forgotten that a theological seminary is a school for specialists... Specialists in the Bible -- that is what Westminster Seminary will endeavor to produce." Machen closed the address: "On that platform, brethren, we stand. Pray that we may be enabled by God's grace to stand firm. Pray that the students who go forth from Westminster Seminary may know Christ as their own Savior and may proclaim to others the gospel of his love." [westminster-seminary-q006, westminster-seminary-q007, westminster-seminary-q011] [PRIMARY: machen-wscal, machen-opctoday]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): The recovered excerpts stress biblical-language competence ("to the study of Greek and Hebrew we must go") and reasoned doctrinal defense against a generic, experience-centered "phenomenon of religion" approach found in "most theological institutions today": Machen insists "is Christianity true?" "should not be evaded," and that "Christianity flourishes not in obscurantist darkness... but in the full light of day." Rian's narrative stresses, in addition, a specifically Reformed/Calvinistic, Presbyterian doctrinal stress (not merely generic "orthodoxy" or "evangelicalism"), a stress the school's own leaders felt was under-recognized in its first years. [westminster-seminary-q003, westminster-seminary-q004, westminster-seminary-q005, westminster-seminary-q006] [PRIMARY: machen-pcahistory, machen-wscal; PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The address rejects a seminary curriculum organized around "the general phenomenon of religion" rather than the Bible specifically, and rejects "a mystic Christ whom we seek merely in the hidden depths of our own souls" as against "the blessed written Word." Later controversy (1936-37, per Rian) shows the faculty rejecting both modernism and, on the other side, a fundamentalism it judged doctrinally loose: Professor Allan MacRae's resigning charge that the faculty had moved against "the great stream of Reformed, Evangelical Christianity" was itself disputed by the remaining faculty and students, so this is recorded as a contested claim, not settled fact, per Rule 9. [westminster-seminary-q001, westminster-seminary-q002] [PRIMARY: machen-pcahistory; PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4 (for the 1937 controversy, summarized, not separately quote-recorded beyond what is already in this dossier)]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): Wikipedia gives the seminary's motto as the Greek "ΠΑΣΑ Η ΒΟΥΛΗ ΤΟΥ ΘΕΟΥ" ("the whole counsel of God"), without dating its adoption; the sources read this session do not establish that this motto was in use at the 1929 founding specifically. [westminster-seminary-q038, westminster-seminary-q039] [SECONDARY: wikipedia-wts; date of adoption UNVERIFIED]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND beyond Rian's own 1940 restatement (already used above) and R. B. ## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): Westminster does not itself license or ordain. Graduates were examined for licensure and ordination by presbyteries and, after 1936, by the General Assembly of the (new) Presbyterian Church of America/Orthodox Presbyterian Church: "The General Assembly had the privilege of examining several graduates of Westminster Seminary for licensure and ordination." Some graduates were sent as missionaries specifically through the Independent Board for Presbyterian Foreign Missions, an agency Machen and colleagues formed in 1933 independent of the denomination's own board; one graduate, Henry W. Coray, was appointed by the Independent Board to China and, as a result, was expelled from his presbytery's roll (12 November 1934) rather than dismissed by his church to go. [westminster-seminary-q033] [PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): By 1940 (per Rian), graduates were serving as "ministers, professors, and missionaries" in twenty-three states and ten foreign lands; no list of the specific foreign countries was located in the sources read this session, beyond the single named case of China (Henry W. Coray, via the Independent Board). [westminster-seminary-q032] [PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): 162 graduates in the seminary's first ten years (to 1939), serving (by 1940) in twenty-three states and ten foreign lands; the sources do not separately break down how many of the 162 went specifically to church pulpits, to foreign mission fields, or to teaching posts. ## R — Fruit
- **R1 J. Gresham Machen (1881-1937)** (PS): The seminary's founding leader and, per Rian, "the central figure of the controversy at Princeton and the theological leader of the conservatives"; he died 1 January 1937 "while on a missionary trip to North Dakota to present the cause of the Bible against modernism in the Presbyterian Church in the USA." [westminster-seminary-q028] [PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY: rian1940-ch4]
