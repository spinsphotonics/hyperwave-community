# Discrepancies — harvard-college

1. **Date of the founding grant vote.** Quincy's 1840 narrative (quincy1840v1-narrative,
   p. 8-9) states the General Court "first assembled" and voted the £400 grant "on the second
   Thursday and eighth day of September" 1636, giving the vote as a single-day act
   [harvard-college-q034]. The record as printed in Appendix No. LXVIII of the same volume
   (charter1650.txt, p. 586) instead prints it as "At a Court holden September 8th, 1636, and
   continued by adjournment to the 28th of the 8th month (October, 1636)," with the vote text
   following that combined heading [harvard-college-q031]. Both are Quincy 1840's own printings
   of the same underlying General Court record (Vol. I, p. 183), so this is not a conflict
   between independent sources, but the book's own narrative chapter and its own documentary
   appendix state the date differently (8 September only, vs. 8 September continued by
   adjournment to 28 October). Recorded here rather than resolved, per Rule 9. The roster's
   known-facts sheet gives only "1636 [HIGH]" with no day, which is consistent with either.

2. **"First class of nine members," 1642 — source and precision.** The only source located this
   session for the size of Harvard's first graduating class is the modern (1938) editorial note
   printed alongside the New England's First Fruits text in Miller & Johnson's anthology
   (nef1643.txt, p. 700): "by 1642 the first class of nine members was graduated from Harvard
   College" [harvard-college-q020, SECONDARY]. This is not a primary-source figure — no
   commencement record, class list, or contemporary primary count of the 1642 class was found
   in nef1643, lawsliberties1642, charter1650, or the Quincy 1840 excerpts fetched this session.
   The number is recorded in the dossier (field L7) labeled [SECONDARY] and should not be
   upgraded to a primary fact in the chapter without a further Fetcher pass against the
   College's own early class/commencement records (e.g., Quincy 1840's later chapters, or
   Sibley's Harvard Graduates, neither read this session).
