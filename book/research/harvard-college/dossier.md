# Dossier — Harvard College (`harvard-college`)

Note on sourcing: five text files were read this session. Three are direct transcriptions of
1636-1650 primary records (nef1643 = "New England's First Fruits," 1643, college section, via
Miller & Johnson's 1938 verified reprint of the 1643 original; lawsliberties1642 = the College's
own "Laws, Liberties, and Orders...1642-1646," printed by Quincy 1840 from the College's early
manuscript record books; charter1650 = the Massachusetts General Court's 1636-1650 votes and the
Charter of 30/31 May 1650, printed by Quincy 1840 from the General Court's own records). The fifth
(quincy1840v1-narrative) is Josiah Quincy's own 1840 narrative (SECONDARY), except where it
quotes the College's early record books verbatim (treated PRIMARY per-quote, following the
convention used in the geneva-academy and emmanuel-cambridge dossiers). A sixth file
(nef1643-orig-scan, a direct OCR scan of an actual 1643 copy of the pamphlet) was fetched and
read for corroboration but its OCR error rate was far above the 5/100-word threshold (Procedure F
step 3); it was NOT used as the source of any quote record — see sources.csv and verification.md.
Morison's 1935 "The Founding of Harvard College" (the roster's other named key document) was
searched for but returned only as controlled-digital-lending items on Internet Archive (HTTP 401
on the full-text endpoint); it is cited only at second hand, via the editorial note printed inside
nef1643.txt, and never quoted directly — see sources.csv row foundingofharvard1935-morison.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The college, endowed chiefly by John Harvard's 1638 bequest, was named Harvard College by common consent from its earliest years and confirmed by statute in 1638/9. [harvard-college-q003, harvard-college-q032]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): The Massachusetts General Court voted £400 toward "a Schoole or Colledge" on 8 September 1636 (continued by adjournment from a court that had first assembled, per Quincy, "the second Thursday and eighth day of September"); John Harvard's 1638 bequest of half his estate and his library gave the project its founding resources and its name; the General Court incorporated the College as a seven-person corporation ("President and Fellows of Harvard College") by charter dated 31 May 1650, under Governor Thomas Dudley's seal. [harvard-college-q031, harvard-college-q034, harvard-college-q002, harvard-college-q029, harvard-college-q033]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): "Mr. Harvard," described as "a godly Gentleman, and a lover of Learning," gave half his estate and his library; the College was placed under Nathaniel Eaton (removed from his trust in September 1639 after being "convicted of sundry abuses") and then under Henry Dunster, who arrived in the country in autumn 1640 and was appointed "President"; twelve Overseers, six magistrates and six ministers, were chosen by the General Court to govern it; the 1650 Charter names the first seven-person corporation: Henry Dunster (President), Fellows Samuel Mather, Samuel Danforth, Jonathan Mitchell, Comfort Starr and Samuel Eaton, and Treasurer Thomas Danforth. John Harvard himself was educated at Emmanuel College, Cambridge, and had emigrated in 1637. [harvard-college-q002, harvard-college-q007, harvard-college-q029, harvard-college-q035, harvard-college-q036, harvard-college-q037, harvard-college-q040, harvard-college-q041]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): "the Colledge was, by common consent, appointed to be at Cambridge, (a place very pleasant and accommodate)." [harvard-college-q003]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): For this chapter's abridged charter, the "Rules, and Precepts that are observed in the Colledge," together with the surrounding account "In respect of the Colledge, and the proceedings of Learning therein" and "The times and order of their Studies," printed in New Englands First Fruits (London, 1643), English, document_id `nef1643`. Miller & Johnson's editorial note states this material "was very likely supplied by Henry Dunster." The Massachusetts General Court's Charter of 31 May 1650 (document_id `charter1650`) is treated as a second, complementary founding document (the corporate/legal charter, as distinct from the 1643 rules of student life and study); the General Court's record states it was granted "in answer to the petition of Henry Dunster, President of Harvard College." [harvard-college-q021, harvard-college-q039]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): "One of the next things we longed for, and looked after was to advance Learning and perpetuate it to Posterity; dreading to leave an illiterate Ministery to the Churches, when our present Ministers shall lie in the Dust." The 1650 Charter states the purpose as the "advancement of all good literature, arts, and sciences," so as to "conduce to the education of the English and Indian youth of this country, in knowledge and godliness." The Rules and Precepts state that "the maine end of his life and studies" for every student "is, to know God and Iesus Christ which is eternall life...and therefore to lay Christ in the bottome, as the only foundation of all sound knowledge and Learning." [harvard-college-q001, harvard-college-q028, harvard-college-q009]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The fear of "leav[ing] an illiterate Ministery to the Churches, when our present Ministers shall lie in the Dust" — i.e., that the first generation's own university-trained ministers would die without a supply of literate replacements. [harvard-college-q001]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No separate confession-signing clause was found in the fetched material (contrast Geneva Academy's profession of faith). What the sources give instead: the 1650 Charter's declared aim of education "in knowledge and godliness," and the Overseers' 1642-charter mandate to make orders for the College's government "in piety, morality, and learning." [harvard-college-q028, harvard-college-q030]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: nef1643, lawsliberties1642, charter1650, quincy1840v1-narrative) as an explicit statement by the founders naming an English or continental model. The one documented link is biographical, not programmatic: John Harvard himself was "educated at Emanuel College, Cambridge" (see the Emmanuel College, Cambridge chapter in this book, Part I, for Emmanuel's own founding statutes and purpose). [harvard-college-q036]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): The weekly schedule in New England's First Fruits is organized by "first yeare," "second yeare," and "third yeare" of study; a further period preceded the second ("Master of Arts") degree, but no fixed total number of years for either degree is stated in the fetched material. [harvard-college-q015]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): Monday-Tuesday: Logic (first year, three quarters) and Physics (last quarter); Ethics and Politics (second year); Arithmetic and Geometry (third year, three quarters) and Astronomy (last quarter); afternoons, disputations by year. Thursday: Greek (etymology/syntax; prosody/dialects; then practice in grammar, poesy, and style/composition). Friday: Hebrew and "the Easterne Tongues" (Chaldee/Aramaic, Syriac), with afternoon practice in the Bible, Ezra and Daniel, and the New Testament. Saturday: Rhetoric and monthly declamations; Sunday: "Divinity Catecheticall" and "Common places." Afternoons in winter, history; in summer, "the nature of plants." [harvard-college-q015, harvard-college-q016, harvard-college-q017]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): Instruction was organized by year-cohort (first, second, third year) rather than by named "classes" as at Geneva; each day of the six-day week was assigned a subject, with morning lectures and afternoon exercises. [harvard-college-q015]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Latin and Greek were required for admission; Hebrew and "the Easterne Tongues" (Chaldee/Aramaic, Syriac) were taught from the first year onward on the weekly schedule's "fifth day." Students were forbidden their "mother tongue" except in public oratory exercises, where they were "called to make them in English." [harvard-college-q008, harvard-college-q016, harvard-college-q024]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): "Tully" (Cicero) is named as the standard for the admission examination's Latin; the second-year Thursday poetry exercise names "Nonnus, Duport, or the like" as authors; the Friday New Testament exercise for the third year is done "in Trostius['s] New Testament." [harvard-college-q008, harvard-college-q043]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): Students were required to read Scripture "twice a day" and be ready to account for their "proficiency therein" to their Tutor; Saturday's "Divinity Catecheticall" and "Common places" lectures, and monthly declamations (below, C7), were the chief practical/devotional exercises found. [harvard-college-q010, harvard-college-q017]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): Public declamations in Latin and Greek and Logical and Philosophical disputations were held "besides their ordinary Exercises in the Colledge-Hall)...in the audience of the Magistrates, Ministers, and other Schollars, for the probation of their growth in Learning...constantly once every moneth"; declamations were also scheduled weekly, "So ordered that every Scholler may declaime once a moneth." [harvard-college-q006, harvard-college-q017]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): A first degree was granted to any scholar found able "to read the Originalls of the Old and New Testament into the Latine tongue, and to resolve them Logically," of "godly life and conversation," and approved by the Overseers and Master at a public Act; a second degree (Master of Arts) required submitting a written synopsis of Logic, Natural and Moral Philosophy, Arithmetic, Geometry and Astronomy, and defending theses, again with "godly life and conversation" approved by the Overseers and Master. [harvard-college-q018, harvard-college-q019]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): See C2 above for the six-day weekly subject schedule; daily, scholars attended their Tutor's chamber "at the 7th. houre in the morning...at his opening the Scripture and prayer, so also at the 5th. houre at night." [harvard-college-q015, harvard-college-q016, harvard-college-q017, harvard-college-q013]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): Scholars attended their Tutor's chamber at the 7th hour each morning for Scripture and prayer, and again at the 5th hour each evening to account for their private reading; meal-and-recreation time was capped by rule at "an hour at morning bever, half an hour at afternoon bever, an hour and a half at dinner, and so long at supper," beyond which absence from studies without leave was a fault. [harvard-college-q013, harvard-college-q026]
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): NOT FOUND as a specific rule in the fetched Rules and Precepts or Laws and Orders (New England's First Fruits describes the Edifice's "Chambers and studies also fitted for, and possessed by the Students," but this descriptive sentence about the building was not formalized into a quote record this session; not a rule of residence).
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): Scholars were required to read Scripture twice daily and account for it to their Tutor; in "the publike church assembly" they were required to "carefully shun all gestures that show any contempt or neglect of God's ordinances." No fixed weekly count of chapel/church attendances was found. [harvard-college-q010, harvard-college-q042]
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): Absence from prayer or lectures "above once a weeke" without necessary impediment made a scholar "lyable to Admonition"; transgression of "any of the Lawes of God, or the Schoole," after two admonitions, brought "correction" for a minor and referral to the Overseers "to be admonished at the publick monethly Act" for an adult; scholars were forbidden the company of men leading "an unfit, and dissolute life," and could not leave town without their Tutor's (or, absent him, parents' or guardians') permission. Nathaniel Eaton himself, the College's first master, was removed from his trust in September 1639 "having been convicted of sundry abuses." [harvard-college-q012, harvard-college-q014, harvard-college-q040]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): Scholars were to "honor as their parents, magistrates, elders, tutors, and aged persons, by being silent in their presence...not gainsaying," with bowing and standing uncovered among the customary marks of respect; none was to "pragmatically intrude or intermeddle in other men's affairs"; a scholar was called "by his surname only, till he be invested with his first degree," except fellow-commoners or noblemen's sons; no scholar could "buy, sell, or exchange any thing, to the value of sixpence, without the allowance of his parents, guardians, or Tutors." No dress code was found in the fetched material. [harvard-college-q023, harvard-college-q022, harvard-college-q025, harvard-college-q027]
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): NOT FOUND as a fee schedule in the fetched material (contrast Emmanuel College's specific shilling-and-pence figures). The College itself was supported by the General Court's original £400 grant, the Charlestown-Boston ferry revenue, John Harvard's bequest, and further private gifts; the 1650 Charter empowered the Corporation to hold land up to £500 per annum and to receive further gifts and bequests. [harvard-college-q031, harvard-college-q029]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): "By 1642 the first class of nine members was graduated from Harvard College" [SECONDARY: Miller & Johnson's 1938 editorial note]. [harvard-college-q020]
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: nef1643, lawsliberties1642, charter1650, quincy1840v1-narrative)

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): Nathaniel Eaton was "chosen Professor of the said school" in 1637 and managed its early building accounts before being removed for "sundry abuses" in September 1639; "Master Corlet," master of the adjoining Grammar School, is separately named and praised "for his abilities, dexterity and painfulnesse"; Henry Dunster, President from autumn 1640, "trained up his Pupills in the tongues and Arts, and...seasoned them with the principles of Divinity and Christianity."
