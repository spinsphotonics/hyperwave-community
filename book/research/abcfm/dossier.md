# Dossier — American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions (`abcfm`)

Note on sourcing: the ABCFM is a missionary sending board, not a school, so several template
fields designed for an educational institution (Admission, Curriculum, most of Common Life
and Teachers) do not apply to it in the way they apply to a seminary; those fields are
answered honestly as NOT APPLICABLE / NOT FOUND rather than stretched to fit. The chapter's
"charter" document, per the E-abcfm ticket instruction, is the 1812 Instructions given by the
Prudential Committee to Judson's departing party (document_id firstten1834), quoted in full
in charter_abridged.md. The Constitution's purpose clause is quoted separately, at second
hand, from Anderson 1861 (see discrepancies.md item 2). Passages Anderson quotes directly
(in quotation marks, from the charter, from the 1812 General Assembly reply, or from
first-hand reminiscence letters written for his 1861 volume) are treated PRIMARY; Anderson's
own connective narrative is SECONDARY, cited [SECONDARY: anderson1861, page].

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): "American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions," also referred to in its own documents simply as "the Board" or "Board of Commissioners." [abcfm-q010]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): The Board originated in the 1810 memorial of four Andover students to the General Association of Massachusetts, meeting at Bradford; Anderson dates the Board's institution to 29 June 1810 in his own retrospective summary ("From the first appointment of the Board, at Bradford, June 29, 1810, to the embarkation of the missionaries in February, 1812..." -- quoted within Samuel Nott's reminiscence letter). The Board's first ordained missionaries (Judson, Nott, Newell, Hall, and Rice) were ordained at Salem on 6 February 1812. [abcfm-q011, abcfm-q013] See discrepancies.md item 1 for the fuller multi-stage sequence (1810 founding meeting; 1811 second meeting and correspondence with the Presbyterian General Assembly; 1812 Act of Incorporation, not independently fetched this session).
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): The "four names appended to the memorial to the General Association of Massachusetts, which was the immediate occasion of forming the Board, were Adoniram Judson, Samuel Nott, Samuel J. Mills, and Samuel Newell" (Judson's own graduate class at Andover). [abcfm-q011, abcfm-q012]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Bradford, Massachusetts (the 1810 founding meeting of the General Association); Salem, Massachusetts (the 1812 ordination and the Prudential Committee's Instructions, dated at Salem).
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): "Instructions given by the Prudential Committee of the American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions, to the Missionaries to the East," dated Salem, 7 February 1812, addressed to Judson, Nott, Newell, Hall, and Rice by name. English. document_id: firstten1834. [abcfm-q001, abcfm-q008]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The Board's charter purpose, as quoted by Anderson, is "for propagating the gospel in heathen lands by supporting missionaries and diffusing a knowledge of the Holy Scriptures"; the Board itself was earlier "created for 'devising ways and means, and adopting and prosecuting measures, for the spread of the gospel in heathen lands.'" The 1812 Instructions restate the aim in terms of the individual mission: "The great object of your Mission is to impart to those who sit in darkness, and in the region and shadow of death, the saving knowledge of Christ." [abcfm-q009, abcfm-q010, abcfm-q004]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PS): [SECONDARY: anderson1861, p. 41] Anderson frames the Board's origin as arising from "the desire of several young men in the Andover Theological Seminary to preach the gospel in the heathen world" -- a felt personal call among students rather than an institutional crisis narrative. [abcfm-q011]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND as a distinct creedal-subscription clause comparable to Andover's Associate Creed (searched: firstten1834, anderson1861); the 1812 Instructions are pastoral and practical rather than confessional, though article 10 requires that converts show "credible evidence that they are true believers" before admission to baptism and church membership. [abcfm-q006]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): The Board's own immediate antecedent, per Anderson, was the "Society of the Brethren," a secret student missionary society "formed, through his [Samuel J. Mills's] agency, called 'The Brethren,' which had for its object 'to effect, in the persons of its members, a mission or missions to the heathen,'" begun at Williams College and transferred, with its constitution and records, to Andover Seminary. [abcfm-q014, abcfm-q011]

## T — Teachers
Not applicable in the seminary sense. The "Prudential Committee" functioned as the Board's executive body issuing Instructions; its Secretary at the time of the 1812 Instructions was Samuel Worcester, who signed the attestation "A true copy from the Records of the Prudential Committee." [abcfm-q008]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The Instructions open by placing the missionaries' "first concern...must be personal" -- their own holiness -- before any missionary technique: "Your first concern, dear Brethren, must be personal. As you have given yourselves to the service of God in the gospel of His Son among the Gentiles, it will be of the utmost importance...that your hearts be kept constantly burning with love to God." They close by reminding the party, "Yo are made a spectacle to God, to angels and to men." [abcfm-q002, abcfm-q007]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Personal piety first; unity and charity among the missionaries themselves and with other Protestant missionary societies already on the field; strict economy of time and money; early formation of a gathered church among the missionaries and, in time, among converts; and evangelism aimed at "the great object...to impart to those who sit in darkness...the saving knowledge of Christ." [abcfm-q002, abcfm-q004, abcfm-q005]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Interference "with the powers that be" and "intermeddling with political concerns"; vehement denunciation of the heathen's "superstitions" in place of patient positive instruction ("not vehemently to declaim against their superstitions, but...to bring them as directly as possible to the knowledge of divine truth").
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: firstten1834, anderson1861) this session.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: firstten1834, anderson1861) this session.

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The Prudential Committee of the Board itself issues written Instructions directly to each departing party, addressed by name; the 1812 Instructions are addressed "To the Rev. Adoniram Judson, Samuel Nott, Samuel Newell, Gordon Hall, and Luther Rice, Missionaries to the East...under the American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions." [abcfm-q001]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PO): "Our present desire is, that the seat of this mission should be in some part of the empire of Birmah," though the missionaries were left "at your discretion as to the place where to make your station" upon fuller information after arrival in India. [abcfm-q003]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): The first party sent under these Instructions numbered five ordained missionaries (Judson, Nott, Newell, Hall, Rice), per the address of the Instructions itself; a fuller count over a longer period was not found this session. [abcfm-q001]
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): The Instructions direct the missionaries to observe "strict economy, in regard both to your time and expenditures" and, "so far as you can consistently with your missionary duties," to apply themselves "to the most eligible ways and means of procuring a support for yourselves and families, agreeably to the example of European missionaries, and even of the apostles" -- i.e., partial self-support is encouraged alongside support from the Board.
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND in the documents fetched this session as a formally quoted casualty figure (searched: firstten1834, anderson1861); general scholarship records the early death of Harriet Newell in 1812, but no passage from the fetched texts was formally quoted to that effect this session.
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): "Instructions given by the Prudential Committee...to the Missionaries to the East," Salem, 7 February 1812, document_id firstten1834 -- reprinted in full in charter_abridged.md. [abcfm-q001, abcfm-q008]

## R — Fruit
- **R1 Adoniram Judson** (PS): One of the five addressed by the 1812 Instructions; changed his views on baptism en route and became the founding figure of the American Baptist foreign mission (see the `judson-triennial-convention` dossier for the fuller account). [abcfm-q001]
- **R2 Samuel Newell** (PS): One of the five original missionaries named in the Instructions; per Anderson's narrative, went on to Bombay and Ceylon; his wife Harriet Newell's early death was noted by Anderson as among the "providential and gracious aids to the establishment of the first foreign mission" (paraphrase; not formally quoted this session). [abcfm-q001]
- **R3 Gordon Hall** (PS): One of the five original missionaries; per Anderson, later wrote a widely circulated appeal on the scarcity of missionaries worldwide (quoted at length in anderson1861, not extracted as a formal quote record this session). [abcfm-q001]
- **R4 Samuel J. Mills** (PS): Not among the five ordained missionaries sent in 1812, but the founder of the "Society of the Brethren" at Williams College and, per Anderson, co-signer of the 1810 Bradford memorial that led directly to the Board's formation. [abcfm-q011, abcfm-q014]
