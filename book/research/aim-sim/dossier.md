# Dossier — Africa Inland Mission and Sudan Interior Mission (`aim-sim`)

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Two separate missions treated together per the roster. The Sudan Interior Mission (SIM) was so named from its 1893 expedition [aim-sim-q022, aim-sim-q025]. The Africa Inland Mission (AIM) was so named from its 1895 founding [aim-sim-q013 is SIM's own article, cf. general knowledge in the roster [HIGH] for AIM's name]. [HIGH, roster]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): SIM: the pioneer expedition of Gowans, Kent, and Bingham reached the Sudan in 1893 [aim-sim-q022, aim-sim-q025]; SIM was incorporated in Canada 14 September 1909 and in the United States 24 April 1926 (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Memorandum of Agreement, recitals (a)-(b); not separately quote-tagged, summary only). AIM: Scott's party of eight sailed in August 1895 and reached Kenya by the end of October 1895; the Nzawi station and three further stations were opened by the end of 1896 [aim-sim-q006]. [HIGH]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): SIM: Walter Gowans, Thomas Kent, and Rowland V. Bingham, the three who set out in 1893 [aim-sim-q022, aim-sim-q013, aim-sim-q025]; Bingham later served as General Director [sim-burden-of-sudan front matter, "ROWLAND V. BINGHAM, General Director," not separately quoted]. AIM: Peter Cameron Scott, founder and leader of the first party, sailing from Philadelphia [dossier field F2 sources; general AIM leaflet material, e.g. aim-sim-q001 context]; Charles E. Hurlburt succeeded Scott as leader after Scott's 1896 death [aim-sim-q007 context].
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): SIM organizing base Toronto and later New York; AIM organizing base Philadelphia. Both missions worked in interior/inland Africa (SIM: West Africa/the historic "Sudan" belt; AIM: British East Africa, later Tanganyika, Belgian Congo, Uganda). [HIGH, roster; aim-sim-q018 context: SIM fields include Nigeria, French West Africa, Ethiopia, Sudan, Eritrea, Somalia, Liberia]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No 1893/1895/1898 founding constitution text for either mission was located and fetched this session (see discrepancies.md items 4-5 and sources.csv rows NONE-1, NONE-3). The nearest primary rule-document fetched is *The Principles and Practice of the Sudan Interior Mission*, revised 1958, English, document_id `sim-principles-practice` [aim-sim-q001 names the underlying "constitution" this booklet summarizes for AIM in a separate leaflet, not the same document]. This 1958 SIM document is used as the chapter's abridged "Charter" text, with the gap from it back to the 1893/1898 originals stated plainly.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): SIM: "formed in 1893 by Messrs. Gowan, Kent and Bingham, under a deep sense of the pressing need in the vast areas across Africa then known as the Sudan," with the aim "to bring men to a saving knowledge of the love of God in Christ by means of itinerant and localized work" [aim-sim-q013]. AIM: the constitution's doctrinal and faith principle as summarized in a mid-century leaflet [aim-sim-q001, aim-sim-q002]; the Mission's operating policy of full information without solicitation [aim-sim-q012].
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Gowans "felt the burden of the Sudan so strong that he was impelled to cross the Atlantic," after failing to secure any missionary board's support in America or England [aim-sim-q022]. Scott, recovering from fever in Philadelphia after two years in the Congo, "studied the map of Africa" and "conceived a p[l]an to plant mission stations" across the unreached interior tablelands (aim-leaflets, "The Work Begun" leaflet narrative, general paraphrase; not independently quote-tagged beyond aim-sim-q005 for the diary line).
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): AIM's doctrinal basis, as summarized from its constitution: the Trinity; the deity and humanity of Christ, virgin birth, substitutionary atonement, physical resurrection; the personality of the Holy Spirit and inspiration of Scripture; human sinfulness and the necessity of the new birth; salvation by grace; baptism and the Lord's Supper; the one true church and its responsibility for world evangelization; the Second Coming; and eternal heaven and hell [aim-sim-q001, aim-sim-q002]. SIM's doctrinal statement (Article 3 of the 1958 Memorandum) is a nine-point list — Scripture's plenary inspiration, the Trinity, the Fall, the substitutionary Atonement, the Resurrection and Ascension, justification by faith, regeneration by the Holy Spirit, the Second Coming and resurrection of the dead, and eternal blessedness/punishment — plus a tenth article on sanctification (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 3(a)-(j); summarized, not separately quote-tagged beyond the general candidate-subscription rule at aim-sim-q015/aim-sim-q017/aim-sim-q019). Missionary candidates of both missions were required to subscribe: SIM required signing the endorsement statement [aim-sim-q019]; AIM candidates were "expected to satisfy the Directors and the Council" as to soundness in the faith (paraphrase from the leaflet material at aim-sim-q001-q002, no separate direct quote located for this exact clause in AIM's own text this session — the analogous CIM clause is quoted in the china-inland-mission dossier, and SIM's own near-identical clause is at text/sim-principles-practice.txt Article 9(b), not separately tagged).
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): SIM's 1958 Principles and Practice closes its article on Relation to Governments with the sentence "The weapons of our warfare" must be practically recognized as spiritual and not carnal" [aim-sim-q016] — word for word the closing sentence of the China Inland Mission's own Principles and Practice, quoted in this book's china-inland-mission chapter (quote_id cim-q011). No source read this session states in so many words that SIM copied CIM's document, but the identical closing sentence, and the shared structure (Object, Character/Constitution, doctrinal statement, Personnel before/after appointment, Support, Relation to Governments, Church Government), is direct textual evidence of imitation, consistent with the roster's lineage map ("Brethren and Mildmay -> Hudson Taylor, China Inland Mission (1865) -> faith missions: AIM (1895), SIM (1893), WEC (1913)"). No comparable direct-quotation evidence for AIM's founding document was located this session; AIM's imitation of CIM is stated in the roster and in secondary web material, not independently confirmed here from a primary AIM text, and is therefore not asserted in the chapter as more than roster-level.

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): AIM: candidates "must not be over thirty years of age" [aim-sim-q009]. No source read this session states an AIM minimum age or any SIM age rule.
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): AIM: "at least a high school education and training in an approved Bible school, or the equivalent" [aim-sim-q009]. No SIM source read this session states a required prior education.
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): AIM's "Spiritual Qualifications" open with assurance of one's own salvation "through the finished work of Christ" [aim-sim-q010]. SIM candidates were required to submit "a statement of their convictions as to the Doctrinal Principles of the Mission" (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 9(b), paraphrase) and to "count the cost" of privation, toil, loneliness, and danger before offering themselves [aim-sim-q015]; SIM candidates were also required to abstain from tobacco and alcohol [aim-sim-q017].
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: aim-leaflets, aim-life-out-of-death, sim-principles-practice, sim-burden-of-sudan). No formal entrance examination, as distinct from the doctrinal statement and candidate interview process, is described in any source read this session.
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): Scott's 1895 party included his sister Margaret and, by AIM's own 1951 leaflet, the first party of eight comprised "five men and three women" (aim-leaflets, "Beginning" leaflet, paraphrase; text present in text/aim-leaflets.txt around the "This nondenominational work began in 1895" passage but not separately quote-tagged this session). No source read this session states a distinct role or restriction for women beyond their presence among the missionaries sent.
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): No prior preaching or field experience is stated as a formal requirement for either mission in the sources read this session. Physical qualifications ("healthy and thoroughly sound in constitution") are stated for AIM candidates as a qualification rather than a credential of experience [aim-sim-q011]. SIM candidates were required to "count the cost" of hardship [aim-sim-q015].

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all four documents). Neither mission is described in the sources read this session as running a resident training course with a fixed length of its own; SIM's Personnel articles instead describe a two-year probation followed by recognition as Junior Missionary, paralleling CIM's structure (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 10(b)-(d), paraphrase; not separately quote-tagged this session).
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): NOT FOUND. No subject list or class sequence is given in any source read this session; both missions functioned as sending bodies governing missionaries already in the field, on the CIM model, rather than as resident schools (see F9 above and the china-inland-mission chapter's Curriculum section for the same structural point).
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): SIM: probation (up to two years) leading to Junior Missionary status, then Senior Missionary "five years after arrival on the field" upon passing "all the required examinations in the language" (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 10(b), (d); paraphrase, not separately quote-tagged this session). NOT FOUND for AIM.
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): SIM required "examinations in the language" for advancement to Senior Missionary status (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 10(d); paraphrase). No specific language is named in the sources read this session; NOT FOUND for AIM.
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all four documents).
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a formal instructional program; SIM missionaries-under-appointment were placed "under the superintendence of Senior Missionaries in charge of stations" (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 10(c); paraphrase), an apprenticeship arrangement rather than a class.
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): SIM required passing "all the required examinations in the language" before Senior Missionary recognition (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 10(d); paraphrase). No form or content of the examination is described. NOT FOUND for AIM.
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): No degree or certificate is described. SIM candidates instead signed the endorsement statement approving the Principles and Practice as the condition of being received as a candidate [aim-sim-q019].

- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): SIM: a missionary whose doctrinal views changed after appointment "must be prepared to resign his connection with the Mission" (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 9(c); paraphrase, context for aim-sim-q017). No AIM discipline rule was located this session.

- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): AIM: by a 1917 leaflet-collection figure, 180 missionaries at thirty-two stations after twenty-two years (aim-leaflets, "What Is a Faith Mission?" leaflet, paraphrase, context surrounding aim-sim-q008); by 1950-51 figures elsewhere in the same bound volume, 369-385 missionaries at fifty-nine to sixty stations (aim-leaflets, multiple leaflets; paraphrase, not separately quote-tagged). SIM: fields listed in the 1958 Principles and Practice span nine named territories (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, opening address list; paraphrase). Scott himself died of fever 4 December 1896, little over a year after AIM's first party sailed (aim-leaflets, "The Work Begun" leaflet, paraphrase, context surrounding aim-sim-q005).

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Scott's diary entry, his last, read "Here I am, Lord, use me in life or death" [aim-sim-q005]. The SIM Council's own first-issue statement of the venture: "If it is of God, it will succeed; if it is not of Him it will fail" [aim-sim-q007]. SIM's Principles and Practice restates CIM's own closing emphasis verbatim: "'The weapons of our warfare' must be practically recognized as spiritual and not carnal" [aim-sim-q016].
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Both missions are explicitly "faith missions" on the CIM model: "The Africa Inland Mission is what is called a 'faith mission'" [aim-sim-q008], with no appeal for funds [aim-sim-q003, aim-sim-q004, aim-sim-q012, aim-sim-q014].
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): SIM missionaries were warned against reliance on government protection: "Too great caution cannot be exercised... to avoid difficulties and complications with the people, and especially with the authorities" [aim-sim-q020].
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND. No single fixed motto is stated as such in any source read this session for either mission.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): The 1958 SIM Principles and Practice restates the faith principle in near-identical terms to the earlier support clause quoted in the leaflets [aim-sim-q014, cf. aim-sim-q003-q004], showing the emphasis held constant across at least six decades of SIM's own documents.

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): SIM's Object clause states the aim "to train and send forth missionaries to Africa... to preach the Gospel... with the aim of establishing churches which are self-governing, self-supporting and self-propagating" [aim-sim-q018]. Converts were "taught to study the Word of God and encouraged to carry the administration responsibilities of the church from the beginning" [aim-sim-q021].
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): AIM: Kenya from 1895, spreading to Tanganyika (1909), Belgian Congo (1912), Uganda (1918), French Equatorial Africa (1924), and Anglo-Egyptian Sudan (1949) (aim-leaflets, "Beginning"/"Development" leaflet, paraphrase). SIM: by 1958, fields in Nigeria, French West Africa, Ethiopia, Sudan, Eritrea, Somalia, Liberia, Aden Protectorate, and Kenya (text/sim-principles-practice.txt, Article 3; paraphrase).
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): See L7 above; AIM figures of 180 (1917) and 369-385 (1950-51) missionaries are recorded there rather than repeated here to avoid double statement.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): AIM: "the policy is and has always been — as to the work, full presentation; as to funds, no solicitation" [aim-sim-q004]; the constitution's faith principle holds "that God can and does and will... meet all the financial requirements of the work" without solicitation [aim-sim-q003]; Life out of Death's front-matter policy statement states the same rule in near-identical terms, including that the Mission "does not solicit individuals to make contributions nor ask Churches for collections" [aim-sim-q012]. SIM: "The Mission is supported entirely by the free-will offerings of the Lord's people" [aim-sim-q014], word for word the same formula as CIM's own support article (china-inland-mission dossier field S4, quote_id cim-q004), further textual evidence for F9.
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): Kent died at Bida; Gowans died shortly after reaching Zaria, within the first year of the 1893 expedition [context surrounding aim-sim-q023, aim-sim-q024a-b]. A mother who had already given a daughter to China and then a son to the Sudan is quoted saying she "would rather have had Walter go out to the Sudan and die... than have him home now disobeying his Lord" [aim-sim-q024a, aim-sim-q024b]. Scott died of fever 4 December 1896 (aim-leaflets, "The Work Begun" leaflet, paraphrase, context at aim-sim-q005-q006). No aggregate casualty count comparable to CIM's Boxer-era figures was located for either mission this session.
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): SIM's Principles and Practice itself functioned as the standing instruction, formally endorsed by each candidate's signed statement [aim-sim-q019]. No separate instructions document, comparable to the ABCFM's 1812 Instructions to Judson's party, was located for either mission this session.

## R — Fruit
- **R1–R4 Up to six named alumni or missionaries: name, dates, field, one-sentence outcome, source** (PS):
  1. Peter Cameron Scott: founder and first leader of the Africa Inland Mission; sailed with his party in August 1895; died of fever 4 December 1896, having written in his diary, "Here I am, Lord, use me in life or death" [aim-sim-q005, F2/F3 context].
  2. Walter Gowans: with Kent and Bingham, led the 1893 expedition that founded the Sudan Interior Mission; died at Zaria after the expedition's supplies were exhausted and its members scattered by illness [aim-sim-q022, context at aim-sim-q023-q024].
  3. Thomas Kent: member of the 1893 expedition; died at Bida; his last recorded written words, in a fellow missionary's autograph album, affirmed "Our citizenship is in heaven... who shall fashion anew the body of our humiliation" [aim-sim-q023].
  4. Rowland V. Bingham: the surviving member of the 1893 expedition; returned to Canada in broken health, later became SIM's General Director, and signed the front matter of the pamphlet *The Burden of the Sudan* in that capacity [aim-sim-q022, sim-burden-of-sudan front matter].
  5. Charles E. Hurlburt: AIM's General Director following Scott's death; his 1916 return from Africa and address on the "Victorious Life" are recorded in a later AIM leaflet (aim-leaflets, "Is It Worth Sending?" leaflet, paraphrase; context near the doctrinal-basis leaflet).

No further named individual's one-sentence outcome, with citation, was located and formally quoted this session.
