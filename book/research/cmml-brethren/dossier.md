# Dossier — Christian Missions in Many Lands (`cmml-brethren`)

Coverage note: CMML is not a school, and this dossier's template (built for institutions with
courses, teachers, and classrooms) fits it awkwardly by design — the roster itself calls out
that "the Brethren principle of no formal seminary is itself a charter position." Fields under
C (Curriculum), T (Teachers), and several under L (Common life) are honestly NOT FOUND because
the sources describe no such thing to find, not because the search was incomplete. Two document
families were fetched and read this session: (1) Anthony Norris Groves's 1825/1829 *Christian
Devotedness*, the foundational faith-missions text named in the roster and ticket, read in full
from a public-domain Project Gutenberg transcription; and (2) CMML's own current website
(cmml.us), which is not an archival founding document but is the organization's own live
self-description of its history, its commendation practice, and its 2021 Statement of Faith.
No historical (i.e., 1921-era or 1952-era) CMML constitution, "statement of principles," or a
specimen Brethren letter of commendation was located and fetched this session; the Christian
Brethren Archive at the University of Manchester (named in the roster) returned nothing to a
direct fetch attempt (see sources.csv). Facts about CMML's practice are therefore dated to when
the source itself is dated (mostly "as CMML describes it today"), and are not assumed to hold
unchanged back to 1921 or to 1952 without a citation that says so.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): "Christian Missions in Many
  Lands, Ltd." was the organization's name at its 1921 incorporation; it was reorganized as
  "Christian Missions in Many Lands, Inc." in a 1971 merger of four earlier missionary service
  groups. CMML's own history page traces informal roots back to 1894 (the earliest commendations
  it records) and names three earlier, separate service ministries — Voices from the Vineyard
  (1904), the Julia Hasse Memorial Missionary Home (1929), and The Fields (1938) — as among the
  four bodies merged into the 1971 Inc. [cmml-brethren-q009, cmml-brethren-q013,
  cmml-brethren-q015]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): 1921 (incorporation of
  CMML, Ltd.); 1971 (merger and reorganization as CMML, Inc.). CMML's own account also gives 1894
  as the year its "roots" begin, with named individuals first commended in that year.
  [cmml-brethren-q009, cmml-brethren-q013, cmml-brethren-q014, cmml-brethren-q015]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): NOT FOUND for named founders or first
  officers of the 1921 CMML, Ltd. specifically, in the material fetched this session. The sources
  read do name individuals from CMML's pre-history: Thomas Melville, Eliza Logan, and Eliza
  Lennox (commended 1894, the earliest instance CMML's own history records); Captain L. A. Steen
  and R. J. MacLachan (founders of Voices from the Vineyard, 1904); and Richard Hill, who with his
  wife began a Training School for Missionaries in 1917. None of these individuals is stated by
  the source to have founded CMML, Ltd. itself. For the wider Brethren faith-missions movement
  that CMML serves, Anthony Norris Groves (1795-1853) is the author of the movement's founding
  devotional-doctrinal text (see F5). [cmml-brethren-q014, cmml-brethren-q008]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): United States. CMML's current headquarters, per its own history page, is
  a Guest Home property in Wall Township, New Jersey, purchased in 1972; the roster row for this
  institution instead names Spring Lake, NJ, as the office location — the two New Jersey
  locations are close together and may reflect a mailing address versus a physical property, or a
  later move; this is recorded as a discrepancy rather than resolved. [cmml-brethren-q026; see
  discrepancies.md]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): Per the ticket's
  own instruction, the doctrinal "charter" text for this chapter is Anthony Norris Groves,
  *Christian Devotedness, or The Consideration of Our Saviour's Precept, "Lay not up for
  yourselves treasures upon earth"* (1st ed. 1825; text used here is the 2nd ed., London, James
  Nisbet, 1829). document_id: `groves-christian-devotedness-1829`. This is not a constitution of
  CMML itself — CMML did not yet exist in 1825 — but the roster explicitly names it as the
  foundational faith-missions text behind the Brethren commendation system CMML serves, and no
  earlier or more official CMML founding instrument was located this session (see F1-F2 and
  sources.csv). [cmml-brethren-q008]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Groves opens by describing his purpose as
  answering "the strange and melancholy fact — that Christianity has made little or no progress
  for fifteen successive centuries," which he attributes to a want of the "entire and real...
  devotion of themselves, their property, time and talents to Christ, their Lord and King" that
  he finds in the New Testament church. [cmml-brethren-q001, cmml-brethren-q002] CMML's own
  present-day statement of purpose is "advancing the spread of the Gospel globally" by serving
  the missionaries commended by Brethren assemblies. [cmml-brethren-q009]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO/PS): Groves states the crisis
  in doctrinal terms — centuries of stalled Christian advance, which he traces to Christians not
  taking Christ's command to "lay not up... treasures upon earth" literally.
  [cmml-brethren-q001, cmml-brethren-q006] CMML's own history states a narrower, practical need
  behind its 1921 incorporation: assemblies' individually commended missionaries needed "a
  recognized legal entity to fulfil the requirements of worldwide governments" — i.e., an
  incorporated body governments would recognize, since no single Brethren assembly is such a
  body. [cmml-brethren-q013]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): CMML's current Statement of
  Faith is a twelve-point evangelical confession (Scripture's inspiration and inerrancy, the
  Trinity, the deity and virgin birth of Christ, universal sinfulness, Christ's atoning death and
  bodily resurrection, salvation by faith alone, the church's commission to preach the Gospel, and
  the personal return of Christ), explicitly dated: "Approved by the CMML Board of Directors,
  2021." [cmml-brethren-q022, cmml-brethren-q023, cmml-brethren-q024] No historical (1921- or
  1952-era) doctrinal statement was located this session, so it is UNVERIFIED whether or how this
  2021 wording differs from what a commended worker would have subscribed to in Jim Elliot's day;
  do not state the 2021 wording as what Elliot himself subscribed to.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): The
  practice of commendation is grounded by CMML's own explanation in the biblical account of Paul
  and Barnabas being commended by the church at Antioch (Acts 13:2-4, 14:26), which CMML cites as
  the model its assemblies follow. [cmml-brethren-q016, cmml-brethren-q017] As a historical
  antecedent within the Brethren movement itself, CMML's own account of "how it began" traces
  organized commendation and missionary support back to 1894, before CMML's own 1921 incorporation.
  [cmml-brethren-q014] A widely repeated claim that Groves's *Christian Devotedness* directly
  shaped George Müller's later faith principle was found only in a WebSearch results summary, not
  read within any primary or secondary text fetched and opened this session; it is therefore
  UNVERIFIED here and is not stated as fact.

## A — Admission
Note: CMML does not admit students; assemblies "commend" workers. The questions below are
answered, where possible, from CMML's account of what commendation requires.
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: cmml-commendation, cmml-whoweare,
  cmml-whatwedo, cmml-our-history)
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): NOT FOUND. No source read this session states an
  educational requirement for commendation; see M3 on the Brethren rejection of dependence on
  formal ministerial training as such.
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): Commendation
  requires that "both the worker and the assembly sense God's clear calling" and that the workers
  be "both qualified and proven before going," within the assembly where the worker is "in
  regular fellowship." [cmml-brethren-q016, cmml-brethren-q017, cmml-brethren-q018] The workers
  are "called and qualified persons" who are "handed over to the grace of God." [cmml-brethren-q017]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): No formal examination is described. Instead, "the
  leaders of the sending church" must be "satisfied that the potential workers are both qualified
  and called by God," a discernment process rather than a set examination.
  [cmml-brethren-q016, cmml-brethren-q020]
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PS): Yes, as commended missionaries in their own
  right, from the earliest instances CMML's history records: Eliza Logan and Eliza Lennox (with
  her twelve-year-old daughter Clara) were among those commended in 1894 to join a missionary team
  in China, and Elisabeth Murrain was commended with her husband George to Angola in 1892.
  [cmml-brethren-q014, cmml-brethren-q027]
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): Commendation is practiced "by
  the assembly where the missionary worker is in regular fellowship" — i.e., it presupposes prior,
  proven standing and service within a home assembly, though no source read this session specifies
  a minimum length of that service. [cmml-brethren-q018]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND; not applicable in the
  sense the template intends (CMML is not a residential school).
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): Not applicable to commended workers in
  the same sense, but CMML has, since 1972, operated a Guest Home in Wall Township, New Jersey,
  providing "accommodations for the office, a clothing center, and rooms and apartments for
  missionaries," serving as a place of lodging for workers in transit to or from the field.
  [cmml-brethren-q026]
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): NOT FOUND for any fixed worship pattern among commended
  workers as a body; CMML's history does record recurring "Missionary Prayer Meetings" that spread
  across North American assemblies from the 1930s onward, without a fixed liturgical form
  described. [see cmml-our-history.txt narrative; not independently formalized as a quote_id this
  session — flagged for a follow-up pass]
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): NOT FOUND.
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND.
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): Commended workers are supported by
  "unsolicited, free-will gifts" that CMML forwards to them, not by salary or contract; CMML
  states plainly that it "does not send or superintend missionaries but simply serves them," and
  that workers "look to the Lord alone for direction and to supply their financial needs, without
  contractual links to any agency." [cmml-brethren-q011, cmml-brethren-q012] Commendation itself
  "neither guarantees nor establishes eligibility for financial support, though the assembly
  should support the missionary as God enables." [cmml-brethren-q021] This is the same
  faith-principle of daily dependence that Groves argues for as a matter of doctrine, not merely
  of missionary practicality. [cmml-brethren-q006]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): NOT FOUND for any historical count. CMML's current

  figure, not a historical one, and not independently formalized as a quote_id this session.
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): NOT FOUND in the sources read

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Groves: real, entire devotion of
  "themselves, their property, time and talents to Christ, their Lord and King" as the mark of
  primitive Christianity that he wants recovered. [cmml-brethren-q002] He grounds the missionary
  imperative directly in the Great Commission: "'Go ye into all the world and preach the gospel to
  every creature'... was the parting command of our Blessed Saviour." [cmml-brethren-q003]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): The faith principle of daily, undertaken dependence on God for
  provision, argued by Groves as the literal meaning of "lay not up for yourselves treasures upon
  earth," [cmml-brethren-q006] and continued by CMML today in its description of workers who
  "look to the Lord alone for direction and to supply their financial needs, without contractual
  links to any agency." [cmml-brethren-q011] This is paired with the assembly-based, non-agency
  sending mechanism of commendation itself, discussed under S1.
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Groves rejects the
  "loose general statements" reading of Christ's command to "lay not up... treasures," insisting
  on a literal reading against contemporaries who treated it as rhetorical
  exaggeration. [see raw text, Section I; general paraphrase, not independently quoted beyond
  cmml-brethren-q001] CMML today explicitly denies that commendation creates ecclesiastical
  control or organizational dependency: "Commendation does not imply the independence of the
  worker" and, conversely, "Commendation does not confer any ecclesiastical authority or
  position." [cmml-brethren-q019, cmml-brethren-q020] CMML likewise states of itself that it "does
  not send or superintend missionaries" — an explicit rejection of the mission-board model.
  [cmml-brethren-q012]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND for a historical motto. CMML's
  current website tagline is "Advancing the spread of the Gospel globally," used as the site's
  header strap-line; not independently formalized as a quote_id this session, and not attested as
  historic.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): CMML's 2021 Statement of
  Faith restates the Great Commission emphasis in article 11: "The Church is commissioned to
  preach and spread the Gospel of Jesus Christ throughout the world for the purpose of leading
  persons to salvation and making disciples." [cmml-brethren-q023, cmml-brethren-q024]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)**
  (PO): Commendation by a local, autonomous Brethren assembly — not a mission board — is the
  sending mechanism. CMML defines it as "that act of an assembly whereby called and qualified
  persons are handed over to the grace of God for the purpose of accomplishing the goals of
  evangelism, discipleship, and church planting in another locality," grounded in Paul and
  Barnabas's commissioning at Antioch (Acts 13:2-4). [cmml-brethren-q016, cmml-brethren-q017] CMML
  itself is explicitly not the sending body: "As a service organization, CMML does not send or
  superintend missionaries but simply serves them." [cmml-brethren-q012] Elisabeth Elliot herself
  is described, in material from Wheaton College's own archives, as "a Plymouth Brethren
  missionary to Ecuador" at the time her husband Jim and four other missionaries were killed —
  independent confirmation, from a source outside CMML's own website, that the Elliots' Ecuador
  mission ran through this Brethren commendation system. [wheaton-college-q023, in
  research/wheaton-college/quotes.jsonl] No document read this session names CMML specifically (as
  opposed to "Plymouth Brethren" generally) in connection with Jim Elliot's own 1952 commendation;
  do not state that CMML by name commended him beyond what the roster itself asserts, which this
  session did not independently verify against a primary record of his commendation.
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): China (1894, the earliest instance CMML's history
  records); Angola (1892, the Murrains). [cmml-brethren-q014, cmml-brethren-q027] For the specific
  Ecuador destination of Jim Elliot, Pete Fleming, Ed McCully, Roger Youderian, and Nate Saint, see
  the `wheaton-college` dossier (Sending section) — no CMML-side primary document naming Ecuador as
  a field or naming these five men was located and fetched this session.
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND for any historical period count. A
  present-day figure ("700+ missionaries" in "80+ countries") appears on CMML's website but is not
  a historical count and was not independently formalized as a quote_id this session.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): Faith principle, not
  salary: workers are supported by voluntary gifts CMML forwards, without contractual obligation,
  and commendation itself "neither guarantees nor establishes eligibility for financial support."
  [cmml-brethren-q011, cmml-brethren-q021] Groves argues the doctrinal case for exactly this kind
  of dependence. [cmml-brethren-q006]
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND in any CMML
  document read this session as a formal casualty record. The deaths of Jim Elliot and four
  companions in Ecuador in January 1956 are independently attested in material from Wheaton
  College's own archives (see the `wheaton-college` dossier, S5/R1), not in a CMML source fetched
  this session.
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): NOT FOUND. No
  document comparable to CIM's *Principles and Practice* or the 1812 ABCFM *Instructions* to
  Judson's party was located for CMML or the wider Brethren commendation system this session.
