# Dossier — Inter-Varsity Christian Fellowship (US) and the Urbana Missions Convention (`inter-varsity-urbana`)

**Coverage note:** No 1941 IVCF/USA founding charter, incorporation document, or period
doctrinal-basis text was located and fetched this session, nor a 1946 Toronto or 1948 Urbana
convention program. The Billy Graham Center Archives' own IVCF finding aid (Collection 300,
Wheaton College) was BLOCKED by a Cloudflare Turnstile gate (see
`text/wheaton-ivcf-collection300.REQUEST.md`). Two priority secondary works — Hunt and Hunt's
*For Christ and the University* (1991/1992) and A. Donald MacLeod's *C. Stacey Woods and the
Evangelical Rediscovery of the University* (2007) — were not located as digitized texts. In
their place, this dossier rests on IVCF/USA's own present-day institutional history pages,
Wikipedia, a Gale/Encyclopedia.com reference-work entry, Urbana's own conference-history page
(which reproduces short verbatim speaker quotations from 1946 onward), a 2007 InterVarsity
Press release, and a short copyrighted excerpt of Jim Elliot's own 1948 journal via the
Elisabeth Elliot Foundation. The Charter section reproduces IVCF/USA's *current* (adopted
2000) Statement of Agreement, explicitly flagged as a present-day substitute, not the 1941
founding document.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Inter-Varsity Christian
  Fellowship traces to a British student movement founded at Cambridge in 1877; the U.S. body
  became "InterVarsity Christian Fellowship" as an official organization in 1941; its
  publishing arm InterVarsity Press was founded in 1947 (per IVCF's own site) or 1948 (per
  Encyclopedia.com — see discrepancies.md #3); the movement's triennial student missions
  conferences, first held in Toronto in 1946, came to be called "Urbana" from the 1948
  conference at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign onward. [quote_ids: ivu-q001,
  ivu-q002, ivu-q005, ivu-q011, ivu-q014, ivu-q019, ivu-q029, ivu-q035]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): IVCF/USA's own site
  dates its "official beginning" to 14 November 1941; a Gale/Encyclopedia.com reference entry
  instead gives September 1941, and a 2007 InterVarsity Press release gives 1939-1940 as the
  year Stacey Woods "founded" the U.S. work (three unresolved readings — see discrepancies.md
  #1-2). The first missions convention (the Urbana predecessor) was held in Toronto in 1946;
  the second was held at the University of Illinois in 1948. [quote_ids: ivu-q001, ivu-q008,
  ivu-q014, ivu-q019, ivu-q021, ivu-q026, ivu-q032, ivu-q036]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): C. Stacey Woods, then General
  Secretary of the Canadian Inter-Varsity Christian Fellowship, is named as the U.S. movement's
  founder and first General Secretary, leading both the U.S. and Canadian movements with three
  staff on loan from Canada. [quote_ids: ivu-q001, ivu-q004, ivu-q016, ivu-q027, ivu-q032,
  ivu-q036]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): The U.S. movement's first chapter was formed at the University of
  Michigan (1938 visit, 1941 official organization); the first missions convention was held at
  the University of Toronto, Ontario (1946); from 1948 the convention was held at the
  University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. [quote_ids: ivu-q004, ivu-q019, ivu-q024]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND
  (searched: ivcf-doctrinal-basis-1941 [no hit], ivcf-first-convention-program-1946 [no hit],
  wheaton-ivcf-collection300 [BLOCKED]). No 1941 constitution, incorporation paper, or period
  doctrinal-basis text was located and fetched this session. `charter_abridged.md` substitutes
  IVCF/USA's current (adopted 2000) Statement of Agreement, document_id
  `ivcf-statement-agreement`, explicitly flagged as not the founding-era document.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): No period (1941-era) statement of
  purpose was located. The 1947 gathering at Harvard that produced the International
  Fellowship of Evangelical Students (IFES), of which IVCF/USA was a founding member, is
  recorded as meeting "to bring together the existing free movements which possess the same
  doctrinal basis and evangelical outlook and to give the utmost possible assistance to
  evangelical students who desire to take the gospel of Christ to the universities of the
  world" — a purpose statement from the founding generation, though for the international
  federation rather than IVCF/USA specifically. [quote_id: ivu-q010]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all
  text/ files). No source read this session gives the founders' own stated diagnosis of a
  crisis or need behind the 1941 U.S. organization; the closest is the background narrative of
  independent evangelical student groups "springing up" on U.S. campuses in the late 1930s to
  which the Canadian movement was responding, which is secondary narration, not a founders'
  quotation. [quote_id: ivu-q004, context]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No 1941-era doctrinal
  basis text was located. IVCF/USA's current Statement of Agreement (Basis of Faith plus
  Purpose) was "Adopted by the Board of Trustees October 20, 2000" and is signed by subscribers
  ("I hereby subscribe to the above Doctrinal Basis and Purpose") — reproduced in full in
  `charter_abridged.md` and flagged there as a present-day document only. A 2010 institutional
  article states a staff member "has written a seven-part study guide for reflecting on
  InterVarsity's Doctrinal Basis," confirming that some version of the Basis functioned as a
  standing subscribed document by that date, without dating its original 1941/1947 wording.
  [quote_ids: ivu-q038, ivu-q039, ivu-q047]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO):
  The U.S. movement's own pages trace its roots to a British student movement begun at
  Cambridge in 1877, exported to Canada by Howard Guinness in 1928, and brought to the
  University of Michigan by Stacey Woods in 1938. The 1946 Toronto convention is described as a
  joint gathering of the Student Foreign Missions Fellowship (absorbed into IVCF in 1945, per
  Encyclopedia.com) with InterVarsity USA and Canada; Encyclopedia.com further states the
  Urbana conference "continued the traditions of the Student Volunteer Movement and other
  evangelical youth mission organizations." [quote_ids: ivu-q002, ivu-q003, ivu-q009, ivu-q015,
  ivu-q017, ivu-q019, ivu-q026, ivu-q033, ivu-q034]

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files). No source
  states an age requirement for chapter membership or Urbana attendance; Urbana's own history
  page notes an adult "doesn't need to be a student to attend Urbana," but that is a
  present-day (2012) policy statement, not a founding-era admission rule.
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files). IVCF chapters
  were and are college/university-based, implying enrolled-student status, but no source states
  a formal prior-education requirement.
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): NOT FOUND
  (searched: all text/ files). No source states a conversion or church-membership testimony
  requirement for chapter membership; the current Statement of Agreement requires a signed
  subscription to the Basis of Faith and Purpose from those using it as a large-group agreement
  document, but no source dates or describes this practice for 1941. [quote_id: ivu-q039]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files). Not
  applicable in any source read this session; IVCF is a campus fellowship, not an examining
  institution.
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): Women are attested as central to the
  movement from its first decade: "gifted women took over the staffing of large areas in both
  Canada and the US" during World War II as male staff entered military service, and named
  women (Jane Hollingsworth, Rosalind Rinker, Barbara Boyd) are credited with developing core
  Bible-study practices and training programs. No source states a formal rule admitting women
  as members or students (chapters were mixed by default, not a subject of a stated admission
  rule). [quote_ids: ivu-q031, ivu-q045; context at ivcf-ifes-history 1940s/1950s sections]
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all
  text/ files) as a formal admission rule. One informal case is attested: Robert Finley, "an
  NCAA boxing champion from the University of Virginia," was hired as IVCF's first evangelist
  in 1945 — an appointment to staff, not an admission rule for ordinary chapter membership.
  [quote_id: ivu-q006]

- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files) as a

- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): The current Statement of Agreement functions as the

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/

  seminary's; no source states one.

  files) for the founding decades. IVCF later developed its own training/retreat centers —
  Campus by the Sea (1951), Hudson House (1952), Bear Trap Ranch (1953), Cedar Campus (1954) —
  but these postdate the 1941 founding and are training camps, not term-time residences.
  [context at ivcf-ifes-history 1950s section]
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files) as a formal

  groups, meeting each morning for Bible study and every evening for prayer" (Urbana 76, per
  wiki-urbana), but this is a specific later conference's practice, not a stated 1941 rule for
  ordinary chapter life.
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files). No
  source states a disciplinary code.
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/
  files) for the founding decades specifically. No source gives a membership fee or funding
  principle for IVCF/USA in 1941.
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): By 1946, "InterVarsity had 18 staff and
  chapters on 277 campuses across the country"; by 1950, "there were 35 staff serving students
  in 499 InterVarsity chapters." IVCF's opening chapters numbered forty-six U.S. campuses (per
  Encyclopedia.com), growing to more than five hundred chapters by the early 1950s. The first
  Toronto convention (1946) drew "151 colleges, universities, and seminaries... represented,
  along with 100 missionaries"; the second, 1948 convention drew 1,300 students from 154
  campuses. [quote_ids: ivu-q007, ivu-q013, ivu-q021, ivu-q024, ivu-q028, ivu-q033]

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): Three staff on loan from Canada at
  the 1941 founding, led by General Secretary Stacey Woods; 18 staff by 1946; 35 staff by 1950;
  more than 200 field staff by the early 1970s. Robert Finley was hired as "InterVarsity's
  first evangelist" in 1945. [quote_ids: ivu-q001, ivu-q006, ivu-q007, ivu-q013, ivu-q027,
  ivu-q028]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/
  files); no founding-era charter was located to state teachers'/staff-workers' formal duties.
- **T3 Forms of teacher-student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence,
  preaching class)** (PO): The attested forms are the campus Bible study (small-group,
  student-led, using the inductive/Manuscript method later formalized by staff), large-group
  meetings addressed by visiting speakers (governed, in the movement's current handbook, by a
  "Statement of Agreement" a speaker signs), and the triennial mass convention addressed by
  named speakers from Bakht Singh (1946) onward. [quote_ids: ivu-q019, ivu-q020, ivu-q030,
  ivu-q038]
- **T4 Named examples of teacher-student interaction with source** (PS): Dave Howard's
  retained 1946 Urbana "commitment card" and his account that "the 1946 Toronto conference
  deeply impacted the course of his life" is the clearest named first-generation example;
  Barbara Boyd's account of Urbana's preaching shaping her own subsequent Bible-study program
  is a second. [quote_ids: ivu-q043, ivu-q044, ivu-q045]
- **T5 Teacher:student ratio if computable (show the two numbers and years)** (PS): 3 staff to
  an unstated number of students at the November 1941 founding; 18 staff to chapters on 277
  campuses by 1946; 35 staff to 499 chapters by 1950 (ratios of students per chapter, not per
  staff member, are not computable from the sources fetched — no total-student count is given
  for these years). [quote_ids: ivu-q001, ivu-q007, ivu-q013]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The clearest founding-era
  statement of purpose comes from the 1947 IFES founding meeting at Harvard (attended by IVCF
  leaders as founding members): "to bring together the existing free movements which possess
  the same doctrinal basis and evangelical outlook and to give the utmost possible assistance
  to evangelical students who desire to take the gospel of Christ to the universities of the
  world." A. Donald MacLeod, Woods's biographer, is quoted (at second hand, via the 2007 press
  release) as saying Woods "initiated a whole new approach toward the university campus" and
  "was a pivotal figure in the revival of evangelicalism after the Second World War." At the
  first missions convention, Indian evangelist Bakht Singh is recorded as telling students in
  1946: "Young people, don't follow Christ lightly. He gave his life for the world; he wants
  you to give yours." [quote_ids: ivu-q010, ivu-q020, ivu-q037]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): A Gale/Encyclopedia.com reference entry characterizes IVCF as
  distinctive among comparable groups (Campus Crusade, the Navigators) "in that its enduring
  British heritage has emphasized the life of the mind over pragmatic revivalism," expressed
  institutionally through InterVarsity Press's publishing program. [quote_id: ivu-q035]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The clearest attested
  rejection is racial: in 1945, after a staff member invited Black students to a Bible study at
  a Trustee's home, "the Board passed a resolution forbidding racial segregation at InterVarsity
  events and calling for unity in the body of Christ" — a stance the organization's own later
  retrospective notes ran against the American norm of the time ("segregation was still the
  norm in the U.S. even until the 1970s"). [quote_ids: ivu-q012, ivu-q018]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): The 1948 Urbana convention's slogan,
  "From Every Campus to Every Country," is the earliest dated motto-like phrase found in the
  sources fetched this session. [quote_id: ivu-q024]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): IVCF/USA's current
  Purpose statement (part of the 2000 Statement of Agreement) restates the mission as
  establishing "witnessing communities of students and faculty who follow Jesus as Savior and
  Lord: growing in love for God, God's Word, God's people of every ethnicity and culture and
  God's purposes in the world" — a restatement the organization's own 2025 anniversary blog
  post explicitly frames as continuous with 1941. [quote_id: ivu-q038]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)**
  (PO): IVCF/USA did not itself commission or ordain missionaries; its sending mechanism, as
  attested across the sources fetched this session, was formational and connective rather than
  a licensing body: the triennial Urbana convention "call[ed] every student generation to
  consider global missions" and connected students to established mission boards and agencies,
  while, from 1981, the InterVarsity Link program built "a more organized relationship between
  InterVarsity and other IFES national movements." [quote_ids: ivu-q008, ivu-q019]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): NOT FOUND as a founding-era destinations list.
  By the 2010s IVCF's own retrospective names alumni serving in Uganda, and IFES itself, which
  IVCF helped found in 1947, today unites "more than 160 countries." [context at
  ivcf-urbana-decades and ivcf-ifes-history]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): "Since 1946, InterVarsity has hosted 22
  student mission conferences, involving over a quarter of a million students... 250,000+
  Urbana alumni," and, per a statement attributed to Billy Graham (at second hand, via a 2012
  IVCF president's retrospective article), "half of American missionaries can trace their
  vocational call — at least in part — to Urbana." No source gives a founding-era (1940s-1950s)
  count of students actually sent to the field as a direct result of Urbana. [quote_ids:
  ivu-q041, ivu-q042]
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND
  (searched: all text/ files). IVCF/USA is not itself a sending agency with a stated support
  principle; students were connected to other mission boards, whose own support principles
  are outside the scope of the sources fetched this session.
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): Jim Elliot (1927-1956),
  connected to the movement through the Foreign Missions Fellowship (a branch of Inter-Varsity
  Christian Fellowship, per his own 1948 journal) and, per a secondary source, an Urbana 1948
  attendee, was killed in Ecuador in 1956 — see discrepancies.md #4 for the unresolved question
  of whether the primary source (his own journal) actually corroborates the convention
  attendance claimed by the secondary source. Nate Henn, a 2000s Urbana alumnus, was killed by
  a bomb in Uganda in 2010 while volunteering with former child soldiers; his parents are quoted
  ("Nate's legacy is the pursuit of peace and a future for the children of Uganda"). [quote_ids:
  ivu-q023, ivu-q040; context at ivcf-urbana-decades 2000s section]
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  all text/ files). No document comparable to a formal sending instruction was located.

## R — Fruit
- **R1-R4 Up to six named alumni or missionaries: name, dates, field, one-sentence outcome,
  source** (PS):
  1. **Dave Howard** attended the first (1946 Toronto) missions convention as a student,
     retained his commitment card, served as a missionary to Costa Rica and Colombia, directed
     two later Urbanas, and became president of both Latin America Mission and the World
     Evangelical Fellowship. [quote_ids: ivu-q043, ivu-q044]
  2. **Barbara Boyd** attended fifteen Urbanas, first as a student and later as IVCF staff, and
     developed the Bible and Life Discipleship Training Program (1964), used, by the 2012
     source's account, in every IVCF/USA region and most Canadian provinces and by twenty-six
     sister IFES movements. [quote_id: ivu-q045]
  3. **Tim Keller** became a Christian at Bucknell University through IVCF's ministry, later
     served as IVCF staff, and founded Redeemer Presbyterian Church, New York City; Urbana
     "contributed to his commitments to evangelism, social justice, and God's global purposes."
     [quote_id: ivu-q046]
  4. **Jim Elliot** (1927-1956) is connected to the movement through a summer 1948 gospel-team
     trip under the Foreign Missions Fellowship "a branch of Inter-Varsity Christian
     Fellowship," per his own journal, and, per a secondary source (Wikipedia, citing Sheppard
     2010), attended the December 1948 Urbana convention itself; he was killed in Ecuador on 8
     January 1956. [quote_ids: ivu-q023, ivu-q040]
  5. **Nate Henn**, a 2000s Urbana alumnus, volunteered among former child soldiers in Uganda
     and was killed in a 2010 bombing at age twenty-five. [context at ivcf-urbana-decades
     2000s section]
