# Discrepancies — herrnhut-moravians

1. **Naming and dating of the 1727 founding documents.** The roster (`02_institution_roster.md`)
   and the search-string file (`05_repositories_and_search_strings.md`) both refer to a single
   document, the "Brotherly Agreement" (German *Brüderlicher Verein und Willkür*), dated
   12 May 1727. Paul Peucker's 2020 critical edition ("The 1727 Statutes of Herrnhut," abstract
   only, see `sources.csv` row `peucker-2020-statutes`) is described in WebSearch snippets as an
   edition of *two* separate 1727 documents: the "Manorial Commandments" and the "Brotherly
   Agreement," both said (per the snippet) to have been signed 12 May 1727. Hutton's 1909
   narrative (`hutton-church-1909`), by contrast, describes two documents with different names
   and different dates: the "Manorial Injunctions and Prohibitions," read aloud and agreed to
   on 12 May 1727 [quote_id herrnhut-moravians-q005], and a second, separate document, the
   "Brotherly Union and Compact," which Zinzendorf drew up afterward and signed first himself
   on 4 July 1727 [quote_id herrnhut-moravians-q008]. It is not established in the sources read
   this session whether Hutton's "Brotherly Union and Compact" (4 July) is the same document as
   the "Brotherly Agreement" that Peucker's abstract and the roster date to 12 May, or a
   different, later document. The full text of Peucker's translation could not be obtained
   (paywalled; see `sources.csv`), so this cannot be resolved from a source actually read this
   session. Do not state a single title or a single date for "the Brotherly Agreement" in the
   chapter without qualifying it against this discrepancy.

2. **Full primary text of the Brotherly Agreement / Statutes not obtained.** Despite the
   Scout searches specified in `05_repositories_and_search_strings.md`, no free full-text
   English translation of the 1727 statutes/agreement documents themselves was located this
   session. Peucker 2020 (the scholarly critical edition and translation) is paywalled. The
   Unitätsarchiv Herrnhut online catalogue was not interactively searchable via curl (no
   search-string interface reachable without WebFetch, which is blocked in this environment).
   The chapter's "Charter" section is therefore built from Spangenberg's 1788 *Account of the
   Manner...*, which is the only substantial continuous primary English text obtained this
   session among the roster's named "Key primary documents" for this slug -- not from the
   Brotherly Agreement/Statutes themselves. This is a real gap, not a silent substitution: it is
   flagged here, in `sources.csv`, and in the chapter's Charter-section headnote.

3. **"Ordinary lay members" framing not found verbatim.** The roster's "why included" cell
   states Herrnhut was "the first Protestant church to send ordinary members as missionaries."
   No source read this session uses this exact phrase. What the sources do establish: Leonard
   Dober was, per Hutton, "only a joiner's apprentice" and David Nitschmann "a carpenter"
   [see herrnhut-moravians-q022, herrnhut-moravians-q009 area; occupations reported in
   hutton-missions-1922 narrative, not yet given a dedicated quote_id this session] -- i.e., not
   ordained clergy. The chapter should describe the trades of Dober and Nitschmann as sourced,
   rather than asserting the roster's summary phrase as if it were a quotation.

4. **Casualty figures.** Two different casualty statistics for the St Thomas mission appear in
   `hutton-missions-1922`: "one hundred and sixty missionaries died" in St Thomas "during the
   first fifty years" [herrnhut-moravians-q026], and, separately, a St Croix death toll ("ten,
   including Tobias Leupold, died; then... seven more died of fever," out of an original party
   of eighteen and a further eleven) [herrnhut-moravians-q028]. These are different islands
   (St Thomas vs. St Croix) within the same Danish West Indies mission and are not in conflict,
   but a careless reading could conflate them. Both figures, and which island each belongs to,
   are recorded separately in the dossier field S5.

5. **Zinzendorf's estate/settlement date vs. the roster's "1722."** The roster's known-facts
   sheet gives "Zinzendorf's estate settled 1722" for Herrnhut's founding. `hutton-church-1909`
   dates the first refugees' arrival at the site to 8 June 1722 and the felling of the first tree
   to 17 June 1722 [herrnhut-moravians-q001], consistent with the roster. No conflict; recorded
   for completeness per Rule 9's instruction to record agreements as well as disagreements where
   relevant to a date used in the chapter.
