# Dossier — Herrnhut and the Moravian missions (`herrnhut-moravians`)

**Coverage note.** Three documents were fetched and read in full this session: Spangenberg's
*Account of the Manner...* (1788, PRIMARY), and two secondary histories by J. E. Hutton (1909,
1922) whose narrative quotes and reports primary material (Zinzendorf's own words, the 1727
statutes, participants' recorded speech). The full text of the 1727 Brotherly Agreement/Statutes
themselves was not obtained (see `discrepancies.md` items 1-2; the scholarly translation,
Peucker 2020, is paywalled). Fields on curriculum (C1-C9) are mostly NOT FOUND: Herrnhut was a
settled community and sending congregation, not a school with an enrolled course of study, and
the sources read this session describe common life and the sending of missionaries, not a
classroom curriculum. This gap is honest, not an oversight — flagged for a human/Coordinator
decision on whether the "Curriculum" chapter section should instead be renamed or answered as
"the sources are silent" per Editor procedure step 4.

Every field: one to three summary sentences (no quotation marks), then the supporting `quote_id`s
in square brackets, then the confidence label. Field types: **PO** = PRIMARY-ONLY; **PS** =
PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The settlement was named
  Herrnhut ("the Lord's Watch"), a name recorded as having arisen from steward Heitz's prayer
  that the town might "abide under the Lord's Watch" and that its inhabitants might "also
  continue on the Lord's Watch." [herrnhut-moravians-q002, herrnhut-moravians-q003] [SECONDARY:
  hutton-church-1909, quoted material treated PRIMARY-adjacent]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): Refugees from Moravia
  first settled on Zinzendorf's estate on 8 June 1722 and felled the first tree for a house on
  17 June 1722. [herrnhut-moravians-q001] Zinzendorf read the "Manorial Injunctions and
  Prohibitions" to the settlers on 12 May 1727 and they agreed to obey them.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q005, herrnhut-moravians-q036] He then drew up a further document, the
  "Brotherly Union and Compact," which he signed first himself on 4 July 1727.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q006 context, herrnhut-moravians-q008] The whole community joined in Holy
  Communion at Berthelsdorf parish church on Monday, 13 August 1727, afterward kept as "the true
  spiritual birthday of the Renewed Church of the Brethren." [herrnhut-moravians-q009 context,
  herrnhut-moravians-q010] The first two missionaries, Leonard Dober and David Nitschmann, left
  Herrnhut for St Thomas at three o'clock in the morning of 21 August 1732.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q023] See `discrepancies.md` item 1 on the naming/dating of the 1727
  documents. [SECONDARY: hutton-church-1909, quoted material PRIMARY-adjacent]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Nikolaus Ludwig, Count von Zinzendorf,
  was lord of the manor on whose estate the refugees settled; his steward Heitz oversaw the first
  building; Christian David led the emigrants from Moravia and was later appointed Chief Elder.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q001, herrnhut-moravians-q004] The two men sent to St Thomas in 1732 were
  Leonard Dober, "a Lutheran from Wurtemberg" and by trade a potter, and David Nitschmann, "a
  Moravian from Zauchtenthal" and by trade a carpenter. [herrnhut-moravians-q038,
  herrnhut-moravians-q037] [SECONDARY: hutton-church-1909, hutton-missions-1922; quoted/reported
  material PRIMARY-adjacent]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Herrnhut, on Zinzendorf's estate near Berthelsdorf, Saxony (Upper
  Lusatia); the site was a "dismal, swampy stretch of ground about a mile from the village,"
  on common pasture land called the Hutberg. [herrnhut-moravians-q001, herrnhut-moravians-q002,
  herrnhut-moravians-q003] [SECONDARY: hutton-church-1909]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): The document the
  book's roster and search-string files call the "Brotherly Agreement" could not be obtained in
  full primary text this session (see discrepancies.md items 1-2). What the fetched sources
  establish, in Hutton's paraphrase with quoted fragments, is that Zinzendorf read out the
  "Manorial Injunctions and Prohibitions" on 12 May 1727 [herrnhut-moravians-q005,
  herrnhut-moravians-q036] and separately drew up and first signed, on 4 July 1727, a document
  titled (in Hutton's English rendering) the "Brotherly Union and Compact," submitting a further
  set of "Statutes" as its basis. [herrnhut-moravians-q006, herrnhut-moravians-q007,
  herrnhut-moravians-q008] For the chapter's abridged-charter section, the Writer/Abridger has
  used Spangenberg's *An Account of the Manner in which the Protestant Church of the Unitas
  Fratrum... Preach the Gospel* (English translation, London, 1788; document_id
  `spangenberg-1788`) as the principal continuous primary English text obtained this session; see
  the headnote in `charter_abridged.md` for the reasoning and the caveat that this is not the
  community's internal Statutes. [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): "The whole place," said Zinzendorf himself,
  "represented a visible tabernacle of God among men." [herrnhut-moravians-q009] Spangenberg
  records that the "rules and measures" he describes "served the Brethren as a direction from the
  beginning," and that the congregation "undertook the service of the gospel among the heathen
  without having any very extensive views or intentions." [herrnhut-moravians-q029,
  herrnhut-moravians-q030] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788; quoted Zinzendorf material in
  hutton-church-1909 treated PRIMARY-adjacent]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): At home, the settlers'
  factional quarrelling and "sectarian" disputes on the Hutberg were the crisis Zinzendorf's 1727
  documents answered, by his own account, to "organize all who wished into a systematic Church
  within the Church." [herrnhut-moravians-q004, herrnhut-moravians-q036] For the mission, the
  occasion was, per Spangenberg, a baptized West Indian slave in Copenhagen (named Antony Ulrich
  in Hutton) who "ardently wished that his sister, a slave in St Thomas, might also be instructed
  in the way of life," which "moved the hearts of two brethren... that they would undertake a
  voyage to St. Thomas." [herrnhut-moravians-q031; corroborated in hutton-church-1909,
  herrnhut-moravians-q016, herrnhut-moravians-q017] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788; PRIMARY-adjacent
  quoted material in hutton-church-1909]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): Settlers signed a promise "to
  end their sectarian quarrels, to obey the 'Statutes,' and to live in fellowship with Christians
  of all beliefs and denominations." [herrnhut-moravians-q007] The sources read this session do
  not give the text of a separate doctrinal confession or creed distinct from these Statutes; NOT
  FOUND (searched: hutton-church-1909, hutton-missions-1922, spangenberg-1788).
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO):
  Spangenberg states that in writing his account he first considered, "in the presence of the
  Lord, the method of the disciples of Christ in preaching the gospel among the Gentiles" —
  i.e., the New Testament apostolic mission is the explicit model claimed. [herrnhut-moravians-q029
  (preface material, see text/spangenberg-1788.txt near "thofe rules and meafures")] Separately
  (not yet given its own quote_id this session), Hutton's narrative states that Zinzendorf later
  discovered, in July 1727, a copy of Comenius's Latin version of the old Bohemian Brethren's
  "Ratio Disciplinae" ("Account of Discipline") at Zittau, and found its rules "almost the same as
  the rules which he had just drawn up for the refugees at Herrnhut" — an antecedent recognized
  only after the fact, according to Hutton, not one consciously imitated at the time. [SECONDARY:
  hutton-church-1909, narrative paraphrase, not independently quote-recorded this session —
  UNVERIFIED as a direct quotation; the underlying claim is NOT FOUND as a primary quotation and
  should not be stated as a direct quote in the chapter.]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): The community held daily singing
  and prayer meetings in summer 1727, and after 27 August 1727 organized a round-the-clock
  "Hourly Intercession" in which each Brother or Sister took an appointed turn, said to have
  continued unbroken for over a hundred years (narrative claim, not independently quote-recorded
  as a direct quotation this session — UNVERIFIED as to exact wording, though the practice itself
  is corroborated by the twelve-Elders and community-order material at
  [herrnhut-moravians-q011]).
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): NOT FOUND with a specific quotation on
  household arrangements this session (Single Brethren and Single Sisters' choirs are known from
  general Moravian history but were not located as a directly quoted passage in the texts read).
  UNVERIFIED.
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): The community joined in Holy Communion at Berthelsdorf
  parish church on 13 August 1727. [herrnhut-moravians-q009 context, herrnhut-moravians-q010]
  Daily prayer/singing meetings and, from late August 1727, hourly intercession are described (see
  L1). [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in hutton-church-1909]
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): All male adult settlers had a voice in electing
  twelve Elders, who "had control over every department of life" and "enforced the Injunctions
  and Prohibitions with an iron hand." [herrnhut-moravians-q011] Settling, travelling, changing
  trade, taking in a lodger, and marrying all required the Elders' prior permission.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q012, herrnhut-moravians-q013] The rule of business was stated directly:
  "No brother... shall compete with his brother in trade." [herrnhut-moravians-q015]
  [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in hutton-church-1909]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): Marriage required the Elders' prior approval, and
  a man was "not allowed to propose to the choice of his heart" before it was obtained.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q013] No further quotation on dress or recreation was located this session.
  UNVERIFIED beyond this.
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): Settlers supported themselves by
  trade under the Elders' regulation of competition; no fee or tuition structure applies (this was
  a working settlement, not a fee-charging school). [herrnhut-moravians-q015]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): Not applicable in the sense of a school
  enrollment. Casualty/mission numbers are recorded under S3/S5 instead.
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): See S5 (mission casualties);
  within Herrnhut itself, no specific mortality record was located and quote-recorded this
  session. NOT FOUND.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "The whole place," said
  Zinzendorf, "represented a visible tabernacle of God among men." [herrnhut-moravians-q009] "We
  do not work to live," said the Count; "we live to work." [herrnhut-moravians-q014] Spangenberg
  records the Brethren's resolve "never [to go] to any heathen without a call, in which the hand
  [of] God was evident to them; and on the other hand, never to refuse any call among the heathen,
  in which they could perceive the finger of the Lord, though they should see beforehand that such
  a call might endanger their lives." [herrnhut-moravians-q032] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788;
  PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in hutton-church-1909]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): Direct, personal evangelism without national ambitions: "In our
  labour among the heathen, we will particularly endeavour, that they become converted to Christ
  Jesus with all their heart," and "we are firmly persuaded, that it is not our call to aim any
  where at national conversions, or to introduce the Christian religion among whole nations."
  [herrnhut-moravians-q033, herrnhut-moravians-q034] Non-competition in trade was likewise stated
  as a rule of the settlement's common life. [herrnhut-moravians-q015] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): National, mass conversion
  as a goal was explicitly disclaimed. [herrnhut-moravians-q034] Going to the heathen "without a
  call" was likewise rejected as a principle, alongside a refusal to decline a genuine call out of
  fear of danger to life. [herrnhut-moravians-q032] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): The Brethren treated a daily "Watch-Word"
  (a scripture text) as a form of guidance; the specific watchword Dober consulted before
  volunteering read, "It is not a vain thing for you... because it is your life; and through this
  thing ye shall prolong your days." [herrnhut-moravians-q018] [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material
  in hutton-church-1909]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): Spangenberg's 1788 Account
  itself, written at the request of Dr. Walch of Göttingen and dated by its preface to Barby,
  12 December 1780, is the fetched sources' clearest later (Spangenberg succeeded Zinzendorf as
  head of the Unity) restatement of the mission's founding rules and measures.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q029] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO):
  The Herrnhut congregation itself decided, by "the Lot," whether to permit volunteers to go; a
  full year elapsed between the volunteers' letter and the congregation's resolution to allow
  Dober and a companion to go to St Thomas and Greenland. [herrnhut-moravians-q031] For Dober
  specifically, the Lot gave the decisive word: "Let the lad go, for the Lord is with him."
  [herrnhut-moravians-q020] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788; PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in
  hutton-church-1909]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): St Thomas (Danish West Indies) was the first
  field, occasioned by the account of the slave Antony Ulrich. [herrnhut-moravians-q016,
  herrnhut-moravians-q017] Zinzendorf is quoted naming further intended fields in the same breath:
  "Sir, among these young men there are missionaries to St. Thomas, Greenland, Lapland, and many
  other countries." [herrnhut-moravians-q019] St Croix, St John, Jamaica, Antigua, Barbados, and
  St Kitts followed within the mission's first decades. [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in
  hutton-church-1909]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): "They had 13,000 baptized converts before a
  missionary from any other Church arrived on the scene" — a converts figure, not a count of
  missionaries sent, but the only quantitative figure of this kind located this session.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q025] [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in hutton-church-1909]
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): The two 1732
  missionaries received "no clear instructions from the Count, except 'to do all in the Spirit of
  Jesus Christ,'" carried about thirty shillings between them, and expected to support themselves
  by trade: Nitschmann told the King of Denmark's chamberlain, "We shall work... as slaves among
  the slaves," and, pressed further, "I am a carpenter, and will ply my trade."
  [herrnhut-moravians-q021, herrnhut-moravians-q022] For fifty years the Brethren "laboured in the
  West Indies without any aid from any other religious denomination."
  [herrnhut-moravians-q024] [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in hutton-church-1909]
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): "In St. Thomas alone,
  during the first fifty years, one hundred and sixty missionaries died."
  [herrnhut-moravians-q026] On St Croix, of an initial party of eighteen Brethren sent in 1734,
  ten died (including Tobias Leupold); of eleven more who arrived in 1735, seven more died of
  fever — leaving three of twenty-nine. [herrnhut-moravians-q027] Leonard Dober himself "toiled
  alone in St. Thomas" for fifteen months and "nearly died of starvation."
  [herrnhut-moravians-q028] [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in hutton-missions-1922]
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): No separate written
  instructions document (comparable to the ABCFM's 1812 *Instructions* to Judson's party) was
  located; the sources this session record only the Count's verbal charge, "to do all in the
  Spirit of Jesus Christ." [herrnhut-moravians-q021] Ordination, where given, was conferred in
  writing: Bishop David Nitschmann "sent a written ordination to brother Frederic Martin in St.
  Thomas." [herrnhut-moravians-q035] [PRIMARY: spangenberg-1788; PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material
  in hutton-church-1909]

## R — Fruit
- **R1 Leonard Dober** (PS): A potter by trade from Württemberg, drew the Lot that sent him
  to St Thomas in 1732; toiled alone there for fifteen months and nearly died of starvation before
  a companion arrived. [herrnhut-moravians-q037, herrnhut-moravians-q028] [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted
  material in hutton-church-1909 and hutton-missions-1922]
- **R2 David Nitschmann** (PS): A carpenter from Zauchtenthal, Moravia, sailed with Dober to St
  Thomas in 1732 and told the King of Denmark's chamberlain, "We shall work... as slaves among the
  slaves." [herrnhut-moravians-q038, herrnhut-moravians-q022] [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in
  hutton-church-1909]
- **R3 Frederick (Friedrich) Martin** (PS): Received a written ordination in St Thomas from Bishop
  David Nitschmann so that he could minister to the converts there; per Hutton, his death in 1750
  closed the "pioneer period" of the West Indies mission (narrative claim, not independently
  quote-recorded this session — UNVERIFIED as to exact wording of the "pioneer period" phrase,
  though the ordination fact is PRIMARY per spangenberg-1788, herrnhut-moravians-q035).
- **R4 Nikolaus Ludwig, Count von Zinzendorf** (PS): Lord of the manor at Herrnhut; issued the 1727
  Manorial Injunctions and the Brotherly Union and Compact; personally interviewed Dober before
  the Lot was cast, and drove the two 1732 missionaries part of the way to Copenhagen himself.
  [herrnhut-moravians-q005, herrnhut-moravians-q006, herrnhut-moravians-q018,
  herrnhut-moravians-q023] [PRIMARY-adjacent quoted material in hutton-church-1909]
