# Abridged charter — Herrnhut and the Moravian missions (`herrnhut-moravians`)

**Source document:** *An Account of the Manner in which the Protestant Church of the Unitas
Fratrum, or United Brethren, Preach the Gospel, and Carry on their Missions among the Heathen*,
1788 English translation (German original dated by the author's preface to Barby, 12 December
1780), London: printed for the Brethren's Society for the Furtherance of the Gospel among the
Heathen, document_id `spangenberg-1788`.

**Coverage note (read before using this file).** This is **not** the 1727 Brotherly Agreement /
Statutes of Herrnhut named in the book's roster and search-string files. That document's full
text could not be obtained this session: the one scholarly English translation located,
Paul Peucker's "The 1727 Statutes of Herrnhut" (2020), is paywalled on Project MUSE and could
not be fetched (WebFetch is blocked in this working environment; see `sources.csv` and
`discrepancies.md` items 1-2). What follows is instead the fullest continuous PRIMARY English
text actually obtained this session among the roster's named "Key primary documents" for this
institution: August Gottlieb Spangenberg's 1788 *Account*, an eyewitness description, by
Zinzendorf's own successor, of how and why the Herrnhut congregation began and organized its
mission — including the mission's founding occasion, its ordination of missionaries, its
governing "invariable principles," and the sending mechanism by which missionaries were called,
instructed, and dispatched. It functions in this chapter as the closest primary equivalent to a
"charter" of the *sending*, as distinct from a charter of Herrnhut's internal common life (which
the 1727 documents would supply, if obtained). A human/Coordinator follow-up ticket should
attempt to obtain Peucker 2020 through institutional/library access.

**Translation:** none needed (contemporary English translation, 1788; translator not named on
the title page).

**OCR quality warning:** The 1788 typeface used the long s; Internet Archive's OCR renders it
inconsistently as "f" or "s" (e.g. "fervice" for "service," "gofpel" for "gospel," "brethren" is
usually correct). Per Rule 03/book-plan Fetcher procedure and 01_book_design.md section 6.7,
this text is **not** modernized or corrected — it is reproduced exactly as OCR'd, including
these apparent errors and occasional stray characters from damaged type or scanning artifacts
(e.g. "rt." for "1." before the first numbered principle in §32). Interleaved footnote citations
(marked with *, +, t, || in the original) are part of the OCR stream and are kept in place rather
than silently removed, since removing them would require judgment calls about what is
"administrative" versus substantive in running 18th-century footnotes; a human copy-editor should
decide whether to typeset them as footnotes proper in a final print edition. Two stray OCR
renderings of the original printed page-number brackets ("[42]" and an unclosed "[J", both
appearing beside an apparently duplicated catchword — "our [42] our call," "might [J might be
valid") have been elided with this file's own "[...]" cut-mark, in the two places they occur
below, rather than reproduced literally — the only departure from a verbatim transcription in
this file — solely because a literal, un-paired "[" collides with this book-plan's own convention
(square brackets = editorial insertion) and, left as "[", silently swallowed one of this file's
own genuine "[...]" cut-markers when machine-checked. No word on either side of either elision
was changed, added, or removed; only the bracket-and-page-number fragment itself, in these two
spots (each under five characters), was cut.

word count:** approx. 1,720.
