# Dossier — Princeton Theological Seminary (`princeton-seminary`)

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The institution's own founding document names it once and for all: "The Theological Seminary of the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America." No later renaming is recorded in the sources read this session. [princeton-seminary-q001] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): Three dated steps are recorded across the sources. The Plan itself was adopted (with two articles held over) by the General Assembly meeting in May 1811 and printed that year. The Board of Directors' own minute, printed in the 1812 inauguration volume, records that on 12 August 1812 the Institution "has been founded, and is now open for the reception of pupils." The Seminary's own later institutional register additionally dates an Act of the General Assembly establishing the Seminary to May 1810, the Plan's ratification to May 1811, and the opening for instruction to 12 August 1812. See discrepancies.md #1 for how these three dates relate. [princeton-seminary-q022, princeton-seminary-q029, princeton-seminary-q030, princeton-seminary-q031] [PRIMARY: plan1811, inaug1812; PS: biocatalogue1932]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): The Plan itself is a corporate act of the Presbyterian General Assembly, not of named individual founders; the Assembly is repeatedly named as the institution's "patron, and the fountain of its powers." Archibald Alexander (1772-1851) was elected and inaugurated the Seminary's first professor, of Didactic and Polemic Theology, on 12 August 1812, and served in that chair until 1851. Samuel Miller (1769-1850) was appointed the second professor, of Ecclesiastical History and Church Government, in 1813. Philip Milledoler delivered the charge to Alexander and the students at the 1812 inauguration. Jacob J. Janeway signed the 1811 Plan document as Stated Clerk of the General Assembly. [princeton-seminary-q020, princeton-seminary-q028, princeton-seminary-q032, princeton-seminary-q033] [PRIMARY: plan1811, inaug1812; PS: biocatalogue1932]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Princeton, New Jersey. The 1811 Plan's closing minutes record that the Assembly first bounded the possible location only in general terms -- "that the rivers Raritan and Potowmac be the limits within which the Seminary shall be located" -- and appointed a committee to confer with the Trustees of the College of New Jersey about locating the new Seminary at the same place as that College; the Institution was in fact located, and inaugurated its first professor, at Princeton (see F9). [PRIMARY: plan1811, discrepancies.md #3]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): *The Plan of a Theological Seminary, Adopted by the General Assembly of the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America, in their Sessions of May last, A.D. 1811; together with the measures taken by them to carry the Plan into effect.* Philadelphia: Jane Aitken, 1811. English. document_id `plan1811`.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The Plan's Introduction states the design of the Institution at length, in a series of "It is to..." clauses, before a single Article of constitutional text is given. It is "to establish anew Institution, consecrated solely to the education of men for the Gospel ministry, and to be denominated, The Theological Seminary of the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America"; it is to unite religion and literature, "that piety of the heart which is the fruit only of the renewing and sanctifying grace of God, with solid learning"; it is to provide the Church with men able "to defend her faith against infidels, and her doctrines against heretics"; and, among its final clauses, "to found a nursery for missionaries to the heathen, and to such as are destitute of the stated preaching of the gospel." [princeton-seminary-q001, princeton-seminary-q002, princeton-seminary-q003, princeton-seminary-q004] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The Plan's Introduction states that ministerial supply had fallen behind the Church's growth: "So rapid, however, has been the extension of this Church, and so disproportionate, of late, has been the number of ministers educated, to the call which has been made for ministerial service, that some additional and vigorous efforts to increase the supply are loudly and affectingly demanded." Samuel Miller's 1812 sermon frames the same need christologically from 2 Timothy 2:2, on transmitting the ministry to "faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also," and Alexander's own Inaugural Discourse opens by naming "the same causes which have operated to render such an institution urgently necessary" without further specifying those causes in the passage read this session. [princeton-seminary-q037, princeton-seminary-q023, princeton-seminary-q027] [PRIMARY: plan1811, inaug1812]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): Every professor, on inauguration, must "solemnly, and ex animo adopt, receive, and subscribe the Confession of Faith and Catechisms of the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America, as the confession of my faith... and I do solemnly ex animo profess to receive the Form of Government of said Church, as agreeable to the inspired oracles," promising not to teach anything contrary to that Confession or opposed to Presbyterian church government (Article III, Sect. 3; full formula read in text/plan1811.txt but not separately quote-recorded beyond the summary here). Every director, before taking a seat on the board, subscribes a formula approving the Plan and promising to carry it into effect (Article II, Sect. 10). Directors of the associated Theological Academy and its tutors subscribe the same or an analogous formula. Students subscribe a distinct declaration of personal intention and obedience, not a doctrinal confession as such (see C8). [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): The Plan's own closing minutes record that the 1811 Assembly appointed a committee "to confer with the committee of the Trustees of New Jersey College," with power to frame a joint constitutional arrangement and, if agreeable to both parties, to locate the new Theological Seminary at the same place as the existing College of New Jersey; this committee was instructed to treat the adopted articles of the Plan as its non-negotiable guide in that conference. The sources read this session do not otherwise name an earlier seminary, academy, or document as a model the framers of the 1811 Plan said they were imitating (no reference to Geneva, Halle, Herrnhut, or a Log College precedent was found in the fetched text). Samuel Miller's 1812 sermon grounds the principle of a trained, ordained succession of ministers directly in 2 Timothy 2:2 (Paul to Timothy), read as the scriptural warrant for the undertaking rather than as an institutional antecedent. [princeton-seminary-q021, princeton-seminary-q023] [PRIMARY: plan1811, inaug1812] See discrepancies.md #3 on the College of New Jersey relationship.

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811, inaug1812). No numeric minimum or maximum age for admission was found in the fetched text of the Plan.
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): A candidate must have "passed through a regular course of academical study; or, wanting this, he shall submit himself to an examination in regard to the branches of literature taught in such a course" (Article VI, Sect. 1). [princeton-seminary-q015] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): Every applicant must "produce satisfactory testimonials that he possesses good natural talents, and is of a prudent and discreet deportment," and must be "in full communion with some regular Church" (Article VI, Sect. 1). The Plan's Article IV also requires (of a graduating student, not explicitly of an entering applicant) evidence of sustained personal piety through the whole course, and the Theological Academy attached to the Seminary requires two-thirds of its own pupils to have already made "a public and credible profession of religion, by joining in communion with the Church." [princeton-seminary-q015, princeton-seminary-q016] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): For a candidate who has not passed through a regular course of academical study, "he shall submit himself to an examination in regard to the branches of literature taught in such a course" (Article VI, Sect. 1); the Plan does not itemize those branches beyond that phrase in the passage read. [princeton-seminary-q015] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811, inaug1812). Admission throughout Article VI is framed only in terms of "every student" and masculine pronouns ("he," "his"); no provision, positive or negative, for women students was found in the sources read this session.
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): No prior preaching or ministerial service is required for admission in the passages read; instead, "the first six months of every student in the Seminary shall be considered as probationary," during which the professors may find a student "not qualified to proceed in his studies" and report him for dismissal by the board of directors (Article VI, Sect. 2). [princeton-seminary-q017] [PRIMARY: plan1811]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): "The period of continuance in the Theological Seminary shall, in no case, be less than three years, previously to an examination for a certificate of approbation," though a student may enter and take instruction for a shorter time without earning that certificate (Article IV, Sect. 2). [princeton-seminary-q010] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): Article III fixes three professorships once the Seminary is "completely organized": "one of Didactic and Polemic Divinity; one of Oriental and Biblical Literature; and one of Ecclesiastical History and Church Government." Article IV then specifies, not a sequence of courses, but the attainments every student must reach by the end: skill in the original biblical languages; ability to resolve scriptural difficulties; Jewish and Christian antiquities, ancient geography and oriental customs; the "deistical controversy" and defense of the Christian faith; proof of the Confession of Faith and Catechisms from Scripture; Natural, Didactic, Polemic, and Casuistic Theology; general history and chronology and church history; practical religious writers; composition and delivery, including at least two lectures and four popular sermons approved by the professors; the pastoral care; and the Scriptural form of Church government and its Protestant administration. [princeton-seminary-q006, princeton-seminary-q007, princeton-seminary-q008, princeton-seminary-q009] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): The Plan specifies attainments rather than a fixed class sequence, stating this is deliberate: "the particular course of study pursued in any Institution will, and perhaps ought to be modified in a considerable degree, by the views and habits of the teachers," so the Plan fixes required attainments (Article IV) rather than a rigid syllabus. The three professorships of Article III correspond to three broad divisions of instruction. Two vacations of six weeks each fall in spring (from the Monday before the third Thursday of May) and autumn (from the first Wednesday of October). [princeton-seminary-q006] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Every graduating student "must be well skilled in the original languages of the Holy Scriptures" (Article IV, Sect. 1); the attached Theological Academy separately teaches Hebrew as a standing part of its own course, under the Seminary's professor of Oriental Literature and Biblical Criticism. [princeton-seminary-q008] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811). The Plan specifies required attainments and subject areas (Article IV) but does not name specific set texts or authors in the passages read this session; Article VII instead directs the professors to keep a running "Prospectus of a Catalogue of a Theological Library" of titles they judge proper for the library, rather than fixing a syllabus of authors.
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): Every student must be examined at course's end on composition, and "must have composed at least two lectures and four popular sermons, that shall have been approved by the professors" (Article IV, Sect. 1). Article VI additionally requires written composition on prescribed subjects with increasing frequency by year -- once a month in the first year, once every three weeks in the second, once every two weeks in the third -- and each student must monthly commit a piece of his own composition to memory and "pronounce it in public, before the professors and students." [princeton-seminary-q009] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): After three years of regular and diligent study, students "shall be admitted to an examination on the subjects specified in this article. The examination shall be conducted by the professors, in the presence of the board of directors, or a committee of them" (Article IV, Sect. 3); those who pass receive a signed certificate and are remitted to their presbyteries, while those who fail remain longer in the Seminary. [princeton-seminary-q010] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): The Plan issues no academical degree; a student who passes the three-year examination receives "a certificate of the same, signed by the professors, with which they shall be remitted to their several presbyteries, to be disposed of as such presbyteries shall direct" (Article IV, Sect. 3) -- licensure and ordination remain the business of the presbyteries, outside the Seminary's own authority. Before taking his standing in the Seminary, every student must also subscribe a personal declaration: "Deeply impressed with a sense of the importance of improving in knowledge, prudence, and piety, in my preparation for the Gospel Ministry, I solemnly promise, in a reliance on divine grace, that I will faithfully and diligently attend on all the instructions of this Seminary" and observe its rules (Article VI, Sect. 9). [princeton-seminary-q010, princeton-seminary-q019] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND as an hour-by-hour table (searched: plan1811). The Plan fixes only the yearly composition schedule (monthly/three-weekly/two-weekly written work by year of study, C6) and the two six-week vacations (spring from the Monday before the third Thursday of May; autumn from the first Wednesday of October, Article VI, Sect. 10); it leaves daily "hours of study and of recreation" to be "fixed by the professors, with the concurrence of the directors" (Article VI, Sect. 3) rather than specifying them in the Plan itself, and likewise leaves the hours of daily morning and evening worship to the faculty (Article III, Sect. 9). No separate faculty-issued daily timetable document was fetched this session.

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): The Plan itself delegates the fixing of daily "hours of study and of recreation for the students" to the professors, with the directors' concurrence (Article VI, Sect. 3), and likewise delegates the hours of daily morning and evening "social worship" to the faculty (Article III, Sect. 9), rather than specifying either in the constitutional text. NOT FOUND as a specific clock schedule (searched: plan1811, inaug1812).
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): NOT FOUND as a specific residence arrangement (searched: plan1811, inaug1812). The Plan's Article VI regulates student conduct and study but the fetched text does not describe dormitory or lodging arrangements.
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): Article V, "Of Devotion, and Improvement in Practical Piety," expects "that every student in the Theological Seminary will spend a portion of time every morning and evening in devout meditation, and self-recollection and examination... and in humble fervent prayer and praise to God in secret." "The whole of every Lord's day is to be devoted to devotional exercises, either of a social or secret kind," with ordinary intellectual pursuits "forborne" that day; it is additionally "wished and recommended, that each student should ordinarily set apart one day in a month for special prayer and self-examination in secret," and attend on suitable occasions "to the duty of fasting." Daily communal morning and evening worship for the whole body of students is fixed in its hour and manner by the faculty (Article III, Sect. 9). [princeton-seminary-q011, princeton-seminary-q012, princeton-seminary-q013] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): A student "unsound in his religious sentiments," "immoral or disorderly in his conduct," or judged by the faculty "a dangerous, or unprofitable member of the Institution" on any account may be dismissed by the faculty (Article III, Sect. 10). Within Article V specifically, a student showing "levity or indifference in regard to practical religion" is first to be admonished privately and tenderly by the professor who observes it; if he "persist[s], after due admonition, in a system of conduct not exemplary in regard to religion, he shall be dismissed from the Seminary." The first six months of residence are probationary (Article VI, Sect. 2, see A6), and every student subscribes a declaration promising obedience to "all the lawful requisitions" of the professors and directors (Article VI, Sect. 9). [princeton-seminary-q011, princeton-seminary-q013, princeton-seminary-q017, princeton-seminary-q019] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): "Strict temperance in meat and drink is expected of every student, with cleanliness and neatness in his dress and habits; while all excessive expense in clothing is strictly prohibited" (Article VI, Sect. 8). Students must also treat teachers "with the greatest deference and respect, and all other persons with civility" (Article VI, Sect. 5), and "diligence and industry in study shall be considered as indispensable in every student," health permitting (Article VI, Sect. 7). [princeton-seminary-q018] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): The Plan states an aim, in its Introduction, "to bring to the service of the Church genius and talent, when united with piety, however poor or obscure may be their possessor, by furnishing, as far as possible, the means of education and support, without expense to the student" (Introduction; read in text/plan1811.txt but not separately quote-recorded from this exact sentence this session). Article VIII directs that, after supporting the professors and the Seminary's necessary charges, funds be applied "to defray or diminish the expenses of those students who may need pecuniary aid, as well as to lessen, generally, the expense of a residence at the Seminary" (Article VIII, Sect. 6, read in text/plan1811.txt; summarized here without a separate quote record). [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): The Seminary's own 1933 biographical catalogue begins its alumni list with the class that entered in 1812 and completed study in 1815; the first several entries under "1815" name individual students (for example John Covert, Leverett Israel Foote Huntington, Henry Rowland Weed, Halsey Augustus Wood), but the fetched catalogue text was not tallied into a precise headcount for the founding class this session. NOT independently counted (searched: biocatalogue1932, alumni section beginning "1815").
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811, inaug1812, biocatalogue1932 front matter). The biographical catalogue's individual alumni entries do record death dates for each alumnus in the format sampled (for example "d, 22" for John Covert), but no aggregate hardship or mortality statement specific to the student body as such was found or quoted this session.

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): Article III sets a floor of three professors once the Seminary is fully organized: of Didactic and Polemic Divinity; of Oriental and Biblical Literature; and of Ecclesiastical History and Church Government. In fact Archibald Alexander was inaugurated the first professor (Didactic and Polemic Theology) on 12 August 1812 and served until 1851; Samuel Miller was appointed the second professor (Ecclesiastical History and Church Government) in 1813 and served until 1849. [princeton-seminary-q006, princeton-seminary-q032, princeton-seminary-q033] [PRIMARY: plan1811, inaug1812; PS: biocatalogue1932]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): Beyond lecturing, examining, and disciplining, "the professors are particularly charged, by all the proper means in their power, to encourage, cherish, and promote devotion and personal piety among their pupils, by warning and guarding them, on the one hand, against formality and indifference, and on the other, against ostentation and enthusiasm; by inculcating practical religion in their lectures and recitations; by taking suitable occasions to converse with their pupils privately on this interesting subject" (Article V, Sect. 4). [princeton-seminary-q014] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **T3 Forms of teacher-student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): The Plan specifies daily lecture or recitation for each professor's own pupils (Article III, Sect. 7); private admonition and private conversation "on this interesting subject" of personal piety (Article V, Sects. 2 and 4, quoted above); public pronunciation of memorized student compositions before the assembled professors and students (Article VI, Sect. 4); and the board of directors' own duty to inaugurate professors and to direct the forms and services used on such occasions (Article II, Sect. 9). Philip Milledoler's 1812 Charge frames the professor's task as being called "to train up for her service, bands of intelligent, intrepid, and faithful champions of the cross." [princeton-seminary-q014, princeton-seminary-q020, princeton-seminary-q026] [PRIMARY: plan1811, inaug1812]
- **T4 Named examples of teacher-student interaction with source** (PS): NOT FOUND as a narrated specific instance (searched: plan1811, inaug1812, biocatalogue1932 front matter). The sources read this session establish the constitutional duty and the general form (T2, T3) but no single narrated episode of a particular professor and a particular student was located and quoted.
- **T5 Teacher:student ratio if computable (show the two numbers and years)** (PS): NOT independently computed this session. Two professors are attested in office by 1813 (Alexander from 1812, Miller from 1813); L7 records that the Seminary's alumni catalogue begins listing individual students under the class completing in 1815, but the exact size of that first class was not tallied from the fetched text this session.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The Plan's Introduction closes its list of purposes by summarizing the whole: it is "finally, to endeavour to raise up a succession of men, at once qualified for and thoroughly devoted to the work of the Gospel ministry"who, whatever their particular gifts, would all possess a portion of "the spirit of the primitive propagators of the Gospel; prepared to make every sacrifice, to endure every hardship, and to render every service which the promotion of pure and undefiled religion may require." At the 1812 inauguration, Philip Milledoler's charge to Alexander stated the Assembly's aim as securing "a learned, orthodox, pious, and evangelical Ministry," opening with the plainest form of the same claim: "We want a learned Ministry." [princeton-seminary-q005, princeton-seminary-q024, princeton-seminary-q025] [PRIMARY: plan1811, inaug1812]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): The Plan repeatedly pairs, rather than ranks, learning and piety: it is founded to unite "religion and literature; that piety of the heart which is the fruit only of the renewing and sanctifying grace of God, with solid learning," on the express ground that "religion without learning, or learning without religion, in the ministers of the Gospel, must ultimately prove injurious to the Church" (Introduction, second clause read in text/plan1811.txt; the "religion without learning" sentence itself was read but not separately quote-recorded from the exact adjoining line this session). Article V then gives that stated pairing its own dedicated constitutional article on devotion and practical piety, distinct from and additional to Article IV's article on study and attainments. [princeton-seminary-q002] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Professors are charged to guard their pupils "on the one hand, against formality and indifference, and on the other, against ostentation and enthusiasm" in matters of personal devotion (Article V, Sect. 4). Every inaugurated professor must promise not to "inculcate, teach, or insinuate any thing" contrary to the Confession of Faith and Catechisms, nor to "oppose any of the fundamental principles of Presbyterian church government" (Article III, Sect. 3). [princeton-seminary-q014] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811, inaug1812, biocatalogue1932 front matter). No single motto or watchword phrase attached to the Institution was found in the sources fetched this session.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND beyond the 1812 inauguration pieces themselves (searched: inaug1812; Calhoun and Moorhead not fetched this session, see sources.csv). Philip Milledoler's 1812 charge to the newly inaugurated Alexander and the students restates the Assembly's founding aim in almost the Plan's own terms, as a "learned, orthodox, pious, and evangelical Ministry" (see M1); no later (post-1812) leader's restatement was located and quoted this session.

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The Seminary itself does not license or ordain. A student who completes the three-year course and passes examination receives a certificate and "shall be remitted to their several presbyteries, to be disposed of as such presbyteries shall direct" (Article IV, Sect. 3); it is the presbytery, not the Seminary, that licenses and ordains for the ministry. The Introduction separately states the founders' aim "to found a nursery for missionaries to the heathen, and to such as are destitute of the stated preaching of the gospel," describing the Seminary's role toward missionary sending as formative ("appropriate training which may lay a foundation") rather than as itself an agency that commissions or dispatches missionaries. [princeton-seminary-q004, princeton-seminary-q010] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): The Plan's own missions clause speaks generally of "the heathen" and of "such as are destitute of the stated preaching of the gospel," without naming a specific field, region, or country. NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811, inaug1812) as a named destination or sending record specific to Princeton graduates in the sources fetched this session. [princeton-seminary-q004] [PRIMARY: plan1811]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811, inaug1812, biocatalogue1932 front matter). No aggregate count of missionaries sent, by period, was located and quoted this session; the biographical catalogue's individual alumni entries record each graduate's subsequent presbytery, pastorate, or (occasionally) mission field individually (for example the abbreviation "miss, SC & Ga" against John Covert's 1815 entry), which would allow such a count to be built up by a future, more exhaustive pass, but that tally was not performed this session.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a field-support principle specific to sent missionaries (searched: plan1811, inaug1812). Article VIII of the Plan addresses support of students while resident at the Seminary (defraying the expenses of those "who may need pecuniary aid," see L6) but the fetched text does not address the support of graduates once sent out.
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND as an aggregate figure (searched: plan1811, inaug1812, biocatalogue1932 front matter). Individual death dates are recorded against individual alumni in the biographical catalogue (see L8) but no summary casualty or martyrdom figure was located and quoted this session.
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: plan1811, inaug1812). No separate title of "Instructions" issued by the Seminary (comparable to the ABCFM's 1812 Instructions to Judson's party, treated in this book's `abcfm` chapter) was found in the sources fetched this session; sending, licensing, and any instructions to candidates for the ministry remained the business of the several presbyteries under Article IV, Sect. 3 (see S1).
