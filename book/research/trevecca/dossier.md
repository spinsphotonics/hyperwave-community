# Dossier — Trevecca College (`trevecca`)

Note on sourcing: one volume was fetched and read this session (searched for "Trevecca" and
"Fletcher"), Seymour's 1840 biography, which reprints letters of Selina, Countess of
Huntingdon, John Fletcher, Howel Harris, and others verbatim, in quotation marks, with
attribution. Letters so quoted are cited PO/PRIMARY; Seymour's own narrative sentences are
cited PS/SECONDARY. Coverage is thin relative to Yale: this volume does not, in the material
located this session, name Fletcher's specific title at the College, reprint a "Rules of
Trevecca" document, or give a founding-ceremony account with a specific opening date. Three
further sources named in the roster or reasonably expected to hold this material — Seymour's
own Volume II, the Cheshunt Foundation archive, and a dedicated collection of Fletcher's
letters to students — were not located/fetched this session; see sources.csv.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Referred to throughout the
  material read this session as "her College at Trevecca," "the College of Trevecca," "Lady
  Huntingdon's College at Trevecca," and, in the Countess's own words, "the school of the
  prophets at my beloved Trevecca." [trevecca-q001]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): NOT FOUND as a
  specific opening-day account in the material read this session. The roster gives 1768
  [HIGH]. What the material read this session shows is preparation before that date: "In April,
  (1767), Lady Huntingdon and Miss Orton went from Brighton to Bath, and thence through Wales
  into Yorkshire. Her Ladyship was much occupied in arrangements for her College at Trevecca,"
  and by "the summer of 1768 she was again at Brighton," implying the College's establishment
  fell between these dates. [context surrounding trevecca-q002/q003, not independently quoted
  as a distinct passage this session]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Selina, Countess of Huntingdon, is
  named throughout as the College's founder and patron ("her College," "Lady Huntingdon's
  College"). John Fletcher (vicar of Madeley) appears repeatedly in close association with her
  and with Trevecca, meeting her there and conducting her onward to Madeley in May 1767, and
  writing to Whitefield of his plan "to Trevecca, to meet her Ladyship there." [trevecca-q002]
  No passage located and quoted this session states Fletcher's formal title (e.g.
  "president") at the College; this is UNVERIFIED in the material fetched this session, though
  it is the roster's stated fact [HIGH]. [trevecca-q002]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO/PS): Trevecca, in Brecknockshire, Wales — already, before the College,
  the site of a religious settlement gathered around Howel Harris: "near one hundred and
  twenty, men, women, and children, from very distant parts of Wales, came and fixed their
  tents at Trevecca," in Mr. Venn's own quotation of Harris. [trevecca-q004] Lady Huntingdon
  had visited the same place "which exactly twenty years after became her chief residence and
  scene of action" — i.e., decades before the College itself. [trevecca-q003]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND. No
  charter, deed, or "Rules of Trevecca" document was located and fetched this session (see
  sources.csv). This chapter's "Charter" section instead reprints, in place of a founding
  document, Lady Huntingdon's own letter describing the College's purpose (see X3).
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): In a letter to Mr. Milner, the Countess
  wrote: "It affords me the most cordial satisfaction to see a goodly number of godly young men
  offering themselves to the service of our adorable Saviour, of whose talents and piety I
  judge most favourably. The school of the prophets at my beloved Trevecca affords great
  advantages to young men, as preparatory to the work, and so easy of access." [trevecca-q001]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PS): The same letter continues:
  "but the labourers are still few and the harvest plenteous," echoing Christ's own words (Luke
  10:2) about the shortage of laborers — the closest the material read this session comes to a
  stated crisis. [trevecca-q001]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  seymour1840-v1). No signed confession or subscription requirement for Trevecca students is
  described in the material read this session.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PS):
  The College was built on ground already gathered by Howel Harris's religious community at
  Trevecca, "near one hundred and twenty" persons who had come to live and work together around
  him after his health failed; Harris is called, in Venn's quotation, "the father of that
  settlement, and the founder" (of the settlement, not explicitly of the later College, in the
  passage read). [trevecca-q004] No passage locating a specific earlier academy (e.g. Bristol
  Baptist Academy or an English dissenting model) as Trevecca's own stated model was found this
  session.

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: seymour1840-v1).
- **A2 Prior education required** (PS): Not a fixed requirement, on the evidence read this
  session: one student, Mr. Tyler, is described as having been "instructed in the ordinary
  branches of education" as a boy, then apprenticed to a watchmaker, without further schooling,
  before being received at Trevecca, where "he would fain have acquired that knowledge from
  which the scantiness of his early instruction had excluded him, especially the original
  languages of the Holy Scriptures" — implying the College offered, rather than presupposed,
  such instruction. [trevecca-q005]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PS): Not stated as
  a formal rule in the material read this session, but illustrated by two named cases: after
  the 1768 expulsion of six Methodist-sympathizing students from St Edmund Hall, Oxford, at
  least two of the expelled men — "Mr. Mathews," charged with "having been instructed by Mr.
  Fletcher, a declared Methodist," and "Mr. Shipman" — were afterward "received into Lady
  Huntingdon's College, at Trevecca" and "admitted to the College of Trevecca" respectively,
  suggesting the College received students whose profession of faith had made them unwelcome
  elsewhere. [context near trevecca-q005/q006, this specific Oxford-expulsion passage located
  but not independently entered as a quote record this session — see discrepancies.md]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: seymour1840-v1).
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: seymour1840-v1). All
  named students in the material read this session are male.
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  seymour1840-v1); if anything, the material read this session shows the reverse — students

  preached before entry.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PS): NOT FOUND as a stated term of years. Mr. Tyler's case suggests
  it could be short and interrupted: "no sooner had he arrived in Wales than his hand was put to
  the plough, and his time was devoted to preaching in different parts of the country," with his
  "sermons... for the most part... studied on horseback." [trevecca-q006]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): NOT FOUND as a syllabus in the material read this
  session. The one subject named is "the original languages of the Holy Scriptures," which Mr.
  Tyler hoped, but was not able, to study in depth before being sent out to preach.
  [trevecca-q005]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: seymour1840-v1).
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PS): The "original languages of the Holy
  Scriptures" (i.e., Hebrew and Greek) are named as subjects the College offered, though Tyler's

- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: seymour1840-v1).
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PS): Students preached during

  at the request of a local layman, and a York chapel "was regularly supplied by the students of
  Trevecca until 1779" in rotation. [trevecca-q007; context near "The names of the first

- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: seymour1840-v1).
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): NOT FOUND as a College

## R — Fruit
- **R1 Mr. Tyler** (PS): received into Trevecca College after conversion from a dissolute early
  life in London; sent out to preach while still a student, "for the most part" studying his
  sermons "on horseback"; later, at a Hull patron's suggestion, left Trevecca and the
  Connexion, was sent to Magdalen College, Cambridge (1778), took a Bachelor's degree, was
  ordained deacon in the Church of England (1782), and served parishes in Lincolnshire until
  his death in 1808. [trevecca-q005, trevecca-q006]
- **R2 Mr. Mathews** and **R3 Mr. Shipman** (PS): two of six students expelled from St Edmund
  Hall, Oxford, in 1768 for Methodist sympathies and associations (Mathews specifically "charged
  with having been instructed by Mr. Fletcher, a declared Methodist"); both were afterward
  received at Trevecca College — Mathews "received into Lady Huntingdon's College, at
  Trevecca," Shipman "admitted to the College of Trevecca." [this Oxford-expulsion passage
  located but not independently entered as a quote record this session — see discrepancies.md;
