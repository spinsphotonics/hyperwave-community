# Discrepancies -- omf

## 1. Year/date of the reorganization meeting that changed CIM's structure to admit non-Western full members

- **Source A:** `aim25-cim-omf-finding-aid` (SOAS Library's own archival catalogue description of the CIM/OMF archive). Text: "At a meeting of the Mission' Overseas Council held in October 1964, the name became the Overseas Missionary Fellowship (OMF)... The structure of the Mission was altered so that non-western Christians could become full members and set up home councils in their own countries." [quote_ids omf-q002, omf-q004]
- **Source B:** `wikipedia-omf-international`, within its own "1950s" chronology subsection: "14 October 1954, The mission was reorganised at a meeting of the mission's overseas council. The council reaffirmed the need for the mission, but changed its structure so that non-Western Christians could become full members and set up home councils in their own countries." [quote_ids omf-q010, omf-q011]

**The disagreement:** Source A dates the Overseas Council meeting that (a) renamed the Mission to "Overseas Missionary Fellowship" and (b) restructured it to admit non-Western full members, to October 1964. Source B places what reads as the same meeting -- same body ("the mission's overseas council"), same specific reform (non-Western Christians becoming full members, national home councils) -- on "14 October 1954," ten years earlier, and does not itself claim in that sentence that the name became simply "OMF" at that point.

**Note that this is a discrepancy the Wikipedia article has with itself**, not only with AIM25: elsewhere in the same article, the infobox states "Origins: China Inland Mission (till 1964)," the prose overview states "The name was changed to the Overseas Missionary Fellowship in 1964" [omf-q007], and the article's own 1960s-chronology entry states "China Inland Mission renamed Overseas Missionary Fellowship in 1964" [omf-q008]. Only the single 1950s-chronology line gives 1954. This looks like a plausible transposition error in that one entry (1954 for 1964), but this dossier does not resolve it -- it is recorded here per Rule 9.

**Not resolved.** The chapter states only the well-corroborated fact -- the name became Overseas Missionary Fellowship in 1964 -- and does not repeat the single 1954-dated chronology line as an alternative date, since three other passages in the same source and a second, independent source (AIM25) agree on 1964, and the "14 October" day-of-month match across the two accounts suggests one is a copy of the other's date with a slipped decade rather than two genuinely independent events.

## 2. Exact wording of the interim, combined name adopted before the final 1964 shortening

- `aim25-cim-omf-finding-aid`: "New headquarters were established in Singapore and the name was changed to the China Inland Mission Overseas Missionary Fellowship" -- given without its own year, immediately before the sentence dating the October 1964 meeting. [omf-q001]
- `wikipedia-omf-international` (1950s chronology): "In November 1951, a new headquarters was set up in Singapore, and the organisations name was changed to The China Inland Mission Overseas Missionary Fellowship" -- dated November 1951. [omf-q012]

**The disagreement:** it is not clear from either source, read alone, whether the compound name "China Inland Mission Overseas Missionary Fellowship" was adopted in 1951 (as Wikipedia's chronology dates it, alongside the move to Singapore) and only shortened to plain "Overseas Missionary Fellowship" in 1964 (as both sources agree), or whether AIM25's narrative order (Singapore headquarters, then the compound name, then the October 1964 meeting, in one continuous paragraph with no other date given) is meant to place the compound-name adoption closer to 1964 as well. **Not resolved.** The chapter states the well-attested endpoints only -- Singapore headquarters after the 1951 Bournemouth decision and CIM's withdrawal from China, and the final name "Overseas Missionary Fellowship" from 1964 -- without asserting an exact date for the intermediate compound name.

## 3. Roster's own flag

The roster row for `omf` reads "Renamed 1964/1965 [VERIFY exact year]." This session's sources (AIM25, Wikipedia in three of its four mentions, Wikiquote, and the Wikipedia article on J. O. Sanders) converge on **1964**, with AIM25 giving the added precision of "October 1964." No source located this session gives 1965 as the rename year specifically (1965 appears only as the year Leslie Lyall's centenary history was published, and as the year several new Home Councils -- Japan, Malaysia, Singapore -- were formed, per omf-q005/omf-q001, which is a related but distinct fact). This dossier therefore treats 1964 as confirmed, not merely plausible, while recording that the print centenary history that would most likely narrate the decision in the Mission's own words (Lyall, 1965) could not be opened this session (see `text/ia-passion-for-the-impossible-1965.REQUEST.md`).
