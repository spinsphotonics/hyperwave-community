# Dossier -- Overseas Missionary Fellowship (`omf`)

This is a coda dossier, per the task instruction and the roster's own tier note ("A, as coda to CIM chapter"). Its scope is narrower than a full institution dossier: it covers only what changed in name, structure, and *Principles and Practice* when the China Inland Mission became the Overseas Missionary Fellowship, and where the Mission operated by the time of the nearest material found. Fields about the resident school, curriculum, and common life of a training institution do not apply to this coda and are marked **NOT APPLICABLE (coda)** with a pointer to the CIM dossier, which already covers the Mission's admission and training rules under the name China Inland Mission; nothing in the sources fetched this session shows those rules being rewritten at the 1964 renaming.

## F -- Founding (here: the renaming)

- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): China Inland Mission, 1865 to 1964. An interim compound name, "The China Inland Mission Overseas Missionary Fellowship," is recorded once with a date of November 1951 by one source [omf-q012] and once without its own date, in a sentence immediately preceding the October 1964 renaming, by a second source [omf-q001]; the two sources are not reconciled (see `discrepancies.md`, item 2). "Overseas Missionary Fellowship" (OMF), from 1964. "OMF International," from 1993. [omf-q007, omf-q008, omf-q009, omf-q014, omf-q020]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): The name became the Overseas Missionary Fellowship at a meeting of the Mission's Overseas Council in October 1964, per the SOAS archive's own catalogue description of the CIM/OMF records [omf-q002]; three further, independent statements in a second source (an encyclopedia article's infobox, its overview prose, and its own 1960s-dated chronology entry) agree on the year 1964 without giving a month [omf-q007, omf-q008]. A fourth statement inside that same second source, in its 1950s-chronology subsection, dates what reads as the same Overseas Council restructuring meeting to "14 October 1954" [omf-q010, omf-q011] -- ten years earlier, same day and month. This is recorded as an unresolved discrepancy (`discrepancies.md`, item 1) and is not treated as a competing date for the rename itself, since it is contradicted by three other passages in the very same source. The name changed again, to OMF International, in 1993 [omf-q009].
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): The rename and restructuring took place under John Oswald (J. O.) Sanders, General Director from 1954 to 1969, who according to a secondary biography "led the reorganization of the CIM into the Overseas Missionary Fellowship" after becoming general director in 1954 [omf-q019, omf-q017, omf-q020]. Michael C. Griffiths succeeded him as General Director, 1969-1981 [omf-q018]. No source read this session names any other officer as personally responsible for the 1964 decision, or gives the composition of the "Overseas Council" that made it.
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Headquarters moved to Singapore (per one source, in November 1951, alongside the interim compound name) [omf-q012]; the renamed OMF went on to form national Home Councils in Japan, Malaysia, and Singapore (1965), Hong Kong and the Philippines (1966), and Germany and the Netherlands (1967) [omf-q005]. "An international centre in Singapore" is given as OMF International's headquarters in a current-day description [omf-q014].
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: no digitized copy of any post-1964 edition of the OMF *Principles and Practice* -- the roster's named key primary document for this slug -- could be located or opened this session; see `sources.csv` rows `omf-org-mrt-hongkong-council` and the BLOCKED omf.org rows, and scout_notes.md). No abridged charter is produced for this coda for that reason; see `charter_abridged.md`, which documents the block rather than a text.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: as F5 -- no primary OMF text of the purpose, in the Mission's own words, was located). A secondary paraphrase is available: the reorganization "acknowledged the additional need for work amongst non-Chinese nationals in the new fields of work" opened after CIM's withdrawal from China [omf-q003; SECONDARY: aim25-cim-omf-finding-aid]. A second secondary source states more generally that "the original goal of the mission that began dedicated to China has grown to include bringing the Gospel to East Asia" [omf-q015; SECONDARY: wikipedia-omf-international].
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): NOT FOUND in a primary OMF document. SECONDARY paraphrase only, as F6: the need to organize for "work amongst non-Chinese nationals in the new fields of work" that CIM's missionaries had been redeployed to after leaving China in 1950-1953 [omf-q003].
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: no post-1964 P&P or doctrinal statement text was located). The CIM dossier's field F8 for the pre-1964 doctrinal statement (Article 7 of *Principles and Practice*, on "soundness in the faith") is not extended to OMF in this coda for lack of a post-1964 text to compare it against.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): NOT APPLICABLE (coda) beyond the obvious and undisputed one: OMF is CIM continuing under a new name after 1964, not a new foundation with a separate lineage. See the CIM chapter and dossier for CIM's own antecedents (there recorded as NOT FOUND).

## M -- Emphasis

- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): NOT FOUND in a primary post-1964 OMF document (see F6/F8). A secondary source's enumerated list of "Missiological Distinctives of the CIM," carried forward without a stated post-1964 revision, includes as its seventh point "Headquarters on the field, director rule; leaders and workers serving shoulder to shoulder" [omf-q016; SECONDARY: wikipedia-omf-international, itself citing Broomhall (1984), 356 -- a secondary source's citation of a further secondary source, not independently checked this session].
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PS): The one clearly attested change of stress at the 1964 reorganization is structural rather than doctrinal: admitting "non-western Christians" to full membership and letting them "set up home councils in their own countries" [omf-q004], which by 1967 had produced Home Councils in Japan, Malaysia, Singapore, Hong Kong, the Philippines, Germany, and the Netherlands [omf-q005]. Governance was correspondingly reorganized: "The General Director remained the head of the Mission, with the Overseas Director responsible for missionary activities in Asia, and Home Directors responsible for OMF activities in their own countries" [omf-q006]. A secondary source states that this same reorganization's "main emphasis... was to continue to be evangelism, but support would also be given to a literature programme, medical services, radio and TV outreach, student work and linguistic work" [omf-q021; SECONDARY: wikipedia-omf-international] -- continuity of aim, widened means.
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT FOUND in a primary post-1964 document.
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND. As in the CIM dossier, no source read this session names a single fixed motto for the Mission under either name.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): One secondary source attributes words to James Hudson Taylor III (General Director 1981-1991) dated 1989 -- "OMF is still deeply committed to the Chinese people. We can never forget that we came into existence as the China Inland Mission" -- but the source itself marks this passage `[citation needed]`, meaning even that secondary source does not vouch for where the quotation comes from. It is recorded here as UNVERIFIED and is not used as a quotation in the chapter.

## S -- Sending

- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): Unchanged in kind from CIM (see the CIM dossier's S1) so far as any source read this session states, except that governance of the sending body was restructured in 1964 as described under M2 above [omf-q006], and that OMF has since 2006 been led by "Dr. Patrick Fung, a Chinese Christian appointed in 2006," described as "the first Asian to lead the mission" [omf-q013; SECONDARY: wikipedia-omf-international] -- itself evidence that the 1964 structural opening to non-Western leadership was eventually realized at the Mission's top office.
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): After the 1950-1953 withdrawal from China, missionaries were redeployed and OMF/CIM went on to open work, per the sources read this session, in Thailand, Malaysia, Japan, the Philippines, Indonesia, and Taiwan, and later Korea, Singapore, and Hong Kong [context to omf-q001, omf-q002]. The Mission describes its present-day field as East Asia generally [omf-q015].
