# Dossier — Columbia Bible College (`columbia-bible-college`)

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): "Columbia Bible School" at its 1923 opening; "Columbia Bible College" by at least 1939, the earliest source read this session [cbc-q008; cbc-q002].
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): The roster gives 1923 [HIGH]. The 1948 25th-anniversary yearbook confirms this indirectly, describing "full time courses" beginning "after more than four years of prayer and preparation" at the founding site [cbc-q007], and its retrospective closing page states the college's "first twenty-five years" had then elapsed [cbc-q010], consistent with a 1923 opening for a 1948 25th anniversary. No source read this session states the exact month or day of the 1923 opening, or a separate incorporation date.
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Robert C. McQuilkin, founding teacher: "one teacher, Mr. McQuilkin" at the 1923 opening [cbc-q008]; president by 1939 [context, cbc-q001 signature line]. A "President of the Board of Directors of Columbia Bible College since its beginning" is described in 1952 without a full name beyond "McQuilkin" [cbc-q014; see discrepancies.md item 2].
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Columbia, South Carolina, per the yearbooks' own imprint (title pages of all three fetched volumes; not separately quote-tagged). The founding classroom is described as "this one room" with minimal furnishings [cbc-q008], without a street address given in the sources read this session.
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No 1923-1930 catalog, statement of purpose, or founding-era document was located and fetched this session (see discrepancies.md item 1 and sources.csv row NONE-1). The nearest available primary material is the 25th-anniversary founding narrative in the 1948 yearbook [cbc-q007, cbc-q008, cbc-q009], used in the chapter's Charter section in place of an actual founding document.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): McQuilkin's own 1939 address to graduates names the college's purpose in its own motto: "the united testimony of Columbia Bible College with its purpose 'To Know Him and to Make Him Known'" [cbc-q001].
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): No source read this session states, in the founders' own words, a specific crisis. The founding narrative instead frames the 1923 opening as the fruit of sustained preparation: "after more than four years of prayer and preparation, full time courses were begun" [cbc-q007]. No further elaboration of what that preparation was answering is given in the sources read this session.
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND. No text of a doctrinal statement was located in any source read this session.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): NOT FOUND. No source read this session states which earlier school or document the founders consciously imitated. The roster places Columbia Bible College in the lineage descending from Moody Bible Institute (1886) alongside Biola, Glasgow, Toronto, and Prairie; this session's sources do not independently confirm that line.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): McQuilkin's own words to graduates point to conformity to Christ as the aim of the whole enterprise: teachers "would enter into Paul's desire expressed in Colossians 1:28, 'Whom we proclaim, admonishing every man and teaching every man in all wisdom, that we may present every man perfect in Christ'" [cbc-q003]. The founding narrative closes with the same order of priority stated institutionally: "these first faithful few planted, others since have watered; but then, as now, the Lord giveth the increase" [cbc-q009], and, of the college's growth, "[t]he tiny mustard seed is become a tree" [cbc-q011].
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): The recurring motto "To Know Him and to Make Him Known" [cbc-q001, cbc-q004, cbc-q013] states the school's own two-part emphasis: personal knowledge of Christ paired with evangelistic/missionary outreach. McQuilkin's own long association with the Keswick-style "Victorious Life" movement is evidenced by the Ben Lippen conference center's self-description as "The South's Victorious Life Conference" [cbc-q017], though no source read this session states this "deeper life" emphasis as a formal part of Columbia Bible College's own stated purpose, as distinct from McQuilkin's separate conference ministry; this distinction is preserved rather than the two institutions conflated.
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT FOUND.
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): "To Know Him and to Make Him Known," attested in 1939, the earliest source read this session [cbc-q001, cbc-q004], and still current in 1948 [cbc-q013].
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): The motto recurs unchanged in student writing across 1939 and 1948 sources [cbc-q001, cbc-q004, cbc-q013], showing continuity rather than restatement. McQuilkin's repeated appearance as a named speaker at the Ben Lippen "Victorious Life" conferences through at least 1952 [context, cbc-q017] shows the same leader restating a "deeper life" emphasis in a closely associated but formally distinct ministry.

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): No formal licensing or commendation procedure is described. Student-run organizations served a preparatory and promotional sending function: the Student Foreign Missions Fellowship, whose "purpose... is three-fold: to promote the missionary interest on the campus; to study and pray concerning the needs and work on the mission fields of the world; and to present the missionary message to churches and colleges in the vicinity" [context, cbc-q005-006]; and, at the graduate level, the Graduate School of Missions, whose students came "with the goal of bearing the saving gospel of Christ to a lost world" (1952, paraphrase, context surrounding cbc-q015).
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): The 1952 Graduate School of Missions drew students "[r]epresenting 18 states and three foreign countries" [cbc-q016] — a statement about students' origins, not their eventual mission fields; no destination list for graduates sent out was located this session.
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): Not a count of graduates sent, but of students preparing to be sent: the Student Foreign Missions Fellowship numbered 85 members in 1939, "each of whom are preparing definitely for foreign missionary service" [cbc-q006]; the Graduate School of Missions numbered 73 in 1952 [cbc-q015].
