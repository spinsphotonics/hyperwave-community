# Dossier — London Theological Seminary (`london-theological-seminary`)

Note on sourcing: this session located and fetched the single most important primary document
for this institution -- Martyn Lloyd-Jones's own Inaugural Address at the opening of the London
Theological Seminary, 6 October 1977, in full (32 pamphlet pages), posted by the institution's
own present-day successor site (londonseminary.org, formerly londontheologicalseminary.org).
This is a PRIMARY, first-person founding document of exactly the kind Rule 3 calls for, and it
is the principal source for most fields below. It is also copyrighted (Lloyd-Jones died 1981;
UK/EU copyright to 2051): every quotation drawn from it in this dossier is short (under 100
words) and flagged `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]`; see
`book/rights/permissions_log.csv`. LTS's own original 1977 prospectus/aims document (as distinct
from the inaugural address) was NOT located as a digitized text this session; see F5 and
discrepancies.md. Do not conflate London Theological Seminary (1977, this dossier) with London
Bible College (1943, later London School of Theology), which Lloyd-Jones declined to join --
per the roster's known-facts sheet and 07_risk_register_and_qa.md section B. No sentence below
does so.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Founded and known as "The London Theological Seminary" (LTS), 1977. Renamed "London Seminary" in September 2016 (a name-shortening, not a merger or refoundation, per the institution's own 2016 blog post). [lts-q001] [SECONDARY: ls-new-look-explained] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only for lts-q001]`
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): The Seminary was inaugurated on 6 October 1977, at a meeting called "to inaugurate The London Theological Seminary," addressed by Lloyd-Jones as Chairman of the Sponsoring Committee. [lts-q001] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` Corroborated independently by two secondary sources: christiandaily-50th-anniversary ("Founded in 1977 with Dr. Martyn Lloyd-Jones as its inaugural chairman") and en-org-biblical-generation ("London Seminary, LTS as it was called then, was a very small affair when it began in 1977"), and by Powell's 2007 memoir ("On October 6, 1977, the Doctor addressed the new London Theological Seminary"). [lts-q024, lts-q027, lts-q028] [SECONDARY: christiandaily-50th-anniversary; en-org-biblical-generation; eusebeia-powell-2007]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): Lloyd-Jones addressed the inaugural meeting "primarily as the Chairman of the Sponsoring Committee which has been responsible for calling this meeting to inaugurate" the Seminary -- i.e., his own role was chairman of a Sponsoring Committee, not "Principal." [lts-q001] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` Confirmed by christiandaily-50th-anniversary, which calls him "its inaugural chairman." [lts-q024] The first Principal (the seminary's chief academic/administrative officer, distinct from Lloyd-Jones's own chairman role) was Hywel Jones, per a 2017 Evangelicals Now report naming all four Principals in succession (Hywel Jones, then Philip Eveson, then Robert Strivens, then Bill James from January 2018). [lts-q025] [SECONDARY: en-org-biblical-generation] This resolves the roster's "Hywel Jones first principal [VERIFY]" toward CONFIRMED, on the strength of this one fetched source; see discrepancies.md for a WebSearch-only, unconfirmed counter-claim that was NOT used.
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): The inaugural meeting was held, and the Seminary was to function, "in whose premises we are meeting tonight" -- the premises of the Kensit Memorial College, whose Trustees ("Mr. Kensit and the Trustees") are separately thanked. [lts-q002] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` The location is Finchley, London (per orientation reading of wikipedia-london-seminary, not itself cited as a source for this fact; corroborated by Powell's 2007 memoir referring to "the congregation meeting in the Kensit Memorial College (now combined with the London Theological Seminary)"). [lts-q029] [SECONDARY: eusebeia-powell-2007]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): The principal founding document located and used for this dossier and the abridged charter is Lloyd-Jones's own *Inaugural Address at the opening of the London Theological Seminary*, 6 October 1977, English, document_id `mlj-inaugural-address-1977`. A separate written prospectus/aims document and a formal constitution are referred to by a secondary source (Evangelical Times, 2017: "As its constitution puts it, the seminary was to teach the 'great truths recovered at the Reformation'") but that constitution's own text was NOT independently located as a digitized primary document this session. [lts-q023] [SECONDARY: et-forty-years-2017, quoting the LTS constitution at second hand]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): "We are inaugurating a new College, and we are concerned about the whole question of the training for the ministry." Later in the same address: "The greatest need is not for teaching or lecturing; it is for preaching. What is preaching? Preaching is proclamation; it is the powerful presentation of the great message of the Bible." [lts-q003, lts-q021, lts-q022] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]`
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Lloyd-Jones gave several reasons: general dissatisfaction with existing theological colleges (he cites the controversy over *The Myth of God Incarnate* and bishops' own complaints); a felt need to found an Evangelical and "distinctively Protestant College" against a move toward ecumenical, interdenominational colleges; and, summarized in his own words, "it is generally agreed that the existing Colleges for some reason or another ... have failed to produce preachers." [lts-q004] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` Powell's 2007 memoir corroborates in its own words that LTS "had come into being as a result of the general dissatisfaction with the theological training available in the United Kingdom." [lts-q028] [SECONDARY: eusebeia-powell-2007]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): Lloyd-Jones states the Sponsoring Committee's conviction "that we must have an Evangelical Seminary, and not only an Evangelical but a distinctively Protestant College," explicitly over against ecumenism and Roman Catholicism. [lts-q005] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` A secondary source separately reports the Seminary's constitution required teaching "the 'great truths recovered at the Reformation.'" [lts-q023] [SECONDARY: et-forty-years-2017] No subscription formula or list of doctrinal articles, and no statement of who (students, faculty, or both) must subscribe, was located in the text of the inaugural address itself; NOT FOUND beyond these two general statements.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): Lloyd-Jones's address itself is explicitly framed as a rejection of the recent Bible-college and seminary tradition of "the last hundred years," and quotes, in order to disagree with it, an unnamed "great Evangelical scholar" speaking "at the opening of a famous Seminary nearly 50 years ago" (internally dateable to c.1929, and identifiable on general historical grounds with J. Gresham Machen's 1929 Westminster Theological Seminary opening address, though the inaugural address itself does not name Machen and this session did not independently confirm the identification against a fetched source, so it is NOT stated as certain in the chapter). NOT FOUND (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977) for any explicitly named earlier school the founders say they imitated, as opposed to reacted against.

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977; all other documents in sources.csv).
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): NOT FOUND as a stated formal requirement (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977). The address explicitly rejects using prior academic/classical education as a gatekeeping criterion, arguing a preacher does not need extensive Greek and Hebrew scholarship; see C4.
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): "He must be a converted man. He must be a man who is aware of new life in himself; but he must be a spiritually-minded man also." Character requirements are also stated: humility, and not being "a difficult man." [lts-q007] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]`
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): NOT FOUND as an entrance examination; the address explicitly states there are to be no examinations at all in the College (see C7), which by extension rules out an entrance exam, though this is inference from C7 rather than a directly stated A4 fact.
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): "This being a Theological Seminary, no women will be admitted." [lts-q006] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]`
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): "The prospective preacher must have the confidence of the Church to which he belongs," and students were expected to "remain in contact with the churches constantly, with their minister, their deacons, elders if they have any" -- i.e., entrance by the sending church's own testimony/confidence, not by an institutional examination. [lts-q008] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` This directly confirms the roster's and the ticket's "entrance by church testimony" claim.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): NOT FOUND in the inaugural address itself (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977). The roster's and this book's commissioning brief both describe a "two-year course," but no fetched document this session states a course length in so many words; this specific figure is therefore NOT stated in the chapter as a fact, only as an unconfirmed roster claim (see discrepancies.md).
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): In the order Lloyd-Jones presents them: knowledge of the Bible and biblical languages (Hebrew and Greek, at a basic/working level only); exegesis; hermeneutics; (biblical) Theology, explicitly not philosophy; Church History, including the history of doctrines and of denominations; Apologetics, placed deliberately last and given a subordinate, "mainly negative" role; and pastoral training, with an explicit statement that "we have made no provision for the teaching of psychology." [lts-q015, lts-q016, lts-q017, lts-q018, lts-q019] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]`
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977); no class/year/term structure is described in the address itself.
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Hebrew and Greek were taught, but only "sufficient instruction on this matter to enable them to use the Commentaries in an intelligent manner" -- explicitly not to the level of original scholarly argument with "the great professors." [lts-q015] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]`
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND as a reading list (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977). The address names no set textbooks; it does cite, within the argument of the address itself, Anselm's *Cur Deus Homo* and Peter Brown's biography of Augustine, but not as assigned student texts.
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a specific weekly preaching-practice exercise (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977); the address describes the aim (to produce preachers) and the subjects taught toward that aim (see C2), but does not describe a practicum schedule.
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): "There are to be no examinations in this College." Lloyd-Jones explicitly rejects the examination/diploma model as belonging to "a wrong idea," even calling formal examination in this realm "almost blasphemous." [lts-q009] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]`
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): "So we are not going to have any degrees or diplomas or even any examinations." Students' fitness for ministry would instead be judged by "their response to this" and by churches, when the men "appear before churches." [lts-q010] `[RIGHTS: copyrighted, short quotation only]` This directly confirms the roster's and ticket's claim that LTS explicitly refused degrees and academic accreditation.
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: mlj-inaugural-address-1977); no timetable is given in the address.
