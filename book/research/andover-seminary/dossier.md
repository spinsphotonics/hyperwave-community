# Dossier — Andover Theological Seminary (`andover-seminary`)

Note on sourcing: one document was fetched and read this session, Woods 1885, but it is an
unusually document-rich source: Woods was the Seminary's own first professor, and his book's
appendix reprints, verbatim and in full, three separate 1808 constitutional instruments (the
original Constitution of the Theological Seminary, the Additional Statutes containing the
Associate Creed, and the Laws of the Theological Institution as published 1811 with 1817
amendments) as well as a later 1827/1837/1846 revision of the Laws. These reprinted
instruments are treated PRIMARY; Woods's own connective narrative is SECONDARY. Rowe 1933
was fetched but not used for any quote this session (see sources.csv). The "Constitution of
the Society of the Brethren" (1808) named in the roster was searched for and not found in
either fetched Andover text (see discrepancies.md item 2).

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The founding instruments name the institution "the Theological Seminary in Andover" / "this INSTITUTION or SEMINARY," established in connexion with the pre-existing Phillips Academy. [andover-seminary-q001, andover-seminary-q003]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): The original Founders (Samuel Abbot, Phoebe Phillips, John Phillips) founded the Seminary, framed its Constitution, and committed it to the Board of Trustees of Phillips Academy on 31 August 1807. The Associate Founders (Moses Brown, William Bartlett, John Norris) executed their own Associate Statutes on 21 March 1808. The Associate Creed was joined to the Constitution's Catechism-based doctrinal clause by the original Founders' Additional Statutes on 3 May 1808, uniting the two foundations under one doctrinal standard. This dossier does not assert a specific date for the first admission of students or first instruction, which was not located as a quoted passage this session (see discrepancies.md item 3). [andover-seminary-q016, andover-seminary-q017]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): Founders of the Institution: Samuel Abbot, Phoebe Phillips (relict of Lieutenant Governor Samuel Phillips), and John Phillips of Andover. Associate Founders (donors of the professorship funds on the "Associate Foundation"): Moses Brown and William Bartlett of Newburyport and John Norris of Salem. [andover-seminary-q001, andover-seminary-q016]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): Andover, in the County of Essex, Commonwealth of Massachusetts, in connexion with Phillips Academy (itself founded 1778 in the South Parish of Andover). [andover-seminary-q001]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): The "Constitution of the Theological Seminary" (1807/1808) together with its "Additional Statutes" (3 May 1808, containing the Associate Creed) and the "Laws of the Theological Institution as published in 1811" (with 1817 amendments), all reprinted verbatim in Woods 1885's documentary appendix. English. document_id: woods1885.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The founders state their purpose as "making some provision for increasing the number of learned and able Defenders of the gospel of CHRIST as well, as of orthodox, pious, and zealous Ministers of the New Testament." [andover-seminary-q002]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The same preamble clause (q002) frames the need as a want of "learned and able Defenders of the gospel" and of "orthodox, pious, and zealous Ministers" -- i.e., a shortage of trained, doctrinally sound ministers, phrased as a positive provision rather than as a description of a specific crisis. No additional passage naming a more specific precipitating event (e.g., Harvard's move away from Trinitarian orthodoxy) was located and formally quoted this session; this is commonly stated in general scholarship but is not asserted here without a quoted primary source. [andover-seminary-q002]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): Every Professor, on the day of his inauguration and again every five years, must "publicly make and subscribe a solemn Declaration of his faith in divine revelation, and in the fundamental and distinguishing doctrines of the gospel of Christ, as summarily expressed in the Westminster Assembly's shorter Catechism," and, per the Additional Statutes, "as more particularly expressed in the following Creed" -- the Associate Creed, which opens "I believe that there is one and but one living and true GOD" and continues through a full Calvinist statement including original sin, election, and the sole mediatorship of Christ ("the only REDEEMER of the elect is the eternal SON of GOD"). Visitors of the Associate Foundation must subscribe "the same theological Creed, which every Professor elect is required to subscribe." Ordinary students, if not already in full communion with a church, subscribe a shorter "declaration of his belief of the Christian Religion." [andover-seminary-q006, andover-seminary-q007, andover-seminary-q008, andover-seminary-q009]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: woods1885) as a directly quoted primary statement of imitation. Woods's narrative (not formally quoted this session) situates the founding amid the union of two prior, doctrinally rival Andover-area seminary projects (a "Liberal"/Unitarian-leaning plan at Andover and a strict Calvinist "Associate" plan at West Newbury), which were merged into one institution in 1808 -- this union is the documented origin of the "Associate" terminology throughout the Constitution and Statutes, but no passage explicitly naming an outside model (e.g. a European seminary) was located and quoted this session.

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: woods1885).
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): Candidates must "produce satisfactory evidence, that he possesses good natural and acquired talents, has honorably completed a course of liberal education, and sustains a fair moral character." For the Associate Foundation specifically, "a College education shall be ever deemed an essential condition of admission, except only in some rare case of distinguished talents, information, and piety." [andover-seminary-q004]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): A candidate must "exhibit proper testimonials of his being in full communion with some Church of CHRIST; in default of which he shall subscribe a declaration of his belief of the Christian Religion." He must also "declare that it is his serious inten- tion to devote himself to the work of the gospel ministry," unless the Trustees excuse a "pious Applicant" from that declaration in a peculiar case. [andover-seminary-q004]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): Candidates are "carefully examined by the Faculty in the learned languages, and with reference to his object in pursuing theological studies." For the Associate Foundation, examination in the learned languages is made by a standing Committee appointed by the Visitors, after which the candidate reads the Constitution and Associate Statutes and subscribes a declaration, then serves as a resident Applicant "on trial for two months" before being placed on the list of resident Students.
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): The Constitution states the institution "shall be equally open to Protestants of every denomination for the admission of young men of requisite qualifications" -- admission is stated in terms of "young men" throughout; no provision for women students was found. [andover-seminary-q003]
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND for entering students (no prior-preaching requirement located); for continuing/senior students, permission to preach is itself something granted only after examination (see C6/S1 below), not a precondition of admission.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): Constitution Article 27 fixes ordinary residence at three full years (after the Seminary's first three years, during which the requirement was not yet fully in force); each Professor's course of lectures in his department is to be "always completed within the space of three years."
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): "The public instruction shall be given in Lectures on Natural Theology, Sacred Literature, Ecclesiastical History, Christian Theology, and Pulpit Eloquence," each elaborated in its own Constitution article (Natural Theology: existence and attributes of God, natural theology's coincidence with revelation; Sacred Literature: the Bible's original languages, the Septuagint, textual criticism, English translations; Ecclesiastical History: Jewish antiquities, the early church, heresies, the Reformation; Christian Theology: revelation, the Trinity, the fall, the atonement, regeneration, last things; Pulpit Eloquence: oratory, composition, delivery). [andover-seminary-q005]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): Two terms per year, divided by two six-week vacations (one beginning the fourth Wednesday in September, the other the first Wednesday in May, per the 1811 Laws); the aggregate of vacations is not to exceed twelve weeks in a year per the Constitution.
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Every student is required to devote time "to the study of the learned languages, as shall increase his knowledge of them, especially of the Greek and Hebrew languages," alongside Philology, Rhetoric, and Oratory; students are also examined yearly "in the original languages of the Old and New Testament, and in the Septuagint version" of the Old Testament. [andover-seminary-q010]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND as a specific reading list beyond the Scriptures in the original languages and the Septuagint (searched: woods1885); the Constitution and Laws describe subject areas and required examinations rather than naming individual set authors or textbooks.
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): "The senior Students will be required, not only to prepare sermons, but occasionally to deliver them in public, both in the Seminary and in neighbouring congregations, as may be judged expedient by the said Professors"; the 1811 Laws add that each senior writes four to six sermons a year, each submitted for the Professors' "free, and friendly correction," and that no student may preach in a neighboring congregation without a written permission from the Professors bearing the license "Prcelegere licet" over their signatures -- and this permission never supersedes the "customary approbation or license of some regular Association or Presbytery." [andover-seminary-q013, andover-seminary-q014]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): Each student is examined at least once a year by the Professors in the original languages of the Old and New Testament and the Septuagint, and on the leading arguments of authors read and the style and design of the sacred writers particularly studied (Constitution Article 24 / Laws Chap. III.III).
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): On honorably finishing his course, a student who requests it receives a Certificate, signed by the Professors, stating how long he studied and attended lectures, that he pursued his studies with diligence, and that he sustained a good moral character -- no academic degree is described; ordination/licensure itself remains the business of "some regular Association or Presbytery," outside the Seminary's own authority. [andover-seminary-q014]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): The 1811 Laws set the stated hours of study "from morning prayers to twelve o'clock," excepting one hour for breakfast, and "from two o'clock P.M. to evening prayers," with students expected in their rooms from nine in the evening; morning prayers were at seven and evening prayers at five in winter term, and at six morning and evening in summer term. [andover-seminary-q011, andover-seminary-q012]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): See C9 above for the hours of study and prayer; morning and evening chapel exercises consisted of a short opening prayer, a devotional reading (or, once weekly, an exposition of Scripture), a psalm, and a closing prayer, with Professors presiding and occasionally Students of two years' standing officiating in part. [andover-seminary-q012]
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): NOT FOUND as a specific description of lodging arrangements this session (searched: woods1885); students were required to "reside constantly at the Seminary, during term time, unless prevented by sickness," under the Laws.
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): Morning and evening chapel exercises daily during term time, as described in L1/C9; on Sundays, once a church was formed at the Seminary, "divine services will be publicly celebrated in the chapel...in the forenoon and afternoon of every LORD'S day," usually by the Professors but sometimes by permitted senior Students. [andover-seminary-q012]
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): Students guilty of "gross immorality, or of any insult or oppugnation to the said TRUSTEES, or to any Professor or Officer" are to be "cut off from all the advantages and benefits of this INSTITUTION," unless they make immediate satisfaction (Constitution Article 25); tardiness and absence from chapel are noted by Monitors appointed by the Faculty (1811 Laws, Chap. II.II).
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: woods1885) beyond the general discipline clause above.
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): On the Associate Foundation, "Tuition and room shall be gratis to all approved Applicants and Students," with further gratuitous aid (diet, fuel, light, washing, lodging) granted according to the Visitors' direction "as the state of the said Funds may permit"; no Student on this Foundation is ever to be charged for a Professor's instruction. Beneficiaries on the original (non-Associate) Foundation similarly have their "necessary expenses...for board, lodging, washing, fuel, and light" defrayed from appropriated funds. [andover-seminary-q018]

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): The Constitution provides for a Professor of Christian Theology (the founding endowment, Samuel Abbot's fund) and, via the Associate Foundation, further Professors; officers are titled "Professor," collectively the "Faculty," under the government of the Trustees of Phillips Academy and, for the Associate Foundation, a separate Board of Visitors.
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): Every Professor must, on inauguration and every five years after, subscribe a declaration of faith according to the Westminster Shorter Catechism and the Associate Creed, and must promise to "open and explain the Scriptures to his Pupils with integrity and faithfulness," to maintain and inculcate the Christian faith "in opposition, not only to Atheists and Infidels, but to Jews, Mahometans, Arians, Pelagians, Antinomians, Arminians, Socinians, Unitarians, and Universalists," and to promote "true PIETY and GODLINESS" by instruction, counsel, and example. [andover-seminary-q006, andover-seminary-q007]
- **T3 Forms of teacher-student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): Public lectures by each Professor in his department; private instruction and advice on Scripture study; correction of student compositions "in a free, but friendly manner"; and, per the 1811 Laws, an expectation that Professors "encourage and cultivate social, friendly, and familiar inter- course with their Pupils individually," inviting them to their houses for "free conversation on theological subjects and personal religion" and visiting them at their rooms. [andover-seminary-q015]
- **T4 Named examples of teacher-student interaction with source** (PS): Leonard Woods himself, author of this history, was the Seminary's first Abbot Professor of Christian Theology and an eyewitness of these founding arrangements, per this book's own title page and preface, though no single narrated anecdote of a specific interaction with a specific student was formally quoted this session.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The Associate Creed itself is the clearest statement of what the founders required above all: "I believe that there is one and but one living and true GOD," followed by a full Calvinist confession (the Trinity, human depravity from Adam's fall, unconditional election, Christ's sole atoning mediatorship, the necessity of the new birth, and final perseverance). [andover-seminary-q008, andover-seminary-q009]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Doctrinal precision (the Creed and the Professors' quinquennial subscription, opposed by name to a long list of named heresies) combined with personal piety ("orthodox, pious, and zealous Ministers"), the learned languages (Greek, Hebrew, and Latin-derived rhetoric), and preaching practice under close correction. [andover-seminary-q002, andover-seminary-q003, andover-seminary-q006, andover-seminary-q010]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The Professor's subscription oath is set explicitly "in opposition, not only to Atheists and Infidels, but to Jews, Mahometans, Arians, Pelagians, Antinomians, Arminians, Socinians, Unitarians, and Universalists, and to all other heresies and errors, antient or modern." [andover-seminary-q006 context; text quoted in full in charter_abridged.md]
