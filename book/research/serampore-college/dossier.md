# Dossier — Serampore College (`serampore-college`)

Note on sourcing: no digitized copy of the 1818 *Prospectus of a College for the Instruction
of Asiatic, Christian and Other Youth* itself, nor of the 1827 Danish Royal Charter itself, was
located and fetched this session (see discrepancies.md and sources.csv row
senate-royal-charter-page). Instead, two English-language secondary works were fetched and
read: Marshman's 1859 family memoir (written by the son of co-founder Joshua Marshman, and
his own son the co-signer John Clark Marshman of the 1827 charter), which paraphrases the
Prospectus closely and quotes several of its clauses directly in quotation marks, and describes
the charter negotiation in narrative detail; and George Smith's 1885 biography of Carey, which
reprints King Frederick VI's 1820 letter to Carey verbatim and Carey's own 1826 letter to the
Baptist Missionary Society committee verbatim. Passages given in quotation marks in these
secondary works, and attributed to a named primary document or letter, are treated as PRIMARY
at second hand and cited PO. Ordinary narrative sentences in Marshman or Smith are cited as
[SECONDARY: document_id, page].

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The institution's founding title, per the 1818 prospectus, was "College for the instruction of Asiatic, Christian, and other youth, in Eastern literature and European science"; it is referred to throughout as Serampore College. [serampore-college-q001]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): Carey, Marshman and Ward "determined to concentrate their exertions on the formation of a college" after some years of desiring a higher institution for native Christian education; the prospectus was issued 15 July 1818; the college began instruction with thirty-seven students; a Danish royal charter of incorporation was sought from King Frederick VI, negotiated by Marshman in Copenhagen in 1826, and issued 23 February 1827 [roster known-facts sheet, HIGH; exact charter date not independently found in a quoted passage this session — see discrepancies.md], engrossed on vellum and delivered to Marshman. [serampore-college-q010, serampore-college-q001, serampore-college-q020, serampore-college-q009]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): William Carey, Joshua Marshman and William Ward are named throughout as the "Serampore Brotherhood"/"the missionaries"; the 1818 prospectus was "drawn up by Dr. Marshman, in his peculiar and argumentative style"; the 1827 charter names William Carey and Joshua Marshman, Doctors of Divinity, and John Clark Marshman as the incorporators [per the WebSearch-quoted charter opening, NOT independently verified against a fetched primary text this session — see discrepancies.md]. Carey held the College's chair of Divinity and lectured on botany and zoology; John Mack and John Marshman (Joshua's son) served with him as professors. [serampore-college-q002, serampore-college-q021]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Serampore (Frederiksnagore), a Danish settlement in Bengal, on the Hooghly River. [roster; general background, not independently quoted this session]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): *Prospectus of a College for the Instruction of Asiatic, Christian, and Other Youth in Eastern Literature and European Science*, issued 15 July 1818, English, quoted at second hand in Marshman 1859 (document_id `marshman1859-v2`); the Danish Royal Charter of incorporation, 1827, is described (not quoted verbatim) in the same source and in Smith 1885 (document_id `smith-carey-1885`). Neither founding document was located as a digitized full text and read directly this session; see discrepancies.md. [serampore-college-q001, serampore-college-q002]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The prospectus "announced in unequivocal terms that the institution was intended to be the handmaid of evangelisation"; it argued that "if ever...the Gospel stands in India, it must be by native opposed to native in demonstrating its excellence above [all other systems]." Carey's own 1826 letter states: "About seven years ago we felt convinced of the necessity of erecting a College for native Christian youth, in order to consolidate our plans for the spread of gospel truth in India." The college's threefold object was "the education of non-resident heathen students, the education of resident Christian students, and the preparation of missionaries from those born in the country." [serampore-college-q002, serampore-college-q003, serampore-college-q014, serampore-college-q019]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Native preachers and schoolmasters had defects that "had long been severely felt"; the founders wanted native Christian teachers able to meet learned Hindu opponents on their own ground, arguing a European missionary ignorant of Hindu philosophy could not argue as effectively as Paul had against the Greeks. [serampore-college-q014; SECONDARY narrative, marshman1859-v2, p. 168 (not separately quoted)]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No single confession is stated; admission was open across Christian denominations on condition that "the instruction should be divested of everything of a sectarian character" — an explicitly unsectarian basis rather than subscription to one creed. [serampore-college-q007]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: marshman1859-v2, smith-carey-1885) in a directly quoted passage this session. Marshman is recorded as having obtained a translation of the relevant statutes of the University of Copenhagen, with the help of Professor Rasmus Rask, and used them as a model in drafting the charter's provisions — but this fact was not this session formalized into a quote record (see raw/marshman1859-v2.txt lines ~15095-15100); flagged as a follow-up.

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: marshman1859-v2, smith-carey-1885).
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): NOT FOUND as a stated requirement; the institution was open "to native youths from all parts of India, without distinction of caste or creed," suggesting no fixed prior-education bar for the general (non-theological) student body. [serampore-college-q005]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): For the separate theological division only: the six students admitted to the "class of theological students" in 1826 were chosen for "piety" of which the missionaries had "undoubted evidence" or "considerable ground for hope," and "none will be continued in it who do not manifest undeniable piety and devotedness to the cause of missions" — i.e. an ongoing, not merely entrance, test of piety. No conversion-narrative requirement is stated for the general (non-theological, non-resident) student body, which was open to Hindu and Muslim youths as well. [serampore-college-q015, serampore-college-q016]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): For the theological division, students were to "remain on an average four years," yielding an estimated two or three additional missionary "labourers" a year. General course length NOT FOUND. [serampore-college-q018, serampore-college-q013]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): Sanskrit (for the general and native-teacher divisions, taught first, "that they might be enabled to understand the tenets and principles of the prevailing system"), Arabic ("the canonical language of Mahomedanism"), and English, the last reserved for a select number of advanced students so that they might be able "to dive into the deepest recesses of European science, and enrich their own language with its choicest treasures" — the prospectus explicitly argued that "it would be vain to attempt to enlighten a country through the medium of any language besides its own," while defending English as worth studying "as a learned language...by youths of superior talent." A normal (teacher-training) department and a theological division (see C6) completed the plan. [serampore-college-q003, serampore-college-q004; SECONDARY narrative on Sanskrit/Arabic, marshman1859-v2 p. 170-171, not separately quoted]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): NOT FOUND in a quoted passage beyond the divisional structure in C2 and C6 (general/native, normal, theological).
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Sanskrit and Arabic (the classical/canonical languages of Hindu and Muslim learning) for native and Christian students generally; English at an advanced literary level for a select number of senior students. [serampore-college-q003, serampore-college-q004]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: marshman1859-v2, smith-carey-1885).
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): A distinct "class of theological students in the college, under the Divinity Professor" trained native preachers and missionaries; Carey himself held the chair of Divinity. [serampore-college-q013, serampore-college-q021]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: marshman1859-v2, smith-carey-1885) for the general course. [The upper hall of the college building "was intended for the annual examinations" per Smith 1885's architectural description — SECONDARY, not separately quoted this session.]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): The 1827 royal charter empowered "the College Council, consisting of the Governor of Serampore and the Brotherhood, to confer degrees like those of the Universities of Copenhagen and Kiel," though a Serampore degree carried no state rank in Denmark without the Crown's express sanction; this made Serampore "the earliest degree-conferring college in Asia," though the power was "never...exercised." [serampore-college-q011, serampore-college-q012]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: marshman1859-v2, smith-carey-1885).

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The prospectus was drawn up "in unequivocal terms" to be "the handmaid of evangelisation," arguing that only "native opposed to native" could finally demonstrate the Gospel's excellence in India. Carey's own words to his son on the College's opening: "I pray that the blessing of God may attend it, and that it may be the means of preparing many for an important situation in the Church of God." [serampore-college-q002, serampore-college-q003, serampore-college-q017]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): A deliberately dual, "Oriental and European" emphasis: thorough Sanskrit and Arabic learning paired with theological and English literary training, explicitly not a purely English-medium institution, "of the three objects connected with the College...the two [latter, resident-Christian and native-missionary training] are intimately connected with the progress of the good cause." [serampore-college-q019, serampore-college-q003]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The prospectus rejected the idea of substituting a purely English education for vernacular/classical learning, insisting "it would be vain to attempt to enlighten a country through the medium of any language besides its own." [serampore-college-q004]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: marshman1859-v2, smith-carey-1885). [Carey's general motto "Expect great things from God; attempt great things for God" is recorded elsewhere in Marshman 1859 in connection with the wider Serampore Mission, not specifically the College charter; not treated as the College's own motto without further confirmation.]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): Carey's 1826 letter to the BMS committee restates the founders' purpose at length, distinguishing the College's three objects (see F6, M2) and defending the theological class as a way to multiply "additional labourers" for the mission field. [serampore-college-q014, serampore-college-q018]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The College's own "class of theological students...under the Divinity Professor" was the stated mechanism for producing new missionaries: Carey wrote that allowing each theological student "to remain on an average four years," the College could expect "the acquisition of two, and perhaps three, additional labourers annually," intended to fulfil "the plan we have long cherished, that of placing one missionary in each province in Bengal." [serampore-college-q013, serampore-college-q018]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): Bengal province by province, and "eventually, if means be afforded, in Hindostan" more broadly, per Carey's 1826 letter. [SECONDARY narrative continuation of serampore-college-q018, marshman1859-v2/smith-carey-1885 not separately re-quoted]
