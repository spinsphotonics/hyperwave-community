# Discrepancies — scotland-first-book-of-discipline

1. **Date of the Privy Council's approving Act: 27 January or 17 January 1560/61.** The main
   text printed in Laing's 1848 edition reads "Act of Secreit Counsall, xxvii Januarii, Anno
   &c., lx" (27 January), but Laing's own footnote at this point states: "That is, the 27th (in
   edit. 1621, the 17th) of January 1560-1; as the year did not begin at that time till the 25th
   of March." (laing1848-fbd, p. 257; quote_id scotland-fbd-q026 for the main-text date,
   scotland-fbd-q027 for the footnote fragment recording the variant.) So the edition of 1621
   (the anonymous Calderwood print) gives the 17th where the manuscript tradition followed by
   Laing gives the 27th. Both are Old Style within the year "1560," which by modern reckoning
   (year beginning 1 January) falls in January 1561. Not resolved here; both readings are
   recorded per Rule 9.

2. **Founders' names: not stated in the primary text fetched this session vs. secondary "six
   Johns" tradition.** The document as fetched (laing1848-fbd) never names its own authors in
   the Preface or body text read this session; it is signed only "By your Honouris Most humble
   Servitouris... and Ministeris of Christ Jesus" (see F3). Wikipedia's article on the Book of
   Discipline names a drafting committee of "six Johns" — Knox, Winram, Spottiswood, Willock,
   Douglas, and Row — citing its own secondary source (not opened this session).
   [SECONDARY: wikipedia-book-of-discipline] This is recorded as background only; the chapter
   should not present the "six Johns" list as confirmed by the primary document itself, since no
   primary passage naming them was located and quoted this session.

3. **Roster's known-facts sheet says "Knox and five others"; primary text does not name them.**
   `02_institution_roster.md` row for this slug already states the founders as "Knox and five
   others," consistent with the "six Johns" secondary tradition above, and dates the document
   1560 [HIGH confidence]. This session's primary-text fetch confirms the year 1560 (via the
   29 April 1560 charge and the January 1560/61 Privy Council Act, see F2) but does not itself
   independently confirm the "five others" by name. No contradiction is recorded — only that the
   primary text fetched this session is silent on the point where the roster and Wikipedia agree.
