## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The document's own running head and title in the 1848 edition is "The Buke of Discipline"; it is now universally called the First Book of Discipline (1560), distinguished from the Second Book of Discipline of 1578. [scotland-fbd-q001]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): The authors record that they received a charge from the Great Council of Scotland, dated at Edinburgh 29 April 1560, commanding them to commit their judgments on the Reformation of religion to writing; the finished document was subscribed "Frome Edinburgh" in 1560 (the exact day/month is illegible in this session's OCR); a Privy Council Act approving it is dated "xxvii Januarii" (27 January), in the reckoning "Anno &c., lx" (1560, Old Style: the Scottish year did not begin until 25 March, so this is January 1561 New Style). A footnote records that the 1621 printed edition instead gives the 17th of January, not the 27th — see Discrepancies. [scotland-fbd-q002, scotland-fbd-q026, scotland-fbd-q027]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): The document is addressed to the Great Council of Scotland and signed only "By your Honouris Most humble Servitouris... and Ministeris of Christ Jesus," without naming its individual authors in the text fetched this session. Wikipedia's account, not independently confirmed against a primary source this session, names a drafting committee of "six Johns" — John Knox, John Winram, John Spottiswood, John Willock, John Douglas, and John Row. [SECONDARY: wikipedia-book-of-discipline] [scotland-fbd-q002 for the unnamed self-description]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Edinburgh, where the charge was issued, the document subscribed, and the Privy Council's approving Act dated; the plan itself proposes three university towns — St Andrews, Glasgow, and Aberdeen. [scotland-fbd-q002, scotland-fbd-q015, scotland-fbd-q026]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): "The Buke of Discipline" [the First Book of Discipline], 1560, English (16th-century Scots), document_id `laing1848-fbd`. [scotland-fbd-q001]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The authors say they were charged "to committ to Avriting, and in a Buke to deliver unto your Wisdomes oure jugementis tuiching the Reformatioun of Religioun." Specifically as to schools, they tell the Council it is a "necessitie" that it "be most cairfull for the virtuous educatioun, and godlie up-bringing of the youth of this Realme." [scotland-fbd-q003, scotland-fbd-q007]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): They state that religion in Scotland, "as in utheris" (realms), "hes hene utterlie corrupted" heretofore. [scotland-fbd-q004]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): The First Head, "of Doctrine," requires that the Evangel (Gospel) "be trewlie and openlie preached in everie Kirk and Assemblie of this Realme," and that all doctrine contrary to it be suppressed. Those who put their names to the document (the subscribing lords and others, see the signature list, pp. 257-260) state that they find it "conforme to Goddis Word in all pointis." [scotland-fbd-q005, scotland-fbd-q026]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO/PS): The document's own text, in the schools section, cites Geneva directly and only once found this session: the catechism to be taught in every parish is "the Catechisme... as we have it now translaited in the Booke of our Common Ordour, callit the Ordour of Geneva." [PO; scotland-fbd-q009, in its "before" context] Separately, Archbishop Spotiswood, quoted in Laing's 1848 editorial footnote (not part of the 1560 text itself), states that the whole policy "had been formed by John Knox, partly in imitation of the reformed Churches of Germany, partly of that he had seen in Geneva." [SECONDARY: laing1848-editorial-note; scotland-fbd-q006]

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): No specific minimum or maximum age in years is stated for entry to either the parish grammar school or the university. A testimonial of the candidate's "aige" (among other things) was required for university admission, but no number is given. NOT FOUND (searched: laing1848-fbd) for an explicit age figure.
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): Parish schools themselves presuppose no prior education: every parish was to have a schoolmaster "able, at least, to teache Gramraer and the Latine toung." Admission to the university's first college, by contrast, required that the candidate already be instructed "in the toungis" (the classical languages) at the parish/burgh level. [scotland-fbd-q008, scotland-fbd-q019]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): No narrated conversion testimony is required. Instead, a candidate for the university needed a testimonial "of his learnyng, docilitie, aige, and parentage" from the schoolmaster and the minister of the town where he was taught. Poor children were to be examined for "the spirit of docilitie" before being supported to continue their studies at the church's charge. [scotland-fbd-q019, scotland-fbd-q011]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): Entry to the university's first class (Dialectic) required a testimonial as above; a candidate who could show, by trial before examiners deputed by the Rector and Principals, that he was already "sufficientlie instructit in Dialectick" was promoted immediately, in that same year, to the Mathematics class. Similar testimonial requirements, building cumulatively, gated entry to the classes of Medicine, Law, and Divinity. [scotland-fbd-q019, scotland-fbd-q028]
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: laing1848-fbd). The schools and universities sections read this session make no mention of the admission or exclusion of women or girls; all pronouns for pupils, bursars, and readers in the material fetched are masculine ("he," "his sone").
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND for entry to school or university (searched: laing1848-fbd). The document does specify (Fifth Head, on minister's provision, outside the Schools section proper) that no one be admitted to preach without further "honestie and further knowledge," but this concerns admission to the ministry, not to school or university, and was not formally quoted this session.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): The plan lays out a sequence of allotted years: about two years to learn to read and answer the Catechism; a further three or four years to complete Grammar; four years for Logic, Rhetoric, and Greek; and the remaining years, up to age twenty-four in total, for Law, Medicine, or Divinity, after which the student was to be "removed to serve the Churche or Commoun-wealth." Individual university course-lengths are also fixed by subject (see C3). [scotland-fbd-q013, scotland-fbd-q014, scotland-fbd-q025]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): Reading and the Catechism, then Grammar and Latin, then (at university) a first college of Dialectic, Mathematics, Natural Philosophy, and Medicine; a second college of Ethics/Economics/Politics and Law; a third college of Hebrew and Greek, then Divinity. Parish/burgh grammar schools taught reading, writing, Latin, and (in notable towns) Logic and Rhetoric together with "the Tongues." [scotland-fbd-q008, scotland-fbd-q009, scotland-fbd-q013]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): Three universities were proposed — St Andrews (the "first and principal," with three colleges), Glasgow, and Aberdeen (each with two colleges, ordered like Glasgow). Each college was divided into classes ("seiges"), each with its own Reader and a fixed course-length (commonly one year per class, longer — four or five years — for Law, Medicine, Hebrew/Greek, and Divinity). [scotland-fbd-q015]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Latin from the grammar-school level; at university, Greek and Hebrew formed the third college of St Andrews (and a language chair at Glasgow/Aberdeen). The Hebrew reader was to interpret a book of Moses, the Prophets, or the Psalms after completing the grammar of the language in half a year; the Greek reader was to interpret a book of Plato together with a passage of the New Testament. [scotland-fbd-q017, scotland-fbd-q018]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): Named texts are comparatively few (unlike, e.g., Geneva's classical curriculum): "ane booke of Moses," "the Propheitis, or the Psalmes" for the Hebrew reader; "some booke of Plato" together with a passage "of the New Testament" for the Greek reader; the readers in Divinity were to lecture on the New and the Old Testament respectively. [scotland-fbd-q017, scotland-fbd-q018]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): NOT FOUND specifically within the Schools/Universities section this session (searched: laing1848-fbd). The document elsewhere (Fifth Head) discusses examination and trial of candidates for the ministry, including preaching before a congregation, but this passage was not formally quoted this session as it falls outside the schools/universities material that was the focus of this pass.
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): Grammar-school pupils were examined quarterly by the ministers, elders, and "the best learned in everie toun." University candidates were tried by examiners deputed by the Rector and Principals before admission to a class, and again at the end of a course of study before being "graduat" (see C8). [scotland-fbd-q012, scotland-fbd-q028]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): Successful candidates were made "Laureat and Gradiiat in Philosophie" after the first college's three years; graduates in Medicine, Law, and Divinity are likewise described as being "graduat" in their subject after examination. On completing the whole course (to age twenty-four), the student was "removed to serve the Churche or Commoun-wealth," unless retained as a Reader in the same college. [scotland-fbd-q016, scotland-fbd-q025]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): No timetable of daily or weekly hours is given; the document allots years, not hours, per subject (see C1). NOT FOUND as a table (searched: laing1848-fbd).

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: laing1848-fbd). Nothing in the Schools/Universities section fetched this session describes a daily order for pupils or bursars.

[scotland-fbd-q020]
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): NOT FOUND specifically for schools or universities in the material fetched this session (searched: laing1848-fbd); the document's Second Head concerns sacraments generally but was not examined for school-specific worship provisions this session.
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): Each college's principal-figure held a weekly ("oulklie") assembly of the whole college "for punischement of crymes." Household servants of the college — steward, cook, gardener, porter — were "subject to discipline of the Principale, as the rest" (i.e., as the students were). [scotland-fbd-q023, scotland-fbd-q024]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: laing1848-fbd).
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): Poor children's education was to be borne "on the charge of the Churche." A detailed schedule of one-time entrance and graduation fees, graduated by the father's rank, is given: an earl's son was to pay forty shillings at entry and the same again at each graduation; a lord's son eighty shillings; a free baron's son twenty shillings; a substantial gentleman's son one mark; a substantial husbandman's or burgess's son ten shillings; everyone else (except bursars) five shillings — collected into a common box for building and upkeep. Bursars themselves were maintained "onlie in meit" at the college's charge. [scotland-fbd-q011, scotland-fbd-q020, scotland-fbd-q021]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): The plan specifies twenty-four bursars per college, giving a fixed total of seventy-two bursars for St Andrews (three colleges), and forty-eight each for Glasgow and Aberdeen (two colleges each) — these are the subsidized-poor-student quotas proposed for 1560, not enrollment figures actually achieved (the document is a proposal; Wikipedia states, uncited to a primary source here, that the funding for the wider educational programme was in fact rejected — see S3/M5 and Discrepancies). [scotland-fbd-q020; funding-rejection point SECONDARY: wikipedia-book-of-discipline]

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): Named reader posts at St Andrews' first college: Dialectic, Mathematics, Natural Philosophy, Medicine; second college: Ethics/Economics/Politics, two readers in Law; third college: Hebrew, Greek, two readers in Divinity. Below university level, parish schoolmasters and (in notable towns) college masters teaching Logic, Rhetoric, and "the Tongues." Non-teaching college staff (subject to the same discipline as students) comprised a steward, cook, gardener, and porter per college. [scotland-fbd-q008, scotland-fbd-q009, scotland-fbd-q024]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): Each college's head officer was to "dalie hearkin the dyet comptis" (daily hear the accounts of provisions), take weekly (with a Reader or Regent joined to him) oversight of the readers' and regents' diligence "alsweill in thair reading" as in exercising the youth in what was taught, oversee the good order and upkeep of the place, and hold a weekly assembly for the punishment of offenses. [scotland-fbd-q022, scotland-fbd-q023]
- **T3 Forms of teacher–student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): Readers lectured/read their subject to a fixed class over a fixed term of years; personal, face-to-face examination by deputed examiners gated promotion between classes; the weekly convention of "the haill memberis of the Colledge" (T2 above) was a further collective point of contact, as was the head officer's oversight visits to each reader's teaching. [scotland-fbd-q019, scotland-fbd-q022, scotland-fbd-q023]

No individually named teacher or student example, with a described interaction, was located in the material fetched this session; the document sets out offices and procedures, not named case-histories.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "Off necessitie it is that your Hoiiouris be most cairfull for the virtuous educatioun, and godlie up-bringing of the youth of this Realme" — addressed directly to the civil authorities as a condition of the "advancement of Christis glorie" and the "continewance of his benefits to the generatioun following." [scotland-fbd-q007]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): True preaching of the Evangel in every kirk (doctrinal purity, F8); and, distinctively for the schools material, universal and needs-blind access to education: "The riche and potent may not be permitted to suffer thair children to spend thair youth in vane idilnes", while "The children of the poore must be supported and sustenit on the charge of the Churche" and tested for aptitude regardless of their parents' means. [scotland-fbd-q005, scotland-fbd-q010, scotland-fbd-q011]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Idleness among the children of the rich — being suffered "to spend thair youth in vane idilnes, as heirtofore thei have done" — which the document says must instead be corrected by exhortation and, if necessary, "the censure of the Churche." [scotland-fbd-q010]

## S — Sending
