**Nathanael Saint** (30 August 1923 – 8 January 1956) was an [evangelical Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism "Evangelicalism") [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary "Missionary") [pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator "Aviator") who, along with four others, was killed in [Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador "Ecuador") while attempting to [evangelize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism "Evangelism") the [Huaorani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaorani_people "Huaorani people") people through efforts known as [Operation Auca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Auca "Operation Auca").

## Early life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nate_Saint&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Saint was born in 1923. When he was seven he took his first plane ride with his brother Sam, who would eventually become a commercial pilot for [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines "American Airlines"). While in the airplane he discovered a love of flying. His family was somewhat unusual. His brothers made a sleeping patio on the roof of their home, and [his father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Saint "Lawrence Saint") built a roller coaster in the backyard. When he joined the army he was stationed in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas "Las Vegas"), [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada "Nevada"), but was transferred to several other locations over the years. A leg injury caused him some problems while he was in the army. About a year before he was discharged, he nearly died while climbing a mountain in [Yosemite National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosemite_National_Park "Yosemite National Park").[1]

## Becoming a missionary

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nate_Saint&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Becoming a missionary")]

When Saint was asked by a friend to fix a plane somewhere in Mexico, he agreed. After he repaired the plane, he discovered a need for his skill in the field of mission work and also a new awareness of the value of missions. After going to [Wheaton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheaton_College_\(Illinois\) "Wheaton College \(Illinois\)"), Saint married Marjorie Farris (commonly called Marj) in 1948 and eventually moved to [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell,_Ecuador "Shell, Ecuador"), [Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador "Ecuador"). Here, Saint built his family a house which would also serve as a guesthouse and a radio center with the other missionaries.

## Operation Auca

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nate_Saint&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Operation Auca")]

In September 1955, Saint was joined by his teammates, [Jim Elliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Elliot "Jim Elliot"), [Ed McCully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_McCully "Ed McCully"), [Pete Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Fleming "Pete Fleming"), and [Roger Youderian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Youderian "Roger Youderian"). Saint finally found a Huaorani (also known as Auca) settlement while searching by air. To reach the tribe, Saint and the team lowered gifts to the Huaorani in a [bucket tied to the plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pylon_turn "Pylon turn"). The team decided to try to meet the Huaorani on the ground; and, on January 3, 1956, using the beach as a landing strip, they set up camp four miles from the Huaorani settlement. Their initial contact was encouraging; however, on Sunday, January 8, 1956, the entire team was killed on the beach (known as "Palm Beach") when armed Huaorani met and speared them.[2] Saint was the third of the five missionaries to be speared to death.[3]

Saint and the other four men became famous worldwide as a result. _[Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_\(magazine\) "Life \(magazine\)")_ magazine published a photo essay on the story,[4] which was also covered in _[Reader's Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader's_Digest "Reader's Digest")_ and many other publications. A small school for missionary children in Shell, Ecuador, bore Saint's name for 51 years until the school closed in 2017 due to falling enrollment.[5]

[Rachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Saint "Rachel Saint"), Saint's sister, continued the mission efforts to the Huaorani. This resulted in many of these natives becoming Christians, including those who had killed Saint.[2]

In 1966, Marjorie (Marj) Farris Saint married Abe Van Der Puy, president of [HCJB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HCJB "HCJB") World Radio. Van Der Puy died in 2003, and Marj died in 2004, from cancer. She is buried in Highland Memorial Park, south of Ocala, Florida.

Saint's older son, Steve, spent time as a child visiting his missionary family members and friends and getting to know the Huaorani. Steve was baptized by [Mincaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mincaye "Mincaye"), who was the very man who killed his father but later converted. As of 2006, Steve Saint works with the Huaorani people and travels around the world, preaching the gospel, often accompanied by Mincaye.[6] A documentary based on the story, _Beyond Gates of Splendor_ , was released in 2002. In 2006, a feature film, _[End of the Spear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_Spear "End of the Spear")_ , was released on January 20, a week and a half after the 50-year anniversary of the killings. Steve Saint also helped write _Jungle Pilot_ , based on his father's diary about his time in Ecuador and work with the Huaorani Indians. [Steven Curtis Chapman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Curtis_Chapman "Steven Curtis Chapman") wrote the song "No Greater Love" from his album _[Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_\(Steven_Curtis_Chapman_album\) "Declaration \(Steven Curtis Chapman album\)")_ as a tribute to Saint and his fellow missionaries.
