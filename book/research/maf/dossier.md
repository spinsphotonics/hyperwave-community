# Dossier — Missionary Aviation Fellowship (`maf`)

**Coverage note:** No digitized 1945 CAMF/MAF incorporation document or founding statement of
purpose was located and fetched this session. Russell Hitt's *Jungle Pilot* (1959), the
priority in-copyright biography of Nate Saint named for this institution, was located in four
separate Internet Archive editions but every attempt to fetch its OCR text (via the specified
`_djvu.txt` URL pattern) returned HTTP 401 — all four are under Internet Archive's controlled
digital lending, BLOCKED to this session's unauthenticated fetch. The Billy Graham Center
Archives' own MAF finding aid (Collection 176, Wheaton College) was likewise BLOCKED by a
Cloudflare Turnstile gate. In their place, this dossier rests on MAF's own institutional
website (US, UK/international, and Australian branches), Wikipedia's independently footnoted
accident record, and — most valuably — a Wheaton College Archives staff-authored blog post
that directly quotes, with archival folder citations, several of Betty Greene's own letters
and a 1944 magazine article from the same collection Jungle Pilot and the finding aid could
not supply. The Charter section reproduces MAF-US's *current* Vision, Mission, and Statement
of Faith, explicitly flagged as a present-day substitute.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Founded in 1945 under a
  name recorded three ways across the sources fetched this session — "Christian Airman's
  Missionary Service" (per one account), "Christian Airmen's Missionary Fellowship" (CAMF, per
  two others) — before adopting the name "Mission Aviation Fellowship" later in 1945 or 1946;
  see discrepancies.md #2. [quote_ids: maf-q011, maf-q036, maf-q038, maf-q044, maf-q049]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): "The organization was
  officially incorporated on May 20, 1945, under the leadership of Jim Truxton" in Los
  Angeles. The UK/international branch's own history separately describes "the registration of
  MAF in London in 1945" without a specific date; see discrepancies.md #1. The first flight
  under the new organization was piloted by Betty Greene on 23 February 1946, from Los Angeles
  to Mexico City. [quote_ids: maf-q007, maf-q036, maf-q043, maf-q044]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): Jim Truxton, Grady Parrott, Charlie
  Mellis, and Betty Greene are named as the founding US group, with Truxton as first leader
  ("MAF President"); Murray Kendon (New Zealand/UK) and Edwin Hartwig (Australia) independently
  founded parallel national missionary-aviation organizations that later joined the same
  fellowship — see discrepancies.md #3. Betty Greene "held the distinction of being CAMS' first
  pilot and full-time employee." [quote_ids: maf-q003, maf-q005, maf-q006, maf-q010, maf-q036,
  maf-q038, maf-q049, maf-q050]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Los Angeles, California (US founding, 1945; office space "donated by
  Dawson Trotman, president of The Navigators"); London (UK registration, 1945); Melbourne,
  Australia (Australian founders meeting at Melbourne Bible Institute, leading to formal
  establishment 30 June 1947). [quote_ids: maf-q013, maf-q036, maf-q043]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND
  (searched: maf-statement-of-purpose-1945 [no hit]; hitt-jungle-pilot-1959 [BLOCKED, HTTP
  401]; wheaton-maf-collection176 [BLOCKED, Cloudflare gate]). No 1945 constitution,
  incorporation paper, or period doctrinal statement was located and fetched this session.
  `charter_abridged.md` substitutes MAF-US's current Vision/Mission statement and Statement of
  Faith, document_ids `maf-vision-mission` and `maf-statement-of-faith`, explicitly flagged as
  not the founding-era document.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The organization's founding self-
  description, recorded by the Wheaton Archives blog, was as "a servant of missions" — "a
  nondenominational service agency to evangelical Christian missions, both denominational and
  independent," providing "air transportation, ambulance and medical service, and supply lines
  to remote frontier stations." No source read this session attributes a specific sentence of
  this description to a single named founder as a direct quotation. [quote_id: maf-q037]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): "After World War II, a few
  veteran pilots in the US and UK dreamed of a way they could use their flying skills to reach
  isolated people with the gospel." MAF International's own account frames the same need more
  broadly: "During World War II Christian servicemen and women from around the world
  independently had the vision to use aircraft for good, to reach people living in
  isolation." [quote_ids: maf-q001, maf-q010, maf-q042]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No 1945-era doctrinal
  statement was located. MAF-US's current Statement of Faith affirms, among seven numbered
  points, "the divine inspiration of the Bible; that it is inerrant in the original writings,
  and is of supreme and final authority in faith and practice," and "the spiritual unity of all
  believers as comprising the true Church, which has the duty to preach the Gospel to every
  creature" — reproduced in full in `charter_abridged.md` and flagged there as a present-day
  document only. [quote_ids: maf-q047, maf-q048]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO):
  MAF's earliest flight served Wycliffe Bible Translators directly, and Betty Greene's second
  flight carried Wycliffe founder Cameron Townsend himself to his own jungle training camp near
  Tuxtla Gutierrez, Mexico — an immediate operational link to another institution covered
  elsewhere in this book. Dawson Trotman of the Navigators donated the new mission's first
  office space. The UK organization drew members (Murray Kendon, Trevor Strong, Stuart King,
  Jack Hemmings) independently of the US founders before the two merged efforts. [quote_ids:
  maf-q008, maf-q036, maf-q050]

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files) as a formal
  founding-era rule. MAF's own work is aviation service, not a school in the ordinary sense of
  this book's other institutions; pilot licensure and mechanical training would have been
  practical prerequisites, but no source states a formal admission standard.
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): NOT FOUND
  (searched: all text/ files) as a stated formal rule for the founding era.
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): Betty Greene "held the distinction of being
  CAMS' first pilot and full-time employee," flying MAF's inaugural flight in February 1946
  and serving "as an MAF pilot for 16 years, flying in 12 countries." Her own 1966 account,
  however, records that the organization was "consistently hesitant to recruit women pilots":
  "as a very definite general rule MAF does not accept women to do as I have done," citing
  practical objections about mechanical training, cargo handling, and the danger of solo
  remote deployments for women. Marjorie Farris Saint, Nate Saint's wife, is separately
  attested as serving alongside her husband at the Shell Mera, Ecuador base from 1948, where
  their children were born. [quote_ids: maf-q009, maf-q024, maf-q038, maf-q040]
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all
  text/ files) as a formal rule. Several founders brought prior military flying experience
  (Truxton, Greene, and others as World War II pilots), attested as a shared background rather
  than a stated admission requirement. [quote_id: maf-q001]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): Not applicable in the ordinary sense; MAF is an operating
  aviation service, not a school with a fixed course of study. NOT FOUND (searched: all text/
  files) as a training-course length.
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files) as a formal
  founding-era curriculum. What is attested is field-invented technique: Nate Saint's own
  "bucket drop technique" for delivering supplies to an uncontacted people without landing —
  "he dropped a bucket from the plane and flew in circles around it while the bucket stayed
  steady" — represents the kind of practical, self-taught skill the mission's pilots developed
  in the field rather than a classroom subject. [quote_id: maf-q026]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files) as a
  formal requirement. The Operation Auca team is attested as having spent "months praying and
  planning the endeavor, including learning some key Waodani phrases from a Waodani woman named
  Dayuma who had fled from the tribe" — field-specific language preparation for a single
  mission, not a general MAF curriculum requirement.
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): The clearest attested
  practical instruction is technical and improvised rather than doctrinal: Nate Saint's bucket-
  drop delivery method, and the multi-week gift-drop approach used to build trust with the
  Waodani before any attempt at landed contact ("for thirteen weeks, Nate dropped off items like
  machetes, salt, and a metal kettle from the airplane"). [quote_ids: maf-q026, maf-q031]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  all text/ files). MAF confers no degree; standard aviation pilot licensure would have applied
  but is not described as an MAF-specific certificate.

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/
  files); MAF is a field-based aviation service without a residential daily order comparable to
  a seminary's.
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): The founding organization's first
  home was donated office space in Los Angeles; field missionaries lived at mission bases they
  themselves built, such as Nate and Marjorie Saint's house at Shell Mera, Ecuador, which
  "would also serve as a guesthouse and a radio center with the other missionaries." [quote_id:
  maf-q036; context at maf-nate-saint]
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/ files).
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all text/
  files) as a formal founding-era policy; MAF's early operation depended on donated space (the
  Navigators' Dawson Trotman) and, presumably, donor support, though no source states a formal
  support principle.
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): Not applicable in the school sense; MAF is a
  service organization. As an organizational-size proxy: by 2010, "MAF served in more than 55
  countries, flying 201,710 passengers with a fleet of some 130 aircraft," and current pages
  report a fleet of "135 airplanes" serving "37 different countries."
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): Betty Greene's own 1946
  correspondence records the physical danger of the work directly: "I have to face the question
  of whether or not I will carry a firearm with me... when in jungle country where animal life
  is dangerous and food scarce." [quote_id: maf-q039]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Betty Greene's own words on
  her wartime pilot training frame the mission's whole logic: "The best flight training the
  world had to offer women, which Uncle Sam paid me to take, was not unworthy preparation for a
  would-be missionary servant of Christ." MAF-US's current Vision statement restates the same
  aim in a single sentence: "To see isolated people changed by the love of Christ." [quote_ids:
  maf-q035, maf-q045]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): The distinctive stress across every source read this session is
  technical service in support of others' evangelism, not evangelism or preaching by MAF's own
  staff as such: "MAF would provide air transportation, ambulance and medical service, and
  supply lines to remote frontier stations." [quote_ids: maf-q002, maf-q037]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT FOUND as a direct
  founders' statement of rejection. Betty Greene's own later account of MAF's caution about
  women pilots is the closest attested statement of an internal boundary the organization set
  for itself, though framed as a practical judgment rather than a doctrinal warning. [quote_id:
  maf-q040]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): No founding-era motto was located.
  MAF-US's current Mission statement — "Serving together to bring help, hope, and healing
  through aviation" — is the closest available watchword-like phrase, undated to 1945.
  [quote_id: maf-q046]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): Following the 1956
  Ecuador killings, MAF's own retrospective states that "family members of those killed
  returned to Ecuador and ministered to the tribe, and some of the men who killed the
  missionaries eventually converted to Christianity" — presented, decades later, as a
  continuation rather than an interruption of the founding vision. [quote_ids: maf-q016,
  maf-q028, maf-q033]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)**
  (PO): MAF sent its own pilots directly as an operating agency rather than through a licensing
  or commendation process comparable to other institutions in this book: Betty Greene's own
  first flight, in February 1946, "transport[ed] two missionaries of Wycliffe Bible
  Translators," and Nate Saint, for eight years, "flew medical supplies, supported missionaries,
  and helped isolated villages" as MAF's own field pilot. The five men killed in 1956 belonged
  to three different sending agencies (MAF for Saint; Gospel Missionary Union for Youderian; the
  Plymouth Brethren mission for Elliot, McCully, and Fleming), meeting in Ecuador rather than
  being sent by a single body. [quote_ids: maf-q007, maf-q025, maf-q030]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): "The earliest MAF fields of service were
  Mexico, Peru, and Ecuador. Over the years, the organization expanded to serve many countries
  of Africa, Latin America, Asia, and Eurasia." The UK branch's first survey flights covered
  "central and east Africa" (1948), with its "first programme... established in Sudan" in 1950.
  [quote_ids: maf-q012, maf-q043]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): No founding-decade sending count is given.
  Betty Greene alone "served as an MAF pilot for 16 years, flying in 12 countries and touching
  down in some 20 more." By 2010, MAF flew "201,710 passengers" across "more than 55 countries"
  with "some 130 aircraft." [quote_id: maf-q009]
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND
  (searched: all text/ files) as a stated formal principle.
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): On 6 August 1951, MAF
  Australia co-founder Edwin "Harry" Hartwig "was killed when his aircraft crashed in the
  Asaroka Pass" in New Guinea. On 8 January 1956, MAF pilot Nate Saint and four other
  missionaries — Jim Elliot, Ed McCully, Roger Youderian, and Pete Fleming — "were martyred on a
  beach in Ecuador" by Waodani warriors; "Nate Saint's watch stopped at 3:12 p.m." A 1967 flight
  in Papua New Guinea vanished with no trace found despite a ten-day search; a 1968 crash in the
  Philippines killed the pilot and passenger; a 1969 crash killed missionaries whose ten-year-old
  son survived; a 1994 Twin Otter crash in Papua New Guinea killed all 28 aboard; a 2005 crash
  killed both pilots; a 2006 crash killed the pilot; an October 2008 crash killed the pilot; and
  on 12 May 2020, pilot Joyce Lin was killed when her aircraft crashed in Lake Sentani,
  Indonesia. [quote_ids: maf-q014, maf-q015, maf-q017, maf-q018, maf-q019, maf-q020, maf-q027,
  maf-q032]
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  all text/ files).

## R — Fruit
- **R1-R4 Up to six named alumni or missionaries: name, dates, field, one-sentence outcome,
  source** (PS):
  1. **Betty Greene** (1920-1997), MAF's first pilot and full-time employee, flew the
     organization's inaugural flight in February 1946, served sixteen years in the field across
     twelve countries, and later worked in recruitment and advocacy at MAF headquarters until
     her death. [quote_ids: maf-q003, maf-q009, maf-q038, maf-q041]
  2. **Nate Saint** (1923-1956), MAF pilot at Shell Mera, Ecuador, from 1948, invented the
     "bucket drop" contact technique, and was martyred with four other missionaries on 8
     January 1956 while attempting to reach the Waodani people. [quote_ids: maf-q021, maf-q024,
     maf-q026, maf-q027]
  3. **Edwin "Harry" Hartwig**, a founder of MAF Australia and a World War II pilot, established
     MAF service in New Guinea in 1951 and was killed there in an aircraft crash that same
     year. [quote_id: maf-q014]
  4. **Joyce Lin**, an MAF pilot, was killed in a 2020 crash in Lake Sentani, Indonesia, while
     carrying cargo on a solo flight — the most recent named casualty found this session.
     [quote_id: maf-q020]
