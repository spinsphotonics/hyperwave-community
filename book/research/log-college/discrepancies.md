# Discrepancies — The Log College (`log-college`)

Count: 3.

## 1. William Tennent Sr.'s year of arrival in America: 1716 or 1718?

- **Source A:** alexander1845, p. 14 (quote_id log-college-q014): "In the Memoir of William Tennent, jr., it is said that his father arrived in America in the year 1718."
- **Source B:** alexander1845, p. 14 (same passage, quote_id log-college-q014): "but in the sketch of the life of Gilbert Tennent, in the Assembly's Magazine, for May, 1805, 'that he came over in 1716,' which last is believed to be the more accurate statement."

Alexander himself records both figures and states a preference for 1716, but does not resolve the disagreement with a primary document (no ship record, letter, or Synod minute is cited for either year). Both values are recorded here rather than silently adopting Alexander's own preference. Note this predates the Log College's own founding (c.1726-27) by roughly a decade either way, so it does not bear directly on the founding date itself.

## 2. William Tennent Sr.'s year of death: 1743 or 1746?

- **Source A:** alexander1845, p. 22 (quote_id log-college-q015): "It is stated in the sketch of the life of Gilbert Tennent, in the Assembly's Magazine, that the Rev. William Tennent, sen., died in the year 1743."
- **Source B:** alexander1845, p. 22 (same passage, quote_id log-college-q015): "but this is not correct; for we find a record in the minutes of the New Brunswick Presbytery for the year 1746... 'It is reported to the Presbytery that Mr. William Tennent, sen., deceased, since our last.' The exact date of his death was May 6, 1746, aged 73." Alexander states this exact date (May 6, 1746) was given to him directly by the Rev. Dr. Samuel Miller, who had it "transcribed... from his tombstone."

Here Alexander does adjudicate between the two figures, and cites a documentary source (the New Brunswick Presbytery's minute book) plus an eyewitness transcription of the tombstone for his preferred date. Both values are nonetheless recorded per Rule 9, since this dossier does not independently verify either the Presbytery minute book or the tombstone. 1746 is markedly better attested (two independent lines of evidence Alexander names) than 1743 (an uncited magazine sketch).

## 3. The Whitefield journal's own printed date for the Neshaminy visit: "November 11" (as OCR'd) vs. "22"

- **Source A:** whitefield-journal5-1739, p. 43-44 (quote_id log-college-q001): the Internet Archive OCR of the 1740 printed journal renders the entry's dateline as "Thmfday, Ncvnnhcr 11."
- **Source B:** alexander1845, p. 18-19 (quote_id log-college-q012), independently quoting the same journal entry from (evidently) a cleaner copy or a more careful eye: "Nov. 22. [1739.]"

This is treated in this dossier as an OCR error rather than a genuine discrepancy between two sources: the dateline in Source A falls between an entry explicitly dated "Wednesday..., November 21" and one explicitly dated "Friday, November 23" in the same document, so the entry itself can only be Thursday the 22nd; "11" is not a coherent date in that sequence. Source B, an independent 1845 transcription of the same passage, gives "22" directly. Both readings are recorded here per Rule 9; the chapter follows the internally consistent and independently corroborated date, 22 November 1739, and text/whitefield-journal5-1739.txt carries a note on the OCR misreading at the point where it occurs. No fact in this dossier is built on "November 11" as a date.

## Not treated as a discrepancy: the exact founding year of the Log College itself (1726 vs "c.1726-27")

No source read this session states 1727 or any other single year for the opening itself; the roster's "c.1726-27" appears to allow for uncertainty about the exact point within that window (call received vs. building erected vs. first pupil), not a conflict between named sources.
