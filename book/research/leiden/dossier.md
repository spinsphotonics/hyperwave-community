# Dossier — Leiden (`leiden`)

Note on sourcing: no full primary charter or statute text was obtained this session (the
Molhuysen transcriptions at resources.huygens.knaw.nl are BLOCKED by network egress policy;
see discrepancies.md item 5). All facts below come from secondary sources: Leiden University's
own official history pages (which reproduce short primary phrases verbatim), a Leiden Special
Collections Blog post by a former curator, a biographical article on Antonius Walaeus that
quotes short passages from the Seminarium Indicum's Ordinances and a 1625 school order, and two
Dutch Wikipedia articles used for background/orientation and cited as SECONDARY per the
Extractor procedure's allowance for PRIMARY-OR-SECONDARY fields. As discrepancies.md item 1
explains, the roster's single `leiden` entry in fact covers three related but distinct bodies:
Leiden University (1575), the Statencollege (1592), and the Seminarium Indicum (1622-1633).
This dossier treats all three where the sources allow, distinguishing them throughout.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Leiden University (Universiteit Leiden), founded 1575. Within it, the Statencollege (Staten Collegie; in Latin, Collegium Theologicum), founded 1592, a residential college for theology students. Separately, the Seminarium Indicum ("Indian Seminary"), founded 1622, closed 1633. [leiden-q024 SECONDARY: statencollege-nlwiki; leiden-q027 SECONDARY: indisch-seminarium-nlwiki]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): The University's charter is dated 6 January 1575, though it was only drawn up after the University's inauguration on 8 February 1575. The Statencollege was founded in 1592. The Seminarium Indicum was opened by Walaeus in his own home in 1622 and existed until 1633. [leiden-q001, leiden-q024 SECONDARY, leiden-q018, leiden-q027 SECONDARY]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PS): The University's charter, though drawn up on the advice of William of Orange to the States of Holland, bears the seal and signature of Philip II of Spain, not of Orange; the statutes were signed by William of Orange. Jan van Hout (1542-1609), city secretary of Leiden and secretary of the University, performed the Statencollege's 1592 opening. Antonius Walaeus (1573-1639), Leiden professor of theology, was sole rector of the Seminarium Indicum throughout its existence. [leiden-q002, leiden-q003, leiden-q026 SECONDARY, leiden-q027 SECONDARY]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Leiden, in the province of Holland. The Statencollege occupied the former Cellebroederklooster on the Cellebroedersgracht (present-day Kaiserstraat 13). The Seminarium Indicum's classes and lodging were in Walaeus's own house on the Rapenburg. [SECONDARY: statencollege-nlwiki; SECONDARY: indisch-seminarium-nlwiki — background facts, not independently quoted this session]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): For the University: a charter (Dutch, dated 6 January 1575) and statutes (Dutch and Latin, signed by William of Orange), both transcribed by P.C. Molhuysen in *Bronnen tot de geschiedenis der Leidsche universiteit 1574-1811* (1913) — neither the charter nor the statutes text itself was obtained this session (BLOCKED, see discrepancies.md item 5); document_id for the transcriptions: `leiden-charter-molhuysen-transcription` and `leiden-statutes-molhuysen-transcription` (both unread). For the Seminarium Indicum: an "Ordinances" document is referred to but not independently located; short passages from it are quoted at second hand in the Deddens article. [leiden-q003, leiden-q004, leiden-q005, leiden-q016]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): William of Orange described the proposed university to the States of Holland and Zeeland in 1574 as "Een vast blochuys ende bewaernisse der gantscher landen" — "a firm stronghold for the whole country," per the University's own translation. According to tradition, he founded the University to reward the citizens of Leiden for their resistance to the Spanish. [leiden-q008, leiden-q009, leiden-q006]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): For the University, tradition holds it rewarded Leiden's resistance during the Spanish siege; William of Orange, as stadtholder, judged Leiden a suitable place for the first university of the Northern Netherlands. For the Statencollege, the States of Holland and West-Friesland acted "met het oog op het nijpende tekort aan predikanten in de jonge, groeiende Nederduits Gereformeerde Kerk" ("with a view to the pressing shortage of ministers in the young, growing Dutch Reformed Church"). For the Seminarium Indicum, the classis of Delft judged in 1614 that a college for training missionaries was necessary, and the VOC's first governor-general, Jan Pietersz. Coen, complained of a lack of ministers for the church in the East Indies. [leiden-q006, leiden-q007, leiden-q025 SECONDARY (translation NEW-DRAFT), leiden-q014, leiden-q015]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: leiden-foundation-documents, leiden-founding-royal-family, leiden-1592-curriculum-blog, walaeus-christianstudylibrary, statencollege-nlwiki, indisch-seminarium-nlwiki) — no passage located this session states a specific confession subscribed by students or faculty at Leiden, the Statencollege, or the Seminarium Indicum, though the Reformed character of all three is assumed throughout the sources read.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents) — no passage located this session states that the founders of Leiden, the Statencollege, or the Seminarium Indicum explicitly cited an earlier school as a model. Walaeus himself had studied at Geneva under Theodore Beza, per the Deddens article, but this is Walaeus's personal education, not a stated institutional model, and the sentence describing it was not itself formalized as a quote this session (see follow-up note under R below).

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents) for the University or Statencollege specifically. For the Seminarium Indicum, admission followed a full theological course at the University's faculty of theology (Walaeus's own proposal called for twenty separate courses before a candidate could be sent out), implying substantial prior study, though no specific entrance requirement is stated. [leiden-q017]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): NOT FOUND for entrance specifically. Wednesdays and Saturdays at the University were reserved for disputations and examinations generally. [leiden-q013]
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents). Seminarium Indicum students were explicitly barred from independent preaching before completion: they "were not allowed to take an examination on their own account, nor to speak an edifying word in the villages." This is a restriction during training, not a prior-experience requirement, and is recorded under L4 below. [leiden-q019]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): NOT FOUND for the University generally or the Statencollege. The Seminarium Indicum operated for ten years in total (1622-1633) under Walaeus, though this is the institution's lifespan, not an individual student's course length, which is NOT FOUND. [leiden-q018, leiden-q021]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): At the University, the 1592 printed lecture timetable (series lectionum) lists lectures by the hierarchy of the four faculties — theology, law, medicine, and letters — with named examples: the theology professor Lucas Trelcatius lectured on the first epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians at nine in the morning, and the law professor Everardus Bronchorst on the fourth book of the Codex at two in the afternoon. Walaeus's proposal for the Seminarium Indicum called for twenty separate courses, including theology and philosophy, interspersed with prayer, fasting, visitation of the poor, and the practice of piety; the Ordinances required instruction, as far as possible, in the religion of the "heathen" and of Islam, and, without delay, in the customs of the "heathen countries" and in the Malay language. [leiden-q011, leiden-q012, leiden-q017, leiden-q016]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): The University's lecture timetable was issued twice yearly, one series for the summer months (March-October) and one for the winter (November-February), and organized by the four faculties in hierarchical order (ordinary then extraordinary professors). [leiden-q010, leiden-q011]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Latin was evidently the medium of instruction generally (theology, law lectures as above). At the Statencollege's 1592 opening, Jan van Hout stressed that the incoming theologians, who had to master Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, should also apply themselves to Dutch, which their future pastoral duties required — this fact is SECONDARY (Dutch Wikipedia, not independently quoted this session; noted here for completeness but not supported by a formal quote_id). The Seminarium Indicum's Ordinances required instruction in the Malay language. [leiden-q016; SECONDARY: statencollege-nlwiki, narrative not formally quoted]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): The first epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians (theology, Trelcatius); the fourth book of the Codex (law, Bronchorst); the Nemean Odes of Pindar and the Moralia of Plutarch (Greek, Bonaventura Vulcanius) — all from the 1592 series lectionum. [leiden-q012; NOT FOUND for Vulcanius as a formal quote this session — noted from raw text, not independently formalized]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): Seminarium Indicum students were barred from preaching "an edifying word in the villages" on their own account during training, implying that supervised preaching practice, if it existed, was withheld until later; no description of an actual preaching exercise was found. [leiden-q019]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): At the University, Wednesdays and Saturdays were reserved for disputations, examinations, and professors' private lectures. Seminarium Indicum students could not "take an examination on their own account." [leiden-q013, leiden-q019]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents) in a directly quoted passage; fourteen of twenty-five Seminarium Indicum students are reported (SECONDARY, Dutch Wikipedia) to have become ministers (predikant), twelve of them in VOC service, implying some form of ordination or licensing followed training, but the mechanism is not described. [leiden-q028 SECONDARY]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): The University's printed series lectionum enumerated lectures to the hour, four ordinary lecture days (Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday) with Wednesday and Saturday for disputations, examinations, and private lectures. No comparable daily timetable for the Statencollege or the Seminarium Indicum was found. [leiden-q010, leiden-q011, leiden-q013]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): Statencollege students boarded at the college itself (the former Cellebroederklooster), at state or city expense. Seminarium Indicum students lodged in Walaeus's own house on the Rapenburg; "sometimes they had six students living with them in their house." [leiden-q018; SECONDARY: statencollege-nlwiki for the Statencollege detail, not independently quoted this session]
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): Seminarium Indicum students "were not allowed to speak any language but Latin. Wine, beer, and liquor, as well as the smoking of tobacco were forbidden, 'unless under doctor's order and with consent of the governor.' The students were not allowed to take an examination on their own account, nor to speak an edifying word in the villages. Much less were they permitted to keep company with girls or to get engaged." [leiden-q019]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): See L4 (Latin-only speech, no alcohol or tobacco without medical/governor's leave, no courtship). [leiden-q019]
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): Statencollege students were given "kost, inwoning en onderwijs" (food, lodging, and education) at state or city expense, so that parents lacking means could send talented sons to Leiden — this fact is SECONDARY (Dutch Wikipedia) and was not independently formalized as a quote this session. NOT FOUND in a formal quote_id for this field.
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): Twenty-five persons are recorded as having studied at the Seminarium Indicum over its 1622-1633 existence. [leiden-q028 SECONDARY]
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): The University's 1592 lecture timetable names, among others, Lucas Trelcatius (theology), Everardus Bronchorst (law), and Bonaventura Vulcanius (Greek). Antonius Walaeus was sole rector of the Seminarium Indicum for its entire ten-year existence, teaching from his own home; Sebastiaan Danckert, a former VOC minister, also taught there (SECONDARY, not independently quoted). [leiden-q012, leiden-q018, leiden-q027 SECONDARY]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): NOT FOUND in a document identified as the charter itself (BLOCKED, see discrepancies.md item 5).
- **T3 Forms of teacher–student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence, preaching class)** (PO): At the University: lecture, disputation, and examination, on a fixed weekly schedule. At the Seminarium Indicum: Walaeus lectured to, lodged, and personally regulated a small number of resident students in his own household. [leiden-q011, leiden-q013, leiden-q018, leiden-q019]
- **T4 Named examples of teacher–student interaction with source** (PS): Walaeus's household arrangement, with "sometimes six students living with them in their house," is the clearest named example. [leiden-q018]
- **T5 Teacher:student ratio if computable (show the two numbers and years)** (PS): At the Seminarium Indicum, one rector (Walaeus) taught up to six resident students at a time, out of twenty-five total students over 1622-1633. [leiden-q018, leiden-q028 SECONDARY]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The 1625 school order for the Leiden Latin School, which Walaeus helped design, opened with the words "Piety must be the beginning and the end of all study." In his rectorial addresses at the University, Walaeus stressed four conditions for every student: moderation, modesty, reverence for teachers, and piety. [leiden-q022, leiden-q023]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): For the Seminarium Indicum specifically: instruction in the religion of the "heathen" and of Islam, in local customs, and in the Malay language, alongside theology — a deliberate stress on knowing the mission field before preaching in it. [leiden-q016]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The Seminarium Indicum's discipline rules implicitly reject idleness, drink, tobacco without cause, unsupervised preaching, and courtship among students in training. [leiden-q019]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): "Piety must be the beginning and the end of all study" — the opening words of the 1625 Leiden Latin School order, quoted in the Deddens article; this is a school-order motto, not stated to be the University's or the Statencollege's own motto. [leiden-q022]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The classis of Delft first proposed, in 1614, a training college for missionaries; the VOC (the East Indies Trading Company), under pressure from the classes and the complaints of governor-general Jan Pietersz. Coen, ultimately established and financed the Seminarium Indicum, and VOC service was the destination of most of its ordained graduates. [leiden-q014, leiden-q015, leiden-q020, leiden-q028 SECONDARY]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): The Dutch East Indies generally; Dutch Wikipedia specifies Batavia, another part of Java, Ambon, Dutch Formosa (Taiwan), the Banda Islands, and the Coromandel coast, for the twelve VOC-serving ministers among the seminary's graduates. [leiden-q028 SECONDARY]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): Twenty-five students studied at the Seminarium Indicum over 1622-1633; six did not complete their studies; fourteen became ministers, of whom twelve entered VOC service (Dutch Wikipedia). The Deddens article states, independently, that Walaeus "delivered to the churches in the Dutch East Indies a dozen ministers of the Word." The two figures corroborate one another (see discrepancies.md item 4). [leiden-q028 SECONDARY, leiden-q020]
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: all six documents).
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): The Seminarium Indicum's "Ordinances" (title and date not independently established; referred to only at second hand) required its graduates to be instructed, before being sent, in the religion of the "heathen" and of Islam and in the Malay language. [leiden-q016]
