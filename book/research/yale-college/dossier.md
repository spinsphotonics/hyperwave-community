# Dossier — Yale College (`yale-college`)

Note on sourcing: one volume was fetched and read this session, Dexter's 1916 *Documentary
History of Yale University*, which is itself a compilation, but which reprints Yale's own
primary records (the 1701 Charter "from the official copy in the University Archives," the
Trustees' proceedings "from the original minutes in the University Archives," letters "from
the University Archives," and Assembly acts) each individually labeled by source. Passages so
labeled and reprinted verbatim are cited PO/PRIMARY. Dexter's own short introductory notes
before each document are cited PS/SECONDARY. Two roster-named documents were searched for and
not located this session: a distinct 1745 printed "Laws of Yale College" pamphlet, and the
final adopted text of the 1745 charter itself (the fetched volume's coverage ends in April
1745, before the Act passed in May 1745; see sources.csv rows `yale-laws-1745` and
`yale-charter-1745`).

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The 1701 Act names the
  school "a Collegiate School"; Cotton Mather's 1718 letter to Governor Elihu Yale proposes
  that "what is forming at New Haven" should "wear the name of Yale College" in exchange for
  his benefaction, which the school (established at New Haven by 1718) then adopted.
  [yale-college-q001, yale-college-q012]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): The Connecticut
  General Court's session began 9 October 1701 and "all the Acts of the session bear that
  date," though Dexter's headnote states the exact day the Charter itself was passed "is not
  certainly known, but was probably the sixteenth." [yale-college-q001; headnote to Item IX,
  not separately quoted this session — see text/dexter1916.txt lines 1797-1804]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): The Charter names ten "Reverend
  Ministers of the Gospel" as the founding Trustees, Partners, or Undertakers: James Noyes of
  Stonington, Israel Chauncey of Stratford, Thomas Buckingham of Saybrook, Abraham Pierson of
  Kenilworth, Samuel Mather of Windsor, Samuel Andrew of Milford, Timothy Woodbridge of
  Hartford, James Pierpont of New Haven, Noadiah Russell of Middletown, and Joseph Webb of
  Fairfield. [yale-college-q002]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): The Charter was passed at New Haven, at the General Court's autumn
  1701 session; instruction actually began at Saybrook under Abraham Pierson (see T1); the
  school later moved to New Haven, where Cotton Mather's 1718 letter locates "what is forming
  at New Haven." [yale-college-q001, yale-college-q012]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): "An Act for
  Liberty to erect a Collegiate School," passed by the Connecticut General Court, October
  1701, document_id `dexter1916` (Item IX, "from the official copy in the University
  Archives"). [yale-college-q001]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The Act states its purpose as founding
  and endowing "a Collegiate School... wherein Youth may be instructed in the Arts & Sciences
  who thorough the blessing of Almighty God may be fitted for Publick employment both in
  Church «& Civil State." A 1744 Trustees' vote restates the same purpose in summary: "the
  originall design of this College as declared in the Charter was for the Training up youth in
  the arts and Sciences." [yale-college-q001, yale-college-q014]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The Act's preamble states
  that "several well disposed, and Publick spirited Persons" had petitioned out of "sincere
  Regard to & Zeal for upholding & Propagating of the Christian Protestant Religion by a
  succession of Learned & Orthodox men." [yale-college-q001]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No signed confession or
  profession-of-faith clause for students was found in the material read this session (compare
  Geneva and Harvard, Part I/II). What the Trustees' 1701 orders require instead is doctrinal
  control over instruction: the Rector must ground students "in Theoretical devinity" and must
  not allow them "to be Instructed and Grounded in any other Systems or Synopses of Divinity
  than such as the s*^- Trustees do order and appoint," and must cause students to recite
  weekly, from memory, "the Assemblies Catechism in Latin and Ames's Theological Theses... and
  Ames's Cases," so as to establish them "in the Principles of the Christian protestant
  Religion." [yale-college-q003]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO):
  The Trustees' 1701 orders twice direct the Rector to use Harvard College as the model until
  the Trustees provide otherwise: Scripture is to be read and expounded "according to the
  Laudable orders and usages in Harvard College," and, more generally, the Rector or Tutors
  "Shall make use of the orders and institutions of Harvard College for the instructing and
  Ruling of the Collegiate School... wherein we have not at this present meeting made
  provision." [yale-college-q006, yale-college-q008]

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): No age rule was found in the 1701 documents read this
  session. A 1744 Trustees' vote — prompted, Dexter's headnote states, "by the cases of David
  Brainerd, of the Class of 1743, who had been expelled in 1741, and Samuel Buell... who had
  been irregularly ordained," both "over twenty-one at the beginning of his Freshman year" —
  fixed a maximum for the first time: "no person Shall be admitted a freshman into this
  College who is more than twenty one years old, unless by Special allowance of the Trustees
  or their Committee." [yale-college-q013, yale-college-q014]
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): The Trustees' 1701 orders require the examining
  Rector and minister/trustee to find candidates "Duly prepared And Expert In Latin and Greek
  Authors both Poetick and oratorial As also ready in making Good Latin" before granting
  admission. [yale-college-q004]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): No
  conversion-narrative or church-membership requirement was found. What was required at entry
  was a signed engagement, by the student and by his parent or guardian, to submit to the
  school's discipline: "Desiring the Benefits of Education In the Languages and Liberal
  Sciences... Do Therefore Solemnly Engage and promise to submit our s*^- Children... unto the
  Orders rules Discipline and Government of the Same." [yale-college-q005]
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): The Rector, together with a neighboring
  ordained minister or trustee, was to "attend the Examination of Students in order to their
  admission," testing proficiency in Latin and Greek authors and in Latin composition, as
  quoted at A2. [yale-college-q004]
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: dexter1916). No
  provision for women is mentioned in the material read this session.
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  dexter1916) — not applicable to entering students; the school's own graduates were licensed
  to preach only after completing the course (see C8).

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): The 1701 orders set the Bachelor's diploma at the "Expiration
  of 4 years Continuance in their Studies," with the Master's diploma available "at the
  Expiration of 3 years more so as in all to Complect the number of 7 years," though the same
  article also allows students to demand their diplomas earlier, after 3 and then 2 further
  years respectively, "provided they shall stand and be accepted upon such probation... as the
  Trustees shall institute." [yale-college-q011]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): NOT FOUND as a subject-by-subject syllabus in the
  material read this session; the 1701 orders describe entrance standards (Latin and Greek,
  A2/A4) and doctrinal instruction (catechism and theological theses, F8) but no full course
  list comparable to Harvard's weekly schedule (see Part II chapter 1) was located and quoted.
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): Students are referred to by "several
  Classes" in the 1701 discipline article (Rector empowered to punish "by... Degrading them in
  their several Classes"), and later documents name Freshman, Junior, and Senior classes (see
  L8/R1 on the Class of 1743 and Class of 1741), but no full class-by-class curriculum table
  was found and quoted this session. [yale-college-q007]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): Latin and Greek are required for entrance
  (A2/A4); Latin is also the language of the weekly catechism recitation (F8, "the Assemblies
  Catechism in Latin"). NOT FOUND for Hebrew specifically in the material read this session.
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): William Ames's *Medulla Theologica* ("Ames's Theological
  Theses") and *Cases of Conscience* ("Ames's Cases") are named as the set texts for weekly
  doctrinal recitation and explanation, alongside the Westminster Assembly's Shorter Catechism
  in Latin. [yale-college-q003]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): The Rector was required to
  cause the Scriptures to be read daily, morning and evening except the Sabbath, "at the times
  of prayer," with expositions upon the passage, "according to the Laudable orders and usages
  in Harvard College"; on the Sabbath he was to "Either Expound practical Theology or Cause the
  Students non Graduated, to Repeat Sermons." [yale-college-q006]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): Annually, in September, the Rector with two
  trustees and any tutors were to "attend the Examination of the Candidates," granting, upon
  finding them "Duly Qualified," "under the hand of s*^- Rector and two Trustees... A Diploma
  or Licence for Degrees of Batcheler or Master." [yale-college-q009]
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): See C1/C7: a Diploma
  or Licence for the degree of Bachelor after four years (or, on request and at the student's
  own charge, after three), and for Master after seven years in all (or, on request, after
  five); the same article records that the Trustees "Disallow of Publick Commencements and
  forbid the same at all times," preferring the individual diploma procedure "for the Ease of
  their parents Charges." [yale-college-q011, yale-college-q009]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a full weekly timetable
  comparable to Harvard's (Part II chapter 1); the only fixed daily points located and quoted
  this session are the twice-daily Scripture reading and the Sabbath exposition/sermon-repeating
  described at C6. [yale-college-q006]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  dexter1916) beyond the twice-daily Scripture reading at C6; no rising, meal, or bed-time rule
  was located and quoted this session.
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: dexter1916) as
  a stated rule; the school's early instruction was carried on at Saybrook under Rector Pierson
  (see T1) before the move to New Haven (F4), which implies dispersed early lodging, but no
  residence rule itself was located and quoted.
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PO): See C6: Scripture reading and exposition twice
  daily except the Sabbath, and on the Sabbath either an exposition of practical theology by
  the Rector or the repeating of sermons by non-graduated students. [yale-college-q006]
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PO): Every student and his parent or guardian signed
  an engagement, at admission, to submit to "the Orders rules Discipline and Government" of
  the school (A3); the Rector, with the tutor(s), had power "to punish the non Graduated
  Students According to their faults Either by Imposing Extraordinary School Exercises or by
  Degrading them in their several Classes without the Benefit of appeal for the Delinquents."
  [yale-college-q005, yale-college-q007]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: dexter1916) in the
  material read this session.
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): The 1701 orders fixed tuition at
  "30 Shillings per annum In Country pay" for each undergraduate, paid to the Rector or Tutor
  by the parent or guardian, and 10 shillings per annum for each graduate remaining in
  residence "under the Benefit of s*^- School." [yale-college-q010]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: dexter1916) as an
  explicit enrollment figure for a given year in the material read this session.
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): The one named disciplinary
  case located and quoted this session is David Brainerd's, recorded only as a fact prompting
  a later rule: "of the Class of 1743, who had been expelled in 1741." No detail of the grounds
  for his expulsion is given in this passage; Dexter's headnote states only that he was "over
  twenty-one at the beginning of his Freshman year," offered as context for the 1744 age vote,
  not as the stated cause of the expulsion. [yale-college-q013]

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): The Charter authorizes the Trustees
  to appoint "Rector or Master Tutors Ushers or other officers" and to pay them "annual
  Salaries or Allowances" (F5 document). NOT FOUND this session for a specific early
  Rector/Tutor headcount by year, beyond the 1701 orders' references to "the Tutor or Tutors
  when there shall Be such," which imply the school opened with a Rector alone. [yale-college-q009]
- **T2 Teachers' duties as set out in the charter** (PO): The Rector's duties, set out in the
  1701 Trustees' orders, are to take "Especial Care" for students' "moral Behaviour" and to
  ground them "in Theoretical devinity" only according to systems the Trustees approve, and to
  cause the weekly catechism recitation described at F8. [yale-college-q003]
- **T3 Forms of teacher–student contact (lecture, disputation, tutorial, table, correspondence,
  preaching class)** (PO): The material read this session describes examination (Rector and a
  trustee personally examining candidates, C7), catechetical recitation (F8), and Scripture
  exposition (C6/L3), but no lecture or disputation schedule comparable to Harvard's weekly
  table (Part II chapter 1) was found and quoted.
- **T4 Named examples of teacher–student interaction with source** (PS): NOT FOUND as a
  narrated anecdote in the material read this session; the closest is the bare disciplinary
  fact of Brainerd's 1741 expulsion (L8), which names the student but not the interaction.

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The Act's preamble ties the
  school's purpose directly to a "succession of Learned & Orthodox men" who would uphold and
  propagate "the Christian Protestant Religion." [yale-college-q001]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PO): Doctrinal orthodoxy, controlled by the Trustees rather than left
  to the Rector's discretion (F8), paired with the classical languages required for entrance
  (A2/A4) and with daily direct engagement of Scripture (C6). [yale-college-q003, yale-college-q006]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The 1701 orders
  expressly forbid the Rector from instructing students "in any other Systems or Synopses of
  Divinity than such as the s*^- Trustees do order and appoint" — a rejection of doctrinal
  latitude in the Rector individually. [yale-college-q003]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: dexter1916) in
  the material read this session. (Yale's later Latin motto, "Lux et Veritas," is not
  addressed by any 1701-1745 document read this session and is not stated here.)
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): The 1744 Trustees'
  vote restates the founding purpose in summary form, 43 years on: "the originall design of
  this College as declared in the Charter was for the Training up youth in the arts and
  Sciences." [yale-college-q014]
