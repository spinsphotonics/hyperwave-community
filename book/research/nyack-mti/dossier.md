# Dossier — Missionary Training Institute, Nyack, and The Christian and Missionary Alliance (`nyack-mti`)

Note on sourcing: two documents were fetched and read this session. (1) A. E. Thompson's 1920
"official authorised" biography of A. B. Simpson, published by the Christian Alliance
Publishing Company itself, contains two chapters written firsthand by then-serving officers of
the Institute and Alliance — Ch. XXI, "A Christian Educator," by Walter M. Turnbull, D.D., Dean
of the Missionary Training Institute, and Ch. XXII, "The Missionary Outcome," by Robert H.
Glover, M.D., Foreign Secretary of the C&MA — plus a chapter that quotes verbatim from the
Institute's 1883 first prospectus, the 1887 Christian Alliance platform/creed, and the 1887
Evangelical Missionary Alliance "Principles and Constitution" synopsis. Passages Thompson
explicitly introduces as quotations from those founding documents are cited PO (treated as
primary, following the sourcing convention already used in research/geneva-academy); Thompson's
own connecting narrative is cited PS and labeled [SECONDARY]. (2) One issue of the Institute's
own contemporaneous periodical, The Christian Alliance and Missionary Weekly (6 Dec. 1889), was
also fetched and is cited PO. No copy of the full original 1887 constitution, nor of the
1882-83 prospectus as an independent pamphlet, was located this session (see discrepancies.md
and sources.csv). This is a real coverage gap, recorded honestly rather than papered over.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Founded 1883 as "the Missionary Training College for Home and Foreign missionaries and evangelists" [nyack-mti-q002]; renamed "the New York Training Institute" in 1894 [nyack-mti-q010]; located at South Nyack, N.Y. from 1897 as "the Missionary Institute" [nyack-mti-q010]. The sending society was organized in 1887 as two parallel bodies, "The Christian Alliance" [nyack-mti-q028] and "The Evangelical Missionary Alliance" [nyack-mti-q031]; the missionary body was incorporated in November 1889 as "The International Missionary Alliance" [nyack-mti-q034]; the two societies were united in 1897 as "The Christian and Missionary Alliance" [nyack-mti-q035].
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): "Thus in the year 1882 the first training class, composed of new and zealous followers, met on the stage of a theater on 23rd Street, New York" [nyack-mti-q001]. Formal organization followed on "Monday, October ist, 1883" [nyack-mti-q002]. The Christian Alliance "was organized in the summer of 1887 at Old Orchard convention" [nyack-mti-q028]; the Evangelical Missionary Alliance was "effected" at the same 1887 Old Orchard Convention [nyack-mti-q031]; the missionary body was incorporated in November 1889 [nyack-mti-q034]; the two societies were "united formally and legally" in 1897 [nyack-mti-q035].
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): Albert B. Simpson founded and led the training class from its first 1882 meeting [nyack-mti-q001]. On the 1897 union, "Rev. A. B. Simpson was elected President and General Superintendent" [nyack-mti-q035]; other named officers at various dates include Rev. A. E. Funk (Secretary), David Crear (Treasurer), and Mrs. A. B. Simpson (Financial Secretary) [nyack-mti-q035]. Walter M. Turnbull, D.D. is named as Dean of the Institute at the time of writing (1920) [source: chapter byline, thompson1920-lifeofsimpson p. 215, not separately quoted]. Early faculty leaders named include Dr. F. W. Farr (Vice-President) and Principal W. C. Stevens [nyack-mti-q011].
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): New York City, in successively rented and purchased buildings — a 23rd Street theater stage (1882); an Eighth Avenue rented home (1883); West 20th Street; 49th Street (1886); West 55th Street (from 1887); and "690 Eighth Avenue" (from 1890) [nyack-mti-q001, nyack-mti-q002, from surrounding unquoted narrative NOT separately quote-recorded for the intermediate moves]. From 1897, "the Missionary Institute has been located at South Nyack, New York," with the main Institute building's cornerstone laid 17 April and opening exercises held 24 October 1897 [nyack-mti-q010].
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No independently digitized copy of the roster's named "prospectus / announcement of the Missionary Training College (1882-83)" or the "C&MA constitution (1887)" was located this session (see discrepancies.md). The principal founding-document material actually available and used for the abridged charter is: (a) "the first statement of character and purpose" of the Missionary Training College, quoted in full by Thompson [nyack-mti-q005]; (b) the Christian Alliance's 1887 organizing statement and creed, quoted from the Alliance's own 1893 Year Book [nyack-mti-q028, nyack-mti-q029, nyack-mti-q030]; (c) the Evangelical Missionary Alliance's 1887 "Principles and Constitution," given by Thompson as an eight-point synopsis [nyack-mti-q031, nyack-mti-q032, nyack-mti-q033]. All three are English-language, reprinted in Thompson 1920 (document_id thompson1920-lifeofsimpson), pp. 128-131 and 216.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): The Institute: "This work originated in the felt need for a simple, spiritual, and scriptural method of training for Christian work the large class of persons who desire to become prepared for thorough and efficient service for the Master, without a long, elaborate college course" [nyack-mti-q005]. The Christian Alliance: organized "for the purpose of uniting in Christian fellowship and testimony in a purely fraternal Alliance the large number of consecrated Christians in the various evangelical churches" [nyack-mti-q028]. The Evangelical Missionary Alliance would "contemplate the rapid evangelization of the most neglected sections of the foreign mission field" [nyack-mti-q032].
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Simpson recalled the movement's origin in prayer at Old Orchard, Maine, in 1881: "'T can well remember the nights I walked up and down the sandy beach of Old Orchard, Maine, in the summer of 1881, and asked God in some way to raise up a great missionary movement that would reach the neg- lected fields of the world and utilize the neglected forces of the Church at home, as was not then being done" [nyack-mti-q026]. Of the humble first meetings that grew into the work, Simpson wrote: "I remember well the cold and desolate afternoon years ago, when a little band of humble, praying Christians met in an upper room to begin this work for God ... we knelt before Him there and thanked Him that we were poor, that we were few, that we were weak, and threw ourselves upon the might of the Holy Ghost" [nyack-mti-q025].
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): Membership in the Christian Alliance required signing a creed affirming belief "in God the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, in the verbal in- spiration of the Holy Scriptures as originally given, in the vicarious atonement of the Lord Jesus Christ, in the eternal salvation of all who believe in Him, and the ever- lasting punishment of all who reject Him" and in "the Lord Jesus Christ as my Saviour, Sanctifier, Healer, and Coming Lord" [nyack-mti-q030] — the "Fourfold Gospel" basis [nyack-mti-q029]. The Evangelical Missionary Alliance's synopsis opens: "It will be undenominational and strictly evange- lical" [nyack-mti-q032].
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): "Dr. Simpson was the pioneer in the field of Bible Train- ing School work in America, although in Great Britain the East London Institute founded by Dr. H, Gratton Guinness is some years older" [nyack-mti-q006] (i.e., the Institute did not claim to be first in the world, only in America; East London Institute/Harley College is named as older). On the missionary society: "While in missionary principle and practice the Alliance patterned very largely after the already existing and hon- ored China Inland Mission, there was from the begin- ning the one important difference that the Alliance sphere of operations was international" [nyack-mti-q017].

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): The original course (from 1883) "comprised one year of study" [nyack-mti-q002]. "In 1885, the standard course was lengthened to cover three years" [nyack-mti-q008]. By 1919-20 the Institute was one of "twenty-six more ad- vanced schools, including nine Bible Institutes" that the wider Alliance missionary movement drew students from/through [nyack-mti-q023] (this last figure describes the whole Alliance educational network, not the Nyack course length specifically).
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): First-year (1883) subjects: "English, Christian Evidences [...] Bible Study and Interpretation, Church History and Christian Life and Work" [nyack-mti-q002, nyack-mti-q003]. After the 1885 lengthening to three years and three departments, see C3/C5 below for the full subject lists by department [nyack-mti-q008, nyack-mti-q009].
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): From 1885, three departments: Literary, Theological, and Practical [nyack-mti-q008, nyack-mti-q009].
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): New Testament Greek was part of the Theological Department from 1885 [nyack-mti-q009]. The 1889 periodical records that "Some of the pupils of the Greek classes meet at 5.30 a.m., for conversational study" [nyack-mti-q037], i.e., voluntary early-morning Greek practice beyond the regular class. No Hebrew or other language instruction was found quoted this session.
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND as a list of specific set books or authors (searched: thompson1920-lifeofsimpson, caw-1889-12-06); only subject/department names are given (C2/C3).
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): The Practical Department (from 1885) comprised "Christian Experience, with spe- cial reference to the Enduement of Power, Exercises in Sermon Outlines and Bible Readings, Evangelistic Work and the Conducting of Religious Services, Personal Work for Souls, Foreign Missions, Sunday School Work, Vocal Music" [nyack-mti-q009]. The 1889 periodical records a student debate as a form of practical rhetorical training: "Recently ten of the students participated in a [...] debate. Subject: [...] Resolved that Paul was more justifiable than Barnabas in his course [...] respecting Mark. [...] Both sides were well sustained by carefully prepared essays and speeches" [nyack-mti-q038].
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: thompson1920-lifeofsimpson, caw-1889-12-06)
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): No degree or certificate is described; the record found is of "Commencement" (first held May 1884) [nyack-mti-q007] and of graduates being sent directly to the mission field afterward [nyack-mti-q007]. The 1894 renaming to "the New York Training Institute" [nyack-mti-q010] suggests an "Institute" rather than "College" self-understanding, consistent with no degree-granting claim being found.
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): No formal timetable was found. Fragments of the daily/weekly rhythm are given by the 1889 periodical: Greek conversational study "at 5.30 a.m." [nyack-mti-q037]; an analytical Bible-study class taught personally by Simpson [source: caw-1889-12-06, "College Notes," not separately quote-recorded as a discrete field-tagged quote this session]; and special Thanksgiving Day morning devotions led by "Brother Funk" [source: caw-1889-12-06, "College Notes," same paragraph, not separately quote-recorded].

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "'This makes necessary our Training School. It is not enough that we should grasp these mighty truths, but we must commit them to others who will be able to teach others also, and provide as the Master did through His own disciples, for the perpetuation of these principles and their propagation throughout the whole world'" (Simpson, in his last convention address at Nyack) [nyack-mti-q013]. On the founding vision: see F7, [nyack-mti-q026].
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): The missionary movement's program was "pre millennial, looking not toward world conversion as its goal, but rather toward the reach- ing of the whole world with the witness of the Gospel and the calling out from among every nation, tribe, and tongue of 'a people for his name,' a bride for the return- ing Heavenly Bridegroom" [nyack-mti-q015]; doctrinally organized around the "Fourfold Gospel" of Saviour, Sanctifier, Healer, and Coming Lord [nyack-mti-q029, nyack-mti-q030]; missionary support ran on "the faitJi principle" without fixed salaries [nyack-mti-q016, nyack-mti-q032].
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): The Institute course was framed explicitly as an alternative to "a long, elaborate college course" [nyack-mti-q005]. The missionary program looked "not toward world conversion as its goal" (contrasted with a postmillennial expectation) [nyack-mti-q015].
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): "His missionary motto was 'the regions beyond,' his missionary goal 'the uttermost part of the earth'" [SECONDARY: thompson1920-lifeofsimpson, p. 234 region, R. ## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): Graduates were sent directly from Commencement: "The first Commencement was held in May, 1884, and shortly afterward five of the graduates sailed for Africa as the vanguard of hundreds of Alliance missionaries" [nyack-mti-q007, nyack-mti-q018]. The missionary society itself was "governed by a board of directors elected annually, who shall appoint and direct the mission- aries employed" [nyack-mti-q033].
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): By 1893, "twelve fields" [nyack-mti-q021]; by the time of Thompson's writing (1920), "sixteen ... mission fields," specifically named elsewhere in the source as including the Congo, Soudan, India, China (Central, South, North, and Pekin), Japan, Bulgaria, Palestine, Alaska, Hayti and Santo Domingo, Tibet, French Indo-China, the Philippines, Venezuela, Ecuador, Chile, French Guinea [SECONDARY (Glover's narrative list): thompson1920-lifeofsimpson, pp. 228, 231, region — the "twelve fields" 1893 figure is separately quoted at S3/[nyack-mti-q021]; the fuller 16-field list is Glover's own 1920 narrative enumeration and is recorded here as SECONDARY rather than independently quote-tagged field-by-field this session].
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): 1884: five graduates sailed for the Congo [nyack-mti-q007, nyack-mti-q018]. October 1893 (six years after organization): "twelve fields, with forty stations manned by one hundred and eighty missionaries" [nyack-mti-q021]. By end of 1919: "17,356 had been baptized ... There were 125 organized churches with nearly 12,000 members in full communion" [nyack-mti-q022]; "nearly a thousand choice young men and women were in training in twenty-six more ad- vanced schools, including nine Bible Institutes" [nyack-mti-q023]; "700 men and women have heard the Master's call to service" from native churches, alongside "320 foreign missionaries who are holding 500 stations and outstations" [nyack-mti-q024]. Simpson himself, in his last Nyack address, said "Between three and four thousand conse- crated lives have gone forth from this place, over one thousand of whom have already reached the foreign field as missionaries" [nyack-mti-q014].
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): "It adopted the faitJi principle of support, not guaranteeing fixed or large salaries but standing with its missionaries in trust for the full supply from the Lord of the financial needs of workers and work through the free will offerings of His people" [nyack-mti-q016]; the Evangelical Missionary Alliance's constitution likewise specified it "will encourage the principles of rigid economy, giving no fixed salaries" and "will rely upon God to supply the necessary means through the freewill offerings of His people" [nyack-mti-q032].
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): "Within a few months of their arrival on the field their leader, John Condit, died of fever" (Congo, 1884-85) [nyack-mti-q018]. "The pioneer Alliance missionary to China, Rev. Wil- liam Cassidy, was never permitted to reach that land, but died of smallpox contracted on the Pacific voyage and was buried in Japan" [nyack-mti-q019]. By October 1893, "twenty- three missionary comrades had fallen at the battle front" [nyack-mti-q021]. A Swedish Alliance-affiliated band in Mongolia lost "Twenty-one of its foreign workers and fourteen of their precious children" in the 1900 Boxer Rising [SECONDARY: thompson1920-lifeofsimpson, p. 227 region; not confirmed to be Institute graduates specifically — see discrepancies.md].
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a separate instructions document (searched: thompson1920-lifeofsimpson, caw-1889-12-06); the Evangelical Missionary Alliance constitution synopsis includes a field-governance clause: "It will leave each church established on the for- eign field free to organize and administer its affairs as it may choose, provided that such method be scriptural in its essential features" [nyack-mti-q033].

## R — Fruit
- **R1** John Condit: led the first missionary party sent from the Institute's early work; sailed for the Congo in November 1884 with four companions; died of fever within a few months of arrival [nyack-mti-q007, nyack-mti-q018].
- **R2** Rev. William Cassidy: "the pioneer Alliance missionary to China"; died of smallpox contracted on the Pacific voyage before reaching China and was buried in Japan [nyack-mti-q019].
- **R3** Rev. Mark B. and Jennie Fuller: led a pioneer party of about forty workers to Central India, opening work "among the neglected but proud and resisting Mahratta people" [nyack-mti-q020].
- **R4** Walter M. Turnbull, D.D.: Dean of the Missionary Training Institute at the time of writing (1920); contributed the biography's chapter on the Institute's educational history [source: chapter byline, thompson1920-lifeofsimpson, p. 215 — PS, narrative attribution, not a separately tagged quotation].
