# Dossier — Doddridge's Northampton Academy (`northampton-academy`)

Note on sourcing: one volume was fetched and read this session, Boyd's 1860 memoir, which
reprints Philip Doddridge's own letters verbatim (in quotation marks, with attribution — e.g.
his 1732 letter to the Earl of Halifax) and gives, as "the substance of" Job Orton's earlier
(1766) account, a long paraphrase-with-occasional-quotation of "the METHOD OF EDUCATION
pursued by Dr. Doddridge." Doddridge's own quoted words are cited PO/PRIMARY. Orton's/Boyd's
narrative description of the academy's method — daily order, subjects, public exercises — is
cited PS/SECONDARY, since it is presented as a compiler's summary of Orton, not a document
Doddridge himself wrote as a rule-book. No distinct "Rules of the Academy" document, separate
from this correspondence and this secondary account, was located and fetched this session (see
sources.csv for two further primary collections — the 1776 *Course of Lectures*, and Humphreys's
1829 *Correspondence and Diary* — located but not fetched for lack of time).

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Called "his academy at
  Harborough" at its 1729 founding, and simply "my academy" or "the academy" by Doddridge
  himself after his move to Northampton; Boyd's chapter title calls it "his theological academy."
  [northampton-academy-q001, northampton-academy-q002]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO/PS): "A brief account
  has been already given of the founding of Dr. Doddridge's academy at Harborough in the year
  1729." The academy then moved with Doddridge to Northampton (the move is implied by the 1732
  letter's dateline "Northampton, 1732" and by the prosecution's occurring after "his removal to
  Northampton"). [northampton-academy-q001]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): Philip Doddridge (1702-1751) alone;
  no co-founder or board of trustees is named in the material read this session. After 1734,
  growing enrollment led him to employ an assistant tutor, to whose care the junior students
  were entrusted and who directed the academy in Doddridge's absence, but this assistant is not
  named in the passage read. [northampton-academy-q007]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): Harborough (Market Harborough, Leicestershire) from 1729; Northampton
  from soon after (the 1732 letter to Lord Halifax is dated "Northampton, 1732"). [northampton-academy-q001, northampton-academy-q002]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): No single charter
  or foundation deed was located; dissenting academies of this period operated without royal or
  ecclesiastical charter, which is the direct occasion of the 1732-33 prosecution described below.
  The closest primary document located and fetched this session is Doddridge's own letter to the
  Earl of Halifax (Northampton, 1732), which describes and defends the academy; it is used as
  the chapter's "Charter" document, document_id `boyd1860`. [northampton-academy-q002]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): NOT FOUND as a single programmatic
  statement of purpose in the material read this session (compare Yale's or Harvard's charter
  preambles, Part II chapters 1-2). Doddridge's letter states only that he has "in his hands the
  education of several gentlemen intended for the learned professions," not all of them destined
  for the ministry. [northampton-academy-q009]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The material read this
  session documents a crisis the academy itself provoked rather than one it was founded to
  answer: in 1732 the diocesan chancellor, at a visitation at Northampton, charged that "there
  was a fellow in their parish who taught a grammar-school," "as he supposed without any license
  from the bishop," and ordered the churchwardens to find out and, if so, "present me, so that I
  might be prosecuted according to law." Doddridge's own account states that the case, tried at
  Westminster Hall, was decided in his favor, and that a further royal command "that all further
  prosecution of the matter should be discontinued" followed, "in accordance with... his majesty['s]... maxim... that during his reign there should be no persecution for conscience['] sake."
  [northampton-academy-q001, northampton-academy-q002]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND as a signed
  confession or subscription requirement in the material read this session. The academy's
  religious character is shown instead in its daily worship and Scripture exposition (see L3)
  and in its final-year lectures "on preaching, and the pastoral care."
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PS):
  Before founding his own academy, Doddridge had himself studied "in the academy of the
  celebrated Rev. John Jennings," whose practice — examining candidates "as to their improvement
  in school learning" and "on... their moral character, and the marks of a serious disposition"
  before admission — is described at length in the material read this session; the passage does
  not, however, state in Doddridge's own words that he consciously modeled his Northampton
  practice on Jennings's, so this antecedent is recorded as a documented biographical fact, not
  a founder's own claim of imitation. Flagged as a caution: material describing Jennings's
  academy (where Doddridge was a student) must not be silently attributed to Doddridge's own
  academy (where he was master) in the chapter without this distinction.

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PS): Not stated as a fixed number of years in the material read this
  session; the account of the "last year of the course" (preaching and pastoral-care lectures)
  implies a multi-year sequence with a defined final year, but no total figure was located and
  quoted. [northampton-academy-q009 context]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PS): First year: continuation of school Latin and Greek,
  Hebrew sufficient to read the Old Testament, logic, rhetoric, geography, metaphysics, geometry,
  algebra, trigonometry, conic sections, celestial mechanics, natural philosophy (with
  experimental apparatus), natural and civil history, Jewish antiquities, and human anatomy.
  Theology — "in its widest range, embracing ethics, and what was then styled pneumatology" — was
  "the grand object of investigation" throughout, taught from Doddridge's own compendium of
  propositions and scholia with references to authorities. In the final year, lectures were given
  "on preaching, and the pastoral care," and also "on civil law, on the mythology of the
  ancients, and on English history, particularly the history of non-conformity." [northampton-academy-q008]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PS): Students were organized in "several classes in
  order," attending Doddridge's morning lecture in sequence; junior students' instruction and,
  in Doddridge's absence, the academy's direction, were delegated to an assistant tutor after
  1734. [northampton-academy-q007]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PS): Hebrew, Greek, and French are named
  specifically in connection with the twice-daily family Scripture reading, where a student
  "read a chapter of the Old Testament from Hebrew into English" in the morning and "a chapter
  of the New Testament... from Greek into English" in the evening. [northampton-academy-q005]
  Latin and Greek from school were "pursued and advanced" in the first year, and French was
  "also taught to those who desired to learn it," per C2's passage.
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PS): No set classical author-list (comparable to Geneva's Cicero
  and Homer, or Harvard's Tully) was located and quoted this session; the one named text is
  Doddridge's own theological compendium, "arranged into propositions or problems... and
  scholia," used for the pneumatology/ethics/divinity lectures. [northampton-academy-q008]
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PS): Public exercises occupied
  one day a week, per the material read this session, including junior students' translations
  and orations, theses on pneumatology and ethics "alternately opposed and defended," advanced
  students' orations on the divine perfections and moral virtue, and, for senior students,
  "analyses of Scripture, schemes of sermons, and full sermons," all "submitted to the
  examination and correction of Dr. Doddridge." Final-year lectures on preaching and pastoral
  care are separately noted at C2.
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PS): Weekly, Doddridge "examined [the class] upon the
  contents of the previous lecture," asking whether they had understood his reasoning, what the
  referenced authors said, and inviting objections; this is described as his lecturing method
  generally, not as a formal pass/fail examination comparable to Yale's or Geneva's annual
  exams. NOT FOUND for any promotion or degree-granting examination.
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched:
  boyd1860). The academy is not described in the material read this session as granting any
  formal degree; ordination, where it followed, is not documented by a quoted primary source
  this session (see Sending, below).
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PS): "Soon after breakfast the several
  classes in order attended upon Dr. Doddridge, and a lecture of an hour in length was delivered
  to them. The afternoon he devoted to private study and pastoral visits." Combined with L1's
  rising and worship times, this gives a partial daily order (morning worship, morning lecture,
  afternoon study/visits) but not a full hour-by-hour table comparable to Harvard's weekly
  schedule (Part II, Chapter 1). [northampton-academy-q007]

## L — Common life
- **L1 Daily order: rising, worship, meals, study, bed** (PS): "Every student was obliged to
  rise at six in the summer, and at seven in the winter, soon after which they assembled for
  prayers," conducted by Doddridge or, in turn, by a student; they then withdrew "to their
  study-rooms" until family worship, at which a student read and Doddridge expounded a chapter
  of Scripture (Old Testament from Hebrew in the morning, New Testament from Greek in the
  evening), closing with a psalm and prayer. [northampton-academy-q004, northampton-academy-q005]
- **L2 Residence: where students lived; with whom** (PS): Doddridge "chose to introduce into his
  own family as many of his students as his house could accommodate, that they might enjoy the
  benefit of a more constant supervision and control"; those who could not be housed with him
  "boarded at other houses" but were still required to attend the household's morning and
  evening worship and to conduct family worship where they lodged. [northampton-academy-q003,
  northampton-academy-q006]
- **L3 Worship: form and frequency** (PS): Twice daily (morning and evening), as described at
  L1, with Scripture read by a student from the original language and expounded by Doddridge,
  closing with psalm-singing and prayer. [northampton-academy-q004, northampton-academy-q005]
- **L4 Discipline: rules and penalties** (PS): Boarding students' absence from the household
  worship services "subjected [them] to a fine, and if frequent, to public censure before the
  students." [northampton-academy-q006] Doddridge's own government of the household is
  described, in Boyd's summary, as observing "a due medium between the rigor of school
  discipline and unlimited indulgence" — a secondary characterization, not a quoted rule.
  [northampton-academy-q003]
- **L5 Dress, conduct, recreation rules** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: boyd1860).
- **L6 Fees, board, and how students were supported** (PO): NOT FOUND as a fee schedule in the
  material read this session. The academy's scientific apparatus was funded partly "by a
  society of the principal dissenting ministers and other gentlemen in London" and partly by
  "the small contributions of each student when he entered on this department of study," per
  Doddridge's 1732 letter and the Orton-derived account. [northampton-academy-q002 context;
  northampton-academy-q008 context]
- **L7 Number of students (with years)** (PS): NOT FOUND as a specific enrollment figure in the
  material read this session, beyond the fact that after 1734 numbers had grown enough that
  Doddridge "employed an assistant." [northampton-academy-q007]
- **L8 Health, deaths, or hardship recorded among students** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched:
  boyd1860).

  invited to raise objections (C7); weekly public exercises including disputation ("theses...

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): NOT FOUND as a single
  statement comparable to Yale's or Harvard's charter language in the material read this
  session; Doddridge's own quoted words in this session concern the legal defense of the
  academy's right to exist (F7), not a stated pedagogical or spiritual priority.
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle,
  self-support, other)** (PS): Theology — "embracing ethics, and what was then styled
  pneumatology" — is described as "the grand object of investigation" throughout the course,
  alongside daily direct engagement with Scripture in the original languages at family worship.
  [northampton-academy-q008, northampton-academy-q005]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Doddridge's 1732 letter
  rejects the diocesan chancellor's claim of authority over his teaching, arguing that dependence
  "upon the sovereign pleasure of a bishop to license schoolmasters, or even tutors" would make
  British subjects' "best privileges... owe[d]... to convenience and caprice, rather than to the
  law of the realm." [northampton-academy-q002]

## R — Fruit
- **R1 Philip Doddridge** himself (PS): the academy's sole master, 1729-1751 (Harborough, then
  Northampton); author of the posthumously published *Course of Lectures on the Principal
  Subjects in Pneumatology, Ethics and Divinity* (1763) used as the source of his compendium
  method (C2/C5). [northampton-academy-q001, northampton-academy-q008]
- **R2 Job Orton** (PS): a student of the academy whose own account of "the METHOD OF EDUCATION
  pursued by Dr. Doddridge," first published 1766, is the source (via Boyd's 1860 paraphrase)
