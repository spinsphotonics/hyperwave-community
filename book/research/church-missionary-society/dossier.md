# Dossier — Church Missionary Society and its Islington College (`church-missionary-society`)

Note on sourcing: the Society itself is a missionary sending body, and most template fields
designed for an educational institution (Admission, Curriculum, Common Life, Teachers) do
not apply to the Society's own 1799 founding. Unlike the other two societies in this Part,
however, CMS did found a residential training college at Islington (opened 1825), for which
some of those fields *are* answerable from the sources read this session; that material is
recorded under the relevant fields below and flagged as pertaining to the College, not the
Society's founding meeting. This chapter's charter document, per the roster, is the Society's
own 1799 founding record; the standalone 1799 pamphlet itself ("An Account of a Society for
Missions to Africa and the East") was not independently located this session (see
discrepancies.md item 1), so the four founding Resolutions of 12 April 1799 -- quoted by
Stock as direct reproductions of "the original Minutes" -- are used as the chapter's
primary founding text instead.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): Founded 1799 as the Society for Missions to Africa and the East (per the roster); renamed the Church Missionary Society in 1812 (per the roster; not independently re-verified as a renaming date from a fetched text this session). Its training college at Islington, opened 1825, is referred to by Stock simply as "the Islington College" or "C.M.S. College." [cms-q009, cms-q010]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): The Eclectic Society (an existing clerical discussion group) debated the question of missions on 18 February and 18 March 1799; "it was resolved to form a Society immediately. On April 1st, another meeting was held to prepare the Eules; and on Friday, April 12th, 1799, the public meeting took place which established the Church Missionary Society," at "a first-floor room in a hotel in Aldersgate Street, the Castle and Falcon," where "sixteen clergymen and nine laymen" were gathered under the Rev. John Venn as chairman. [cms-q001] The Islington College's own founding is separately dated: "The inauguration took place on January 31st, 1825." [cms-q009]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): John Venn, Rector of Clapham, chaired the founding meeting of 12 April 1799. [cms-q001] Henry Thornton, M.P., was appointed the first Treasurer; William Wilberforce was asked to be President but declined the prominent position and became a Vice-President instead (per Stock's narrative; not independently extracted as a quote record this session). The Islington College's first Principal was "the Rev. J. Norman Pearson, of Trinity College, Cambridge." [cms-q010]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): London (the Castle and Falcon, Aldersgate Street, for the 1799 founding meetings); Islington, north London, for the College (from 1825).
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): The four founding Resolutions adopted 12 April 1799, document_id `stock1` (quoted by Stock as direct reproductions of "the original Minutes"); "In the original Minutes the Eules [Rules] follow" the third Resolution, but the Rules' own text was not independently located and fetched this session (see discrepancies.md item 1). [cms-q002, cms-q003, cms-q005, cms-q006]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Resolution 1 (12 April 1799): "That it is a duty highly incumbent upon every Christian to endeavour to propagate the knowledge of the Gospel among the Heathen." [cms-q002] Resolution 2 gives the Society's specific justification: existing Societies "confine their labours to the British Plantations in America and to the West Indies," so "there seems to be still wanting in the Established Church a society for sending missionaries to the Continent of Africa, or the other parts of the heathen world." [cms-q003, cms-q004] John Venn's address of 18 March 1799 had already framed the question as "What can we do?" rather than merely "What ought the Church to do?" and concluded the Mission "must be founded upon 'the Church-principle, not the high-Church principle'; and if clergymen cannot be found, send out laymen." [cms-q008]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): See F6, Resolution 2: existing missionary societies (the S.P.C.K. and S.P.G.) were judged, in the founders' own resolution, to leave Africa and "the other parts of the heathen world" unreached. [cms-q003, cms-q004]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): No separate creedal-subscription clause was found in the passages read this session (searched: stock1); the Society's distinguishing basis is instead the "Church-principle" named by Venn -- an Anglican, not interdenominational, footing -- combined with a deliberate refusal of what Venn called the "high-Church principle." [cms-q008] Resolution 4 directs a formal deputation, with the Society's Rules, to the Archbishop of Canterbury, the Bishop of London, and the Bishop of Durham, situating the new Society within, not outside, the Established Church's structure of authority. [cms-q006]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): Resolution 2 explicitly measures the new Society against two existing Church societies, "the Societies for Propagating the Gospel and for Promoting Christian Knowledge" (the S.P.G. and S.P.C.K.), finding their reach insufficient. [cms-q003, cms-q004] Stock separately records, as context rather than as part of the Resolutions themselves, that the London Missionary Society had been founded four years earlier, in the same Castle and Falcon room, and that the Evangelical clergy's decision not to work through that interdenominational body (nor through the S.P.C.K./S.P.G.) was deliberate, on the "Church-principle" ground. [cms-q008]

Not applicable to the Society's own 1799 founding (a sending body, not a school). For

## C — Curriculum
Not applicable to the Society's 1799 founding. For Islington College: the entering
students of 1826 were examined "in Latin, Greek, Divinity, Logic, and Mathematics," and,
three months later, a further "Examination took place of the Oriental Classes conducted by
Professor S. Lee, in Hebrew, Arabic, Sanscrit, and Bengali." [cms-q011, cms-q012] Stock notes
that "the languages of the Mission-field were then regarded as an important part of the
studies." [cms-q011, context] NOT FOUND (searched: stock1, within the passages read this

## L — Common life
Not applicable to the Society's 1799 founding. For Islington College: the first class of
twelve students (1825) used "only the house already standing on" the College's ground (the
"still the Principal's house"); a purpose-built college "to accommodate if necessary fifty
students, with hall, library, lecture-rooms" was begun the following year, its first stones

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): The duty is put in the fewest possible words as Resolution 1: "That it is a duty highly incumbent upon every Christian to endeavour to propagate the knowledge of the Gospel among the Heathen" -- addressed to "every Christian," not merely to "the Church" as a corporate body. [cms-q002] John Venn's 18 March 1799 address stressed dependence on God rather than on human strategy: a missionary "should have heaven in his heart, and tread the world under his foot," and the enterprise should "Begin on a small scale," since "Nature follows this rule. Colonies creep from small beginnings." [cms-q007]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): The "Church-principle" -- an avowedly Anglican basis, distinct from the interdenominational LMS and distinct also from what Venn called the "high-Church principle" -- with lay missionaries admitted if ordained clergy could not be found: "if clergymen cannot be found, send out laymen." [cms-q008] Later, at Islington, a stress on classical, theological, and Oriental-language training together (Latin, Greek, Divinity, Logic, Mathematics, plus Hebrew, Arabic, Sanskrit, Bengali). [cms-q011, cms-q012]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT FOUND as a separately quoted rejection clause in the Resolutions themselves (searched: stock1); Venn's address is reported to have "deprecated beginning by collecting money," placing prayer, study, and personal admonition ahead of fundraising (per Stock's narrative summary of Venn's four points; not independently extracted as a quote record this session).
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: stock1, within the passages read this session) as a formally adopted Society motto; "Church-principle, not high-Church principle" functions, in Stock's own narrative, as the founders' watchword. [cms-q008]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: stock1, within the passages read this session) this session; the roster names Henry Venn (the second of that name, Honorary Secretary from 1841) as a later restater of the Society's principles, but his own restatements fall outside Vol. I's chapters read this session (Vol. I covers 1799-1811 in the section consulted).

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): Resolution 4 (12 April 1799) directs that "a Deputation be sent from this Society to the Archbishop of Canterbury as Metropolitan, the Bishop of London as Diocesan, and the Bishop of Durham as Chairman of the Mission Committee of the Society for Promoting Christian Knowledge, with a copy of the Rules of the Society and a respectful letter" -- placing the new Society's sending activity under the notice of the existing ecclesiastical hierarchy from the outset. ## R — Fruit
- **R1 John Venn (Rector of Clapham)** (PS): Chaired the 12 April 1799 founding meeting and, per Stock, was "the author... of the Rules of the new Society, and of its first Account of itself for the public"; his 18 March 1799 address laid down the Society's guiding principles.
