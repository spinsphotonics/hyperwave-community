# Dossier — Particular Baptist Society for Propagating the Gospel among the Heathen / Baptist Missionary Society (`baptist-missionary-society`)

Note on sourcing: the BMS is a missionary sending society, not a school, so several
template fields designed for an educational institution (Admission, Curriculum, most of
Common Life and Teachers) do not apply to it as such; those fields are answered honestly as
NOT APPLICABLE rather than stretched to fit. This chapter's two charter documents, per the
roster, are Carey's 1792 *Enquiry* (the Society's founding manifesto, quoted for its own
practical proposal of a society) and the Serampore *Form of Agreement* of 7 October 1805
(the field mission's own rule of life and method, quoted in full as Appendix I of Smith
1885). The founding resolutions of 2 October 1792, reprinted in *Periodical Accounts* Vol.
I (1800), are used as the primary record of the Society's actual institution.

## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The Society's own founding resolution names it "The Particular Baptiſt Society for Propagating the Goſpel amongſt the Heathen" (2 Oct. 1792); it is known to later scholarship, and in this book's roster, as the Baptist Missionary Society (BMS). [bms-q009]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PO): A ministers' meeting at Clipstone in 1791 first proposed the question of "attempt[ing] somewhat toward spreading the gospel in the heathen world" [bms-q005]; a further resolution at Nottingham on 31 May 1792 directed that a plan be prepared "for forming a society among ye Baptists for propagating the goſpel among the heathen" [bms-q006]; the Society itself was constituted at a ministers' meeting at Kettering on 2 October 1792, when the ministers "retired to conſult farther on the matter, and to lay a foundation at leaſt for a ſociety" and the founding resolutions were "propoſed, and unanimouſly agreed to" [bms-q007]. The Serampore mission's own rule, the Form of Agreement, was separately "agreed upon at a Meeting of the Brethren at Serampore, on Monday, October 7, 1805." [bms-q011]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO): The committee appointed by Resolution 5 (2 Oct. 1792) was "the Rev. John Ryland, Reynold Hogg, William Carey, John Sutrchff [Sutcliff], and Andrew Fuller," empowered (any three) "to act in carrying into effect the purpoſes of the ſociety"; Reynold Hogg was appointed treasurer and Andrew Fuller secretary. [bms-q010] The full list of subscribers at the founding meeting is also recorded: "John Ryland, Reynold Hogg, John Sutcliff, Andrew Fuller, Abraham Greenwood, Edward Sharman, Joshua Burton, Samuel Pearce, Thomas Blundel, William Heighton, John Eayres, Joseph Timms," whose subscriptions "amounted to" a recorded sum. [bms-q011 (list of subscribers, see sources.csv/pa1800 context)]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PS): Clipstone (1791 proposal), Nottingham (31 May 1792 sermon and resolution), Kettering (2 Oct. 1792 founding meeting), Northampton (31 Oct. and 13 Nov. 1792 follow-up meetings), Northamptonshire generally; the field mission itself at Serampore, near Calcutta, India (from 1800; Form of Agreement, 1805).
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): For this chapter, two documents are treated as the charter: William Carey, *An Enquiry into the Obligations of Christians to Use Means for the Conversion of the Heathens* (Leicester, 1792), document_id `carey1792`; and the *Form of Agreement respecting the Great Principles upon which the Brethren of the Mission at Serampore think it their duty to act*, agreed 7 October 1805, document_id `smith1885` (reprinted in full, Appendix I). The Society's own institution is separately recorded in the 2 October 1792 founding resolutions, document_id `pa1800`. [bms-q001, bms-q007, bms-q011]
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Carey's *Enquiry* proposes: "Suppose a company of serious Christians, ministers and private persons, were to form themselves into a society, and make a number of rules respecting the regulation of the plan, and the persons who are to be employed as missionaries, the means of defraying the expence, &c." [bms-q001] The founding Resolution 1 (2 Oct. 1792) records that the subscribers, "Deſirous of making an effort for the propagation of the goſpel among the heathen, agreeably to what is re-commended in brother Carey's late publication on that subject," "do ſolemnly _ to act in ſociety together for that purpoſe" (an OCR gap stands where a verb, almost certainly "agree," should read; see discrepancies.md item 3). [bms-q008] The Serampore Form of Agreement opens: "THE REDEEMER, in planting us in this heathen nation, rather than in any other, has imposed upon us the cultivation of peculiar qualifications." [bms-q012]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): Carey's proposal follows directly from his argument, in the same *Enquiry*, that "the command of Christ" to preach to all nations remains binding; the practical proposal for a society is introduced as the means "to promote this Work" (the section's own heading, SECT. V). At Clipstone in 1791 the question already put was "Whether it were not practicable, and our bounden duty, to attempt ſomewhat toward ſpreading the goſpel in the heathen world." [bms-q005]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): NOT FOUND as a distinct creedal-subscription clause in the documents read this session (searched: carey1792, pa1800, smith1885); the Society is named "Particular Baptist" in its own founding resolution [bms-q009], identifying its doctrinal position (Calvinistic/Particular-Baptist) by name rather than by a separately quoted confession.
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): Carey proposes the society specifically "amongst the particular baptist denomination," reasoning that "in the present divided state of Christendom, it would be more likely for good to be done by each denomination engaging separately in the work, than if they were to embark in it conjointly." [bms-q002, bms-q003] The 1792 founding resolutions explicitly credit Carey's own pamphlet as their occasion: subscribers act "agreeably to what is re-commended in brother Carey's late publication on that subject." [bms-q008]

## A — Admission
Not applicable in the school sense: the Society appointed and sent missionaries; it did not
admit a student body. NOT FOUND (searched: carey1792, pa1800, smith1885) for a formal
published admission standard for missionary candidates comparable to a school's entrance
requirements.

## C — Curriculum
Not applicable: the Society ran no course of study of its own. The Serampore Form of
Agreement does, however, prescribe a discipline of self-training for missionaries already in
the field: point Ninthly, "It becomes us also to labour with all our might in forwarding
translations of the sacred Scriptures in the languages of Hindoostan." [bms-q014]

## L — Common life
Not applicable in the residential-school sense. The Serampore brethren's mutual life is
addressed by the Form of Agreement rather than by a school's rules of residence: see S4
below (community of goods) and M1/M3 (personal devotion).

## T — Teachers
Not applicable in the school sense. The 1792 committee (Ryland, Hogg, Carey, Sutcliff,
Fuller) functioned as the Society's executive body. [bms-q010]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): Prayer is named first among duties in the *Enquiry*: "One of the first, and most important of those duties which are incumbent upon us, is fervent and united prayer." [bms-q004] The Form of Agreement opens by naming "peculiar qualifications" the missionaries must cultivate. [bms-q012] It closes with a call to entire self-surrender: "Let us give ourselves up unreservedly to this glorious cause. Let us never think that our time, our gifts, our strength, our families, or even the clothes we wear, are our own." [bms-q015]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Denominational self-support and separate action ("each denomination engaging separately in the work" [bms-q003]); raising up a native ministry ("It is only by means of native preachers that we can hope for the universal spread of the Gospel throughout this immense continent" [bms-q013]); Bible translation into the vernacular ("forwarding translations of the sacred Scriptures in the languages of Hindoostan" [bms-q014]); and a community-of-goods financial discipline among the missionaries themselves (see S4).
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): Private property and private trade among the missionaries: "If we give up the resolution which was formed on the subject of private trade, when we first united at Serampore, the Mission is from that hour a lost cause." [bms-q016] The closing section of the Form of Agreement also warns against holding back "time," "gifts," "strength," or possessions as one's own. [bms-q015]
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): NOT FOUND as a formally adopted motto in the documents read this session (searched: carey1792, pa1800, smith1885); the phrase "expect great things from God, attempt great things for God," commonly associated with Carey's 31 May 1792 Nottingham sermon, is a later paraphrase of the sermon's two heads as reported in Periodical Accounts ("(1.) That we should expe# great things -- (2.) That we should attempt great things" [bms-q006, context]) rather than a quotation of that exact wording in the sources read this session; recorded here as UNVERIFIED in the precise phrasing.
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): NOT FOUND (searched: carey1792, pa1800, smith1885) this session.

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): The Society itself, through its committee (Resolution 5, 2 Oct. 1792), was empowered to carry the Society's purposes into effect. [bms-q010]
- **S2 Destinations (regions, countries)** (PS): India (Serampore, near Calcutta, from 1800); the Form of Agreement's own subject is "the work of instructing the Heathen" in that "heathen nation." [bms-q011, bms-q012]
- **S3 Numbers sent, by period, with source** (PS): UNVERIFIED for a signature count on the Form of Agreement itself: a signature list was reported by secondary web sources consulted during scouting (not the fetched text read this session, which excerpted the Agreement's body text, not its closing signatures) as nine names; this is not independently confirmed against smith1885 and is not asserted as fact in this dossier. What is directly attested: the founding 1792 subscription list names twelve individual subscribers plus the five-man committee [bms-q010, bms-q011 context]. NOT FOUND as a running total sent over a longer period in the documents read this session.
- **S4 Support on the field (salary, faith principle, self-support)** (PO): The Form of Agreement records a standing community-of-goods rule: "No private family ever enjoyed a greater portion of happiness... than we have done since we resolved to have all things in common, and that no one should pursue business for his own exclusive advantage," and warns that abandoning "the resolution... formed on the subject of private trade" would make "the Mission... from that hour a lost cause." [bms-q016]
- **S5 Deaths, martyrdoms, or casualties recorded, with source** (PS): NOT FOUND in the documents read this session (searched: carey1792, pa1800, smith1885) as a formally quoted casualty figure.
- **S6 Instructions given to those sent (title, date, document_id)** (PO): The Form of Agreement respecting the Great Principles upon which the Brethren of the Mission at Serampore think it their duty to act, 7 October 1805, document_id `smith1885`, reprinted in full in charter_abridged.md; the brethren themselves "resolve that this Agreement shall be read publicly, at every station, at our three annual meetings." [bms-q011, bms-q017]
