# Discrepancies — basel-mission

1. **Founding year of the mission vs. of the seminary.** The roster's known-facts sheet
   distinguishes "1815" (the Basel Mission itself, [HIGH]) from "seminary 1816" (the training
   house's opening). Ratschiller's 2023 chapter, the only source read this session that gives an
   explicit year for the seminary, states that members of the *Deutsche Christentumsgesellschaft*
   "initiated a seminary for the education of overseas evangelists in Basel in 1815" — i.e. the
   same year as the mission, not 1816. A WebSearch snippet of a Griffith University project page
   (not independently fetched this session — see sources.csv row
   griffith-basel-mission-not-fetched) instead states the seminary "was formally opened on August
   26, 1816." Status: the roster's 1816 date for the seminary's opening is not contradicted by a
   source read this session (Ratschiller's "1815" may describe the society's founding decision to
   have a seminary, not the seminary's first day of instruction), but neither is it confirmed by a
   source read this session. Do not state a specific seminary-opening date more precise than "1816"
   (per the roster) without a source actually opened and read.

2. **The Hausordnung itself was not read.** Every fact in this dossier about the Basel Mission
   seminary's admission criteria, curriculum, and house rules comes from a 2023 English-language
   scholarly chapter that cites and briefly quotes the German-language Hausordnung and related
   Basel Mission Archive documents (by shelfmark, e.g. "BMA, Q-9.31," "BMA, X.III.12"), not from
   the Hausordnung itself. This is a more severe version of the same limitation noted for
   `geneva-academy` and `serampore-college`: there the abridged charter at least contained
   fragments of the founding document quoted at second hand from a source that reproduces
   substantial passages of it; here, only a handful of short phrases (e.g. "to wash his body
   diligently, in order to keep it pure and healthy") are directly quoted from the house rules
   themselves, embedded in the secondary chapter's own analytical prose. A full charter-style
   abridgement of the Hausordnung is not possible from the material fetched this session. This
   is flagged as BLOCKED, not worked around: see charter_abridged.md.

3. **Model for the Hausordnung (Tübingen).** A WebSearch snippet (not independently fetched or
   read this session — see sources.csv row griffith-basel-mission-not-fetched) states that
   Inspector Blumhardt modeled the seminary's Hausordnung on the Evangelisches Stift at Tübingen,
   where he had trained. No source actually opened and read this session confirms or repeats this
   claim. Do not state this connection in the chapter without a source read this session that
   says so.
