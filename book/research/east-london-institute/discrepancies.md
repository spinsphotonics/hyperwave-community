# Discrepancies -- East London Institute for Home and Foreign Missions

Recorded, not resolved, per Rule 9 (`00_README.md`).

## 1. Founding/opening year: 1872 vs. 1873

- Source A: `wideworldourwork00guin` (Fanny Guinness, 1886), p.23 -- "It was the knowledge of facts such as those mentioned in the last chapter that led to the foundation, **in 1872**, of our East London Institute for Home and Foreign Missions." [quote_id eli-q001]
- Source A2: `ourhelpersatwork00guin` (H. Grattan Guinness, [1894]), p.6 -- "Dr. and Mrs. H. Grattan Guinness opened their Missionary College there early **in 1872**, to volunteers of all evangelical denominations..." [quote_id eli-q035], and "Twenty-two years have passed away, and **1872** is exchanged for 1894." [quote_id eli-q037]
- Source B: `notuntousarecord00guinuoft` (H. Grattan Guinness the younger, 1893), p.9-10 -- "they were eventually led...to the estab[l]ishment **in 1873** of a Training Institute in East London" [quote_id eli-q049], and p.30, "The total number of Students trained at Barley [sic, OCR for 'Harley'] College and Doric Lodge since the Inauguration of the Work **in 1873** is 1,316." [quote_id eli-q024]
- Source C (secondary): `drharryguinnessl0000unse` (Mackintosh, [1932]), p.11 -- "In the same year [1873, the year of Harry Guinness's baptism, 29 August 1873] his parents established the East London Institute for Home and Foreign Missions" [quote_id eli-q044].
- Roster's known-facts sheet (`02_institution_roster.md`, "Other dates"): "East London Institute 1873" [HIGH].

Not resolved here. The chapter states both years with their sources rather than silently preferring one. It is possible 1872 marks an informal opening (first applicants received at Stepney Green) and 1873 a more formal "inauguration," but no source read this session states this reconciliation explicitly, so it is not asserted.

## 2. Total number of missionaries sent/trained: differing counts at different dates

These are not necessarily contradictory (they are counts taken in different years and sometimes by different measures -- "now scattered/at work" vs. "total ever trained" vs. "applicant cases considered") but are kept distinct rather than merged:

- 1886 (`wideworldourwork00guin`, p.122): "Between 400 and 500 of such are now scattered throughout the earth" [eli-q017] -- former students currently in the field, as of 1886.
- [1894] (`ourhelpersatwork00guin`, p.6): "800 labourers scattered all over the world, and 120 men and women now in training" [eli-q039]; "The first handful of applicants...has increased to 3,700 [cases]" [eli-q040] -- these are applicant cases considered, not students admitted or sent.
- [1894] (`ourhelpersatwork00guin`, p.5): "over 100 missionaries" sent to China alone, "besides 600 or 700 to other parts of the world" [eli-q038].
- 1893 (`notuntousarecord00guinuoft`, p.30): "1,316" total students trained since 1873 [eli-q024] -- a cumulative training total, not a currently-serving count.
- [1932], secondary (`drharryguinnessl0000unse`, p.11): "no less than fifteen hundred young men and women" sent forth [eli-q044] -- a later, larger cumulative figure, undated as to when the 1,500 mark was reached.

## 3. Founding of "Regions Beyond" magazine: 1887 vs. an earlier date

- `drharryguinnessl0000unse` (Mackintosh, [1932], secondary), p.30: "In 1887 the missionary magazine, Regions Beyond, was begun, under the Editorship of Mrs. Grattan Guinness." [eli-q048]
- A WebSearch result (not a primary source opened this session, and not quotable) independently described a magazine called "The Regions Beyond and Illustrated Missionary News" as begun in 1868, edited by Mrs. Guinness. This may refer to an earlier and differently titled periodical, or the WebSearch summary may itself be imprecise; it was not verified against any primary source read this session, so it is not stated as fact here, and 1887 (from the biography actually opened and quoted) is the only dated claim carried into the chapter, itself SECONDARY.

## 4. Length of course: "two or three years" (1886) vs. "four years" (1893)

- `wideworldourwork00guin` (1886), p.29-30 (Chapter II, narrative context near eli-q001-eli-q003 passages): the founders describe needing to support "each man" for "two or three years of preparation" (paraphrase of the founding-era plan; not separately quote-recorded as it duplicates the sense of the founding discussion already quoted).
- `notuntousarecord00guinuoft` (1893), p.41: "our present course extends over a period of four years" [eli-q025].

These are not treated as a contradiction but as a change over time (the course lengthened between the 1870s-80s and 1893); both years are given with their sources in the Curriculum section rather than one being silently preferred.

## 5. "Timothy Richard" named at the Congo mission (Banza Manteke) is not the well-known China missionary of the same name

`drharryguinnessl0000unse` (secondary), p.39, names a "Mr. Timothy Richard, a former student of Harley House," in charge of the American Baptist Missionary Union station at Banza Manteke, Congo, in 1891 [eli-q047]. This is a different person from the well-known Baptist Missionary Society missionary to China of the same name (Timothy Richard, 1845-1919), who is not stated by any source read this session to have studied at Harley House. The chapter names this individual exactly as the source does, without implying identity with the China missionary, and without further biographical claim beyond what the source states.
