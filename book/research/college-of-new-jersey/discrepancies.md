# Discrepancies — college-of-new-jersey

1. **Text of the first (1746) charter is lost.** Maclean states plainly, writing in 1877, "This
   comprises all that is now positively known respecting this charter, of which neither the
   original nor any copy is to be found" (cnj-q007). All that survives of its actual wording is
   the description given by the parties who received it — "a charter with full and ample
   privileges," under which "equal liberties and privileges are secured to every denomination of
   Christians, any different religious sentiments notwithstanding" (cnj-q004, cnj-q005) — quoted
   at second hand by Maclean from an advertisement and from the recipients' own later statements,
   not from the charter document itself. This is not a disagreement between two sources so much
   as a total loss of the primary text; flagged here per protocol because it constrains what the
   abridged charter in this chapter can be (the 1748 charter only, not both). A WebSearch this
   session found that Princeton University's own Digital Collections and T. J. Wertenbaker's
   *Princeton 1746-1896* (1946, pp. 396-404) are cited elsewhere as printing "the text of the
   charters of 1746 and of 1748," which would appear to contradict Maclean's 1877 statement that
   no copy survives — possibly a copy was located between 1877 and 1946, or Wertenbaker reprints
   only the 1748 charter under a heading naming both years. Neither source was fetched this
   session (see sources.csv, rows `princeton-charter-1748-digital-pul` and
   `wertenbaker-1946-not-fetched`); a human or a follow-up Fetcher pass should resolve whether a
   text of the 1746 charter has since come to light.

2. **F1 founding date shorthand.** The roster row for this institution gives "1746 charter; 1748
   second charter" without further comment on which is "the" founding date. Maclean's own
   account (used throughout this dossier) treats 1746 as the corporate founding (first charter,
   first President, first classes admitted and graduated) and 1748 as a re-chartering that
   superseded the first for legal-security reasons (Hamilton's authority to grant the first
   charter had been questioned) without changing the institution's design or continuity — the
   first graduates, admitted and taught under the 1746 charter, received their degrees at the
   November 1748 Commencement under circumstances Maclean describes as making them "foster-sons
   of the College under the first charter rather than under the second" (paraphrased from the
   passage surrounding cnj-q046; not itself a separate quote record). This is not a contradiction
   between sources, only a nuance the chapter's Founding section should preserve rather than
   flatten into a single date.
