## F — Founding
- **F1 Name(s) of the institution, with dates of each name** (PS): The enterprise was first chartered as "The Chicago Evangelization Society"; upon Moody's death in December 1899 its corporate name was changed to "The Moody Bible Institute of Chicago" in his honor. The school itself was called, at various points in the sources, "Training School," the "Institute," and "Chicago Bible Institute." A volume of lectures delivered there in 1893 was published under the imprint "BIBLE INSTITUTE / Chicago Evangelization Society," showing both names current together. [moody-bible-institute-q002, moody-bible-institute-q012]
- **F2 Founding date(s): decision, opening, charter/incorporation** (PS): Moody delivered an address in Chicago on 22 January 1886 on "City Evangelism" in which he voiced his conviction of the need. The enterprise "was chartered under the name of 'The Chicago Evangelization Society'" following a fund-raising effort (money pledged, preliminary steps taken) narrated just before Moody's four-month Chicago evangelistic campaign that began 31 December 1886. A conference called the "Institute" was held in the Chicago Avenue Church in May 1889, drawing nearly two hundred persons where Moody had expected twenty; "[a]s a result of that conference the Chicago Bible Institute was formed, and formally opened in the fall of that year." The Institute's own building was "formally opened with a week's conference, beginning September 26, 1889." [moody-bible-institute-q011, moody-bible-institute-q002, moody-bible-institute-q010, moody-bible-institute-q003, moody-bible-institute-q013]
- **F3 Founders and first officers (names, roles)** (PO/PS): Dwight L. Moody conceived and drove the plan. Cyrus H. McCormick "at once approved of the plan and gave his check for $10,000.00 to promote it"; he was subsequently joined by John V. Farwell, Elbridge G. Keith, Robert Scott, and E. K. Warren "and others, who contributed to a fund of $250,000.00 for the foundation of the work." Rev. R. A. Torrey, "then a pastor in Minneapolis" and superintendent of the Congregational City Missionary Society of Minneapolis, a graduate of Yale College and Seminary who had also studied at Leipsic and Erlangen, "was called to the position of superintendent" when the building opened, 26 September 1889. [moody-bible-institute-q012, moody-bible-institute-q003, moody-bible-institute-q013]
- **F4 Place(s)** (PO): Chicago. The founding prayer is placed on "a vacant lot, near Chicago Avenue"; the Institute's own building was later erected at 153-163 Institute Place, adjacent to LaSalle and Chicago Avenues. The May 1889 organizing conference was held in the Chicago Avenue Church. [moody-bible-institute-q011, moody-bible-institute-q010]
- **F5 Principal founding document: title, date, language, document_id** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: lifedwight00mood, moodybibleinstit00camp, moodybibleinstit00chic; also searched Internet Archive/HathiTrust/general web for the Chicago Evangelization Society's 1887 articles of incorporation and an 1889-90 prospectus/catalog — see sources.csv rows `ces-charter-1887-not-located` and `mbi-prospectus-1889-90-not-located`). No text of the actual 1887 charter or an early prospectus was located this session. BLOCKED for the literal founding document; see charter_abridged.md for how this gap is handled.
- **F6 Stated purpose, in the founders' words** (PO): Moody's own common expression of the purpose was to train "'gap men' — men who are trained to stand between the laity and the ministers," because he "felt the great need for more lay Christian workers." The Institute's own 1915 restatement of its "objective" was "the training of men and women of all denominations and all lands in the English Bible, gospel music, and practical methods of Christian work, with special reference to soul-winning." [moody-bible-institute-q001, moody-bible-institute-q015]
- **F7 Need or crisis the founders said they were answering** (PO): The "gap men" passage states Moody found "many consecrated men and women read[y] and anxious to do God's will," and "on the other hand... there was plenty of opportunity for them to go to work if they only had the necessary skill and training" — a gap between willing lay workers and trained ones. The 1915 sketch adds that Moody, after his evangelistic tours with Sankey, "realized how few church members were able to use their Bibles in directing a person in the way of salvation," and prayed on a vacant lot "that God would give him that ground for a Bible Training School." The tent/"Little Hell" episode shows the same need translated into practice: when Moody asked at his last Chicago noon meeting "How shall this work be carried on when I am gone?", a hearer called out "Get a tent," and a gospel tent was pitched "in a district so wicked that it was known as 'Little Hell.'" [moody-bible-institute-q001, moody-bible-institute-q011, moody-bible-institute-q009]
- **F8 Doctrinal basis or confession, and who must subscribe** (PO): The 1915 sketch states the Institute "never swerved from the doctrinal basis or the governing purpose of its founder. It still stands four-square on the integrity of the Scriptures as a divine revelation, the Deity and atonement of Jesus Christ, the necessity of the new birth and justification by faith only." No source read this session states that students were required to sign or subscribe this statement individually (as, e.g., the Geneva or CIM documents require); it is stated as the Institute's own institutional standing, not as a subscription clause. [moody-bible-institute-q014]
- **F9 Antecedents the founders cite or imitate (earlier schools, documents, persons)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: lifedwight00mood, moodybibleinstit00camp) — no passage in either source names an earlier school or written plan that Moody consciously imitated in shaping the Institute. The 1915 sketch instead records the reverse influence: "Toronto, Canada, and Glasgow, Scotland, sent representatives to Chicago to study the institution; and now both cities have Bible Institutes after Mr. Moody's model" (not yet formalized into a separate quote record; noted from the fetched text, moodybibleinstit00camp raw lines ~350-354, flagged as follow-up if needed for the Sending/lineage sections).

## A — Admission
- **A1 Minimum and maximum age** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: lifedwight00mood, moodybibleinstit00camp)
- **A2 Prior education required** (PO): Not a uniform requirement; the Institute described "[t]hree special classes of students whose needs the Institute specially aims to meet," the first being "[g]raduates of colleges or theological seminaries who wish to supplement the valuable education received at these schools by a thorough study of the English Bible and methods of aggressive Christian work" — implying prior higher education was welcomed but not required, since the second and third classes describe workers with field experience and people pursuing secular callings. [moody-bible-institute-q008]
- **A3 Testimony of conversion, character, or church membership required** (PO): NOT FOUND as a stated formal requirement in either source read this session (no admission examination or testimony clause is quoted); see A2 for the three classes of intended students, which implies self-selection toward Christian workers rather than a described formal testimony process.
- **A4 Entrance examination (subjects)** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: lifedwight00mood, moodybibleinstit00camp)
- **A5 Women: admitted? in what capacity?** (PO): Women were admitted as full students, with a separate "women's department" and dormitory buildings on LaSalle Avenue; a letter of Moody's own, addressed to "Miss Strong" of the women's department, requests places for his two granddaughters, "and as I have taken some interest in the society, I hope you will give my application a favorable consideration." Charlotte A. Cary served as Superintendent of Women by 1915. [moody-bible-institute-q001 (context), moody-bible-institute-q017 (context, faculty roster reference not separately quoted); direct textual basis is the letter quoted immediately before moody-bible-institute-q001 in text/lifedwight00mood.txt, not separately recorded as its own quote_id this session — flagged as a follow-up formalization]
- **A6 Prior experience required (e.g., preaching, service)** (PO): Not required uniformly; the second of the "[t]hree special classes" the Institute aimed to meet was "[m]inisters, evangelists, returned missionaries, and other Christian workers who have had actual experience in the field, and who wish to give some time to further study," implying prior experience was welcomed in some students but the other two classes (new graduates; those in secular callings) had none. [moody-bible-institute-q008]

## C — Curriculum
- **C1 Length of course** (PO): "Two years of twelve months each are required for the course, but, as it proceeds in a circle, students can enter at any time and by remaining two years complete the full course." The 1915 sketch separately states that the same courses taught in the evening department "requires a year longer to complete" than the day department. [moody-bible-institute-q004]
- **C2 Subjects, in the order taught** (PO): "The system embraces a thorough doctrinal, analytical, and book study of the English Bible under the tuition of resident instructors," supplemented by lectures from visiting Bible teachers "on topics to which they have individually given the closest attention," all "based upon the most careful and scholarly study of the Word." A musical department gave "careful training in the art of singing the Gospel." The 1915 sketch names distinct courses by 1913-15: the Bible Course (director William Evans), Music Course (D. B. Towner), Practical Work Course (Wm. Wallace Ketchum), Missionary Course (Edward A. Marshall), Sunday School Course (E. O. Sellers, covering pedagogy, psychology, and Sunday-school administration in addition to Bible, Missions, and Evangelism), and an English Course (Hanna May Thomas). [moody-bible-institute-q004, moody-bible-institute-q015]
- **C3 Structure (classes, years, terms)** (PO): No fixed-term class structure is described for the day course beyond the two-year, twelve-months-a-year "circle" that students may enter at any time (C1). By 1915 the Institute had also added an Extension Department (1897), a Correspondence Department (1901, offering six named courses: Synthetic Bible Study, Bible Doctrine, Practical Christian Work, Bible Chapter Summary, the Introductory Bible Course, and the Scofield Correspondence Course), and Evening Classes (1903, the same courses as the day department but requiring a year longer). These later departmental additions and their founding years are drawn from the 1915 sketch's own narrative and are not yet formalized into separate quote records this session; flagged as a follow-up if the Curriculum section needs them directly quoted. [context: moodybibleinstit00camp, raw lines ~187-270]
- **C4 Languages taught, and level required** (PO): English only is named for the Bible course ("the English Bible"); no source read this session mentions Hebrew, Greek, Latin, or any language beyond English as part of the course of study. [moody-bible-institute-q004]
- **C5 Set texts and authors** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: lifedwight00mood, moodybibleinstit00camp) — no source names a syllabus of specific set books or authors beyond "the English Bible" itself.
- **C6 Preaching or practical instruction (form, frequency)** (PO): "The morning hours are spent in the class-room, and the afternoons and evenings are divided between study and practical work among the unconverted. Rescue mission work, house-to-house visitation, children's meetings, women's meetings, jail work, inquiry-meeting work, church visitation — every form of effort which can be developed in the heart of a great and wicked city is here supplied." Tents and street meetings were also used, and "[a]t one time thirty-five cottage meetings were being held every week." [moody-bible-institute-q005]
- **C7 Examinations and their form** (PO): NOT FOUND (searched: lifedwight00mood, moodybibleinstit00camp) — the Correspondence Department is stated to grant "certificates... according to the progress made" (see C8), implying some form of assessed progress, but no examination procedure is described for the resident/day course.
- **C8 Degree, certificate, license, or ordination at completion** (PO): No degree is described for the day course. The Correspondence Department (added 1901) is the one department for which certification is explicitly named: "certificates are granted according to the progress made." [context: moodybibleinstit00camp, raw lines ~215-218, not yet formalized as a separate quote_id]
- **C9 Weekly or daily timetable (table if given)** (PO): No hour-by-hour timetable is given; the only schedule division stated is the morning/afternoon-and-evening split described under C6 (class-room study in the morning; study and practical work in the afternoons and evenings). [moody-bible-institute-q005]

## T — Teachers
- **T1 Number and titles of teachers (with years)** (PS): At the 1889 opening, Rev. R. A. Torrey (pastor, Minneapolis; Yale College and Seminary; further study at Leipsic and Erlangen) "was called to the position of superintendent." By 1915, the Educational Department was headed by Dean James M. Gray, D.D., with named directors for each course (William Evans, Bible Course; D. B. Towner, Music Course; Wm. Wallace Ketchum, Practical Work Course; Edward A. Marshall, Missionary Course; E. O. Sellers, Evening Department/Sunday School Course), plus Howard W. Pope (Superintendent of Men) and Charlotte A. Cary (Superintendent of Women). When Torrey "felt called into world-wide evangelistic work" after Moody's 1899 death, "the board of trustees planned to conserve and broaden the energies of the Institute by placing them in control of an executive committee" of Henry P. Crowell, James M. Gray, and E. K. Warren. [moody-bible-institute-q003, moody-bible-institute-q013, moody-bible-institute-q017]

## M — Emphasis
- **M1 What the founders said mattered most (their words)** (PO): "I believe we have got to have 'gap men' — men who are trained to stand between the laity and the ministers," was, the biography states, "a common expression of Mr. Moody's." The Institute was later described as having been "called the 'West Point' of Christian work. It endeavors to embody all the principles which characterize successful Christian workers. Study and work go hand in hand." The 1915 sketch restates the doctrinal core: "the integrity of the Scriptures as a divine revelation, the Deity and atonement of Jesus Christ, the necessity of the new birth and justification by faith only." [moody-bible-institute-q001, moody-bible-institute-q007, moody-bible-institute-q014]
- **M2 Distinctive stress (doctrine, piety, preaching, languages, evangelism, faith principle, self-support, other)** (PO): Practical, hands-on evangelistic work paired with English Bible study, aimed at "soul-winning" specifically (see F6, C6, C2); the tent/"Little Hell" episode and the enumerated practical-work forms (rescue missions, jails, house-to-house, cottage meetings) show the stress fell on direct evangelism among the unconverted poor of the city, not on academic or classical study. [moody-bible-institute-q001, moody-bible-institute-q005, moody-bible-institute-q009, moody-bible-institute-q015]
- **M3 What the founders explicitly rejected or warned against** (PO): NOT FOUND as an explicit rejection clause (contrast e.g. CIM's "spiritual not carnal" or Geneva's Beza on idle spectacle) — no passage read this session has Moody or the Institute state what it explicitly rejected; only the positive statement of purpose (gap men; soul-winning) was located.
- **M4 Motto or watchword, with earliest source** (PS): "The Bible Institute has been called the 'West Point' of Christian work" — stated as a description applied to the Institute (by others), not as a motto the founders themselves coined; no earlier or more formal motto was located. [moody-bible-institute-q007]
- **M5 Later leaders' restatements of the emphasis (with dates)** (PS): The 1915 sketch (26 years after the 1889 opening, under President H. P. Crowell and Dean James M. Gray) restates that the Institute "never swerved from the doctrinal basis or the governing purpose of its founder," quoted in full at F8/M1. [moody-bible-institute-q014]

## S — Sending
- **S1 Sending mechanism (licensing body, mission board, church commendation, self-sending)** (PO): No formal licensing or commendation procedure is described. The Institute's own Extension Department (added 1897) "undertakes to fill calls for teachers, evangelists, pastors and Christian workers of almost every kind," functioning, in the sketch's words, as "a connecting link between the work on different fields and the workers in preparation, or who have just completed their course in training"; 582 such calls were received in 1913 alone "and they were supplied so far as there were students capable of accepting the work."
