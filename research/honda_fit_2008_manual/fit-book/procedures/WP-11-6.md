Procedure ID: P-WP11-6
Chapter: 27 (Brakes: inspection, pads, rotors, fluid)
Title: Inspect the front brake pads through the wheel
Skill level: 1 (anyone — no wheel removal needed for this quick check)
Time: 10 minutes for a first-timer (both front wheels)

Tools:
- Flashlight
- Optional: a ruler or a pad-thickness gauge

Parts and supplies:
- None (this is inspection only, not replacement)

Safety warnings:
- Always let the brakes cool before you get close to the wheel if you just drove — the caliper and rotor can be very hot.
- Do not touch the wheel or brake parts with bare hands right after driving.

Steps:
1. Park on level ground and set the parking brake.
2. Turn the steering wheel toward you so you can see through the wheel spokes at that corner.
3. Shine the flashlight through the spokes at the caliper, the metal bracket that squeezes the pads onto the rotor.
4. Look for the pad material pressed against the flat, shiny rotor surface on each side of the caliper.
5. Estimate the pad's thickness; a healthy pad is clearly thicker than a thin metal backing plate.
6. Compare the inside and outside pad if you can see both; they should wear about evenly.
7. Repeat steps 2 through 6 on the other front wheel.
8. Listen for a metallic screeching sound the next time you brake gently; the manual says this warns you the pads need replacing.

How you know it worked: You can see both pads on each front wheel and estimate their thickness, or you learn you cannot see enough and need to remove the wheel or have a shop check them (see P-WP11-7).

Common mistakes:
- Only checking one side of the caliper and missing uneven wear.
- Mistaking rust or dust on the rotor for pad material.
- Ignoring a screeching sound because it goes away when you press harder.
