Chapter: 43 (Starter, alternator, ignition switch)
Title: Replace the starter

RESEARCH FINDING: this book could not find a plain, factory-style "remove and replace the
starter" page for this generation (the one service-manual-style source found, S-WP14-C, covers
bench overhaul/testing of the starter's internal parts, not the R&R job) — so the steps below
come from community DIY writeups for this exact car (community-reported, Tier 3/4). The starter
sits low on the driver's side of the engine, and reaching its top mounting bolt is widely
reported as the hardest single part of this job.

Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care)
Time: 90 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22), if working from underneath
- 10 mm socket for most of the bolts and hose/harness brackets [Source: S-WP14-E]
- 14 mm socket for the top starter mounting bolt, with a long extension and a swivel/universal joint — reported as the single most useful tool for this job [Source: S-WP14-E]
- Basic wrench set for the battery cables

Parts and supplies:
- New or remanufactured starter motor for the L15A1 engine — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F

Safety warnings:
- Always disconnect the negative battery terminal first, then the positive terminal, before starting this job.
- Always wear eye protection.
- Do not let a wrench touch both a positive battery/starter terminal and any grounded metal at the same time; this can cause a dangerous short and burn.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.

Steps:
1. Disconnect the negative battery terminal, then the positive terminal [Source: S-WP14-E].
2. Remove the two 10 mm bolts holding the wiring harness to the intake manifold.
3. Remove the 10 mm bolt for the dipstick tube bracket.
4. Remove the 10 mm bolt on the starter that holds a nearby hose bracket.
5. Detach the radiator hose holder from the starter bracket [Source: S-WP14-E].
6. Disconnect the electrical connectors from the starter's "S" (signal) and "B" (battery) terminals.
7. Remove the starter's mounting bolts, working the hard-to-reach top bolt with your extension and swivel joint.
8. Guide the starter out from below or through the engine bay, whichever your access allows.
9. Compare the new starter to the old one to confirm it matches before installing.
10. Guide the new starter into place and install its mounting bolts, torquing them per your parts' instructions (Honda torque value UNVERIFIED this pass).
11. Reconnect the electrical connectors to the "S" and "B" terminals.
12. Reattach the radiator hose holder to the starter bracket.
13. Reinstall the dipstick tube bracket bolt and the two wiring harness bolts at the intake manifold.
14. Reconnect the positive battery terminal, then the negative terminal.
15. Start the car to confirm the new starter cranks the engine smoothly.

How you know it worked: The engine cranks over promptly and smoothly when you turn the key or press start, with no grinding, clicking, or slow/labored cranking.

Common mistakes:
- Connecting the battery terminals in the wrong order (always negative off first, positive on last).
- Forcing the top mounting bolt without a swivel joint, stripping it or the bracket.
- Skipping the "S" vs. "B" terminal identification and swapping the connectors.
- Not fully securing the harness and hose brackets back in their original positions, leading to rattles or chafed wiring later.
