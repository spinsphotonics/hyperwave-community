Chapter: 41 (Suspension: struts, shocks, sway bar links, bushings)
Title: Replace the rear shocks

RESEARCH FINDING: unlike the front strut, the rear "damper" (shock) on this car's rear axle
beam does NOT need a spring compressor — the coil spring sits separately in the axle beam, not
wrapped around the shock itself. This is confirmed directly from a real service-manual-style
page for this car (Source: S-WP14-B, hfitinfo.com "Rear Damper Replacement," Removal and
Installation pages), which shows the shock unbolting at top and bottom with a floor jack used
only to support the axle beam's ride height while the bolts come out — no spring compression
tool appears anywhere in the procedure. This makes the rear shock a meaningfully easier job than
the front strut, which is why this book gives it its own, separate card with full steps.

Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care)
Time: 60 minutes for a first-timer (both rear shocks)

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- A floor jack, in addition to the jack stands, to support the axle beam while you work
- Socket set including a 14 mm (or similar) socket for the shock's top and bottom mounting fasteners (exact size UNVERIFIED for this car; confirm at the fastener before buying)
- A hex wrench, to hold the shock shaft still while loosening the top nut
- Torque wrench

Parts and supplies:
- New rear shocks (sold in pairs is common practice, even if only one side leaks) — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F
- New self-locking nut for the shock shaft top mount; Honda's own procedure calls for a new one every time, not a reused one [Source: S-WP14-B]
- New damper mounting bolt at the bottom, per the same source, rather than reusing the old one

Safety warnings:
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands, and keep the floor jack only as a support for raising/lowering the axle beam, not as your only support.
- Always wear eye protection when working under the car.
- Do not let the shock hang loose and strike the fuel tank, brake lines, or wiring when you compress it by hand to remove it — control its movement.
- Always be careful not to damage the body when maneuvering the shock in and out of its mounting location [Source: S-WP14-B].

Steps:
1. Raise the rear of the car and support it on jack stands, then remove both rear wheels.
2. Remove the wire guide grommet from both sides of the axle beam.
3. Position a floor jack under the lower spring seat on the axle beam and raise it slightly until the suspension begins to compress.
4. Remove the damper mounting bolt that connects the axle beam to the bottom of the shock.
5. Remove the access panel from the cargo area's side trim to reach the top mount.
6. Hold the shock shaft still with a hex wrench and remove the self-locking top nut.
7. Remove the top mounting washer and rubber mount.
8. Compress the shock by hand and lift it out of the car, taking care not to scratch the body.
9. Repeat steps 1 through 8 on the other side if replacing both.
10. Fit the rubber mount onto the new shock's top and position the shock between the body and axle beam.
11. Raise the floor jack slowly until the bottom mounting hole lines up, then loosely fit the new bottom mounting bolt.
12. Raise the axle beam with the jack until the car's rear just lifts off the jack stands, then torque the bottom mounting bolt to 40 lbf-ft (54 N-m) [Source: S-WP14-B].
13. Fit the top rubber mount, washer, and a new self-locking nut onto the shock shaft.
14. Hold the shaft with the hex wrench and torque the top nut to spec (exact torque UNVERIFIED for this pass; see Appendix C for any later confirmation).
15. Reinstall the access panel, the wire guide grommets, and both rear wheels.
16. Lower the car and torque the wheel nuts to 80 lbf-ft (108 N-m) in a crisscross pattern.

How you know it worked: The rear of the car sits level and at a normal ride height, there is no clunking over bumps, and the car does not bounce excessively or sag to one side afterward.

Common mistakes:
- Reusing the old self-locking top nut or bottom bolt instead of the new ones Honda's own procedure calls for.
- Not supporting the axle beam with the floor jack before removing the bottom bolt, letting the beam drop suddenly.
- Forgetting to raise the axle beam back to ride height before torquing the bottom bolt, which can pre-load the mount incorrectly.
