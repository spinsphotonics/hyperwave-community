Procedure ID: P-WP14-7
Chapter: 42 (Cooling system: thermostat, radiator, water pump)
Title: Replace the radiator

Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care)
Time: 120 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- Basic socket and wrench set
- A clean drain pan sized for your coolant capacity (see Appendix B)
- Pliers for hose clamps

Parts and supplies:
- New radiator for the 2008 Fit — confirm automatic vs. manual transmission fitment, since the automatic's radiator has extra ATF cooler hose connections [Source: S-WP14-A]; part number UNVERIFIED this pass
- New radiator cap, if the old one is worn
- New O-rings for reassembly, if reusing any brackets or fan shrouds [Source: S-WP14-A]
- Fresh Honda-type coolant (see Appendix B for type and capacity)

Safety warnings:
- Always let the engine cool completely before draining coolant or disconnecting hoses.
- Do not open the radiator cap while the engine is warm or running.
- Always wear eye protection when draining or refilling coolant.
- Do not let coolant contact your skin for long periods or leave a spill where pets or children can reach it.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.
- Always keep your hands clear of the electric radiator fan; it can switch on even with the engine off if the coolant is still hot.

Steps:
1. Make sure the engine is completely cool before starting.
2. Raise the car and support it on jack stands, if needed to reach the lower hose and drain plug.
3. Drain the engine coolant into your drain pan.
4. Disconnect the ECT sensor connector near the radiator and its harness clamp.
5. If your car has A/C, disconnect the A/C compressor clutch connector and its harness clamps out of the way.
6. Disconnect the lower radiator hose.
7. On an automatic transmission car, disconnect the ATF cooler hoses and plug both the hose and the line to keep dirt out.
8. Lower the car (if raised) and remove the coolant reservoir.
9. Disconnect the radiator fan motor connector and, if equipped, the A/C condenser fan motor connector; remove their harness clamps.
10. Disconnect the upper radiator hose.
11. Remove the radiator's upper mounting brackets.
12. Lift the radiator up and out; if equipped with A/C, remove the condenser fan shroud first.
13. Remove the fan shroud, radiator cap, ECT sensor, and drain plug from the old radiator.
14. Reuse the ones that are still good, or transfer them per your new radiator's instructions.
15. Fit new O-rings and assemble the fan shroud, cap, sensor, and drain plug onto the new radiator [Source: S-WP14-A].
16. Set the new radiator in place, making sure its lower rubber cushions seat securely.
17. Reinstall the upper mounting brackets.
18. Reconnect the upper radiator hose.
19. Reconnect the fan motor connector(s) and harness clamps.
20. Reinstall the coolant reservoir.
21. Raise the car again if needed, then reconnect the lower radiator hose.
22. On an automatic, remove the plugs and reconnect the ATF cooler hoses.
23. Reconnect the ECT sensor connector and, if equipped, the A/C compressor clutch connector, with their harness clamps.
24. Lower the car.
25. Refill the radiator with the correct coolant and bleed air from the system per your car's fill procedure.
26. Clean up any spilled coolant immediately.
27. Start the engine, let it reach normal operating temperature, and check every connection for leaks.

How you know it worked: The temperature gauge/indicator stays in the normal range during a test drive, the radiator fan cycles on and off normally, and there are no coolant leaks or ATF leaks (automatic cars) at any hose or fitting.

Common mistakes:
- Forgetting to plug the disconnected ATF cooler line on an automatic, spilling ATF or letting dirt into the transmission cooling circuit.
- Not letting the lower cushions seat properly, leaving the radiator sitting crooked and stressing a hose.
- Skipping the bleed-air step and driving with an air pocket in the system, causing false overheating readings.
- Working on a warm engine and getting burned by pressurized hot coolant.
