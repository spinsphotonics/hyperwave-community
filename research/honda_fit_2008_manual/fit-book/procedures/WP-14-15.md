Title: Replace a window regulator

RESEARCH FINDING and YEAR-CONFIRMATION CAUTION: the only detailed, bolt-by-bolt window
regulator writeup this book could find (Source: S-WP14-E, youcanic.com) is explicitly written
for the 2007-2013 Honda Fit/Jazz "2nd Gen," i.e. the GE chassis (2009-2013) — a different car
from this book's 2007-2008 GD3, per this project's D8 rule. A genuinely first-generation
(GD 01-08) FitFreak.net thread confirms owners of a 2007 Fit DO replace a snapped window
regulator cable themselves (Source: S-WP14-D), which corroborates that this is a real, DIY-able
job on this exact car, but it does not independently confirm the specific bolt count or torque
below. This card's overall shape (door panel off, glass out, regulator out, new one in, 10 mm
bolts) is therefore Tier 3, YEAR-UNCONFIRMED for the exact hardware count, corroborated only
that the job itself is real and DIY-able for GD3 by a Tier 4 GD3-specific source.

Skill level: 2 (needs basic tools)
Time: 90 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- A door/trim panel removal tool kit (plastic pry tools)
- 10 mm socket wrench or nut driver (bolt count/size not independently confirmed for GD3 — see finding above; confirm yours before ordering hardware)
- Basic screwdriver set

Parts and supplies:
- New window regulator (with or without motor, depending on what failed) for this specific door and side — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F

Safety warnings:
- Always wear gloves; broken window glass and sharp door-frame edges can cut your hands.
- Do not remove the window glass while it is only partially supported; make sure it cannot slide down and shatter while the door panel is off.
- Always disconnect the window regulator motor's electrical connector before handling the regulator, to avoid an accidental pinch if the motor is still powered.

Steps:
1. Remove the door panel and the plastic watershield behind it.
2. Move the window glass until its mounting fasteners are accessible.
3. Remove the glass's mounting fasteners and carefully pull the glass out through the window slot, supporting it the whole time.
4. Remove the bolts holding the old regulator to the door.
5. The related generation's writeup reports roughly two bolts near the top and three near the bottom.
6. Confirm your own car's exact count before assuming this.
7. Disconnect the electrical connector for the window regulator motor.
8. Carry the old regulator out of the door.
9. Compare the new regulator to the old one to confirm it matches before installing.
10. Place the new regulator inside the door and reconnect the motor's electrical connector.
11. Line up the regulator to its original position.
12. Reinstall the mounting bolts, tightening them evenly.
13. Reinstall the window glass into its mounting fasteners.
14. Reinstall the watershield and the door panel.
15. Test the window's full up-and-down travel, and its auto up/down function if equipped, before considering the job done.

How you know it worked: The window moves smoothly through its full range with no grinding, binding, or unusual noise, and it stops properly at both the fully up and fully down positions.

Common mistakes:
- Letting the glass slide down and shatter while it is unsupported with the door panel off.
- Reconnecting the motor while it is still receiving power, risking a pinched finger if it activates unexpectedly.
- Not reinstalling the watershield correctly, allowing water into the door and, eventually, into the car's interior.
- Assuming the exact bolt count from a different Fit generation's writeup without checking your own door first.
