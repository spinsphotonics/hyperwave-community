Procedure ID: P-WP06-1
Chapter: 12
Title: Start on a hill (manual transmission)
Skill level: 1 (anyone — no tools, just practice)
Time: 15-20 minutes to learn, in a quiet empty spot with a gentle slope
Tools:
None required.
Parts and supplies:
None required.
Safety warnings:
Always practice this first in an empty parking lot or quiet street with a gentle slope,
not on a busy or steep hill.
Always keep your right foot ready to move to the brake pedal in case the car starts to
roll backward.
Do not practice on a hill with another car stopped close behind you until you are
confident you will not roll back.
Always turn on your hazard lights if you stall on a hill and are blocking traffic, then
try again.
Do not rest your foot on the clutch pedal once you are moving; this wears the clutch out
faster.
Steps:
1. Come to a full stop on the hill using the brake pedal.
2. Pull the parking brake up firmly with your right hand.
3. Push the clutch pedal all the way to the floor.
4. Shift the lever into first gear.
5. Move your right foot from the brake pedal to the gas pedal.
6. Press the gas gently while slowly letting the clutch pedal rise until you feel the
engine pull against the car.
7. When you feel the car want to move forward, release the parking brake fully.
8. Keep easing the clutch pedal up while giving a little more gas so the engine does not
bog down or stall.
9. Once the car is moving smoothly forward, finish releasing the clutch pedal all the way.
How you know it worked: The car moves forward up the hill smoothly, does not roll
backward at any point, and the engine does not stall.
Common mistakes:
Releasing the parking brake before the clutch has caught, so the car rolls backward.
Letting the clutch pedal up too fast, which stalls the engine.
Giving too little gas, which also stalls the engine.
Holding the clutch pedal partway up ("riding the clutch") for too long, which wears the
clutch out faster.
Forgetting to fully release the parking brake once moving, which can overheat the rear
brakes.
