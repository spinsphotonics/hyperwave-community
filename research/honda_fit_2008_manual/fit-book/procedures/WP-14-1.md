Chapter: 40 (Brake job, full)
Title: Replace the front brake pads and rotors

NOTE: This card extends procedures/WP-11-7.md ("Replace the front brake pads"), which already
covers a pads-only job. Everything in WP-11-7's tools/parts/safety/steps applies here too; this
card adds the rotor-removal and rotor-installation steps needed for a full pad-and-rotor job,
per RESEARCH_PLAN.md's instruction to reuse rather than re-research WP-11's brake findings.

Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care)
Time: 90 minutes for a first-timer (both front wheels, pads and rotors)

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- Lug wrench and torque wrench (80 lbf-ft / 108 N-m for wheel nuts) [Source: S-023, F-WP10-001]
- Socket or wrench for the caliper slide-pin bolts and the caliper mounting-bracket bolts (sizes UNVERIFIED for this exact car — check yours before buying; see WP-11-7)
- C-clamp or dedicated brake piston tool
- Rubber mallet, to loosen a rotor stuck to the hub by rust
- Wire brush, brake-parts cleaner, and a small amount of anti-seize compound for the hub face
- Wheel chocks

Parts and supplies:
- New front brake pads for the 2008 Fit base trim — part number UNVERIFIED this pass (ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F)
- New front brake rotors — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; buy rotors sold specifically for the 2008 Fit's 14-inch wheel/base-trim front brake, not a generic size
- Fresh Honda Heavy Duty Brake Fluid DOT 3, in case you need to top off the reservoir [Source: S-001/S-023]
- High-temperature brake grease for the pad's metal backing and the slide pins

Safety warnings:
- Always wear eye protection. Brake dust, rust flakes, and springs under tension can hurt your eyes.
- Do not breathe brake dust; never use compressed air or a dry brush on brake parts. Use brake-parts cleaner instead.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.
- Always work on one side at a time and leave the other wheel on the ground so you have a reference for how parts go back together.
- Do not let the caliper hang by its brake hose; support or hang it so the hose has no weight on it.
- Do not resurface or reuse a rotor that is cracked, deeply grooved, or thinner than the minimum thickness stamped on the rotor's edge; replace it instead.
- Always pump the brake pedal several times before driving, until it feels firm, before you rely on the brakes again.

Steps:
1. Loosen the front wheel nuts, then raise and support the car on jack stands.
2. Remove the wheel and set it aside.
3. Remove the caliper slide-pin bolts and lift the caliper off the bracket; hang it so it does not pull on the hose.
4. Remove the two caliper mounting-bracket bolts and lift the bracket off the knuckle.
5. Check the rotor's edge for a minimum-thickness number stamped into the metal; note it before removing the rotor.
6. If the rotor is stuck to the hub, tap it with a rubber mallet near the center, not on the braking surface.
7. Pull the old rotor off the hub studs.
8. Clean rust and debris off the hub face with a wire brush; wipe it clean.
9. Apply a thin coat of anti-seize to the hub face only, keeping it off the braking surface.
10. Slide the new rotor onto the hub studs.
11. Clean the caliper bracket and slide pins with a wire brush and brake-parts cleaner.
12. Reinstall the caliper bracket and torque its mounting bolts (see Appendix C; community-reported at 80 lbf-ft, not independently confirmed for this car — F-WP10-005).
13. Use the C-clamp against the old pad or a block of wood to push the caliper piston back into its bore.
14. Apply a thin coat of brake grease to the new pads' backing and to the slide pins.
15. Place the new pads into the bracket.
16. Slide the caliper back over the new pads and reinstall the slide-pin bolts.
17. Reinstall the wheel and snug the nuts by hand.
18. Lower the car, then torque the wheel nuts to 80 lbf-ft (108 N-m) in a crisscross pattern.
19. Repeat steps 1 through 18 on the other front wheel.
20. Pump the brake pedal several times with the engine off until it feels firm before driving.
21. Check the brake fluid reservoir level and top it off if needed.
22. Drive slowly at first and test the brakes gently in a safe, empty area before returning to normal driving.

How you know it worked: The brake pedal feels firm (not soft or spongy), the car stops evenly with no pulling to one side, there is no grinding, squealing, or pulsing under braking, and the rotors run true (no visible wobble) when you spin the wheel by hand off the ground.

Common mistakes:
- Getting anti-seize or grease on the rotor's braking surface or the pad linings, which causes squealing or reduced stopping power.
- Reusing a rotor that is warped or below its minimum thickness instead of replacing it.
- Pushing the piston back without first opening the brake fluid reservoir cap slightly, which can push fluid out or damage a seal.
- Forgetting to support the caliper, stretching or kinking the brake hose.
- Skipping the "pump the pedal first" step and finding the pedal goes to the floor on your first drive.
