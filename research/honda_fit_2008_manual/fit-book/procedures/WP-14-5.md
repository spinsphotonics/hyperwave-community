Procedure ID: P-WP14-5
Chapter: 41 (Suspension: struts, shocks, sway bar links, bushings)
Title: Replace sway bar end links and the rear stabilizer bushing

RESEARCH FINDING: this book distinguishes two different, much smaller jobs from a full
stabilizer (sway) bar swap. Replacing the front stabilizer BAR itself on this car requires
dropping the front subframe and the steering gearbox (Source: S-WP14-B, hfitinfo.com "Front
Stabilizer Bar Replacement") — that full-bar job is shop-recommended (skill level 4) and is not
covered step-by-step here. The two jobs below — end links and the rear stabilizer bushing — are
much simpler wear-item swaps that do not require dropping the subframe, and are the jobs most
owners mean by "sway bar links" and "bushings."

Skill level: 2 (needs basic tools)
Time: 90 minutes for a first-timer (both front end links); add 30 minutes for the rear
stabilizer bushing if doing it in the same session

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- Combination wrench and socket set for the end-link nuts (exact size UNVERIFIED for this car)
- A hex key or Allen wrench, to hold the end-link stud still while loosening its nut
- Vice grips, as a backup if a stud spins instead of the nut loosening
- Penetrating oil, for rusted end-link threads
- A cutting tool (rotary tool with a cutting wheel), only as a last resort for a seized nut

Parts and supplies:
- New front sway bar end links, sold as a pair — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F
- New rear stabilizer bushing, if replacing it — part number UNVERIFIED this pass
- Silicone spray (Honda calls for P/N 08209-0001 or equivalent) to ease the rear bushing into place [Source: S-WP14-B]

Safety warnings:
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.
- Always wear eye protection, especially when using penetrating oil or a cutting tool on rusted fasteners.
- Do not use a cutting wheel near brake lines, fuel lines, or wiring; identify what is nearby before you cut a stuck fastener.
- Always cover nearby electrical connectors before using silicone spray, since silicone residue can interfere with switches and sensors [Source: S-WP14-B].

Steps — front sway bar end links:
1. Raise the front of the car and support it on jack stands, with both front wheels off the ground.
2. Spray the end-link nuts with penetrating oil and let it sit a few minutes.
3. Hold the end-link stud still with a hex key while loosening the nut with a wrench.
4. Work the box (closed) end of the wrench against the nut.
5. If the stud spins instead, grip its shoulder (between the rubber boot and the bar) with vice grips.
6. If a nut is fully seized, cut it off carefully with a cutting wheel rather than forcing it.
7. Remove the old end link from both its top (strut or knuckle) and bottom (sway bar) connection points.
8. Fit the new end link, starting the top and bottom nuts by hand.
9. Check the new part's instructions for suspension position: unloaded (wheel off the ground) or loaded to normal ride height (jack under the control arm).
10. Position the suspension accordingly.
11. Tighten both nuts to snug.
12. Repeat steps 2 through 11 on the other side.
13. Lower the car and check that both end links move freely with the suspension through its travel.

Steps — rear stabilizer bushing:
14. Raise the rear of the car and support it on jack stands.
15. Remove the old stabilizer bushing from the axle beam.
16. Apply silicone spray to the new bushing to ease installation.
17. Align the new bushing's end face to the center of its mounting hole and press it into the axle beam.
18. Make sure the stabilizer bar seats securely into the bushing's groove and does not protrude past the end of the axle beam [Source: S-WP14-B].

How you know it worked: There is no clunking or rattling from the front corners over bumps or during hard cornering, the end links show no play when you wiggle them by hand, and the rear bushing sits flush with no visible gap or twist.

Common mistakes:
- Forcing a seized end-link nut without penetrating oil first, snapping the stud.
- Tightening end links fully with the suspension hanging free instead of at normal ride height, which can pre-load the rubber bushings incorrectly.
- Getting silicone spray on nearby electrical connectors or switches.
- Mistaking a full stabilizer BAR replacement (subframe-drop job) for this simpler end-link/bushing job — if your noise or looseness is in the bar's own mounting bushings on the subframe itself, not the end links, that is the bigger, shop-recommended job.
