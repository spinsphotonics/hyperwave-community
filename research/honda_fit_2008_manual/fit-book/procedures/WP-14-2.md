Procedure ID: P-WP14-2
Chapter: 40 (Brake job, full)
Title: Inspect and replace the rear brake drums

NOTE: This card extends procedures/WP-11-8.md ("Replace the rear brake shoes (drum brakes)"),
which already confirms the 2008 Fit's rear brakes are DRUM, not disc, and covers the shoe swap
itself in full. This card covers only what WP-11-8 does not: measuring drum wear and swapping
the drum. Do the shoe replacement steps in WP-11-8 in the same session as this card, since the
drum has to come off to reach the shoes anyway.

Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care)
Time: 20 minutes for a first-timer, added on top of WP-11-8's shoe job (both rear wheels)

Tools:
- Everything listed in WP-11-8
- A drum micrometer or a machine shop visit, to measure drum inside diameter
- A vernier or dial caliper, as a rougher home check if you do not have a drum micrometer

Parts and supplies:
- New rear brake drums, if worn beyond the maximum diameter stamped on the drum — part number UNVERIFIED this pass (ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F)

Safety warnings:
- Always wear eye protection when inspecting or cleaning a drum.
- Do not reinstall a drum that is cracked, deeply scored, or worn past the maximum diameter stamped on its outer face; replace it instead.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.

Steps:
1. Follow WP-11-8, steps 1 through 3, to raise the car, remove the wheel, and pull the drum.
2. Look for a maximum-diameter number cast or stamped into the drum's outer face.
3. Measure the drum's inside diameter with a micrometer or caliper at several points around its circumference.
4. If any measurement exceeds the stamped maximum diameter, or the surface has deep grooves or cracks, plan to replace both rear drums as a pair.
5. If the drum is within limits but glazed or lightly scored, a machine shop can resurface it instead of replacing it.
6. Ask the shop if resurfacing is worth it compared to a new drum's price.
7. If replacing, transfer nothing from the old drum; only the shoes, springs, and adjuster carry over, per WP-11-8.
8. Continue with WP-11-8, steps 4 through 21, using the new (or resurfaced) drum in place of the old one.

How you know it worked: The new or resurfaced drum's inside surface is smooth and free of grooves, the drum slides on and off the hub without binding, and the finished brake job passes WP-11-8's own "how you know it worked" check.

Common mistakes:
- Replacing only one side's drum instead of both, which can cause uneven braking.
- Skipping the measurement step and reusing a drum that is already past its wear limit.
- Confusing the maximum-diameter spec (a wear limit) with a nominal "new" diameter spec; always compare your measurement to the maximum number on the drum itself.
