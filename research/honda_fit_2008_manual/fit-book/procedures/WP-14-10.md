Title: Replace the alternator

Skill level: 4 (shop recommended) — a real service-manual-style page for this car (Source:
S-WP14-C, hfitinfo.com "Alternator Removal and Installation") confirms the intake manifold
itself has to come off to get the alternator out, which is a bigger job than a simple bolt-off
swap and raises the chance of a vacuum leak, gasket problem, or bent linkage if not done
carefully.

Time: N/A for full DIY steps at this skill level — see "What the shop will do" below. A shop
typically quotes 1.5-3 hours of labor for this job on small Hondas of this era; ask for a
written estimate (see facts/WP-13.md, labor-rate cards).

What the shop will do:
- Disconnect the battery, following the proper terminal disconnection order.
- Remove the drive belt.
- Remove the intake manifold to reach the alternator [Source: S-WP14-C].
- Disconnect the alternator's electrical connector and its positive cable, then unbolt and remove the alternator.
- Install the new alternator and torque its mounting bolts to 17 lbf-ft (24 N·m) [Source: S-WP14-C].
- Reconnect the alternator connector and positive cable, making sure the ring terminal's crimped side faces away from the alternator, and torque the cable's terminal bolt to 6 lbf-ft (8 N·m) [Source: S-WP14-C].
- Reinstall the intake manifold with a new gasket if the old one is damaged, reinstall the drive belt, and reconnect the battery.
- Start the engine and confirm the charging system light goes out and the battery charges normally.

Why this book does not give full DIY steps here: intake manifold removal brings real risk of a
vacuum or gasket leak if reassembled incorrectly, and this job is not commonly documented by
first-generation (GD3) owners as a routine driveway job the way starter or window-regulator
swaps are. This book follows D9 (never downplay a safety- or reliability-relevant step) by
naming the real torque values above for the pieces IT could confirm, while still recommending a
shop for the intake-manifold portion of the job.

Safety warnings (read even if a shop is doing this job, so you know what to ask about):
- Always disconnect the battery before this job; the alternator's output terminal stays "hot" (electrically live) even with the key off.
- Always wear eye protection if you help with any part of this job.
- Do not skip a post-repair check of the charging system voltage before considering the job done.

How you know it worked: The dashboard's charging/battery warning light goes off and stays off after starting, and a shop's or your own multimeter check shows the battery charging at a normal voltage (typically in the low-to-mid 14-volt range) with the engine running.

Common mistakes:
- Reconnecting the alternator's positive cable with the ring terminal's crimped side facing the alternator instead of away from it, as the source specifically warns against [Source: S-WP14-C].
- Reusing a damaged intake manifold gasket instead of a new one.
- Skipping a post-job charging-voltage check and not discovering a bad connection until the battery goes flat later.
