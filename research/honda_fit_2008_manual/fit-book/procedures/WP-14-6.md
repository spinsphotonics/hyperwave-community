Chapter: 42 (Cooling system: thermostat, radiator, water pump)
Title: Replace the thermostat

RESEARCH FINDING: this car's service literature refers to the thermostat's housing as the
"water outlet," not a separate "thermostat housing" page — the two names describe the same
part. A real service-manual-style page for this car (Source: S-WP14-A, hfitinfo.com "Water
Outlet Removal and Installation") gives the housing removal/installation steps and even a
specific Honda liquid-gasket part number, but its extracted text does not explicitly show a
sub-step of lifting the thermostat cartridge out of the housing — that step may be a real gap
in this source's text extraction, not proof the housing is sealed shut. A second, independent,
genuinely first-generation (GD 01-08) source (Source: S-WP14-D, FitFreak.net "Thermostat change
instructions") confirms this is a job first-generation Fit owners do report doing themselves,
and a general cross-model summary of the same job (draining coolant, removing the housing cover,
lifting out the thermostat, fitting a new sealing ring) matches this shape. Combined, this book
treats the job below as DIY-level, while flagging the thermostat-cartridge sub-steps themselves
as community-reported rather than directly confirmed word-for-word in Honda's own text.

Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care — this is under-hood coolant work, not an under-car job, but it demands the same "engine off and cool" discipline)
Time: 60 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- Basic socket and wrench set
- A clean drain pan, sized for your coolant capacity (see Appendix B)
- A scraper or gasket-removal tool, for old gasket material
- A torque wrench

Parts and supplies:
- New thermostat for the L15A1 engine — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F
- New sealing ring/O-ring for the ECT (engine coolant temperature) sensor that sits in the housing [Source: S-WP14-A]
- Honda liquid gasket, P/N 08717-0004, 08718-0003, 08718-0004, or 08718-0009, for the housing's mating surfaces [Source: S-WP14-A]
- Fresh Honda-type coolant to refill the system (see Appendix B for type and capacity)

Safety warnings:
- Always let the engine cool completely before opening the cooling system. A hot system can spray scalding coolant when opened.
- Do not open the radiator cap or the housing while the engine is warm or running.
- Always wear eye protection when draining or refilling coolant.
- Do not let coolant contact your skin for long periods or let pets or children near a coolant spill; it is toxic and can taste sweet to animals.
- Do not run the engine until you have refilled the system and bled the air out, per your new thermostat's instructions.

Steps:
1. Make sure the engine is completely cool before starting.
2. Remove the air cleaner assembly to reach the housing area [Source: S-WP14-A].
3. Drain the engine coolant into your drain pan.
4. Disconnect the upper radiator hose and the water bypass hose from the water outlet housing.
5. Disconnect the ECT sensor connector, then remove the water outlet (housing) from the cylinder head.
6. Lift the old thermostat out of the housing and note which side faces the engine.
7. Remove all old liquid gasket material from the housing's mating surfaces, the bolts, and the bolt holes; clean and dry the surfaces [Source: S-WP14-A].
8. Fit the new sealing ring to the ECT sensor and set the sensor aside for reinstallation.
9. Apply the specified Honda liquid gasket to the cylinder head mating surface of the housing, following the bead pattern and size given on the tube.
10. Install the housing within the gasket manufacturer's stated time limit (as little as 4-5 minutes on some Honda liquid gaskets) before it starts to skin over [Source: S-WP14-A].
11. Reconnect the ECT sensor and its connector.
12. Reconnect the upper radiator hose and the water bypass/heater hose.
13. Reinstall the air cleaner assembly.
14. Wait at least 30 minutes before refilling the system with coolant, and do not run the engine for at least 3 hours after installing the housing, per the source's own note [Source: S-WP14-A].
15. Refill the radiator with the correct coolant type and bleed air from the system per your car's fill procedure.
16. Clean up any spilled coolant immediately.
17. Start the engine, let it reach normal operating temperature, and check for leaks around the housing.

How you know it worked: The temperature gauge/indicator behaves normally (no overheating, no staying cold for an unusually long warm-up), there are no coolant leaks around the housing, and the heater blows warm air once the engine is up to temperature.

Common mistakes:
- Opening the system while the engine is still warm, causing a scalding spray.
- Missing the liquid gasket's short install-time window and having it skin over before the housing is bolted down.
- Running the engine before waiting the full time the gasket needs to cure.
- Installing the thermostat backwards (the spring/pellet side must face the engine on most Honda designs — confirm orientation against the old part before disposing of it).
