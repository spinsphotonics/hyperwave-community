Procedure ID: P-WP11-10
Chapter: 28 (Battery: testing, cleaning, replacing, jump starting)
Title: Test the battery with a multimeter
Skill level: 1 (anyone)
Time: 10 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- A basic digital multimeter, set to DC volts (20V DC range)

Parts and supplies:
- None

Safety warnings:
- Always wear eye protection when working near the battery — it gives off explosive hydrogen gas during normal operation, per the manual.
- Do not smoke or bring sparks or flames near the battery while testing it.
- Do not let the multimeter's metal probes touch both terminals or any metal at the same time by accident; this can cause a spark.
- Always wash your hands after handling the battery or its terminals.

Steps:
1. Turn off the engine and every accessory (lights, radio, fan) and let the car sit at least 1 hour.
2. Set the multimeter dial to 20V DC.
3. Touch the red probe to the positive (+) battery post.
4. Touch the black probe to the negative (-) battery post.
5. Read the number on the multimeter's screen; this is the resting voltage.
6. Compare the reading to the chart: 12.6 volts or higher is a full, healthy charge.
7. A reading near 12.2 volts means the battery is only about half charged.
8. A reading below 12.0 volts usually means a weak or failing battery.
9. Optional: have a helper crank the engine while you watch the meter; a healthy battery should not drop below about 9.6 volts while cranking.
10. If the reading is low or the battery struggles to crank the engine, plan to charge or replace it (see P-WP11-11).

How you know it worked: You have a clear resting-voltage number and, if you did the cranking check, a cranking-voltage number, and you know which category the battery falls into.

Common mistakes:
- Testing right after driving, before the battery has rested, which gives a falsely high reading.
- Mixing up the red and black probes (the reading will just show a negative number, not damage anything, but re-check your polarity).
- Assuming a battery that reads 12.6 volts resting is automatically fine under starter load; the cranking test catches batteries that fail only when asked to deliver current.
