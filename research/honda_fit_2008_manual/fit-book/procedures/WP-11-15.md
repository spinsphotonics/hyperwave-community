Procedure ID: P-WP11-15
Chapter: 30 (Coolant, transmission fluid, brake fluid, power steering, washer fluid)
Title: Drain and fill the coolant (cooling system flush)
Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care — you work under the car and the system holds pressure)
Time: 60 minutes for a first-timer, plus cool-down time before you start

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands, or ramps (see P-WP11-22), to reach the underside splash shield
- Wrench or socket to fit the radiator drain plug and the block drain bolt (sizes UNVERIFIED — check yours, see Appendix C)
- Large drain pan (the system holds several liters/quarts)
- Funnel

Parts and supplies:
- Honda Long-life Antifreeze/Coolant Type 2 (pre-mixed 50/50); Honda Extreme Cold Weather Antifreeze/Coolant Type 2 (100% concentrate) only if you are deliberately mixing a stronger, very-cold-climate blend [Source: S-001/S-023, Tier 1]
- New drain-bolt washer for the block drain bolt [Source: S-WP11-A, Tier 3]
- Total system capacity: sources disagree on the exact figure (roughly 3.7 to 5.0 L depending on transmission type and whether the reserve tank is included) — this is logged as a conflict (see queue/conflicts.md); fill to the marks described below rather than pouring a fixed amount, and check Appendix B for the verified number once available

Safety warnings:
- Do not remove the radiator cap while the engine is hot; hot coolant can spray out and scald you. Wait until the engine and radiator are cool.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands or ramps rated for the weight.
- Always relieve pressure slowly: turn the radiator cap counterclockwise to the first stop and let it hiss before pressing down and removing it.
- Do not add plain water alone as a permanent fix, and do not mix Honda's 100% concentrate coolant with plain water beyond what the label describes.
- Always clean up spilled coolant immediately; it is toxic to pets and wildlife if left in a puddle.

Steps:
1. Let the engine and radiator cool completely.
2. Raise the front of the car and support it on jack stands, then remove the splash shield if one is fitted.
3. Relieve pressure at the radiator cap slowly, then remove the cap fully.
4. Place the drain pan under the radiator's drain plug and loosen it to drain the radiator.
5. Place the drain pan under the drain bolt at the rear of the engine block and remove it to drain the block.
6. Let all the coolant drain fully from both points.
7. Reinstall the block drain bolt with a new washer and tighten it securely.
8. Tighten the radiator drain plug securely.
9. Remove, empty, and rinse the coolant reserve tank, then reinstall it.
10. Pour fresh Honda coolant into the radiator up to the base of the filler neck.
11. Fill the reserve tank to the MAX mark.
12. Install the radiator cap loosely, not fully tight yet.
13. Start the engine and let it run until the radiator fan cycles on and off at least twice, to work air out of the system.
14. Turn off the engine, let it cool, and recheck the radiator and reserve tank levels.
15. Top off as needed, then put the radiator cap on fully and tightly.
16. Start the engine again and check underneath for leaks at both drain points.
17. Lower the car and remove the jack stands.

How you know it worked: The reserve tank sits at the MAX mark after a full warm-up-and-cool-down cycle, there are no leaks at either drain point, and the temperature gauge or warning light behaves normally on a test drive.

Common mistakes:
- Not letting the engine run through a full warm-up cycle to bleed trapped air, leaving an air pocket that causes overheating later.
- Reusing the old, crushed drain-bolt washer instead of a new one.
- Removing the radiator cap too fast on a still-warm system.
- Guessing at the exact refill amount instead of filling to the marks and rechecking after a warm-up.
