Procedure ID: P-WP11-1
Chapter: 24 (Oil and filter change)
Title: Change the engine oil and filter
Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care — Honda's own manual says this job needs the car raised and calls it a job for someone with the right tools; many first-timers choose a shop the first time)
Time: 45 minutes for a first-timer (draining, filter swap, refill, leak check, cleanup)

Tools:
- Ratchet or breaker bar with socket to fit the drain bolt (exact size UNVERIFIED — check your bolt before buying a socket; see Appendix C)
- Oil filter wrench ("a special wrench, available from your dealer" per the owner's manual)
- Torque wrench (to hit 29 lbf-ft / 39 N-m on the drain bolt)
- Drain pan that holds at least 4 quarts
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22) or drive-up ramps
- Funnel
- Nitrile gloves and safety glasses
- Shop rags

Parts and supplies:
- Engine oil: Honda 5W-20, API Certification Seal reading "For Gasoline Engines" — 3.8 US qt (3.6 L) with a new filter [Source: S-001/S-023, direct owner's manual read]
- New oil drain bolt washer (crush washer) — part number UNVERIFIED, buy from a Honda parts counter or dealer
- New oil filter — part number UNVERIFIED this pass (no Tier 1/2 source found for this engine's filter number; do not guess — ask your parts counter for "2008 Honda Fit 1.5L oil filter" or check Appendix F)
- A sealed container to carry used oil to a recycling center

Safety warnings:
- Always let the engine run first, then shut it off — the manual says to run the engine to normal temperature before draining so the oil flows and carries out contaminants.
- Always let the engine and exhaust cool before you touch anything underneath the car, once you shut it off — hot oil and hot metal can burn you.
- Do not get under a vehicle held up only by a jack. Use jack stands (see P-WP11-22).
- Always wear eye protection and gloves. Used oil and fresh oil can irritate skin and eyes.
- Do not dump used oil in the trash or on the ground. It is hazardous to the environment.
- Always clean up any spilled oil right away — spilled oil can damage engine parts and creates a slip hazard.

Steps:
1. Warm up the engine by running it, then shut it off and set the parking brake.
2. Raise the front of the car and support it on jack stands (see P-WP11-22).
3. Open the hood and remove the engine oil fill cap so air can enter as oil drains.
4. Slide the drain pan under the drain bolt at the bottom of the engine.
5. Loosen and remove the drain bolt and its washer with your socket.
6. Let all the oil drain fully into the pan; this can take several minutes.
7. Wipe the drain area, then reinstall the drain bolt with a brand-new washer.
8. Tighten the drain bolt to 29 lbf-ft (39 N-m) with a torque wrench.
9. Move the drain pan under the oil filter and remove the old filter with the filter wrench.
10. Check that the old filter's rubber gasket did not stick to the engine; remove it if it did.
11. Wipe a thin film of new oil on the new filter's gasket, then thread it on by hand until snug.
12. Lower the car back to the ground and remove the jack stands.
13. Pour in 3.8 US qt (3.6 L) of the recommended 5W-20 oil through the funnel.
14. Reinstall the oil fill cap and tighten it securely.
15. Start the engine and watch the oil pressure light; it should go out within 5 seconds.
16. Let the engine run a few minutes, then shut it off and check the drain bolt and filter for leaks.
17. Wait several more minutes, then check the level on the dipstick and top up if needed.
18. Seal the used oil in a container and take it to a recycling center.

How you know it worked: The oil pressure warning light goes out within 5 seconds of starting the engine, there are no drips under the car after it has sat overnight, and the dipstick reads between the marks.

Common mistakes:
- Overfilling past the dipstick's upper mark, which can damage the engine (the manual warns against this directly).
- Forgetting the new drain-bolt washer, which causes a slow leak.
- Over-tightening the oil filter by hand past "snug," which can make it very hard to remove next time and can crush the gasket.
- Not fully re-torquing the drain bolt, or over-torquing it and stripping the pan.
- Working under a car held up by only a jack instead of jack stands.
