Procedure ID: P-WP11-9
Chapter: 27 (Brakes: inspection, pads, rotors, fluid)
Title: Bleed the brakes
Skill level: 3 (needs care — a spongy pedal after this job means air is still in the lines)
Time: 45 minutes for a first-timer (all four wheels)

Tools:
- Wrench to fit the bleeder screw on each caliper/wheel cylinder (exact size UNVERIFIED — check yours, see Appendix C)
- Clear plastic tubing that fits snugly over the bleeder screw
- A clean glass or plastic catch bottle
- A helper to press the brake pedal, or a one-person vacuum/pressure bleeder kit
- Turkey baster or fluid syringe, to remove old fluid from the reservoir before starting

Parts and supplies:
- Fresh Honda Heavy Duty Brake Fluid DOT 3 — if unavailable, DOT 3 or DOT 4 from a sealed container only; never DOT 5, which is not compatible with this braking system [Source: S-001/S-023, Tier 1]
- Replace the brake fluid on the schedule the Maintenance Minder shows, or every 3 years, whichever comes first, per the manual [Source: S-001/S-023]

Safety warnings:
- Always wear eye protection. Brake fluid can splash and it damages paint and eyes.
- Do not let the brake fluid reservoir run dry during bleeding, or you will draw air back into the lines and have to start over.
- Do not reuse fluid that comes out of the bleeder; only use fresh fluid from a sealed container.
- Always keep brake fluid off painted surfaces; wipe up any spill immediately.
- Do not drive the car until the pedal feels firm and consistent, not soft or sinking.

Steps:
1. Remove old fluid from the reservoir with the baster, leaving a little so it does not run dry.
2. Fill the reservoir with fresh Honda Heavy Duty Brake Fluid DOT 3.
3. Slide clear tubing over the bleeder screw at the first wheel and route the other end into your catch bottle.
4. Have your helper press the brake pedal down and hold it.
5. Open the bleeder screw briefly to let fluid and air escape, then close it before the pedal is released.
6. Have your helper release the pedal only after the bleeder screw is closed.
7. Repeat steps 4 through 6 until no more air bubbles appear in the tubing.
8. Check the reservoir level and top it off before moving to the next wheel.
9. Move to the next wheel and repeat steps 3 through 8.
10. Recheck the reservoir level after all four wheels and fill to the MAX mark.
11. Press the brake pedal several times and confirm it feels firm and consistent, not soft.

How you know it worked: The pedal feels firm on the first press, not soft or sinking to the floor, and no air bubbles appear in the tubing on a final check at each wheel.

Common mistakes:
- Letting the reservoir run empty partway through, drawing in fresh air.
- Bleeding wheels in a random order instead of a consistent order (a commonly cited order for this car is front-left, front-right, rear-right, rear-left, though this specific order comes from a single community source and was not independently confirmed this pass — when in doubt, follow your bleeder kit's instructions or the sequence a shop manual gives you).
- Opening the bleeder screw too far and having it come out, or over-tightening and snapping it.
- Skipping the final firm-pedal check and driving with air still in a line.
