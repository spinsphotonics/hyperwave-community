Chapter: 45 (Interior fixes: door handles, window regulators, seat repair)
Title: Replace a door handle

RESEARCH FINDING: the steps below come from a real service-manual-style page for this car's
rear door outer handle (Source: S-WP14-C, hfitinfo.com "Handles and Remote Openers"). The front
door handle mechanism on this generation of Fit is very similar in design (door panel off,
handle rod disconnected, handle pried out with a trim tool), so this card is written to cover
either door, with the rear-door page's real steps as the confirmed backbone; treat any exact
bolt count as this-specific-page-confirmed for the rear door and reasonably similar, not
independently confirmed, for the front.

Skill level: 2 (needs basic tools)
Time: 45 minutes for a first-timer, per door

Tools:
- A door/trim panel removal tool kit (plastic pry tools), to avoid scratching the door or breaking plastic clips
- A clip remover tool, for the handle rod fastener
- Basic screwdriver and socket set
- Protective tape, to protect the paint near the handle while prying

Parts and supplies:
- New door handle (confirm front vs. rear and driver vs. passenger side before ordering) — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F
- A replacement plastic access-cover cap, if the old one is cracked or torn during removal [Source: S-WP14-C]

Safety warnings:
- Always wear gloves; sharp metal edges and clips inside the door can cut your hands.
- Do not pry directly against painted door metal without protective tape; a slipped trim tool can scratch the paint.
- Always support the window glass (raise it fully) before working inside the door, so it cannot drop while the panel is off [Source: S-WP14-C].

Steps:
1. Remove the door panel to access the inside of the door.
2. Raise the window glass fully before continuing.
3. If the door has power locks, disconnect the power door lock actuator connector and detach its harness clip.
4. Remove the rear plug cap and the plastic access cover, as needed, keeping them intact if you can.
5. Remove the maintenance caps covering the handle's fasteners.
6. Detach the rod fastener with a clip remover, then disconnect the outer handle rod from the handle.
7. Remove the handle's mounting bolts.
8. Apply protective tape to the door around the handle to protect the paint.
9. Pry the outer handle out with the appropriate trim tool to release its hook, then remove it [Source: S-WP14-C].
10. Compare the new handle to the old one to confirm it matches before installing.
11. Fit the new handle into its opening and press it in until the hook engages.
12. Reinstall the mounting bolts.
13. Reconnect the outer handle rod and refit its clip fastener.
14. Reinstall the maintenance caps, the plastic access cover, and the rear plug cap.
15. If equipped, reconnect the power door lock actuator connector and its harness clip.
16. Reinstall the door panel.
17. Test the handle from outside and, if equipped, the power lock function, before considering the job done.

How you know it worked: The door opens smoothly from outside with normal effort, the handle does not feel loose or gritty, and any power lock function on that door still works correctly.

Common mistakes:
- Prying without protective tape and scratching the paint around the handle.
- Losing or breaking a small plastic access cover or maintenance cap during removal.
- Forgetting to raise the window fully before removing the door panel, risking a dropped or damaged glass.
- Not fully engaging the new handle's hook before tightening the mounting bolts, leaving it loose.
