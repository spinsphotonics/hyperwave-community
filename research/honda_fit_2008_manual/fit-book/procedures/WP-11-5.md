Procedure ID: P-WP11-5
Chapter: 26 (Tires: pressure, rotation, wear, replacement, the spare)
Title: Rotate the tires
Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care — all four wheels come off)
Time: 45 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- Jack and 4 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- Lug wrench or a socket to fit the wheel nuts, and a breaker bar
- Torque wrench that reaches 80 lbf-ft (108 N-m)
- Wheel chocks
- Tire pressure gauge (see P-WP11-4)

Parts and supplies:
- None (no consumables, unless you also replace worn lug nuts — part number UNVERIFIED, ask a Honda parts counter)

Safety warnings:
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use four jack stands.
- Always loosen every wheel nut a bit before you raise that corner off the ground.
- Always chock the wheels that stay on the ground before you start jacking.
- Do not fully tighten wheel nuts with the wheel hanging free; snug them, lower the car, then torque them on the ground.

Steps:
1. Park on level, solid ground and set the parking brake.
2. Chock the wheels you are not lifting.
3. Loosen each wheel nut about a half turn while all four tires are still on the ground.
4. Raise one corner and support it on a jack stand; remove that wheel.
5. Repeat step 4 for the other three corners so all four wheels are off.
6. Move each wheel to its new position: front-left to rear-left, rear-left to front-left, and the same side-to-side pattern on the right, unless your tires are directional.
7. If your tires are marked directional, only swap front-to-back on the same side, never side-to-side.
8. Put each wheel back on its new hub and thread the nuts on by hand.
9. Snug the nuts in a crisscross (star) pattern with the wrench, one corner at a time.
10. Lower the car until the tire touches the ground but still has some jack support.
11. Torque each wheel nut to 80 lbf-ft (108 N-m) in the crisscross pattern.
12. Lower the car fully and remove the jack stands.
13. Check and set the pressure in all four tires once they are all back on the ground (see P-WP11-4).

How you know it worked: Every wheel nut is torqued to 80 lbf-ft, no wheel wobbles when you drive at low speed, and the tires are now in their new rotation positions.

Common mistakes:
- Trying to fully torque a wheel nut while the wheel still hangs free in the air.
- Skipping the crisscross tightening pattern, which can seat the wheel unevenly.
- Rotating directional tires side-to-side, which puts the tread pattern running backward.
- Forgetting to re-torque after the first 50-100 miles of driving on a re-used wheel.
