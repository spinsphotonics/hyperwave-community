Procedure ID: P-WP07-1
Chapter: 16
Title: Change a flat tire
Skill level: 2 (needs basic tools — the tools that came with the car)
Time: 30 minutes for a first-timer
Tools:
Wheel nut wrench (in the tool kit)
Extension/wrench handle (in the tool kit)
Jack (stored in the cargo area)
Gloves (recommended, not in the kit)
Parts and supplies:
Compact spare tire, 60 psi (420 kPa) — F-WP07-001, F-WP07-003
Safety warnings:
Always stop far away from traffic lanes, on firm, level, non-slippery ground, before you start.
Always turn on the hazard warning lights before you get out of the car.
Always get every passenger out of the car before you change the tire.
Do not get under the car at any time. The car can roll off the jack and crush you.
Always follow the jacking steps in order. Do not skip the jacking-point check.
Always handle wheel nuts and the brake hub carefully — they can be hot from driving.
Never exceed 50 mph (80 km/h) on the compact spare. It is a temporary tire only.
Steps:
1. Pull well off the road, onto firm, level, non-slippery ground, away from traffic.
2. Turn on the hazard warning lights.
3. Put the transmission in Park (automatic) or reverse (manual). Set the parking brake.
4. Turn the ignition off. Have everyone get out of the car and stand away from traffic.
5. Open the tailgate and lift the cargo floor lid using its strap. Hook the strap to the head restraint leg to hold it open.
6. Take the tool kit out of the center of the spare tire.
7. Turn the jack's end bracket counterclockwise to loosen it, then lift the jack straight out.
8. Unscrew the wing bolt and lift the spare tire out of its well.
9. Loosen each wheel nut on the flat tire one half turn with the wheel nut wrench. Do not remove them yet.
10. On base-trim cars, pry off the wheel cover using the flat tip of the extension.
11. Find the jacking point nearest the flat tire. Look for the mark molded into the body underside.
12. Set the jack under that jacking point. Turn the end bracket clockwise until the jack meets the point.
13. Check that the jacking point's tab is resting inside the jack's notch before you raise the car.
14. Use the extension with the wheel nut wrench to raise the car until the flat tire is off the ground.
15. Remove the loosened wheel nuts, then pull the flat tire off. Set it down with the outside face up.
16. Wipe dirt off the wheel hub and the spare's mounting surface with a clean cloth.
17. Put the spare tire onto the hub. Thread the wheel nuts on finger-tight in a crisscross pattern.
18. Lower the car to the ground with the jack, then remove the jack.
19. Tighten the wheel nuts the rest of the way in the same crisscross pattern, to 80 lbf·ft (108 N·m).
20. Place the flat tire face down in the spare tire well and secure it with the wing bolt.
21. Store the jack and place the tool kit back in the center of the flat tire.
22. Store the wheel cover or center cap in the cargo area so it does not get scratched.
23. Unhook the strap, lower the cargo floor lid, and close the tailgate.
24. Drive to a service shop soon to have the wheel nut torque checked and the flat tire repaired.
How you know it worked: The spare tire sits flat on the ground, all wheel nuts are snug and torqued, the car sits level, and it drives straight with no pulling, wobble, or noise below 50 mph.
Common mistakes: Loosening wheel nuts after the car is already jacked up (the wheel just spins). Forgetting to check for the jacking-point mark and setting the jack on bodywork instead. Driving the spare above 50 mph or for a long distance instead of repairing the flat promptly. Skipping the shop visit to have wheel nut torque re-checked.
Source IDs: S-001 (F-WP07-001 through F-WP07-010)
