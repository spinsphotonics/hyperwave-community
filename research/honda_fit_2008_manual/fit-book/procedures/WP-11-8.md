Chapter: 27 (Brakes: inspection, pads, rotors, fluid)
Title: Replace the rear brake shoes (drum brakes)
Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care — many owners choose a shop for their first drum brake job; the return spring is widely reported as the hardest part)

RESEARCH FINDING (confirms RESEARCH_PLAN.md's open question for this card): The 2008 Honda Fit
base trim has REAR DRUM BRAKES, not rear discs. The owner's manual states this directly and in
plain language, with no separate carve-out for a different trim: "Your vehicle is equipped with
front disc brakes. The brakes on the rear wheels are drum." [Source: S-001/S-023, Tier 1, direct
quote from the manual's own "Braking System Design" section — high confidence.] This sentence
does not distinguish base from Sport, so it is treated as applying to every 2008 US-market Fit
trim covered by that manual, not just base.

Time: 90 minutes for a first-timer (both rear wheels)

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- Lug wrench and torque wrench (80 lbf-ft / 108 N-m for wheel nuts)
- Large C-clamps or brake-spring pliers, to hold the shoes compressed while hooking the return spring (community-reported as the hardest single step of this job)
- Drum brake spring/hold-down tool kit
- Wire brush and brake-parts cleaner
- Wheel chocks
- Molykote 44MA (or equivalent high-temp brake grease) for the parking-brake lever pivot and shoe contact points

Parts and supplies:
- New rear brake shoe set (and drums, if worn or scored) for the 2008 Fit base trim — part number UNVERIFIED this pass (no Tier 1/2 source found; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F)
- New return springs and hardware kit, if the old ones look stretched, rusted, or discolored
- A new U-clip for the parking brake lever pivot pin, per the repair-manual-style reassembly steps

Safety warnings:
- Always wear eye protection. Springs are under strong tension and can snap free unexpectedly.
- Do not breathe brake dust; never use compressed air or a dry brush on brake parts. Use brake-parts cleaner instead.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.
- Always work on one side at a time, using the untouched side as your reference for how everything goes back together.
- Do not get your fingers between a compressed spring and the backing plate.
- Always keep grease off the brake shoe linings and drum's inner surface; grease on the linings ruins braking.
- Always press the brake pedal several times after reassembly, before driving, to seat the shoes and set the self-adjuster.

Steps:
1. Loosen the rear wheel nuts, then raise and support the car on jack stands.
2. Remove the wheel and set it aside.
3. Release the parking brake, then pull the drum straight off the hub (tap gently if it is stuck).
4. Take a photo of the spring and lever layout before touching anything.
5. Unhook the lower return spring with your spring tool or pliers.
6. Unhook the upper return spring the same way.
7. Remove the hold-down springs and pins that pin each shoe to the backing plate.
8. Lift out the old shoes together with the adjuster and parking-brake link still attached.
9. Transfer the adjuster, parking-brake lever, and springs onto the new shoes in the same layout as the photo.
10. Apply a thin dab of high-temp grease to the backing plate's contact pads and the parking-brake lever pivot, keeping grease off the linings.
11. Set the new shoes onto the backing plate and reinstall the hold-down pins and springs.
12. Hook the lower return spring back into place.
13. Use your C-clamps or spring pliers to compress the shoes enough to hook the upper return spring.
14. Check that the shoes sit fully in their bosses and the adjuster turns freely.
15. Slide the drum back onto the hub.
16. Reinstall the wheel and snug the nuts by hand.
17. Lower the car, then torque the wheel nuts to 80 lbf-ft (108 N-m) in a crisscross pattern.
18. Repeat steps 1 through 17 on the other rear wheel.
19. Press the brake pedal firmly several times with the engine off until the pedal feels normal.
20. Adjust the parking brake if it now feels loose (see your parking brake adjustment steps).
21. Drive slowly at first and test the brakes gently before returning to normal driving.

How you know it worked: The brake pedal feels firm, the parking brake holds the car on a hill, both rear wheels turn freely with light drag when the car is off the ground, and there is no grinding or dragging noise while driving.

Common mistakes:
- Losing track of how the springs and adjuster were arranged, which is why a "before" photo matters so much.
- Getting grease on the shoe linings, which can cause the brakes to grab or fade.
- Forcing the return spring on without a tool and having it snap loose.
- Skipping the "pump the pedal first" step and finding a long, soft pedal on the first drive.
- Not resetting the self-adjuster, leaving the parking brake or pedal feeling loose.

Common mistakes note on skill level: several independent DIY writeups single out the main return
spring as the one step that trips up first-timers; if you are not comfortable with springs under
tension, this is a reasonable job to hand to a shop.
