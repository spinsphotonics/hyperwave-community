Title: Repair a torn or damaged seat

RESEARCH FINDING: this is a general upholstery/mechanism skill, not a Honda-specific procedure
— no Fit-specific "how to patch a torn seat" page exists in any tier of source checked, and this
book's own rules (D7/D8) do not require one for a task this generic. The general technique
below is standard automotive-upholstery practice, not tied to any single source.

Skill level: 2 (needs basic tools)
Time: 60-120 minutes for a first-timer, depending on the size of the tear and whether the seat needs to come out of the car

Tools:
- A ratchet or wrench for the seat mounting bolts, if you need to remove the seat for better access
- Scissors, a heat gun (low setting) or hair dryer, and a stiff-bristle brush, for vinyl/leather repair
- A sewing kit with heavy-duty upholstery thread, for a small fabric tear
- Clean rags and isopropyl alcohol, for cleaning the area before any repair

Parts and supplies:
- A vinyl/leather repair kit (color-matched filler compound and grain paper), for a torn vinyl or leather seat surface
- Matching seat fabric or a patch kit, for a torn cloth seat
- Foam replacement or extra padding, if the seat's foam is also crushed or torn underneath the cover

Safety warnings:
- Always disconnect the seat's electrical connectors (for a power seat, seat heater, or seat-belt pretensioner wiring) before removing a seat from the car.
- Do not cut into or disturb any wiring or components you cannot identify near the seat frame; some seats route side-impact airbag (SRS) wiring through them.
- Always let a repaired area cure fully, per your repair kit's instructions, before sitting on it.

Steps:
1. Clean the damaged area with a rag and isopropyl alcohol, and let it dry.
2. Decide whether the repair can be done with the seat in the car or needs the seat removed for full access.
3. If removing the seat, disconnect its electrical connectors first, then remove its mounting bolts and lift it out with a helper.
4. For a small fabric tear: stitch the edges together from the underside where possible, using heavy-duty thread, then apply a fabric patch behind the tear for strength.
5. For a vinyl or leather tear: trim any loose or frayed edges, then follow your repair kit's instructions to fill the tear with the color-matched compound.
6. Use the heat gun on a low setting, held well away from the surface, to help the vinyl/leather filler cure if your kit calls for heat-setting.
7. Press the grain paper from your kit onto the wet filler to recreate the surface texture, then peel it away once cured, per the kit's instructions.
8. Blend the repaired area's color if needed, using the kit's color-matching guidance.
9. If you removed the seat, reinstall it and reconnect its electrical connectors and mounting bolts.
10. Let the repair cure fully before normal use, per your kit's stated time.

How you know it worked: The repaired area holds together under normal sitting and getting-in/out movement, does not visibly gap or peel after it fully cures, and any seat electrical function (heater, power adjustment, seat-belt warning) still works normally if you removed the seat.

Common mistakes:
- Skipping the cleaning step, so the repair compound does not bond well to a dirty or oily surface.
- Using too much heat too close to the vinyl, scorching it instead of curing the filler.
- Disturbing seat wiring without labeling connectors first, then reconnecting something incorrectly.
- Sitting on the repair before it has fully cured.
