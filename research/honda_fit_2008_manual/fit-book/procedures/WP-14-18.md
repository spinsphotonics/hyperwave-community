Title: Restore cloudy or yellowed headlights

NOTE: this is a general technique for restoring UV-hazed plastic headlight lenses, not a
Honda-specific procedure — the plastic-lens haze this job addresses is a material property of
the headlight lens itself (common to nearly all modern cars with plastic, not glass, headlight
covers), not something specific to this car's design. No dedicated Fit-specific source was
needed or found for this generic step-by-step.

Skill level: 1 (anyone)
Time: 30-45 minutes for a first-timer (both headlights)

Tools:
- A headlight restoration kit (most include sandpaper of several grits, a polishing compound, and applicator pads)
- Masking tape, to protect the surrounding paint
- A clean microfiber cloth
- A spray bottle with water, to keep the sandpaper wet if your kit calls for wet-sanding

Parts and supplies:
- Headlight restoration kit (sandpaper/polish based) or a UV-protectant sealant, if your kit includes one
- Painter's tape

Safety warnings:
- Do not attempt this job in direct, hot sunlight; the plastic and any chemical products can behave unpredictably in high heat.
- Always mask off the paint around each headlight before sanding or polishing, to avoid scratching it.
- Do not use harsh solvents not intended for automotive plastic; they can cloud or crack the lens further.

Steps:
1. Wash both headlights with soap and water, then dry them.
2. Mask off the paint and trim around each headlight with tape.
3. Starting with your kit's coarsest sandpaper, wet-sand the lens in one direction, keeping the surface wet throughout.
4. Rinse the lens and check your progress; the surface will look hazy white during sanding — this is normal and expected.
5. Move to the next-finer grit in your kit and repeat the wet-sanding, rinsing between grits.
6. Continue through each grit your kit provides, finishing on the finest one.
7. Rinse and dry the lens fully.
8. Apply the polishing compound with an applicator pad, working it in small circular motions until the lens looks clear.
9. Wipe off any remaining polish residue with a clean microfiber cloth.
10. If your kit includes a UV-protectant sealant, apply it per the kit's instructions to help slow future yellowing.
11. Remove the masking tape.
12. Repeat steps 2 through 11 on the other headlight.

How you know it worked: Both headlight lenses look clear rather than cloudy or yellowed, and a nighttime check shows noticeably brighter, more evenly spread light on the road or a wall compared to before.

Common mistakes:
- Skipping a grit step in the sanding sequence, leaving visible fine scratches the polish cannot remove.
- Letting the sandpaper run dry, which can gouge the plastic instead of smoothing it.
- Forgetting to mask the surrounding paint and scratching it with sandpaper or polish.
- Skipping the UV-protectant sealant step, if your kit includes one, and having the haze return sooner than expected.
