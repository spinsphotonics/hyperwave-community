Procedure ID: P-WP05-3
Chapter: 9
Title: Set up Long Mode (carry long items like lumber or surfboards)
Skill level: 1 = anyone
Time: 5 minutes for a first-timer
Tools:
- None required
Parts and supplies:
- None required
Safety warnings:
- Always keep at least one working seat belt and seating position free for every person riding in the car — do not exceed the number of seats actually available once you have reconfigured the seats.
- Always tie down or secure long cargo. An unsecured long item can be thrown forward with great force during hard braking, which can seriously injure or kill someone in the front seat.
- Do not let cargo extend into a space where the driver's or a passenger's airbag needs to deploy.
- Do not drive with cargo that blocks your view through the rear window or a side mirror.
- We could not fully confirm these exact steps against the 2008 GD3 owner's manual (see note below). Follow your own owner's manual's cargo-loading warnings if they differ from this book.
Steps:
1. Fold the passenger-side rear Magic Seat cushion up (see P-WP05-2, Tall Mode steps 1-4) or fold the passenger-side rear seat-back down flat (see P-WP05-1), whichever gives you the flattest continuous path to the item you are loading.
2. Move the front passenger seat as far forward as it will go.
3. Recline the front passenger seat-back forward until it rests against the folded rear seat, forming one continuous flat surface from the tailgate to the dashboard on the passenger's side.
4. Leave the driver's-side rear seat upright if you still need to carry a rear passenger.
5. Load the long item (for example, lumber, a surfboard, or a ladder) along the flattened passenger-side path.
6. Tie the item down using the cargo area's tie-down anchors so it cannot slide or shift.
How you know it worked: The item lies flat and secured along a single continuous surface from the tailgate to the dashboard, and it does not shift when you push on it by hand.
Common mistakes:
- Forgetting to move the front passenger seat forward before reclining it, which leaves a gap instead of a flat surface.
- Not tying the item down, so it slides during braking or turns.
- Loading an item so long it protrudes past the rear bumper without a proper reflective flag or marker, which is unsafe and may be illegal in some states.
Note on sourcing: The official 2007-2008 Fit owner's manual documents the individual seat-folding actions used above (Tall Mode's cushion-up step and Utility Mode's seat-back-down step) as Tier 1 facts, and Honda's own 2008 press materials confirm "long object mode" is one of the Fit's five official seating/cargo configurations. However, no Tier 1 or Tier 2 source read this session spells out Long Mode's own combined step-by-step sequence. The specific combination in steps 1-3 above is sourced from a single Tier 4 community source (a FitFreak.net forum thread, reached only through a search-engine summary because the page itself blocked direct access) and is therefore a community-reported, low-confidence procedure, not a manual-verified one. Treat this card as a starting point and use your own judgment and your owner's manual before loading anything long or heavy.
