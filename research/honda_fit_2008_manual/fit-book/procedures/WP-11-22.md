Chapter: 23 (Safety first: jacking, jack stands, wheel chocks, hot parts, fluids)
Title: Jack up the car and set it on jack stands
Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care — this is the single riskiest routine step in this whole maintenance chapter if done wrong)
Time: 15 minutes for a first-timer, done slowly and checked twice

Tools:
- A floor jack, rated for at least the vehicle's weight (the factory scissor jack in the trunk is only rated for one-corner, one-tire emergency changes, not for supporting the car while you work underneath)
- 2 or 4 jack stands (2 for one end of the car, 4 for the whole car off the ground), each rated for at least the vehicle's weight
- Wheel chocks
- A flat, level, solid surface — never gravel, grass, or a slope

Parts and supplies:
- None

Safety warnings:
- Do not ever get any part of your body under a car supported only by a jack. The manual itself warns that the vehicle can easily roll off a jack and seriously injure anyone underneath.
- Always work on a flat, level, hard surface. Never jack up a car on a slope, on soft ground, or on an incline.
- Always set the parking brake and chock the wheels that will stay on the ground before you start jacking.
- Always use the vehicle's marked or reinforced jacking points, never the floor pan, exhaust, plastic under-covers, or a random point on the frame.
- Do not exceed a jack's or jack stand's rated weight capacity; check the rating sticker before you buy or borrow one.
- Always give the car a firm shake test on the stands before working underneath, to confirm it will not slip.

Steps:
1. Park on a flat, level, solid surface and set the parking brake.
2. Chock the wheels that will remain on the ground.
3. Locate the lift point: the owner's manual shows a molded mark on the underbody near each wheel, for the factory scissor jack.
4. For jack-stand work, also find the reinforced pinch-weld seams on each rocker panel and the front subframe, well back from the bumper.
5. Treat step 4's points with caution: they are community-reported, not from the owner's manual, so double-check against your own car and a second reference.
6. Position the floor jack under the chosen point and raise the car slowly.
7. Stop as soon as the tire lifts a couple of inches and check that the jack is still centered on the point.
8. Continue raising to the height you need.
9. Slide a jack stand under a solid point near the jack and adjust it to the right height.
10. Slowly lower the floor jack until the car's weight rests on the jack stand.
11. Repeat steps 6 through 10 for the other end or side, if you are lifting the whole car.
12. Give the car a firm push and shake test before you get near or under it.
13. Leave the floor jack nearby as a backup, but do not rely on it to hold weight once the stands are set.
14. When done, reverse the process: raise slightly with the floor jack, remove the stands, then lower slowly and fully.

How you know it worked: The car sits solidly on the stands with no rocking when pushed, all four (or two) contact points look evenly loaded, and the wheels you did not lift stay chocked and on the ground the whole time.

Common mistakes:
- Placing a jack or stand on the floor pan, a plastic cover, or the exhaust instead of a reinforced point, which can bend the body or crush the part.
- Working under a car held up by the floor jack alone, with no stands.
- Jacking on a slope or on soft ground, letting the car shift or the jack sink.
- Forgetting to chock the wheels that stay on the ground.
