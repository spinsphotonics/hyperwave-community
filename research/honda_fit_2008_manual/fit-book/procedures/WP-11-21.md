Procedure ID: P-WP11-21
Chapter: 32 (Lights and bulbs, wipers, fuses)
Title: Replace a fuse
Skill level: 1 (anyone)
Time: 10 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- The fuse puller tool, stored in the interior fuse box
- Phillips-head screwdriver, only needed for the large fuses in the under-hood boxes

Parts and supplies:
- A spare fuse of the exact same amperage rating as the blown one; the car carries some spares, and the correct ratings for each position are listed on the fuse box's own lid diagram and in the manual [Source: S-001/S-023, Tier 1]
- Never substitute a fuse with a higher amperage rating than the original.

Safety warnings:
- Always turn the ignition switch to LOCK (0) and turn off the headlights and every accessory before checking or changing fuses.
- Do not install a fuse with a higher rating than the circuit calls for; this greatly increases the risk of damaging the electrical system.
- Do not ignore a fuse that blows again right away with a same-rated replacement; that means there is a real electrical problem, and the vehicle should be checked by a qualified technician.

Steps:
1. Turn the ignition switch to the LOCK (0) position.
2. Make sure the headlights and all other accessories are off.
3. Figure out which box holds the fuse: primary under-hood (driver's side), interior (behind the coin tray), or secondary (on the battery).
4. Remove the cover from that fuse box.
5. Find the suspect fuse using the chart on the fuse box lid or in the manual.
6. Pull a small fuse straight out using the fuse puller from the interior box.
7. For a large fuse in an under-hood box, remove it with a Phillips screwdriver instead.
8. Look through the top of the fuse at the thin wire inside; a blown fuse shows a broken or melted wire.
9. If you are not sure which one is blown, check every fuse for that circuit, not just the first one.
10. Push in a new fuse of the exact same amperage rating.
11. Turn the ignition back on and test whether the device now works.
12. Reinstall the fuse box cover.

How you know it worked: The device that stopped working (light, wiper, power window, and so on) now works normally, and the new fuse does not blow again right away.

Common mistakes:
- Installing a higher-rated fuse "just to make it stop blowing," which risks damaging the wiring or causing a fire.
- Checking only one fuse and missing that a different one in the same circuit is the actual problem.
- Forgetting that removing the radio fuse can lock the radio, requiring your 5-digit code to unlock it again.
