Procedure ID: P-WP06-2
Chapter: 12
Title: Drive a manual transmission for the first time
Skill level: 1 (anyone — no tools, just practice)
Time: 30-45 minutes for a first practice session, in a quiet empty spot
Tools:
None required.
Parts and supplies:
None required.
Safety warnings:
Always practice first in an empty parking lot or very quiet street, not in traffic.
Always fasten your seatbelt and adjust your seat and mirrors before you start the engine.
Always press the clutch pedal all the way to the floor before turning the key to start;
the starter will not engage otherwise.
Do not rev the engine hard or ride the clutch pedal partly pressed; this wears the clutch
out faster and can damage it.
Always come to a full stop before shifting into reverse.
Do not try to shift straight from 5th gear into reverse; the transmission has a lockout
and needs a short pause or a pass through a forward gear first.
Steps:
1. Adjust your seat, mirrors, and seatbelt before starting the engine.
2. Push the clutch pedal all the way to the floor; the starter only works with the clutch
down.
3. Turn the key to start the engine, then shift the lever into first gear.
4. Keep the brake pedal or parking brake applied while slowly letting the clutch pedal
rise.
5. Feel for the "friction point," where the engine note changes and the car wants to
creep forward.
6. Gently press the gas pedal while continuing to ease the clutch pedal up.
7. Once the car is moving, finish releasing the clutch pedal and keep your foot off it.
8. Shift up around 15 mph into 2nd, 27 mph into 3rd, 39 mph into 4th, 53 mph into 5th.
9. To shift up, press the clutch fully down, move the lever to the next gear, then ease
the clutch back up.
10. To slow down, press the clutch down fully just before the engine would stall, then
brake normally.
11. Once stopped, shift to neutral and take your foot off the clutch until ready to move
again.
How you know it worked: You can pull away smoothly, shift up and down through the gears,
and come to a full stop, all without the engine stalling or the car jerking.
Common mistakes:
Letting the clutch pedal up too fast, which stalls the engine.
Riding the clutch (holding it partly pressed) while driving, wearing it out faster.
Looking down at the shift lever instead of watching the road.
Forgetting to press the clutch in before the car comes to a stop, causing a stall.
Trying to shift straight from 5th gear to reverse instead of pausing first.
