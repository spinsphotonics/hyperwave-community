Procedure ID: P-WP11-7
Chapter: 27 (Brakes: inspection, pads, rotors, fluid)
Title: Replace the front brake pads
Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care)
Time: 60 minutes for a first-timer (both front wheels)

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- Lug wrench and torque wrench (80 lbf-ft / 108 N-m for wheel nuts)
- Socket or wrench for the caliper slide-pin bolts (community-reported as smaller than the bracket bolts; exact size UNVERIFIED — check yours before buying, see Source note below)
- Socket or wrench for the caliper mounting-bracket bolts (community-reported as metric 17 mm; UNVERIFIED, single source — see torque spec table, Appendix C, when available)
- C-clamp or a dedicated brake piston tool, to push the piston back into the caliper
- Wire brush and brake-parts cleaner
- Wheel chocks

Parts and supplies:
- New front brake pads for the 2008 Fit base trim — part number UNVERIFIED this pass (no Tier 1/2 source found; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F for the confirmed number)
- Fresh Honda Heavy Duty Brake Fluid DOT 3, in case you need to top off the reservoir [Source: S-001/S-023]
- High-temperature brake grease for the pad's metal backing and the slide pins

Safety warnings:
- Always wear eye protection. Brake dust and springs under tension can hurt your eyes.
- Do not breathe brake dust; never use compressed air or a dry brush on brake parts, per the manual's own maintenance-safety caution. Use brake-parts cleaner instead.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.
- Always work on one side at a time and leave the other wheel on the ground so you have a reference for how parts go back together.
- Do not let the caliper hang by its brake hose; support or hang it so the hose has no weight on it.
- Always pump the brake pedal several times before driving, until it feels firm, before you rely on the brakes again.

Steps:
1. Loosen the front wheel nuts, then raise and support the car on jack stands.
2. Remove the wheel and set it aside.
3. Look at how the old pads and springs sit before you remove anything, or take a photo.
4. Remove the caliper slide-pin bolts and lift the caliper off the bracket.
5. Support the caliper with a hook or bungee so it does not hang by the hose.
6. Pull the old pads out of the bracket.
7. Use the C-clamp against the old pad to push the caliper piston back into its bore.
8. Clean the bracket and slide pins with a wire brush and brake-parts cleaner.
9. Apply a thin coat of brake grease to the pad backing and slide pins.
10. Place the new pads into the bracket.
11. Slide the caliper back over the new pads and reinstall the slide-pin bolts.
12. Reinstall the wheel and snug the nuts by hand.
13. Lower the car, then torque the wheel nuts to 80 lbf-ft (108 N-m) in a crisscross pattern.
14. Repeat steps 1 through 13 on the other front wheel.
15. Pump the brake pedal several times with the engine off until it feels firm before driving.
16. Check the brake fluid reservoir level and top it off if needed.
17. Drive slowly at first and test the brakes gently before returning to normal driving.

How you know it worked: The brake pedal feels firm (not soft or spongy), the car stops evenly with no pulling to one side, and there is no grinding or squealing under light braking.

Common mistakes:
- Pushing the piston back without first opening the brake fluid reservoir cap slightly, which can push fluid out or damage a seal.
- Forgetting to support the caliper, stretching or kinking the brake hose.
- Skipping the "pump the pedal first" step and finding the pedal goes to the floor on your first drive.
- Mixing up which side a wear-indicator tab or shim goes on.
