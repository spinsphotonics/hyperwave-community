Chapter: 28 (Battery: testing, cleaning, replacing, jump starting)
Title: Clean the battery terminals
Skill level: 1 (anyone)
Time: 15 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- Small wire brush or an old toothbrush
- A cup for mixing baking soda and water
- Rag or paper towel

Parts and supplies:
- Baking soda (about 2-3 tablespoons)
- Water
- Grease, to coat the terminals afterward and slow future corrosion [Source: S-001/S-023]

Safety warnings:
- Always wear eye protection and protective clothing, or have a skilled technician do this, per the manual — the battery gives off explosive hydrogen gas.
- Do not smoke or bring sparks or flames near the battery.
- Always wash your hands after handling the battery or its terminals; they contain lead and lead compounds.
- Always disconnect the negative (-) cable first if you need to remove a cable, and reconnect it last.

Steps:
1. Look at each terminal for a white or yellowish powder; this is corrosion.
2. Mix baking soda with a little water into a thick paste.
3. Cover the corroded terminal with the baking soda and water solution.
4. Watch it bubble up and turn brown as it reacts with the corrosion.
5. Once the bubbling stops, wash the terminal off with plain water.
6. Dry the battery and terminals with a cloth or paper towel.
7. Coat the clean terminals with a thin layer of grease to help prevent future corrosion.
8. Check that your radio still works; if it shows "ENTER CODE," enter your 5-digit code.

How you know it worked: The white or yellow powder is gone, the terminals look clean and shiny, and the cable clamps sit tight on the posts with no wobble.

Common mistakes:
- Skipping the grease coating, so corrosion comes right back within months.
- Not washing off all the baking soda paste, leaving a residue.
- Forgetting the radio code if a cable had to come off during cleaning.
