Chapter: 26 (Tires: pressure, rotation, wear, replacement, the spare)
Title: Check and set your tire pressure
Skill level: 1 (anyone)
Time: 10 minutes for a first-timer (all four tires plus the spare)

Tools:
- Your own tire pressure gauge (the manual recommends using your own gauge every time, not a shop's, so readings stay consistent)
- Air source: a portable inflator, or a gas station air pump with a gauge

Parts and supplies:
- None (no consumables)

Safety warnings:
- Always check pressure when the tires are cold — parked 3+ hours, or driven less than 1 mile (1.6 km). Hot tires read 4-6 psi high and will fool you into underfilling.
- Do not let air out of a hot tire to try to match the cold-pressure number; you will underinflate it.
- Always check the spare tire too, not just the four road tires.

Steps:
1. Find the recommended pressures on the sticker inside the driver's doorjamb, or use 32 psi front and rear (all trims except Sport, cold) as a fallback.
2. Remove the valve cap from the first tire and set it somewhere you won't lose it.
3. Press the tire gauge firmly onto the valve stem until the hiss stops and read the number.
4. Compare the reading to the recommended pressure for that tire.
5. If the pressure is low, add air in short bursts, checking often, until it matches the number.
6. If the pressure is too high, press the small pin inside the valve to release a little air, then recheck.
7. Reinstall the valve cap.
8. Repeat steps 2 through 7 for the other three tires.
9. Check the compact spare the same way; it should be 60 psi (420 kPa).
10. While you are down there, look each tire over for bulges, cuts, or objects stuck in the tread.

How you know it worked: Every tire, including the spare, reads within about 1 psi of its recommended cold pressure, and no tire shows a bulge, cut, or embedded object.

Common mistakes:
- Checking pressure right after driving, which gives a falsely high reading.
- Using a different gauge each time and not noticing it reads differently from your own.
- Forgetting the spare tire, which slowly loses pressure over months even if never used.
- Not noticing the low-tire-pressure warning light and skipping the physical check anyway.
