Title: Fix a small dent, treat surface rust, and touch up paint

NOTE: this card combines three small cosmetic body jobs (Chapter 46's dent repair, rust
treatment, and paint touch-up) because they share the same basic workflow — clean the area,
work the metal or fill it, then match and blend paint — and a beginner typically does them in
that order on the same spot. This card cross-references facts/WP-02.md's rust finding
(F-WP02-024, the "rear quarter panel rust" spot on first-generation Fits) and facts/WP-13.md's
cost/DIY assessment (F-WP13-009), rather than re-researching those findings from scratch, per
this WP's instructions.

Skill level: 2 (needs basic tools) for a small dent, light surface rust, and a small paint chip
or scratch. If rust has already eaten through the metal (a hole, not just surface staining),
that portion of the job becomes skill level 4 — cutting out rusted metal and welding in a patch
panel is not a beginner job, and a cheap-looking bodywork patch over rusted metal does not
actually stop the rust underneath [Source: F-WP13-009].

Time: 30-60 minutes for a small dent or a touch-up paint chip; 60-120 minutes for early-stage rust treatment on a small area

Tools:
- A plunger or a dedicated dent-pulling tool, for a small, shallow dent in a flat panel
- 220-600 grit sandpaper, for feathering paint edges and prepping rust spots
- A wire brush or rust-removal wheel (drill-mounted), for surface rust
- Masking tape and paper, to protect surrounding paint
- A small foam applicator or fine artist's brush, for touch-up paint

Parts and supplies:
- Rust converter/inhibitor product, for treating surface rust before painting over it
- Rust-inhibiting primer
- Touch-up paint matched to your car's paint code — find the code on your doorjamb certification label per facts/WP-01.md (F-WP01-032/033); treat any specific paint code number as a lead to verify against your own car's label, not a confirmed fact, since this project's own research could not independently confirm exact 2008 paint codes (facts/WP-01.md F-WP01-030)
- Clear coat, matched to your paint type, for the final layer over touch-up paint

Safety warnings:
- Always wear gloves and eye protection when sanding, wire-brushing, or using chemical rust converters.
- Do not use rust converter or primer in a closed, unventilated space; work outdoors or with good airflow.
- Always let each layer (converter, primer, paint, clear coat) dry or cure fully before adding the next, per that product's own instructions.
- Do not sand through to bare metal over a large area without immediately protecting it with primer; bare metal left exposed will start rusting again quickly.

Steps — small dent:
1. Clean the dented area with soap and water, then dry it.
2. For a shallow dent on a flat panel, use a plunger with some water on the rim to create suction, then pull outward in short, firm pulls.
3. For a dent a plunger cannot pull, a dedicated dent-puller kit (glue tabs and a slide hammer) can work on many minor dents without paint damage.
4. Check the repaired area's paint for cracking; a cracked clear coat over a pulled dent may still need a touch-up.

Steps — surface rust (not yet a hole):
5. Clean the rusted area with soap and water, then dry it fully.
6. Sand or wire-brush the rust down to a sound, unrusted surface, feathering the edges into the good paint around it.
7. Wipe away all dust, then apply rust converter/inhibitor per its instructions.
8. Once the converter has cured, apply rust-inhibiting primer over the treated area.
9. If the rust has already progressed to a hole in the metal, stop here and treat this as a level 4, shop-recommended repair — see the note above.

Steps — paint touch-up:
10. Mask off the area around the chip, scratch, or repaired dent/rust spot with tape and paper.
11. Lightly sand the immediate area with fine (600 grit or finer) sandpaper if there is a rough edge, then wipe it clean.
12. Apply touch-up paint in thin coats with a fine brush or applicator, letting each coat dry before the next, rather than one thick coat.
13. Once the paint has cured per its label, apply clear coat in thin coats over the repaired area.
14. Remove the masking tape and paper once everything has cured.
15. Wax the area once the repair is fully cured, blending it into the surrounding panel.

How you know it worked: The dent is pulled flat enough that it is not obviously visible from a normal viewing distance, there is no more active rust bleeding through the paint at the treated spot, and the touch-up paint is close enough in color and gloss that it is not glaringly obvious up close.

Common mistakes:
- Painting directly over untreated rust, which continues to spread invisibly under the new paint.
- Applying touch-up paint in one thick coat, causing it to run or look lumpy.
- Skipping the clear coat step, leaving the touch-up looking dull or mismatched in gloss.
- Trying to patch a rust hole with filler alone instead of cutting out the rusted metal and welding in new metal, which does not actually fix the underlying problem [Source: F-WP13-009].
