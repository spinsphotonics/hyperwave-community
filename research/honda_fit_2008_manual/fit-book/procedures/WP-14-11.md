Title: Replace the ignition switch

RESEARCH FINDING: facts/WP-02.md (F-WP02-013 area) already checked for an NHTSA recall
specific to the 2008 Fit's ignition switch or lock cylinder and found none — this is a
wear-and-tear repair, not a recall-driven one. Community sources are consistent that there are
two different jobs hiding under one name: replacing just the electrical switch behind the
steering column covers (a moderate DIY job with basic tools), versus replacing the whole
lock-cylinder/key-barrel assembly (which needs drilling out security shear bolts and, on many
Hondas, reprogramming the immobilizer to accept the new key) — the second job is significantly
harder [Source: S-WP14-E].

Skill level: 4 (shop recommended) — this book rates the whole job level 4 because the two
sub-jobs above are easy to confuse from the outside (a "hard to turn key" symptom can mean
either one), and choosing the wrong repair path wastes time and money. A shop can diagnose which
part has actually failed before you buy parts.

Time: N/A for full DIY steps at this skill level — see "What the shop will do" below.

What the shop will do:
- Diagnose whether the problem is the electrical switch (key turns but the car does not respond correctly) or the mechanical lock cylinder (key will not turn, or turns roughly) [Source: S-WP14-E].
- If it is the electrical switch only: remove the plastic covers around the steering column, unplug and unbolt the old switch, and install the new one — this part alone is described by community sources as feasible for a confident DIYer with basic tools [Source: S-WP14-E].
- If it is the lock cylinder / full ignition lock housing: remove the steering column covers, drill out the security shear bolts that hold the housing, remove the old housing (often easier than trying to save the roll-pin-retained lock cylinder inside it), and install a new assembly — reported at around 2 hours of labor [Source: S-WP14-E].
- If a new lock cylinder or immobilizer-linked part is installed, program the new key(s) to the immobilizer system so the engine will start (facts/WP-04.md documents this car's immobilizer light and behavior).
- Test that the key turns smoothly through LOCK, ACCESSORY, ON, and START, and that the car starts reliably afterward.

Safety warnings (read even if a shop is doing this job, so you know what to ask about):
- Always disconnect the battery before any steering-column work, per standard practice around airbag (SRS) wiring that routes near the column.
- Do not attempt to bypass the immobilizer system instead of properly reprogramming a new key; a bypassed immobilizer is a theft and safety risk.
- Always confirm with the shop that the SRS/airbag wiring near the column was not disturbed, or that it was properly reconnected if it was.

How you know it worked: The key turns smoothly through all four positions (LOCK, ACCESSORY, ON, START) with no binding or grinding, the car starts reliably every time, and no new dashboard warning lights (especially the immobilizer or airbag light) stay on after the repair.

Common mistakes:
- Buying and installing a replacement electrical switch when the real problem is the mechanical lock cylinder, or vice versa, without a proper diagnosis first.
- Forgetting to reprogram the immobilizer for a new key, leaving the car unable to start even though the mechanical repair is done correctly.
- Disturbing SRS/airbag wiring near the steering column without properly reconnecting and testing it.
