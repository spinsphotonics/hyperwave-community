Chapter: 42 (Cooling system: thermostat, radiator, water pump)
Title: Replace the water pump

RESEARCH FINDING: on this engine, the water pump is driven by the external drive belt (the
same belt the Maintenance Minder calls out for periodic "inspect and adjust," per facts/WP-09.md
F-WP09-007), and its pulley is reached from the right front wheel well — NOT by removing the
timing chain cover. This is confirmed directly from a real service-manual-style page for this
car (Source: S-WP14-A, hfitinfo.com "Water Pump Inspection"), which describes checking the pump
by removing the wheel, splash shield, and drive belt, then spinning the pulley by hand. This is
meaningfully simpler than a timing-chain-driven water pump job on other engines, which is why
this book rates it skill level 3 rather than shop-recommended.

Skill level: 3 (needs jack stands and care)
Time: 90 minutes for a first-timer

Tools:
- Jack and 2 jack stands (see P-WP11-22)
- Socket set for the splash shield and pulley bolts
- A clean drain pan sized for your coolant capacity (see Appendix B)
- Torque wrench

Parts and supplies:
- New water pump for the L15A1 engine — part number UNVERIFIED this pass; ask a Honda parts counter or check Appendix F
- New gasket or O-ring for the water pump body, per your new pump's instructions
- Fresh Honda-type coolant (see Appendix B for type and capacity)

Safety warnings:
- Always let the engine cool completely before draining coolant.
- Do not open the cooling system while the engine is warm or running.
- Always wear eye protection when draining or refilling coolant.
- Do not let coolant contact your skin for long periods or leave a spill where pets or children can reach it.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands.

Steps:
1. Make sure the engine is completely cool before starting.
2. Drain the engine coolant into your drain pan.
3. Raise the car and support it on jack stands, then remove the right front wheel.
4. Remove the splash shield.
5. Loosen the water pump pulley mounting bolts, then remove the drive belt [Source: S-WP14-A].
6. Remove the water pump pulley mounting bolts and the pulley.
7. Remove the water pump's mounting bolts and lift the old pump away from the engine block.
8. Clean the old gasket material off the mounting surface.
9. Fit the new pump with a new gasket or O-ring, per its instructions.
10. Install the pump's mounting bolts and torque them per the pump's instructions.
11. Exact Honda torque value UNVERIFIED this pass; see Appendix C for any later confirmation.
12. Install the water pump pulley and loosely fit its mounting bolts.
13. Install the drive belt.
14. Tighten the water pump pulley mounting bolts [Source: S-WP14-A].
15. Install the splash shield.
16. Install the right front wheel and lower the car.
17. Refill the radiator with the correct coolant and bleed air from the system per your car's fill procedure.
18. Clean up any spilled coolant immediately.
19. Start the engine, let it reach normal operating temperature, and check the pump area for leaks.

How you know it worked: There is no coolant leak or weeping around the new pump, the drive belt runs quietly with no squeal, the temperature gauge/indicator stays normal on a test drive, and there is no "weeping" at the pump's bleed holes beyond the small, normal amount the source itself notes as acceptable on a healthy pump [Source: S-WP14-A].

Common mistakes:
- Mistaking normal, very slight weeping at the bleed holes on the OLD pump for proof it must be replaced — the source notes this is normal on a good pump; replace based on the "does the pulley turn freely" test or a visible active leak, not a trace of weeping alone.
- Reinstalling the drive belt with the wrong tension, causing a squeal or premature wear.
- Skipping the bleed-air step after refilling, leading to false overheating readings.
- Working on a warm engine and getting burned.
