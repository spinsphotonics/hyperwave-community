## Section A: OBD-II port location (Chapter 35)

Fact ID: F-WP12-001
Chapter: 35
Question: Where is the OBD-II diagnostic (data link) connector located on the 2008 Honda Fit?
Value: Under the dashboard, on the driver's side, directly below/left of the steering column
— roughly level with, or a little above, the brake and gas pedals. It is not usually labeled
and may be tucked up against the underside of the dash rather than hanging down where it is
easy to see; you generally need to crouch down and feel with your hand, or get your head down
near the pedals to see it. A directly-read Tier 3 reference (carcodefinder.com) states this
explicitly for this exact car: "The Honda Fit and HR-V place the port directly below the
steering column, which is more conventional" (i.e., more conventional than some other Honda
models, which Honda has moved around over the years — see Note below). A 2008-Fit-specific
Tier 4 forum thread independently describes it as "on the driver's side but toward the center
console, not the door, and you need to get your head way down there to get the right angle to
see it" — consistent with the same driver's-side-under-dash zone, phrased from the center-
console side of that same area rather than the steering-column side (not a real conflict, just
two people describing the same tucked-away spot from different angles).
Note (general automotive knowledge, no Fit-specific citation needed): the general zone is not
arbitrary. Every 1996-and-newer US gasoline vehicle is legally required to have this 16-pin
connector, and its placement is governed by SAE standard J1962: it must be within the area
from the driver's end of the dashboard to a point just past the vehicle's centerline, mounted
to the instrument panel, and reachable from the driver's seat without tools (a snap-off plastic
cover is allowed; needing a screwdriver is not). This is why "under the dash, driver's side,
near the steering column" is the correct first place to look on almost any 1996+ US car,
including this one.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes — the OBD-II connector and its required location are
federally mandated and identical across all trims/model years of a given generation; this is
not a base-vs-Sport equipment difference)
Source: carcodefinder.com "Where Is My OBD-II Port?" guide (Tier 3, directly read via curl —

Fact ID: F-WP12-002

Question: OBD-II code — P0300
Value: Meaning: Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire Detected — the ECM sees fewer regular
crankshaft-speed "steps" than a normal firing cycle should produce, on more than one cylinder
or moving between cylinders. First thing to check: on this car, forum consensus is to check the
spark plugs and (especially) the factory ignition coil packs first, since owners report
multiple coil packs failing around the same time is not unusual; the recommended OEM
replacement coil named in discussion is Hitachi IGC0053, and only NGK or Denso spark plugs are
generally recommended over other brands. A flashing (not steady) malfunction indicator lamp
with this code means an active, severe misfire — see the owner's manual guidance already
confirmed in WP-04 (flashing MIL = pull over, do not keep driving, per D9 safety stance) and
stop driving, since raw fuel dumped into a hot catalytic converter can destroy it.

code in this list: it is confirmed by an actual Honda Technical Service Bulletin (13-021,
already vetted in WP-02/F-WP02-009) that names "2007-11 Fit — ALL — ALL" in its own affected-
vehicles table, not just a forum guess.

## Section G: "Won't start" decision tree (Chapter 36)

Grounded in: the clutch-pedal and Park+brake starting requirements (F-WP04-002/003), the
immobilizer system indicator behavior (F-WP04-029), the jump-start procedure and battery/
terminal locations already confirmed from the owner's manual (F-WP07-011 through 015), and
otherwise general, widely-taught battery/starter/fuel/ignition troubleshooting logic. No step
below has more than 3 branches.

1. Turn the key to ON (II) without starting. Do the dashboard warning lights and gauges light
   up normally? If yes, go to step 2. If no (dash is completely dark/dead), go to step 8.
2. For a manual transmission: is the clutch pedal pressed all the way to the floor? For an
   automatic: is the shift lever fully in Park (or Neutral) with the brake pedal pressed? (The
   starter will not engage at all unless this is satisfied — F-WP04-002/003.) If yes and it
   still will not start, go to step 3. If no, correct this and try again — if it now starts,
   you are done.
3. Turn the key to ON (II) and watch the key-shaped immobilizer indicator. Does it flash or
   stay lit instead of going out normally? If yes, go to step 4. If no, go to step 5.
4. Try your spare key. If the spare key starts the car, your primary key or its chip is
   likely damaged — stop here and have the primary key replaced by a dealer. If the spare key
   also fails to start it, the immobilizer system itself may be at fault — stop here and see a
   dealer or qualified shop.
5. Turn the key to START. Does the engine crank (turn over/spin) at a normal speed? If yes,
   go to step 6. If it makes no sound and does not move at all, go to step 8. If you hear a
   single click, or a slow "rrr-rrr" grinding sound and the engine barely turns, go to step 8.
6. The engine cranks normally but does not fire. Do you smell strong raw gasoline, or did you
   pump the gas pedal several times before trying to start? If yes, this may be a flooded
   engine — wait a few minutes without touching the pedal, then try again without pumping the
   pedal; if it still will not fire, go to step 7. If no, go to step 7.
7. Check the fuel gauge. Is the tank empty or very close to empty? If yes, add fuel and try
   again. If no, this points to a fuel-delivery or ignition problem (fuel pump/relay/fuse,
   spark plugs, or ignition coils) that needs a scan tool and a shop visit — stop here and
   arrange service.
8. Check the battery terminals. Are they clean, tight, and free of white/blue corrosion? If
   no, clean and tighten them, then go back to step 1. If yes, go to step 9.
9. Jump-start the car using the terminals and procedure already confirmed in the owner's
   manual (positive-to-positive on both batteries first, then negative on the booster battery,
   then the unpainted metal ground point on the dead car — F-WP07-012). Does it start? If yes,
   drive for at least 20-30 minutes to recharge, then have the battery and charging system
   tested soon. If no, the starter, alternator, or a deeper electrical fault is likely — stop
   here and arrange a tow to a shop.
