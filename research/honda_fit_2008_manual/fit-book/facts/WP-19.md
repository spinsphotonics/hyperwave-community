## Section A: Seat and harness rules — SCCA Solo (autocross) (Chapter 57)

Fact ID: F-WP19-001
Chapter: 57
Question: What does the current SCCA National Solo rulebook require for seat belts/driver
restraints in a car that has not been modified with a roll bar (i.e., a typical stock or
lightly-modified base 2008 Fit running Street, Street Touring, Street Prepared, or Street
Modified category)?
Value: Seat **lap belts are required in all cars** and must be installed in any car whose passive
restraint system doesn't already include one. Installing and using shoulder belts or a harness is
"strongly recommended," but is **not itself required** for a closed car. Critically, **non-factory
upper-body restraints (aftermarket harnesses) may ONLY be used in open cars, cars with targa-tops
in the open position, or cars with T-tops in the open position** — and only then if (1) the car's
roll structure meets SCCA Solo Appendix C or the SCCA Club Racing GCR Section 9.4, AND (2) the top
of that roll structure is not below the top of the driver's helmet in normal driving position. A
closed car (like a stock hardtop Fit) is expected to use its factory-installed seat belt system;
the rulebook does not authorize swapping in a racing harness on a closed car at all.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes — this is a car/class-agnostic rulebook rule, not
Fit-specific, but it applies fully to a stock closed-body Fit)

Question: What helmet standard does SCCA Solo require, and does it apply to every class/run
group or only some?
Value: Helmets are required for **every competitor on course, in every class, with no exception**

securely fastened, and event organizers should have loaner helmets on hand for competitors/

Fact ID: F-WP19-003

Question: Does SCCA Solo require a roll bar or roll cage for a stock, closed-body car (Street /
Street Touring / Street Prepared / Street Modified category), and if a competitor chooses to add
one anyway, what governs it?
Value: **No.** Roll bars/cages are "strongly recommended in all cars" but are **not mandatory**
for a closed car in the Street, Street Touring, Street Prepared, or Street Modified categories —
mandatory-use classes are listed separately (see F-WP19-004). If a Street/ST/SP/SM competitor

otherwise clear. This does not apply to the base 2008 Fit, which is a closed hardtop with no
factory convertible/targa/T-top configuration (per WP-01), so a stock-classed base Fit at
autocross needs neither a roll bar nor a harness — only its factory lap-and-shoulder belt and a
helmet.

Fact ID: F-WP19-004
Chapter: 57
Question: In which SCCA Solo classes IS a roll bar or roll cage mandatory, and by what spec?
Value: A roll bar meeting Solo Appendix C, or a roll cage meeting SCCA Club Racing GCR Section
9.4 (GT/Production-based cars) or GCR Section 9.4.5 (formula/sports-racing cars), is **required**
in: **all A Modified (AM), B Modified (BM), C Modified (CM), and F Modified (FM) vehicles**, and
**all open cars in the Prepared category, D Modified (DM) class, and E Modified (EM) class.**

2008 Fit run as a stock or lightly-modified street car would not be classed into any of these
(see WP-20 for the Fit's actual class). Separately, in the ground-up-built "Modified Category"
formula-style classes (Section 18), a roll bar conforming to Appendix C AND a 5-, 6-, or 7-point
driver restraint system per Club Racing GCR Section 9.3.19 are both explicitly required together
— i.e., the two requirements (cage + harness) are bundled once a car reaches that level of
purpose-built construction.

Fact ID: F-WP19-006
Chapter: 57
Question: If a competitor installs a harness bar (a bar to relocate/mount aftermarket belts)
without a full cage, what does SCCA Solo say about that, and does it substitute for a roll bar?
Value: SCCA Solo explicitly allows the standard factory seat belts to be removed to install a
horizontal "harness bar" for mounting alternate belts — described in several category sections
(Street Prepared, Prepared, Modified) as either a straight bar bolted to the chassis at two
points, or a "C-type" bar bolted at two points plus two supporting connections to resist rotation.
Truss-type harness bars with more than two attachment points are explicitly restricted in some of
these sections. Importantly, a harness bar is a **belt-mounting fixture, not a certified roll
structure** — it does not by itself satisfy the Appendix C / GCR 9.4 roll bar requirement that
Section 3.3.1 demands before a non-factory upper-body restraint may be used at all (see
F-WP19-001). In practice this means a harness bar alone, with no roll bar/cage, is not sufficient
grounds under these rules to run an aftermarket harness in place of the factory belts on a closed
car.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (unclear — only relevant if a competitor is considering a harness
bar; general Street Prepared/Prepared/Modified category rule text, not Fit-specific)

## Section B: Seat and harness rules — NASA (HPDE / Time Trial and wheel-to-wheel Competition) (Chapter 57)

Fact ID: F-WP19-007
Chapter: 57
Question: What does NASA's Club Codes and Regulations (CCR) require for seat belts in an HPDE
(High Performance Driving Event) or Time Trial car — the run-group/class structure a Fit owner
doing track days, not wheel-to-wheel racing, would actually use?
Value: The driver (and any passenger) must use "modern style stock seatbelts in very good
condition," or a DOT-approved restraint system. **Lap belts used without any shoulder restraint
are explicitly not permitted.** Four-point aftermarket belt sets are restricted: only sets
carrying an FIA "B-xxx.T/98" (or newer) certification, or a manufacturer label stating the belts
meet Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) 209 AND that they were specifically designed
for that exact vehicle, are allowed in HPDE/Time Trial — four-point sets carrying only a DOT
certification (with no FIA or vehicle-specific-FMVSS label) are explicitly prohibited. The CCR
states plainly: "Time Trial vehicles must meet the minimum standard for HPDE" — i.e., NASA does
not define a separate, more relaxed or more strict seatbelt standard for Time Trial versus
ordinary HPDE track days; they run under the same Section 11 rules.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes — car/event-type-agnostic rule that applies directly to a
stock Fit doing an NASA HPDE or Time Trial day)

Fact ID: F-WP19-010
Chapter: 57
Question: Does NASA require a roll bar for HPDE or Time Trial cars based on the car's SPEED or
LAP TIME (as the research plan assumed it might), or on something else — and would a stock
closed-body 2008 Fit ever need one for HPDE/TT?
Value: **Neither speed nor lap time gates the roll bar requirement in NASA's HPDE/Time Trial
rules — it is gated entirely by whether the car is an OPEN car.** The CCR states: "All open cars
should have a roll bar installed to help protect the occupant(s) from injury during a roll-over,"
with the same NASA-published main-hoop/weld/padding/seatback-brace standards applying whenever a
roll bar is fitted. No provision in Section 11 (HPDE/TT vehicle technical regulations) ties a
roll bar requirement to a driver's recorded lap time, top speed, or run-group assignment (Novice
through Time Trial/Advanced groups all fall under the same Section 11 rules per F-WP19-007). A
stock, closed-body 2008 Fit — which has no factory open-top, T-top, or targa configuration — is
therefore **not required to have a roll bar for any NASA HPDE or Time Trial run group at any
speed or lap time**, under this specific rulebook. This is a real, useful correction to the
research plan's own working assumption that HPDE/TT roll-bar rules "vary by speed/lap-time
thresholds" — in NASA's CCR they do not; only the open-car/closed-car distinction matters, plus
whatever a competitor's own added equipment (e.g., a fixed-back racing seat, F-WP19-008) triggers.

Fact ID: F-WP19-011
Chapter: 57
Question: For wheel-to-wheel Competition/racing (as opposed to HPDE/Time Trial), what does NASA
require for a roll cage, and is it ever optional based on car type or speed?
Value: For NASA's "Competition" activity type — defined as "any speed contest where more than
one (1) vehicle is on course at the same time... and recognition is given to the top finishers,"
i.e. wheel-to-wheel racing — a full roll cage built to CCR Section 15.6 is **always required,
with no car-type or speed-based exception.** Minimum requirements include: a continuous-piece
main hoop as wide as the interior and as close to the roof as possible, no more than four bends
totaling 180°±10°, a diagonal brace, high-density padding on any surface the driver's head/knees/
elbows could contact, mandrel-bent tubing with no crimping, minimum tubing sizes calculated from
the car's as-raced weight (Section 15.6.21), and mounting to the floor/rocker-panel area at six,
seven, or eight points (never through the firewall — the 7th/8th points attach at the firewall).
The chapter's own stated purpose: "The basic purpose of the roll cage is to protect the occupant
in case of a rollover or a collision." Cars already homologated to another sanctioning body's
rules (FIA, SCCA, IMSA, SRO, etc.) must instead conform to that body's own roll cage and safety
rules when competing at a NASA event.

Question: For wheel-to-wheel Competition, what specific harness/restraint certification does

Value: A 5-, 6-, or 7-point seat belt/harness system is required, with the point-count keyed to

cutoff. Any driver involved in a high-impact crash must send all safety belts back to the
manufacturer for inspection/re-webbing/re-certification before they can be used again, with proof
of re-certification being the driver's own responsibility.

## Summary for downstream chapters

- **Autocross (SCCA Solo):** a stock base 2008 Fit needs only its factory lap-and-shoulder belt
  and a helmet meeting a specific Snell/SFI/FIA list — no harness, no roll bar, at any class or
  speed, while classed in Street/Street Touring/Street Prepared/Street Modified. Roll bars/cages
  only become mandatory in specific heavily-modified classes (AM/BM/CM/FM, open Prepared cars,
  DM, EM) that a base-trim Fit would not normally reach. Aftermarket harnesses are flatly not
  authorized on a closed car under these rules at all.
- **HPDE / Time Trial (NASA CCR):** stock seatbelts (2-point-minimum-shoulder, DOT/FIA/FMVSS-
  vehicle-specific 4-point) are fine; a roll bar is required ONLY for open-top cars, never gated
  by lap time or speed — directly correcting the research plan's own assumption that this
  threshold would be speed/lap-time-based. Adding a fixed-back racing seat effectively requires a
  harness (CCR Section 15.5 spec) even without a cage requirement.
- **Wheel-to-wheel Competition (NASA CCR Section 15 / SCCA Club Racing GCR referenced from Solo
  Appendix C):** a full, spec-built roll cage and an SFI/FIA-certified 5/6/7-point harness are
  always mandatory, for every car, with zero car-type or speed exception.
- **Weight reduction:** the most solid GD3-specific numbers found are the spare tire (21 lb), its
  cover (7.76 lb), the jack and tools (4.91 lb) — about 30-35 lb combined — and the full A/C
  system (23.46 lb), giving roughly 55-60 lb of confirmed, reasonably realistic weight savings

  an undercount) and is a genuinely modest item on this car. Rear seat weight could NOT be
  confirmed for the GD3 specifically (the only source found names the 2nd-generation GE8 instead,
  ~67 lb, given only as unconfirmed cross-generation context per D8). A 534.67 lb "everything
  out, race-trim" ceiling exists in the community record but requires interior gutting,
  aftermarket wheels, and a fixed-back seat that Street category's own rules do not permit — it
  belongs to a dedicated build, not a typical weekend autocrosser.
