## Section A: NHTSA Recalls (2007-2008 Honda Fit specifically named)

Fact ID: F-WP02-001
Chapter: Appendix-I
Question: NHTSA recall — power window master switch water intrusion (first campaign)
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 10V033000 (Honda recall no. R28). CORRECTED 2026-09-07: the
original "announced approx. January 29, 2010" date was wrong. Directly confirmed via NHTSA's
own recall record (`api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignsById?campaignNumber=10V033000`): NHTSA
received the report February 2, 2010, and the record's own remedy text states the safety
recall officially began March 19, 2010. (One earlier source cross-referenced this campaign as
"12V-073" but three independent sources, and now the direct NHTSA record itself, confirm
10V033000 as the correct campaign ID — treat 12V-073 as unconfirmed/likely error.) Affects
certain model year 2007-2008 Fit. Component: power window master switch, driver's door.
Defect: under severe conditions, rain water or spilled liquid entering through an open
driver's window can reach the power window master switch and damage its printed circuit
board. A damaged PCB can short and overheat the switch connectors, which can melt and, in
rare cases, produce smoke or fire. Approx. 141,140 units sold in the US were affected.
Remedy: dealer inspects the switch and installs a cover around the switch housing to prevent
water entry; if the switch is already damaged, it is replaced and a cover installed — free of
charge. Owners may contact Honda at 1-800-999-1009.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes) — all-trims recall, not base-only
Source: NHTSA recalls API, direct read (`recallsByVehicle` and `campaignsById` for
10V033000), corroborating the original NHTSA Part 573 Safety Recall Report
(RCMN-10V033-4115.pdf) and prior third-party coverage (theweeklydriver.com, repairpal.com,
cars.com)

Question: NHTSA recall — headlight low-beam wiring harness / combination switch
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 10V624000. RESOLVED 2026-09-07 (conflict C-001): the recall
officially began January 24, 2011. Confirmed directly against NHTSA's own campaign record
(`api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignsById?campaignNumber=10V624000`), whose remedy text states

Defect: an error during installation of the wiring harness for the combination switch that controls low-beam headlight function caused the low-beam circuit wires to be pulled tighter than intended. Repeated use of the headlight switch can wear the electrical connector terminal for the low-beam circuit, lowering conductivity and allowing copper oxide to build up at the contacts. Consequence: low-beam headlights can become inoperative, reducing the driver's visibility and the vehicle's visibility to other traffic, increasing crash risk. Remedy: dealer inspects the low-beam terminal and performs the necessary repair, free of charge. Contact: Honda customer service 1-800-999-1009.

Fact ID: F-WP02-004
Chapter: Appendix-I
Question: NHTSA recall — passenger frontal airbag inflator (Takata-related, recall-remedy/replacement part)
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 19V501000 (Honda's internal recall numbers: E5D and X5C). Recall began on or about August 12, 2019. Affects certain model year 2007-2008 Fit vehicles specifically equipped with a passenger frontal air bag inflator that was itself installed as a recall remedy part or replacement service part (i.e., a Fit that had ALREADY had its original passenger airbag inflator swapped under an earlier Takata recall, or had one installed as a service replacement). Component: passenger frontal airbag inflator. Defect: these replacement/remedy inflators may explode due to propellant (phase-stabilized ammonium nitrate) degradation from long-term exposure to high absolute humidity, high temperature, and temperature cycling — this is the same underlying Takata inflator defect mechanism as the original mass recall, but affecting the second-round replacement parts. Consequence: an inflator explosion can send sharp metal fragments toward the driver or other occupants, risking serious injury or death. Remedy: dealer replaces the passenger frontal airbag inflator again, free of charge. Contact: Honda customer service 1-888-234-2138.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes) — all-trims recall, but only for vehicles whose passenger inflator had already been serviced/replaced under a prior campaign; see F-WP02-009 for the direct Takata yes/no answer

Question: NHTSA recall — drive shaft corrosion (manual transmission)
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 20V770000. American Honda notified NHTSA of this recall on or about December 10, 2020 (NHTSA's ReportReceivedDate field via direct API pull shows 10/12/2020, DD/MM formatted). RESOLVED 2026-09-07 (conflict C-005): confirmed directly against NHTSA's
own campaign record (`api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle`) that the recall's Summary text

Defect: the protective coating on certain drive shafts may not have been applied properly during manufacturing, making the shaft more susceptible to corrosion from road salt or other contaminants; a sufficiently corroded shaft can break. Consequence: a broken drive shaft can cause a sudden loss of drive power (and, per Consumer Reports coverage of this recall, a rollaway risk in certain conditions). Honda stated it was aware of over 350 field reports of drive shaft failure across all vehicles covered by this recall (Fit, Accord, Civic Hybrid, and others combined — not a Fit-only count). Remedy: dealer inspects the drive shaft(s) and replaces them if necessary, free of charge.

Fact ID: F-WP02-027
Chapter: Appendix-I
Question: NHTSA recall — frontal airbag Occupant Detection System (ODS) / Occupant Position Detection System (OPDS) wire harness corrosion (ADDED 2026-09-07 — this campaign was missing from the original research pass; see verify/WP-02.md top-line finding 1)
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 07V549000 (Honda recall no. Q66). The recall began December 12, 2007. Affects certain model year 2007-2008 Fit vehicles originally sold, or currently registered, in 21 listed salt-belt states plus DC: Connecticut, Delaware, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin, and the District of Columbia. Component: frontal airbags — ODS/OPDS wire harness. Defect: the wire harness for the front passenger Occupant Detection System (ODS) and Occupant Position Detection System (OPDS) is routed underneath the carpet on the driver's side floorboard. In salt-belt areas, road salt tracked in on a driver's shoes can melt and seep through the carpet, reaching the harness. If the harness corrodes and a wire breaks, the SRS indicator light illuminates, and — more importantly — the ODS/OPDS may fail to detect the presence of a child seat or an out-of-position passenger, so the front passenger's frontal air bag and side air bag will NOT be suppressed when they should be. Consequence: in a crash, a deploying front passenger air bag or side air bag increases the risk of injury to small or out-of-position occupants. Remedy: dealers inspect and, if necessary, replace the wire harness, free of charge. Owners may contact Honda at 1-800-999-1009.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes, but only if the vehicle was originally sold in, or has ever been registered in, one of the 21 listed salt-belt states or DC — this is a geography-limited recall, not a nationwide one)
