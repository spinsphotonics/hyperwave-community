## Section A: NHTSA Recalls (2007-2008 Honda Fit specifically named)

Fact ID: F-WP02-001
Chapter: Appendix-I
Question: NHTSA recall — power window master switch water intrusion (first campaign)
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 10V033000. Announced by Honda approx. January 29, 2010 (one source cross-referenced this as "12V-073" but three independent sources confirm 10V033 as the campaign ID — treat 12V-073 as unconfirmed/likely error). Affects certain model year 2007-2008 Fit. Component: power window master switch, driver's door. Defect: under severe conditions, rain water or spilled liquid entering through an open driver's window can reach the power window master switch and damage its printed circuit board. A damaged PCB can short and overheat the switch connectors, which can melt and, in rare cases, produce smoke or fire. Approx. 141,140 units sold in the US were affected. Remedy: dealer inspects the switch and, depending on condition, applies a protective skirt to the original switch, installs a new switch with a protective skirt, or replaces the driver's door harness and installs a new switch with a protective skirt — free of charge.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes) — all-trims recall, not base-only
Source: NHTSA Part 573 Safety Recall Report (RCMN-10V033-4115.pdf) and corroborating coverage (theweeklydriver.com, repairpal.com, cars.com)
Source URL: https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2010/RCMN-10V033-4115.pdf

Fact ID: F-WP02-003
Chapter: Appendix-I
Question: NHTSA recall — headlight low-beam wiring harness / combination switch
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 10V624000. Recall expected to begin "on or before January 21, 2011" per one source; a second source states the recall began "January 24, 2011" (dates conflict by a few days — both point to the same late-January 2011 window). Affects 143,083 model year 2007-2008 Fit vehicles in the US. Component: exterior lighting — headlights, high/low beam dimmer (combination) switch and its wiring harness. Defect: an error during installation of the wiring harness for the combination switch that controls low-beam headlight function caused the low-beam circuit wires to be pulled tighter than intended. Repeated use of the headlight switch can wear the electrical connector terminal for the low-beam circuit, lowering conductivity and allowing copper oxide to build up at the contacts. Consequence: low-beam headlights can become inoperative, reducing the driver's visibility and the vehicle's visibility to other traffic, increasing crash risk. Remedy: dealer inspects the low-beam terminal and performs the necessary repair, free of charge. Contact: Honda customer service 1-800-999-1009.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes) — all-trims recall
Source: NHTSA recall records (RCAK-10V624-3082.pdf, RCRIT-10V624-9345.pdf) and Justia auto-recalls listing
Source URL: https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2010/RCAK-10V624-3082.pdf ; https://auto-recalls.justia.com/honda/fit/2007/10v624000/

Fact ID: F-WP02-004
Chapter: Appendix-I
Question: NHTSA recall — passenger frontal airbag inflator (Takata-related, recall-remedy/replacement part)
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 19V501000 (Honda's internal recall numbers: E5D and X5C). Recall began on or about August 12, 2019. Affects certain model year 2007-2008 Fit vehicles specifically equipped with a passenger frontal air bag inflator that was itself installed as a recall remedy part or replacement service part (i.e., a Fit that had ALREADY had its original passenger airbag inflator swapped under an earlier Takata recall, or had one installed as a service replacement). Component: passenger frontal airbag inflator. Defect: these replacement/remedy inflators may explode due to propellant (phase-stabilized ammonium nitrate) degradation from long-term exposure to high absolute humidity, high temperature, and temperature cycling — this is the same underlying Takata inflator defect mechanism as the original mass recall, but affecting the second-round replacement parts. Consequence: an inflator explosion can send sharp metal fragments toward the driver or other occupants, risking serious injury or death. Remedy: dealer replaces the passenger frontal airbag inflator again, free of charge. Contact: Honda customer service 1-888-234-2138.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes) — all-trims recall, but only for vehicles whose passenger inflator had already been serviced/replaced under a prior campaign; see F-WP02-009 for the direct Takata yes/no answer

Fact ID: F-WP02-005
Chapter: Appendix-I
Question: NHTSA recall — drive shaft corrosion (manual transmission)
Value: NHTSA Campaign Number 20V770000. American Honda notified NHTSA of this recall on or about December 10, 2020. Affects certain 2007-2008 (and later, up to about 2013-2014 depending on source — ranges given were "2007-2013" and "2007-2014" in different write-ups) Honda Fit vehicles equipped with a MANUAL transmission. Component: powertrain — driveline — drive shaft. Defect: the protective coating on certain drive shafts may not have been applied properly during manufacturing, making the shaft more susceptible to corrosion from road salt or other contaminants; a sufficiently corroded shaft can break. Consequence: a broken drive shaft can cause a sudden loss of drive power (and, per Consumer Reports coverage of this recall, a rollaway risk in certain conditions). Honda stated it was aware of over 350 field reports of drive shaft failure across all vehicles covered by this recall (Fit, Accord, Civic Hybrid, and others combined — not a Fit-only count). Remedy: dealer inspects the drive shaft(s) and replaces them if necessary, free of charge.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes, IF manual transmission) — this recall is limited to manual-transmission cars; automatic-transmission 2008 Fits are not affected
Source: NHTSA Part 573 Safety Recall Reports and Consumer Reports coverage
