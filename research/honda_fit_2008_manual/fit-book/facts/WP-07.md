Fact ID: F-WP07-012
Chapter: 16
Question: Jump-start cable connection order and terminal locations
Value: (1) Connect one jumper cable to the positive (+) terminal on the dead battery. (2)
Connect the other end of that cable to the positive (+) terminal on the booster battery. (3)
Connect the second jumper cable to the negative (-) terminal on the booster battery. (4) Connect
the other end of that second cable to "the stay" on the dead vehicle — a metal ground point in
the engine bay shown in the manual's illustration, explicitly NOT the dead battery's negative
terminal and NOT any other part of the engine. This grounds the circuit away from the battery to
reduce spark risk near battery gases.

Fact ID: F-WP07-015

Value: The high temperature indicator should normally be off (except a few seconds at ignition

```
Fact ID: F-WP07-016
Chapter: 16
Question: Overheating procedure steps from the owner's manual
Value: 1) Safely pull to the side of the road; shift to neutral (manual) or Park (automatic);
set the parking brake; turn off accessories; turn on hazard warning lights. 2) If you see steam
or spray from under the hood, turn off the engine and wait for steam/spray to stop before opening
the hood (do not open the hood while steam is coming out — scald risk). 3) If no steam/spray is
visible, you may leave the engine running and watch the high temperature indicator; if the
overheating was from overloading, it should start cooling immediately — wait for the indicator to
go off, then continue driving. 4) If the indicator stays on, turn off the engine. 5) Look for
obvious coolant leaks (e.g., a split hose); a leak must be repaired before continuing to drive.
6) If no obvious leak, check the coolant level in the radiator reserve tank; add coolant if below
MIN. 7) If the reserve tank was empty, you may need to add coolant to the radiator itself — but
first let the engine and radiator cool down until the indicator goes off. 8) Using gloves or a
heavy cloth, turn the radiator cap counterclockwise without pressing down, to the first stop; let
pressure release; then press down and continue turning to remove it. 9) Start the engine, set the
temperature dial to maximum heat, and add coolant to the radiator up to the base of the filler
neck (plain water is acceptable in an emergency if the correct coolant mix is unavailable — have
the system drained/refilled properly as soon as possible after). 10) Reinstall the radiator cap
tightly; run the engine and watch the indicator; if it blinks or comes on again, the engine needs
repair. 11) If temperature stays normal, top the reserve tank to MAX and reinstall its cap
tightly.
Applies to: 2008 GD3 base trim (yes)
Source ID: S-001
Source URL: https://techinfo.honda.com/rjanisis/pubs/om/AAA080/AAA0808OM.pdf
Exact location in source: p.228-229, "If the Engine Overheats," full numbered procedure

## Red and other dashboard warning lights (roadside-relevant subset)

```
Fact ID: F-WP07-018
Chapter: 16
Question: Which instrument-panel indicator is explicitly labeled a red/critical light in the
owner's manual, and what its two functions are
Value: Only "Parking Brake and Brake System Indicator (Red)" carries an explicit color label
("(Red)") in the owner's manual text. Function 1: it comes on whenever the ignition switch is
turned to ON as a parking-brake reminder, and stays on until the parking brake is fully released
(a beeper also sounds if you drive with it partly applied). Function 2: if it stays on after the
parking brake is released, or comes on while driving, the brake fluid level is probably low or
there is a brake system problem — press the brake pedal lightly to check if it feels normal; if
normal, check brake fluid level at the next stop; if the pedal does NOT feel normal (goes down
much farther, needs harder pressure), slow down by downshifting and pull over immediately — do
not continue driving; have the vehicle towed and repaired. If the ABS indicator comes on together
with this indicator, have the vehicle inspected by a dealer immediately.
