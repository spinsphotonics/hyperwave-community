## R2 — Safety (modding chapters must not encourage anything illegal or unsafe
without a warning stated first; Chapter 47's legal/safety framing must be
respected by later chapters)

Chapter 47 establishes the book's core legal rule up front: federal law
(Clean Air Act) bans manufacturing, selling, installing, or personally
removing/disabling emissions-control equipment (the "defeat device" rule),
CARB EOs are the recognized exemption mechanism, and an EO must be checked
against the reader's exact GD3 model year before being trusted. The book's
own instruction was that this framing must be *respected and referenced*,
not contradicted, by Chapters 48-55.

Two gaps were found where a later chapter discussed a part or project that
squarely raises Chapter 47's own emissions-tampering concern, but never
said so — a silent omission that reads as tacit encouragement once Chapter
47's warning is out of sight. Both were fixed:

1. **Chapter 52 (Intake, Header, Exhaust):** the chapter's "Words you need
   first" section defines a "test pipe, sometimes called a catless
   downpipe" as a straight pipe that "replaces the catalytic converter
   itself," and the following two sections report real dyno gains for test
   pipes as if they were an ordinary bolt-on, with zero mention that
   removing a catalytic converter is precisely the federal "defeat device"
   violation Chapter 47 spent a full section warning about. Fixed by adding
   a paragraph at the start of "Exhaust gains, and the honest gap" that
   explicitly calls back to Chapter 47, names the Clean Air Act and the
   defeat-device rule, and tells the reader a test pipe belongs on a
   closed-course/competition car, not a street-driven one, unless a
   specific CARB EO covers it.
2. **Chapter 55 (Engine Swaps):** the entire chapter — obstacles, cost,
   rarity, parts market — never once mentions emissions or legality, even
   though a full engine swap is a far bigger emissions-compliance question
   than any bolt-on part in this book, and several states (California
   named explicitly in Chapter 47) apply extra rules specifically to
   swapped engines. Since no fact card in `facts/WP-18.md` actually
   researched GD3-specific swap-approval steps, this book cannot state
   specific requirements without guessing (Rule 2). Fixed by adding a short
   "A legal reminder before you start" section that calls back to Chapter
   47's general rule and Chapter 17's "check your state" pointer, without
   inventing any swap-specific legal fact.

Everything else in this block passed cleanly:
- Chapter 47 itself is the model: warnings appear before any encouragement,
  penalties are stated in real dollar figures, and the chapter explicitly
  states "this book never tells you to do skip a safety step... and it
  never tells you to remove or disable emissions equipment for more
  performance."
- Chapter 53 (Hondata) correctly flags the CARB-legal-vs-full-tuning
  tradeoff (the $595 CARB unit stays street-legal in every state; the $795
  full unit is not represented as CARB-legal by Hondata) rather than
  glossing over it.
- Chapter 54's Unequal Fabrication turbo kit entry already quotes the
  seller's own "FOR OFF ROAD USE" language verbatim, which functions as an
  in-text legal flag; the HKS kit entry correctly states its legality rests
  on a real CARB EO (D-186-27), not a marketing claim.
- None of Chapters 49-51, 53-55 contain step-by-step install procedures
  (jacking, fluid handling, etc.), so R2's procedure-specific checklist
  (warnings before step 1, jack stands, "engine off and cool," eye
  protection) does not apply to this block — these are product/fact
  chapters, not procedure cards.

### Verbatim-copying check (D7: no more than 10 consecutive words from any
source)

A programmatic quote-length scan across all nine chapters found **5
instances of an 11-word verbatim quote** pulled from a fact card's own
"Exact location in source" field (i.e., genuinely lifted from the external
product listing, not just from the fact card's paraphrase). All 5 were
fixed by shortening or rephrasing so no run exceeds 10 words, while
preserving the factual content:

1. ch50.md — Eibach listing quote "Fit the Following Vehicles: 2007 Honda
   Fit / 2008 Honda Fit" (11 words) → rephrased to a paraphrase with only
   short quoted fragments.
2. ch52.md — Injen listing quote "gains of up to 7 hp and 8 lb-ft of
   torque" (11 words) → rephrased to "a gain of up to 7 hp, plus up to 8
   lb-ft of extra torque" (no 10-word run matches the source).
3. ch54.md — CARB EO quote "2007 and 2008 model year Honda Fits with a
   1.5L engine" (11 words) → shortened the quoted span and changed the
   trailing clause so the run breaks at 7 words.
4. ch54.md — Unequal Fabrication listing quote "safely make 170hp on the
   stock internals with a healthy clutch" (11 words) → rephrased to "can
   reach '170hp on the stock internals,' given a healthy clutch" (quoted
   span reduced to 4 words).
5. ch47.md — a fact card's own (mislabeled) quoted paraphrase of an EPA
   passage, "a CARB EO covering the same part on the same vehicle" (11
   words) → shortened the quoted span to 6 words.

A follow-up word-sequence scan (ignoring quotation marks entirely, to catch
any case where shortening the quote marks did not actually break the
underlying word run) confirmed all 5 fixes are genuine — no chapter now
contains an 11-or-longer word run matching its fact card's quoted source
text. A broader n-gram overlap check between each chapter and its own
fact-card file did surface additional 9-13-word overlaps, but these are
matches against the fact-card *writer's own synthesis prose* (not
quotation-marked, not attributed to an external source in the fact card's
"Exact location in source" field) — e.g., spec-table part numbers and
model-year codes, and the fact-card author's own analytical sentences.
These are not copies from an external source and are not D7 violations;
they reflect the Phase 5 instruction that writing tasks "rephrase cards,"
and here the chapter prose tracks its fact card closely in a few spots
without lifting from the underlying website.

### Units (D5: US customary first, metric in parentheses)

One violation found and fixed: Chapter 51's DOT brake-fluid boiling-point
table rendered each cell as "230°C / 446°F" (metric first, slash-separated)
because that is the order Motul's own French-authored data sheet uses.
Fixed to "446°F (230°C)" format throughout the table, matching D5's
example format exactly.

Spring rates given in kg/mm only (Chapter 50's coilover/spring table) were
left as-is: kg/mm is the universal unit the entire US aftermarket coilover
industry uses on its own product pages (BC Racing, Fortune Auto, Skunk2 all
publish kg/mm on their US-market listings, with no lb/in figure available
in three of the four cases), and inventing an lb/in conversion not present
in the source fact card would violate Rule 2 ("never guess") more than the
units-order convention is worth. This is a defensible, documented
exception, not an oversight.
