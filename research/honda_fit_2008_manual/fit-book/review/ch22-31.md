## R2 — Safety (warnings before steps; jack stands whenever lifted; engine off and cool; eye protection for brake/battery work)

This was the highest-priority check for this batch. Findings:

**Warning placement:** every one of the 15 procedure sub-sections across
ch23-ch30 (jacking, oil/filter, engine air filter, cabin filter, tire
pressure, tire rotation, brake-pad inspection, front-pad replacement,
rear-shoe replacement, brake bleeding, battery test/replace/clean, spark
plugs, coolant check, coolant drain/fill, MT fluid check/drain, ATF
check/drain) has its `**Safety warnings...**` block immediately before
its `**Steps:**` block, with zero exceptions. Confirmed by a full grep
pass across all ten files.

**Jack stands mentioned whenever the car is lifted:** confirmed for every
job that raises the car — Chapter 23 (the dedicated jacking chapter),
Chapter 24 (oil change), Chapter 26 (tire rotation, not the pressure
check, which correctly needs no jacking), Chapter 27 (front-pad and
rear-shoe replacement), and Chapter 30 (coolant drain/fill and both
transmission-fluid drains). Jobs that do not require lifting the car
(air/cabin filter, tire-pressure check, brake-pad visual inspection,
battery work, spark plugs, coolant level check) correctly have no jacking
language, matching their source procedure cards.

**"Engine off and cool" for under-hood fluid work:** present everywhere a
hot-surface or hot-fluid risk applies — Chapter 24's oil change ("Always
let the engine and exhaust cool before you touch anything underneath the
car"), Chapter 30's coolant work ("Do not remove the radiator cap while
the engine is hot... Wait until the engine and radiator are cool"), and
Chapter 29's spark plugs ("Always let the engine cool completely before
starting"). Where the underlying job specifically requires checking a
fluid *warm* rather than cold (transmission fluid, both manual and
automatic — Chapter 30), the chapter correctly still shuts the engine off
before the hands-on work begins, matching the source procedure cards'
own sequencing; it does not claim "cool," because these two jobs are
correctly warm-fluid checks by design.

**Eye protection for brake and battery work:** present in every relevant
procedure — Chapter 27's front-pad replacement, rear-shoe replacement, and
brake bleeding, and Chapter 28's battery test, battery replacement, and
terminal cleaning all open with "Always wear eye protection."

**Two gaps flagged, not fixed (per the instruction not to invent warnings
absent from the source procedure card):**
1. **Chapter 27, "Bleed the brakes"** (sourced from `procedures/WP-11-9.md`):
   bleeding a brake system normally requires wheel removal to reach the
   bleeder screw, yet neither the source procedure card nor the chapter
   mentions raising the car or removing a wheel at all — the steps go
   straight from filling the reservoir to "slide clear tubing over the
   bleeder screw." This looks like a real omission in the underlying
   research pass, not a deliberate simplification (some cars' bleeder
   screws are reachable with the wheel on, but this was never confirmed
   for the Fit). Recommend a follow-up research pass on `WP-11-9` to
   confirm whether wheel removal is needed, rather than inventing a
   jacking warning here.
2. **Chapter 30, "Check and change the automatic transmission fluid"**
   (sourced from `procedures/WP-11-17.md`): the manual-transmission drain
   procedure explicitly says "continuing from a **cool** transmission"
   before the drain step, but the automatic-transmission procedure says
   only "continuing after the level check" — meaning the ATF drain
   happens right after warming the transmission to check its level, with
   no cool-down step and no hot-fluid handling warning. This asymmetry
   traces to the source procedure card itself. Recommend a follow-up
   research pass on `WP-11-17` to confirm whether a cool-down step (or at
   minimum a "hot fluid" caution) belongs before the ATF drain step.

Neither gap was patched with an invented warning in the draft text,
consistent with the review brief.
