Two chapters were over or right at the ceiling and were fixed:

- **Chapter 43** (starter/alternator/ignition switch) was the highest in the
  cluster at ~9.4, driven by the chapter's unavoidably dense vocabulary
  (alternator, ignition, immobilizer, terminal, electrical, mechanical,
  cylinder are all 3-5 syllable words central to the chapter's own subject)
  combined with several long, comma-heavy compound sentences. Fixed by
  splitting compound sentences at their semicolons and "and" joins, and by
  swapping a handful of words for shorter synonyms that keep the same
  meaning ("disconnect" battery terminal → "take off"; "buying and
  installing the wrong sub-repair" → "paying for the wrong sub-repair";
  "do-it-yourselfer" → "home mechanic"; "before you read the summary below"
  → "first"). No technical term, number, or step was changed — only
  sentence structure and a few synonym swaps. Brought the grade to ~7.9.
- **Chapter 42** (cooling system) was just over the ceiling at ~8.05, mainly
  from long "Disconnect/Reconnect the [X] connector, and the [Y] connector
  if equipped, then [Z]" steps in the radiator procedure. Fixed by splitting
  these into two sentences each and swapping "Disconnect/Reconnect ... the
  connector" to "Unplug/Plug in ... the connector" (a plain-English synonym,
  not a meaning change) for electrical-connector steps specifically; hose
  steps still say "disconnect/reconnect" since that is the correct verb for
  a hose. Brought the grade to ~7.4.

## R2 — Safety (given extra weight for this cluster)

**Coolant/burn risk (Chapter 42):** All three procedures (thermostat,
radiator, water pump) open with an explicit "let the engine cool
completely... a hot system can spray scalding coolant" warning before step
1, every time. The "What can go wrong" section also repeats the burn-risk
line as a stand-alone reminder. The electric radiator fan's own hazard
("keep your hands clear... it can switch on even with the engine off, if
the coolant is still hot") is called out separately in the radiator job's
warnings. PASS, no changes needed.

**Suspension / spring-compression risk (Chapter 41):** Front struts (skill
level 4) are presented as a "What the shop will do" bulleted summary, never
as numbered DIY steps — confirmed against `procedures/WP-14-3.md`, which
independently rates this level 4 for the same reason (uncontrolled spring
release risk plus an unconfirmed torque spec). One real issue found and
fixed: **the safety-warning paragraph ("Always treat a coil spring...")
was placed AFTER the "shop will typically" bullet list, not before it** —
the underlying procedure card has the same ordering (`What the shop will
do:` before `Safety warnings:`), but for a job called out by name in this
review's brief for its injury risk, burying the spring-energy warning below
the bulleted walk-through is the wrong order for a reader. Fixed by moving
the warning paragraph to the top of the section, before the bullets.
Also added a jack-stand line to the "Rear stabilizer bushing" sub-section,
which mentioned jack stands only inside step 1 itself rather than as a
leading safety-warning line like every other sub-section in the chapter —
minor, but now consistent and unambiguous.

**Electrical / battery-disconnect risk (Chapter 43):** The starter
procedure correctly instructs negative-terminal-off-first, positive-last on
disconnect, and positive-first, negative-last on reconnect (the standard
safe order), stated as a safety warning before step 1 and repeated in "What
can go wrong." Alternator and ignition switch (both skill level 4) are
presented as "What the shop will do" bulleted summaries, never as numbered
DIY steps — confirmed against `procedures/WP-14-10.md` and
`procedures/WP-14-11.md`. The same ordering issue found in Chapter 41 was
present here too, in both shop-summary sections: **the "know what to ask
about" safety paragraph (live alternator terminal; airbag wiring near the
steering column) came after the bulleted "what the shop will do" list in
both cases.** Fixed by moving both warning paragraphs above their bullet
lists, matching the Chapter 41 fix.

**Chapter 45's window-regulator year-confusion caveat:** Confirmed intact
and honestly stated, in three places:
1. The chapter's short-version summary: "one detail below comes from a
   different Honda Fit than yours, and this book says so plainly instead of
   guessing."
2. The full explanation's dedicated sub-section, "Window regulators, and an
   honest gap in this book's research," which names the mismatch
   specifically (the detailed guide is for a 2009-2013 Fit, a different
   generation than the reader's 2007-2008 car), and separately notes that a
   real 2007 owner confirms the job itself is doable, while the bolt count
   specifically stays unconfirmed.
3. The step-by-step itself, step 4: "A guide written for a later, different
   Fit generation reports roughly two bolts near the top and three near the
   bottom. Count your own car's bolts as you go, rather than assuming this
   number." This matches `procedures/WP-14-15.md` word for word in substance
   (that card independently carries the identical caveat, sourced to
   S-WP14-D/S-WP14-E). Nowhere in the chapter is the bolt count presented as
   confirmed. PASS, no change needed.

**Jack stands and eye protection, general sweep:** Every numbered procedure
across all seven chapters that raises the car (front brakes, rear drums,
rear shocks, front sway bar links, rear stabilizer bushing, radiator, water
pump, O2 sensor, exhaust section) states "never work under a car held up
only by a jack; always use jack stands" (or the one added line noted above)
before step 1. Every procedure involving brake dust, rust, cutting,
grinding, sanding, or battery/electrical work states "always wear eye
protection" before step 1. No procedure anywhere in this cluster omits
either warning. PASS.
