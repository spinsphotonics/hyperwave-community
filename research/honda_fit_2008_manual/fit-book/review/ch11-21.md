## R1 — Readability

**Method note:** this sandbox has no network access, so the real Flesch-Kincaid
tool (which needs the CMU pronouncing dictionary) could not run. I used a
vowel-group syllable-counting heuristic instead and treat its output only as a
directional signal, not a certified score. The plan's own numeric proxy for D6
("sentences under 20 words on average") **is met in every chapter** — average
sentence length ranges 12.3-18.9 words across all 12 files, all under 20. The
heuristic FK estimate lands at or slightly above 8.0 in ch13, ch15, ch18, ch20, and
ch21 (range ~8.0-8.9), driven by unavoidable multi-syllable domain vocabulary
(transmission, maintenance, diagnostic, recommendation, inspection, occupant,
corrosion, appraisal). All such terms are already defined on first use. Given the
D9/D7 constraint against inventing content and the risk of blurring precise
figures, I stopped short of further mechanical rewriting once sentences were
under 30 words, semicolon run-ons were split, and jargon was defined — this is
flagged for the writing team as a possible follow-up vocabulary-simplification
pass rather than force-fit further.

### Sentences over 30 words — found and fixed (4)
- **ch11.md**, "What can go wrong" bullet: reworded "the transmission stays stuck in
  those lower gears" → "the transmission stays stuck there" to cut it under 30 words.
- **ch17.md**: "chain of title, or a power of attorney" sentence split into three
  short sentences (also fixes the missing-definition issue below).
- **ch18.md**: EPS-definition sentence split in two (was 34 words after the R1 term-fix
  below pushed it over).
- **ch20.md**: two sentences (clutch cylinder item, clearcoat item) split in two, each
  for the same reason.

### Technical terms used without a same-sentence definition on first use — found and fixed (4)
- **ch18.md**: "**electric power steering (EPS)**" was named and its warranty
  extension discussed, but never explained. Fixed: added "is the electric motor that
  helps you turn the steering wheel" as its own defining sentence.
- **ch20.md**: "clutch master or slave cylinder" was used with no explanation of what
  it does. Fixed: added "are the small hydraulic parts that move the clutch when you
  press the pedal."
- **ch20.md**: "clearcoat peeling" was used with no definition of clearcoat. Fixed:
  added "Clearcoat is the clear protective top layer over the paint."
- **ch17.md**: "chain of title" and "power of attorney" were named with no
  definition. Fixed: added a one-clause definition for each.

All other bolded/first-use terms already carried a same-sentence or same-paragraph
definition (checked: title, bill of sale, Buyers Guide, odometer disclosure
statement, VIN, recall, campaign number, TSB, NHTSA, Takata, salt-belt states, DTC,
MIL, PPI, DTCs, milliamps, appraisal-tool estimate, actual asking prices, Viscosity,
MTF, ATF, DOT, differential, friction point, engine braking, aerodynamic drag,

### Sentence-level cleanup (semicolon run-ons split, for readability, not length)
Split 8 semicolon-joined sentences into two shorter sentences each in ch14, ch15,
ch18 (table cell), ch20 (×3), and ch21, e.g. ch15: "Honda asks you to pay extra
attention... (1,000 km); this is called the break-in period." → two sentences.

## R2 — Safety (extra scrutiny on Chapter 16)

**Chapter 16 is the focus of this check, being the roadside-emergency chapter.**
All three procedures (Change a flat tire / Jump start the car / The car is
overheating) were checked line-by-line against their source procedure cards
(`fit-book/procedures/WP-07-1.md`, `WP-07-2.md`, `WP-07-3.md`).

**Ordering — PASS for all three.** Every procedure's "Safety warnings, before you
touch [X]" block appears immediately before its numbered "Steps:" list, matching
the procedure cards exactly. No safety warning was found after its own step 1 in
any procedure in this batch.

**Content — PASS with one fix and one flagged gap:**
- **Fixed (R3/legal, not a safety omission):** the bumper-towing warning
  ("Do not lift or tow the vehicle by the bumpers...") reproduced a 10-word run
  verbatim from its fact card (`F-WP07-030`), sitting right at the copy-limit
  boundary. Reworded to "Do not lift the vehicle, or tow it, using the bumpers.
  They cannot support the vehicle's weight and will be seriously damaged." No
  fact changed.
- **Hot-parts warnings — present and correctly placed.** Flat tire: "handle wheel
  nuts and the brake hub carefully — they can be hot from driving" is in the
  before-steps warning block. Overheating: steam/spray/hot-coolant scald warnings
  are in the before-steps warning block, and the steps still reinforce "Everything
  is still very hot" before the radiator cap step.
- **Traffic-safety warnings — present.** Flat tire: "stop far away from traffic
  lanes," "turn on hazard lights," "get every passenger out," "do not get under the
  car" are all in the before-steps block, matching the procedure card.
- **Battery-gas warnings — present.** Jump start: "keep sparks, open flames, and
  smoking materials away from the battery — it can explode," frozen-electrolyte
  warning, and "keep loose jumper cable ends away from each other and from metal"
  are all in the before-steps block.
- **Flagged gap, NOT FIXED (do not invent):** the plan's Section 8 checklist asks
  to "confirm eye protection is listed for brake and battery work." The jump-start
  procedure is battery work, but neither the draft nor its source procedure card
  (`WP-07-2.md`) lists eye protection anywhere. Per this task's instruction not to
  invent warnings absent from the source procedure card, I did not add one — this
  is flagged for the fact/procedure-writing team to check against the owner's
  manual and, if warranted, add to the procedure card first.
- **Considered and judged not applicable:** the same Section 8 checklist asks to
  "confirm jack stands are mentioned any time the car is lifted." The flat-tire
  procedure raises the car on the vehicle's own scissor jack only, which matches
  both the owner's manual and the source procedure card — a roadside flat-tire
  change is not a shop jack-stand job (jack stands belong to the separate Chapter 23
  procedure, "jack up the car and set it on jack stands," `WP-11`). No gap here.

**Other chapters (11-15, 17-21, appendix B):** none contain a step-by-step safety
procedure, so the before-steps-warning-ordering check does not apply. No hidden
procedures were found disguised as prose.
