# Chapter 24: Oil and Filter Change

## What you will learn

- Why your Fit needs an oil and filter change, and what oil actually does
- The exact oil type and amount your Fit needs
- The full step-by-step process, from warming the engine to disposing of the old oil
- The one torque number that matters most in this job
- Common mistakes that cause leaks or engine damage

## The short version

Engine oil lubricates the moving parts inside your engine and carries away heat and dirt. Your Fit needs 3.8 US quarts (3.6 L) of 5W-20 oil and a new filter each time you change it. <!-- F-WP09-017, F-WP09-016 --> This is rated as a job that needs the car raised on jack stands, so read Chapter 23 first if you have not already. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->

## The full explanation

### Why oil matters, and what your Fit needs

Oil coats the metal parts inside your engine so they can move against each other without wearing out. Over time, oil picks up heat, dirt, and tiny bits of metal, and it needs to be drained out and replaced.

Your owner's manual calls for API Premium-grade 5W-20 detergent oil, carrying the API Certification Seal marked "For Gasoline Engines." <!-- F-WP09-016 --> Honda's own motor oil is the preferred choice, though a synthetic oil that meets the same seal and viscosity is acceptable too. <!-- F-WP09-016 --> No oil additive is needed or recommended. <!-- F-WP09-016 -->

A full oil change, with a new filter, takes 3.8 US quarts (3.6 L) of oil. <!-- F-WP09-017 --> Without a filter change, it takes slightly less, 3.6 US quarts (3.4 L). <!-- F-WP09-018 --> After a full engine rebuild, when the engine is completely dry, it takes 4.4 US quarts (4.2 L). <!-- F-WP09-019 --> You will not need that last figure for a routine oil change. It is here so you recognize it if you ever see it on a parts receipt or in a shop's paperwork.

### When to change it

Your Fit uses a computer called the Maintenance Minder instead of a printed mileage chart, as Chapter 21 explains in full. Even so, one fixed rule always applies: if a full 12 months pass without the display asking for an oil change, change the oil anyway. <!-- F-WP09-014 -->

### The one number to memorize: drain bolt torque

The bolt at the bottom of your oil pan that lets old oil drain out is called the drain bolt. Tighten it to 29 lbf·ft (39 N·m, 4.0 kgf·m) with a torque wrench, no more and no less. <!-- F-WP10-002 --> Honda gives no specific torque number for the oil filter itself. Instead, install a new filter according to the instructions printed on it. This usually means turning it by hand until it is snug against the engine, plus a small extra turn. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->

## Step by step: Change the engine oil and filter

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always let the engine run first, then shut it off. The manual says to run the engine to normal temperature before draining so the oil flows and carries out contaminants. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
- Always let the engine and exhaust cool before you touch anything underneath the car, once you shut it off. Hot oil and hot metal can burn you. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
- Do not get under a vehicle held up only by a jack. Use jack stands, as Chapter 23 explains. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
- Always wear eye protection and gloves. Used oil and fresh oil can irritate skin and eyes. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
- Do not dump used oil in the trash or on the ground. It is hazardous to the environment. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
- Always clean up any spilled oil right away. Spilled oil can damage engine parts and creates a slip hazard. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->

**Steps:**
1. Warm up the engine by running it, then shut it off and set the parking brake. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
2. Raise the front of the car and support it on jack stands, following Chapter 23. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
3. Open the hood and remove the engine oil fill cap so air can enter as oil drains. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
4. Slide the drain pan under the drain bolt at the bottom of the engine. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
5. Loosen and remove the drain bolt and its washer with your socket. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
6. Let all the oil drain fully into the pan; this can take several minutes. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
7. Wipe the drain area, then reinstall the drain bolt with a brand-new washer. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
8. Tighten the drain bolt to 29 lbf·ft (39 N·m) with a torque wrench. <!-- F-WP10-002 -->
9. Move the drain pan under the oil filter and remove the old filter with the filter wrench. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
10. Check that the old filter's rubber gasket did not stick to the engine; remove it if it did. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
11. Wipe a thin film of new oil on the new filter's gasket, then thread it on by hand until snug. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
12. Lower the car back to the ground and remove the jack stands. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
13. Pour in 3.8 US quarts (3.6 L) of the recommended 5W-20 oil through the funnel. <!-- F-WP09-017, F-WP09-016 -->
14. Reinstall the oil fill cap and tighten it securely. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
15. Start the engine and watch the oil pressure light; it should go out within 5 seconds. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
16. Let the engine run a few minutes, then shut it off and check the drain bolt and filter for leaks. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
17. Wait several more minutes, then check the level on the dipstick and top up if needed. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
18. Seal the used oil in a container and take it to a recycling center. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->

**How you know it worked:** the oil pressure warning light goes out within 5 seconds of starting the engine. There are no drips under the car after it has sat overnight. The dipstick reads between the marks. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->

## What can go wrong

Overfilling the engine past the dipstick's upper mark can damage the engine; the manual warns against this directly. <!-- P-WP11-1 --> Forgetting the new drain-bolt washer, called a crush washer, causes a slow leak. <!-- P-WP11-1 --> Over-tightening the oil filter by hand past snug can make it very hard to remove next time and can crush its gasket. <!-- P-WP11-1 --> Not fully re-torquing the drain bolt, or over-torquing it, can strip the pan's threads. <!-- P-WP11-1, F-WP10-002 --> And working under a car held up by only a jack, instead of jack stands, is the most dangerous mistake on this list. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->
