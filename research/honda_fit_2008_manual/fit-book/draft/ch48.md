# Chapter 48: Stage 0 — Get Your Baseline Right First

## What you will learn

- What "Stage 0" means, and why it comes before any performance part
- Why fresh fluids matter before you add stress to your Fit
- Why good tires and good brakes matter more than any bolt-on part
- Why this book cannot hand you an alignment procedure, and what to do instead

## The short version

Before you buy a single performance part, make sure your Fit's basic maintenance is already current. Stage 0 means fresh oil, tires in good shape, and brakes that already work correctly. Stage 0 costs less than most single mods, and it protects every mod you add after it.

## The full explanation

### What "Stage 0" means

Most modding guides number their steps: Stage 1, Stage 2, and so on, from mild to wild. **Stage 0** is the step before all of those. It means bringing your Fit's existing maintenance up to date before you spend a dollar on power, handling, or looks. A stiffer suspension on old, worn shocks will not behave the way a review video shows. A grippier tire on a car with a slipping alignment will wear out fast and unevenly.

This chapter is short by design. Every Stage 0 item already has a full procedure card written in an earlier chapter of this book. This chapter tells you which chapter to open and why that item matters before you mod, instead of repeating steps you can already find there.

### Fresh fluids

The first Stage 0 item is fresh engine oil and a fresh filter. <!-- F-WP15-015 --> Old oil has already picked up dirt and lost some of its ability to protect your engine. A car that will work harder, longer, or hotter than normal deserves oil that is not already tired. Chapter 24 covers the full oil-change procedure, including the tools, the drain-bolt torque spec, and every safety warning. <!-- F-WP15-015 --> Open Chapter 24 and follow its steps rather than guessing at your own order.

### Good tires

The second Stage 0 item is good tires: correct, even air pressure, and no uneven wear from a rotation you skipped. <!-- F-WP15-016 --> This is a check, not a rebuild. Chapter 26 already covers how to check and set your tire pressure, and how to rotate your tires so they wear evenly. <!-- F-WP15-016 --> Read Chapter 26 and confirm both are already current before you spend money on new wheels or tires, which Chapter 49 covers next.

Stage 0 is not about buying new tires yet. It is about confirming the tires you already have are properly maintained, so any new tire or wheel choice starts from a clean baseline instead of hiding an existing problem.

### Good brakes

The third Stage 0 item is good brakes. This means pads or shoes and fluid that are already in solid condition before you add track driving or spirited street driving to the mix. <!-- F-WP15-017 --> Your Fit's front brakes use pads and rotors, and its rear brakes use shoes inside a drum, which is a different design with different parts and a different repair. Chapter 27 covers inspecting the front pads through the wheel, replacing the front pads, replacing the rear shoes, and bleeding the brake fluid. <!-- F-WP15-017 --> Work through Chapter 27's checks before you plan any brake upgrade in Chapter 51.

### Alignment: the one gap in this book

Here is where this book has to be honest with you instead of guessing. **Alignment** is the process of adjusting your wheels' angles, camber, caster, and toe, so your car drives straight, handles predictably, and wears its tires evenly. This book does not include a step-by-step alignment procedure. <!-- F-WP15-018 --> We could not confirm a home alignment procedure for the 2008 Fit in this book's research. Check your owner's manual, and plan on a shop visit instead. <!-- F-WP15-018 -->

There is a practical reason for this gap. Alignment normally needs an **alignment rack**, a specialized shop machine that measures your wheel angles precisely and then adjusts them. <!-- F-WP15-018 --> This is not a typical home-garage job the way an oil change or a brake-pad swap is. For Stage 0, the right move is to have a shop check your alignment, especially if the car has ever been in a curb strike or a minor accident. Once you get to Chapter 50, you will also want a fresh alignment any time you change springs, coilovers, or camber parts.

### A simple Stage 0 checklist

- Fresh engine oil and filter, per Chapter 24. <!-- F-WP15-015 -->
- Correct, even tire pressure and a current tire rotation, per Chapter 26. <!-- F-WP15-016 -->
- Healthy front brake pads, healthy rear brake shoes, and fresh brake fluid, per Chapter 27. <!-- F-WP15-017 -->
- An alignment check at a shop, especially after any curb strike, accident, or suspension work. <!-- F-WP15-018 -->
