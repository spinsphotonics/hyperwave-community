# Chapter 21: The maintenance schedule

## What you will learn

- What the Maintenance Minder is, and why your Fit has no printed mileage chart for service
- How the Maintenance Minder decides when your car needs service
- What the codes "Item A," "Item B," and the numbered sub items mean
- Five fixed rules that apply no matter what the Maintenance Minder says
- What you, the owner, should check on your own, and how often
- An honest note on cost, since this chapter's sources do not cover shop prices

## The short version

Your 2008 Fit does not use a printed table of miles for oil changes and other service. <!-- F-WP09-001 --> Instead, a computer called the Maintenance Minder watches how you drive and shows a code on the dashboard display when something is due. <!-- F-WP09-001 --> A handful of items still follow a fixed schedule no matter what that display says, and this chapter lists every one of them. <!-- F-WP09-013, F-WP09-012, F-WP09-014, F-WP09-005, F-WP09-006, F-WP09-008 -->

## The full explanation

### What the Maintenance Minder is

The Maintenance Minder is a computer system built into your Fit. It tracks your driving and tells you when service is due, instead of relying on a fixed mileage chart. <!-- F-WP09-001 --> Many older Honda models, and some other cars, print a table with two columns: one for normal driving and one for severe driving, such as short trips or dusty roads. The driver has to decide which column applies. <!-- F-WP09-015 --> Your Fit's owner's manual has no such table. <!-- F-WP09-001, F-WP09-015 --> The Maintenance Minder computer factors driving severity into its own math, so you do not have to guess which column you fall into. <!-- F-WP09-001 -->

The system tracks something called oil life. Oil life is a percentage the computer calculates from your driving conditions and how many times your engine has turned over. <!-- F-WP09-001 --> As oil life drops, the display eventually shows a maintenance code telling you it is time for service. <!-- F-WP09-001 --> Chapter 7 shows you exactly what this looks like on your instrument panel and covers every other dashboard light and icon. <!-- F-WP09-001 -->

### Reading the codes: Item A, Item B, and sub items

When the Maintenance Minder decides service is due, it shows one main code and, sometimes, one or more numbered sub items.

Main Item A means it is time to replace your engine oil. <!-- F-WP09-002 --> Main Item B means more. It calls for a new engine oil and filter, plus a shop inspection. <!-- F-WP09-003 --> That inspection covers your front and rear brakes, your parking brake adjustment, and your tie rod ends, steering gear box, and boots. <!-- F-WP09-003 --> It also covers your suspension parts, driveshaft boots, and brake hoses and lines. <!-- F-WP09-003 --> Last, it covers all your fluid levels, your exhaust system, and your fuel lines. <!-- F-WP09-003 -->

Alongside Item A or B, a sub item number can appear for other jobs the Maintenance Minder tracks. These include rotating your tires, <!-- F-WP09-004 --> replacing your engine air filter, <!-- F-WP09-005 --> and replacing your cabin air filter, the filter that cleans the air coming through your heater and air conditioner. <!-- F-WP09-006 --> They also include inspecting and adjusting your drive belt, <!-- F-WP09-007 --> replacing your transmission fluid, <!-- F-WP09-008 --> replacing your spark plugs, <!-- F-WP09-009 --> and replacing your engine coolant, the liquid that keeps your engine from overheating. <!-- F-WP09-011 --> None of these sub items has a fixed mileage number printed in your owner's manual. The Maintenance Minder decides the timing for each one based on how you drive. <!-- F-WP09-004, F-WP09-005, F-WP09-006, F-WP09-007, F-WP09-008, F-WP09-009, F-WP09-011 -->

One more item rides along with services A, B, 1, 2, or 3: your valve clearance. Valve clearance is the small gap inside the engine that lets the valves open and close correctly. <!-- F-WP09-010 --> It gets checked at those visits, but it only gets adjusted if the engine sounds noisy. <!-- F-WP09-010 --> There is no separate mileage number for this adjustment. It is tied to whichever service visit comes up next. <!-- F-WP09-010 -->

### Five fixed rules that beat the Maintenance Minder

Most service on your Fit runs on the Maintenance Minder's own timing. But five items follow a fixed rule that applies no matter what the dashboard display says. These are the only true "fixed schedule" items in the whole book for this car, and every new driver and every parent should know all five.

1. **Brake fluid, every 3 years.** Change it every 3 years regardless of any message on the display. <!-- F-WP09-013 -->
2. **Idle speed, every 160,000 miles (256,000 km).** Have it inspected at this mileage, independent of the Maintenance Minder. <!-- F-WP09-012 -->
3. **Engine oil, at least once a year.** If a full 12 months pass since your last reset without the display asking for an oil change, change the oil anyway. <!-- F-WP09-014 -->
4. **Air filter and cabin filter, every 15,000 miles (24,000 km) if conditions are dusty or sooty.** If you drive in dusty conditions, replace your engine air filter at this interval instead of waiting for the display. <!-- F-WP09-005 --> If you drive mainly in urban areas with heavy soot from industry or diesel traffic, replace your cabin filter at this same interval. <!-- F-WP09-006 -->
5. **Automatic transmission fluid, on two special conditions.** Say you drive an automatic-transmission Fit regularly at low speed in mountains. Change the transmission fluid at 60,000 miles (100,000 km), then every 30,000 miles (48,000 km) after that. <!-- F-WP09-008 --> Say instead that you tow a Fit behind a motorhome. Change the automatic transmission fluid every 2 years or 30,000 miles (48,000 km), whichever comes first. <!-- F-WP09-008 --> Both of these rules apply to the automatic transmission only. <!-- F-WP09-008 -->

Outside of these five, your owner's manual names no other severe-condition trigger with a fixed number. <!-- F-WP09-015 --> If you are shopping for parts or fluid, note that the Fit's automatic transmission fluid, Honda ATF-Z1, has since been discontinued industry-wide. Ask your parts store for its replacement, Honda ATF DW-1, if ATF-Z1 is not on the shelf. <!-- F-WP09-029 -->

### What you check yourself, and how often

Between service visits, a few checks are yours to do, and none of them need a mechanic.

- Engine oil level and engine coolant level: check both every time you stop for fuel. <!-- F-WP09-060 -->
- Automatic transmission fluid level and brake fluid level: check monthly. <!-- F-WP09-060 -->
- Tire pressure and tread: check monthly. <!-- F-WP09-060 -->
- Headlights, parking lights, taillights, high-mount brake light, and license plate lights: check monthly that they all work. <!-- F-WP09-060 -->
- Battery condition: check monthly using the test indicator window on top of the battery. <!-- F-WP09-061 -->

### A note on cost

This chapter's sources are Honda's own owner's manual and parts-fitment pages. None of them give a dollar figure for what any of this costs, whether you do it yourself or pay a shop. ## Step by step: Making sense of your maintenance display

1. Look at your information display for an oil life percentage or a maintenance code. <!-- F-WP09-001 -->
2. If the code is Item A, plan to change your engine oil. <!-- F-WP09-002 -->
3. If the code is Item B, plan to change your engine oil and filter, and have the longer inspection list checked too. <!-- F-WP09-003 -->
4. Check for any sub item number next to the main code, and match it to the job list above. <!-- F-WP09-004, F-WP09-005, F-WP09-006, F-WP09-007, F-WP09-008, F-WP09-009, F-WP09-011 -->
5. Even with no code showing, change your oil once a year, change your brake fluid every 3 years, and check idle speed at 160,000 miles. <!-- F-WP09-014, F-WP09-013, F-WP09-012 -->
6. If you drive in dusty or sooty conditions, or tow, or drive slowly in mountains with an automatic, apply the matching fixed rule above instead of waiting on the display. <!-- F-WP09-005, F-WP09-006, F-WP09-008 -->

## What can go wrong

Because most of your Fit's maintenance runs on a computer instead of a printed chart, it is easy to assume the dashboard will catch everything. It will not. The five fixed rules in this chapter run on their own clock, separate from the Maintenance Minder, and the display will not necessarily remind you about them. <!-- F-WP09-013, F-WP09-012, F-WP09-014, F-WP09-005, F-WP09-006, F-WP09-008 --> A parent or new driver who only reacts to dashboard codes could miss a 3-year brake fluid change or a dusty-conditions air filter swap for years. Write these five rules down, or set your own calendar reminders, instead of relying on the display alone.
