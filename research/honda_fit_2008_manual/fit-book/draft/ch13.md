# Chapter 13: Parking, Reversing, and Tight Spaces

## What you will learn

## The short version
Reverse sits next to fourth and fifth gear in the Fit's shift pattern, and the transmission has a lockout so you cannot grind into it from fifth by mistake. On a hill, turn your wheels, leave the car in gear, and set the parking brake. Beyond that, the sources for this book do not cover detailed parking or reversing technique for this specific car.

## The full explanation

### Reverse gear, on a manual Fit

In the Fit's five-speed "H" shift pattern, reverse sits in the bottom row of the pattern, next to fourth gear.<!-- F-WP06-001 --> The transmission has a lockout that prevents you from shifting straight from fifth gear into reverse.<!-- F-WP06-002 --> To get into reverse smoothly, come to a full stop, push the clutch pedal all the way down, and then either pause a few seconds or shift briefly into a forward gear before moving the lever into reverse.<!-- F-WP06-002 --> Either action gives the gearbox's spinning input shaft time to stop turning before reverse engages.<!-- F-WP06-002 --> On an automatic Fit, reverse is one of the seven positions on the base-trim shift gate, alongside Park, Neutral, Drive, D3, Second, and First.<!-- F-WP06-004 -->

### Parking on a hill

The owner's manual's page on parking covers what to do when you park on a hill. Turn your front wheels toward the curb or the edge of the road, leave the transmission in first gear if you are facing uphill or in reverse gear if you are facing downhill, and set the parking brake.<!-- F-WP06-009 --> This gives the parked car a second way to resist rolling, in addition to the parking brake, if it were to slip.

### What this chapter does not cover, and why

The sources used for this book do not include specific technique for parallel parking, backing into a space, using mirrors while reversing, or judging distances in tight spaces for the 2008 Fit. We could not confirm Honda-specific guidance on these points. Rather than invent tips that are not in the source material, this book directs you to general safe-driving practice: go slowly, check your mirrors and look over your shoulder before and during any reversing move, and if you are unsure a space is tight, get out and look before you try it. A driving instructor or your state's driver's manual is a good place to learn parking and reversing technique step by step.
