# Chapter 55: Engine Swaps

## What you will learn
- What an engine swap is, and why it is a much bigger job than a bolt-on part.
- The one documented engine family that has actually been swapped into a GD3 Fit.
- The real obstacles builders report: mounts, wiring, the ECU, the transmission, and more.
- Why this is one of the thinnest, smallest topics in this whole book.
- What was searched for and not found, so you know the honest limits of this research.

## The short version
An engine swap means removing your car's factory engine and installing a different one <!-- background -->. Exactly one engine family has a real, documented swap into a GD3 Fit: Honda's K-series engine <!-- F-WP18-011 -->. This is a genuinely small, niche corner of the Fit hobby; one community estimate puts the total number of K-swapped GD3 Fits worldwide at fewer than 50 <!-- F-WP18-014 -->.

## The full explanation

### Words you need first
An engine swap replaces a car's original engine with a different one, often from another model <!-- background -->. Honda's K-series is a family of engines used in cars like the Civic Si and the Acura TSX <!-- background -->. A wiring harness is the bundle of wires that connects an engine and its ECU to the rest of the car <!-- background -->. An immobilizer is an anti-theft system that stops the engine from starting without the correct key <!-- background -->. A vehicle speed sensor, or VSS, tells the ECU how fast the car is moving <!-- background -->. A limited slip differential, or LSD, is a transmission part that helps both drive wheels get power during hard cornering <!-- background -->.

### A legal reminder before you start
An engine swap is a much bigger emissions question than any bolt-on part in this book. Chapter 47 already covered the basic rule: federal law bans removing or defeating emissions equipment, and many states, especially California, apply extra rules specifically to swapped engines. This book's research did not confirm the exact swap-approval steps for this exact car, so this book will not guess at them. Check your own state's rules on engine swaps before you start, the same way Chapter 17 already told you to check your state's inspection and emissions rules.

### The one documented swap family
Researchers found real, detailed build documentation for exactly one swapped-in engine family: Honda's K-series <!-- F-WP18-011 -->. Two specific K-series engines have documented builds: the K24A2, pulled from a wrecked Acura TSX, and the K20Z3, pulled from a Civic Si <!-- F-WP18-011 -->. No documented, completed swap of any other engine family was found for a GD3 chassis <!-- F-WP18-011 -->. That includes other Honda engine families, non-Honda engines, and electric motor conversions <!-- F-WP18-011 -->.

Two independent, named builders documented their own K-series swaps <!-- F-WP18-012 -->. One builder runs a website, pbomers.com, showing his own K24A2 swap into a GD3 Fit in detail <!-- F-WP18-012 -->. He says he has a running K24-swapped Fit <!-- F-WP18-012 -->. He also points to his brother's YouTube channel for video coverage of four completed swaps between the two of them <!-- F-WP18-012 -->. A second builder posted a 2014 forum thread about a K20Z3 swap into a 2007 GD Fit <!-- F-WP18-012 -->. He used an engine, ECU, and wiring harness pulled from a Civic Si <!-- F-WP18-012 -->. A different forum member replied that he had "just went through this" the same way, about a month earlier <!-- F-WP18-012 -->. That second, independent reply shows this is a known method within the small community, not a one-off guess <!-- F-WP18-012 -->.

### The real obstacles
Builders report real problems in several different car systems <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. On motor mounts: without stiffer aftermarket mounts, or reinforcing the stock ones, the engine is reported to rock and rub inside the tight engine bay <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. Hasport is the one mount brand specifically named by builders <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. On body clearance: the front support structure often has to be cut to fit the larger K24 intake manifold <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. A smaller K-series intake needs less cutting <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. The stock radiator and its fans can also foul the taller intake manifold, so some builders relocate the radiator and switch to slimmer fans <!-- F-WP18-013 -->.

Wiring is reported as one of the hardest parts of this swap <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. One builder said he contacted more than half a dozen harness companies and was ignored by all of them <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. His solution was to hand-build a harness, using an Acura TSX ECU, since the TSX shares similar wiring architecture with the Fit <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. The car's immobilizer has to be disabled or re-synced to the new ECU and key, at a Honda dealership <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. Builders also report that only certain donor transmissions work correctly <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. Honda changed its vehicle speed sensor design at some point <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. An older-design sensor will not read correctly on a newer ECU <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. Builders report using axles from a 2002-2004 Acura RSX, instead of the stock Fit axles, to make everything fit together <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. Because the Fit uses electric power steering, an idler arm borrowed from a Honda Civic Si is also reported as necessary <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. Even the exhaust header is reported as a genuinely tough part to source for this swap <!-- F-WP18-013 -->.

### Cost and rarity
No total build-cost figure was found for a full K-series swap into a GD3 <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. This is left honestly unverified, rather than estimated or guessed at <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. One real, single component price point was found instead: a commercial Hasport-brand engine mount kit for this exact swap sells for $249 to $285, depending on the retailer <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. That is only a small fraction of what a complete swap would actually cost <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. On rarity, one experienced forum member estimated that fewer than 50 K-swapped GD3 Fits exist worldwide <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. That is a rough personal guess, not an official count <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. A second, separate long-time forum member said he could not remember any currently active members with a K-swapped Fit <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. Both statements point the same direction: this swap is genuinely rare <!-- F-WP18-014 -->.

### A real parts market, even so
Even though this swap is rare, a real commercial parts market exists to support it <!-- F-WP18-015 -->. Three separate retailers each independently sell the same Hasport-brand engine mount kit, specifically titled for the "07-08 Honda Fit" K-series swap <!-- F-WP18-015 -->. That does not confirm any one builder's total cost, but it does show real, ongoing commercial demand, from more than one seller <!-- F-WP18-015 -->.

### Claims checked and set aside
One claim, that a professional shop called King Motorsports built a K24-swapped Fit for the SEMA car show, was searched for directly and could not be confirmed <!-- F-WP18-016 -->. Only an ordinary parts listing for a mount kit was found, no build article or show record <!-- F-WP18-016 -->. This claim is not used anywhere in this book <!-- F-WP18-016 -->.

A forum thread asking about swapping in a B-series or D-series Honda engine, a different engine family than K-series, was found <!-- F-WP18-017 -->. No completed, documented build of that kind into a GD3 chassis was found, though <!-- F-WP18-017 -->. This is recorded honestly as "few to no documented examples," not as a confirmed impossibility <!-- F-WP18-017 -->.

An electric motor, or EV, conversion was also searched for <!-- F-WP18-018 -->. One forum thread shows a GD3 owner asking questions about an EV conversion, which suggests at least planning-stage interest <!-- F-WP18-018 -->. No completed, documented GD3-specific EV conversion build was found <!-- F-WP18-018 -->. General EV conversion kits exist for other cars, but none found named the Fit specifically <!-- F-WP18-018 -->.

### Putting it together
This is one of the smallest, most niche topics in this entire book <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. Exactly one engine family, Honda's K-series, has real documented swaps into a GD3 Fit <!-- F-WP18-011 -->. The obstacles are real and touch nearly every system in the car: mounts, wiring, the ECU, the transmission, the axles, even the steering <!-- F-WP18-013 -->. No total cost figure exists, and the swap is genuinely rare, at well under 50 known worldwide by one rough estimate <!-- F-WP18-014 -->. If you are considering this, expect a long, expensive, mostly self-taught project, not a weekend job <!-- F-WP18-013 -->.
