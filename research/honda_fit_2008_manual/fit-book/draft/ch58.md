# Chapter 58: Autocross

## What you will learn

- What autocross is and how it works
- What SCCA class a stock 2008 base Fit runs in
- What kinds of changes move your Fit into a faster class
- Whether an older Fit is still allowed to compete
- What to know before your first event

## The short version

Autocross is a timed event where you drive one car at a time through a course marked out with cones, trying to beat the clock without hitting anything. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> A stock 2008 base Fit currently runs in SCCA's H Street class. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> Adding suspension, brake, or wheel and tire upgrades moves it up into a faster class, and adding race tires and engine tuning moves it up again. <!-- F-WP20-003, F-WP20-004 -->

## The full explanation

### What autocross actually is

SCCA, the Sports Car Club of America, calls its autocross program "Solo." <!-- F-WP20-001 --> In Solo, you drive one car at a time through a course laid out with cones on a large paved lot, usually a parking lot or an unused runway. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> You are timed, not racing side by side against another driver, so your only competition is the clock and everyone else's best run of the day. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> Cars are sorted into classes so that similar cars compete against each other, which is why knowing your class matters. <!-- F-WP20-001 -->

### Your Fit's class: H Street

The current SCCA National Solo rulebook lists the Fit in H Street, written as HS. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> This classing was checked in two separate rulebook editions and is identical in both, so it has not changed recently. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> Street is SCCA's entry-level category. The rulebook itself calls it "the lowest barrier of entry." <!-- F-WP20-001 --> It allows only limited changes: mainly shocks, anti-roll bars — suspension parts that resist body lean in turns — and replacement tires. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> Beyond those changes, a Street-classed Fit is expected to stay close to how it left the factory. <!-- F-WP20-001 -->

Your 2008 model-year Fit is still fully eligible to compete. <!-- F-WP20-002 --> SCCA's own age rule allows a car to run in National events through the end of the 30th calendar year after its model year. <!-- F-WP20-002 --> For a 2008 car, that rule stays in effect through 2038, so age is not a concern for many years to come. <!-- F-WP20-002 --> Regional-only Solo events do not use this same age limit at all. <!-- F-WP20-002 -->

### Moving up a class: Street Touring and Street Prepared

If you add the kinds of parts Street category does not allow, your Fit moves into a different class instead of staying in H Street. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> The next class up is E Street Touring, written EST. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> SCCA describes Street Touring as sitting between Street and the more heavily modified Street Prepared category. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> Moving into EST means adding aftermarket suspension parts, like shocks, springs, or sway bars. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> It can also mean a different differential — the part that lets your two drive wheels spin at different speeds through a turn — bolt-on engine parts, or a bigger brake kit. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> Wider or different-sized wheels and tires than Street category allows are permitted too. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> A car in this class is still expected to stay street-legal. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> It must keep its emissions equipment and OBD-II system — the car's onboard diagnostic computer, which tracks emissions and engine problems — working. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> Its factory anti-lock brakes must also keep working, though they may be turned off electronically. <!-- F-WP20-003 -->

Going further still moves your Fit into F Street Prepared, written FSP. <!-- F-WP20-004 --> Street Prepared builds on everything Street Touring allows, then adds a further step that is not really meant for driving on public roads. <!-- F-WP20-004 --> This includes race-compatible tires and permanent bodywork changes, such as cutting or flaring fenders for tire clearance. <!-- F-WP20-004 --> It also includes front splitters, rear spoilers, and an aftermarket ECU — an aftermarket computer that controls engine tuning — for engine tuning. <!-- F-WP20-004 --> Emissions equipment can be removed, and the intake and exhaust become unrestricted. <!-- F-WP20-004 --> A Street Prepared car is also allowed further weight-reduction changes, like removing the air conditioning system, and more suspension upgrades than Street Touring allows. <!-- F-WP20-004 -->

Put together, the modification ladder for your Fit looks like this. Street or H Street keeps the car stock, aside from shocks, sway bars, and tires. <!-- F-WP20-001, F-WP20-003 --> Street Touring or EST adds bolt-on suspension, brake, and wheel upgrades while staying street-legal. <!-- F-WP20-003 --> Street Prepared or FSP adds race tires, tuning, and body changes, and is no longer necessarily street-legal. <!-- F-WP20-004 -->

### Getting ready for your first event

Before your first event, confirm which class you plan to run. If your Fit is stock, or close to it, that class is H Street. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> Read Chapter 57 for what your Fit needs, and does not need, in terms of seat belts, harnesses, and helmets. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> Read Chapter 60 for a full checklist of what to bring and do the night before, the morning of, and between runs. <!-- F-WP20-001 --> Knowing your class ahead of time means you register correctly and compete against the right group of cars from your very first run. <!-- F-WP20-001 -->
