# Chapter 59: HPDE / track days

## What you will learn

- What HPDE means and how it differs from racing
- What NASA's tech inspection checks on the car itself
- What safety and general-condition items tech inspection checks
- What the actual paper tech form looks like
- The most common reasons cars fail tech inspection

## The short version

HPDE stands for High Performance Driving Event, a track day where you drive your own car on a real track to build skill, not to race other drivers. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> Before you can drive, your car has to pass a tech inspection covering its mechanical condition and its safety equipment. <!-- F-WP20-005, F-WP20-006 --> A well-maintained, stock Fit with good tires and brakes should pass without trouble. <!-- F-WP20-007 -->

## The full explanation

### What tech inspection checks on the car itself

NASA's Club Codes and Regulations, the rulebook covering NASA's HPDE program, lists specific mechanical items every car must pass. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> Wheels and tires must show no cracked or damaged wheels and no exposed cords or bubbles on the tire, with every lug nut present and tight. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> Steering and suspension get checked for tightness, since very little or no play is allowed in the wheel bearings, suspension, or steering. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> The engine bay must show no significant fluid leaks from the engine, radiator, or hoses, and hoses must be in good condition with no cracks. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> The radiator needs an overflow container holding at least one liter. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> Any oil breather or vent line must either return to the engine, or route to a catch tank of at least one liter. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> Brakes must work well enough to stop the car in a reasonable, safe distance, with adequate pedal pressure. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> Fluid must sit at or above the minimum level, and brake lines must be in good condition. <!-- F-WP20-005 --> The car's general appearance also gets checked against what the rulebook calls the "50/50 rule": it should look undamaged and straight at 50 mph from 50 feet away. <!-- F-WP20-005 -->

### What tech inspection checks beyond the mechanicals

Tech inspection also covers safety items that are not part of the car's mechanical systems. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> Seatbelts and harnesses must be in good, undamaged, factory configuration, or use an approved aftermarket harness if the car has an aftermarket hard-shell seat. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> Roll bars are required on all open cars, but not on a closed car like your Fit. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> The battery must be securely fastened — a bungee cord or rubber cord alone is not acceptable — and its positive terminal must be covered with a non-conductive material. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> The fuel cap must seal well enough that fuel does not spill under hard driving. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> No exposed, live wiring is allowed inside the cabin, and seats must be securely fastened to the car's structure. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> Every loose object must come out of the passenger compartment before you go on track. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> At least one working rear-view mirror and at least two working brake lights, visible from 300 feet, are both required. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> Tow eyes — front and rear hooks a tow truck can safely pull from — are strongly recommended, though not always required. <!-- F-WP20-006 -->

On the process itself, you review the Technical Inspection Form before the event, then either have the car pre-teched at an authorized shop or teched at the track. <!-- F-WP20-006 --> Once the form is signed, you place it on the dash or windshield so a grid marshal can collect it and apply a tech sticker. <!-- F-WP20-006 -->

### The real tech form, item by item

A real NASA HPDE/Time Trial Technical Inspection Form organizes its checklist into five boxes. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Wheels and tires covers tire condition, all lug nuts present and tight, and no hubcaps or beauty rings left on. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Steering and suspension covers front and rear wheel-bearing play and steering play. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Engine covers fluid leaks from oil, fuel, or water, the radiator overflow and hose condition, and a battery secured with no bungee cords and covered terminals. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Brakes covers pedal pressure, fluid level, line condition, and brake lights. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Safety Equipment covers roll bars on open cars, seat belts or a harness, a Snell SA2005-or-newer helmet, and a covered battery if it sits inside the cockpit. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Miscellaneous covers a secured gas cap, no exposed wires, and seats bolted down tight. <!-- F-WP20-007 -->

The form also reminds you to put contrasting-color numbers, at least 10 inches tall, on both sides of the car. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> It tells you to remove all loose objects, including the spare tire and floor mats. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Finally, place your tech sticker at the top center of the windshield. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> The most common first-timer tech-inspection failures are worn brake pads, a loose battery, corded or cracked tires, leaking fluids, loose wheel bearings, and a missing tow hook. <!-- F-WP20-007 --> Inspect your car several days before the event, not the morning of, so you have time to fix or order parts for anything you find. <!-- F-WP20-007 -->
