# Chapter 15: Getting Good Fuel Economy

## What you will learn
- The shift points that give the best fuel economy on a manual Fit
- What the owner's manual and the EPA say hurts and helps your fuel economy
- Why the break-in period matters for your car's long-term health
- Why you should not tow a trailer with this car

## The short version
Drive smoothly, keep your tires inflated, keep up with maintenance, and stay off the gas pedal in stop-and-go traffic. On a manual, shift up early. Above about 45 to 50 mph, wind resistance starts eating into your mileage fast.

## The full explanation

### Shifting for economy, on a manual

The owner's manual's recommended shift points, for normal acceleration, are: first to second around 15 mph (24 km/h), and second to third around 27 mph (43 km/h).<!-- F-WP06-003 --> Third to fourth comes around 39 mph (62 km/h), and fourth to fifth around 53 mph (85 km/h).<!-- F-WP06-003 --> The general rule is to drive in the highest gear that still lets the engine run and speed up smoothly.<!-- F-WP06-003 -->

### What the owner's manual says hurts and helps your fuel economy

Factors that lower fuel economy, according to the owner's manual, include aggressive driving, meaning hard acceleration or hard braking, and excess idling and stop-and-go acceleration and braking.<!-- F-WP06-012 --> Running the engine cold, driving with a heavy load or the air conditioning on, and under-inflated tires also hurt it.<!-- F-WP06-012 --> Tips to improve it include keeping up with maintenance, using the correct oil viscosity, and keeping your tires properly inflated.<!-- F-WP06-012 --> Avoid excess weight in the car, and keep the underside clean of snow or mud buildup.<!-- F-WP06-012 --> Drive moderately, meaning you avoid rapid acceleration, hard cornering, and hard braking, and observe the speed limit.<!-- F-WP06-012 --> Aerodynamic drag, the resistance of the air pushing back against the car, rises sharply above 45 mph (75 km/h).<!-- F-WP06-012 --> Roof racks, carriers, and trailers add extra drag.<!-- F-WP06-012 --> On a manual transmission, use the highest gear that still lets the engine run smoothly.<!-- F-WP06-012 -->

### What the EPA says, in general

The EPA's general driving-habit guidance applies to any gasoline car, including the Fit. It says aggressive driving, meaning speeding and hard acceleration or braking, can cut your gas mileage by roughly 15 to 30 percent at highway speeds.<!-- F-WP06-013 --> In stop-and-go traffic, aggressive driving can cut mileage by 10 to 40 percent.<!-- F-WP06-013 --> Gas mileage usually drops quickly above 50 mph. The EPA estimates that each 5 mph you drive over 50 mph acts like paying about $0.29 more per gallon.<!-- F-WP06-013 --> That figure is tied to national average fuel prices at the time it was published, and is not specific to the Fit.<!-- F-WP06-013 --> This is general EPA guidance for gasoline cars, not text written specifically about the Fit.<!-- F-WP06-013 -->

### The break-in period

Honda asks you to pay extra attention to your driving style for the vehicle's first 600 miles (1,000 km); this is called the break-in period.<!-- F-WP06-010 --> During this time, avoid full-throttle starts and rapid acceleration for the whole 600 miles, and avoid hard braking for the first 200 miles (300 km).<!-- F-WP06-011 --> Do not change the engine oil early; wait until the regular scheduled maintenance interval.<!-- F-WP06-011 --> The same advice applies again after an engine overhaul or exchange, or after the brakes are replaced.<!-- F-WP06-011 --> Driving gently during break-in protects the engine and brakes, which pays off in reliability and economy for the life of the car.

### Towing

The owner's manual states that this vehicle is not built to tow a trailer at all, and that doing so can void the vehicle's warranties.<!-- F-WP06-016 --> No numeric trailer-towing weight rating is given anywhere in the manual, because the manual's position is that trailer towing should not be done with this car.<!-- F-WP06-016 --> Since a trailer adds weight and drag, both of which the manual already lists as hurting fuel economy, towing one would work against everything else in this chapter as well.<!-- F-WP06-012 -->
