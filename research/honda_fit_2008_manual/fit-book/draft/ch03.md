## The full explanation

### Engine

Horsepower is a measurement of how much work an engine can do over time; torque is a measurement of an engine's twisting force, the pulling power you feel when you accelerate. RPM stands for revolutions per minute, or how many full turns the engine makes every minute. Compression ratio compares the space inside a cylinder before and after the piston squeezes the air-fuel mixture; a higher number means a tighter squeeze. Bore and stroke describe the width of each cylinder and the distance its piston travels; cc stands for cubic centimeters, a way of measuring the total volume of all the cylinders together. Redline is the highest RPM the engine is designed to reach safely.

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Engine code | L15A1 <!-- F-WP01-001 --> |
| Displacement | 1.5 L (1,497 cc) <!-- F-WP01-002 --> |
| Horsepower | 109 hp @ 5,800 rpm <!-- F-WP01-003 --> |
| Torque | 105 lb-ft @ 4,800 rpm <!-- F-WP01-004 --> |
| Compression ratio | 10.4:1 <!-- F-WP01-005 --> |
| Bore x stroke | 73.0 mm x 89.4 mm <!-- F-WP01-006 --> |
| Required fuel | Regular unleaded gasoline <!-- F-WP01-007 --> |
| Redline | 6,500 rpm <!-- F-WP01-008 --> |

Honda's own specification sheet names the required fuel as "Regular Unleaded" without giving an exact minimum octane number, a number that measures a gasoline's resistance to knocking. <!-- F-WP01-007 --> Standard US regular-grade gasoline is commonly labeled 87 octane, but no source specific to this car states that number, so this book does not claim it as confirmed for your Fit.

### Transmission

A gear ratio is the number of times the engine turns for every one turn of the wheels in a given gear; a higher ratio gives more pulling power at low speed, and a lower ratio saves fuel at speed. Your base Fit could be ordered with a 5-speed manual transmission, which was standard, or an optional 5-speed automatic transmission. <!-- F-WP01-009 -->

### Crash test ratings

NHTSA, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, and IIHS, the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, are the two US groups that crash-test new cars and publish star or letter ratings so buyers can compare safety.

NHTSA rated the 2008 Fit this way, on a 1-to-5-star scale where 5 stars is the best score: <!-- F-WP01-036 -->

| Test | Rating |
|---|---|
| Frontal crash, driver side | 5 stars |
| Frontal crash, passenger side | 5 stars |
| Side crash, driver side | 5 stars |
| Side crash, passenger side | 3 stars |
| Rollover | 4 stars (13.3% rollover risk) |

Rollover risk is NHTSA's estimate of how likely a car is to roll over in a single-vehicle crash; a lower percentage is safer. NHTSA did not publish a single combined overall star rating for the 2008 Fit, because NHTSA did not start using one combined score until testing for the 2011 model year. <!-- F-WP01-036 -->

IIHS graded the same car on a scale of Good, Acceptable, Marginal, and Poor: <!-- F-WP01-037 -->

| Test | Rating |
|---|---|
| Moderate overlap front crash | Good |
| Side crash | Good |
| Head restraints and seats (whiplash protection) | Poor |

IIHS states that these results apply to both the 2007 and 2008 model years, since the two years share the same structure. <!-- F-WP01-037 --> IIHS had not yet created its small overlap front crash test when your Fit was built, so no small overlap rating exists for this car. <!-- F-WP01-037 -->
