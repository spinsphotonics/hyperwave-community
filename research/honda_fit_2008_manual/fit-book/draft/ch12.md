# Chapter 12: Driving a Manual Transmission from Zero

## What you will learn
- How the clutch pedal works and why letting it up too fast stalls the engine
- The Fit's shift pattern, and how to get into reverse without grinding the gears
- The recommended speeds for shifting up, for the best fuel economy
- A full first-drive routine, plus a routine for starting off on a hill

## The short version
The clutch pedal disconnects the engine from the wheels so you can change gears. Let it up slowly and smoothly, with a little gas, and the car moves off without stalling. Practice in an empty lot before you try traffic or hills.

## The full explanation

A manual transmission gives you full control of which gear the engine is in, using a gear lever and a clutch pedal. The clutch pedal is the leftmost of the three pedals. Pressing it all the way down disconnects the engine from the wheels, so you can move the gear lever without grinding the gears.

### The shift pattern

The Fit's manual gearbox uses a standard five-speed "H" pattern.<!-- F-WP06-001 --> First and third gear are in the top row of the pattern; second, fourth, and reverse are in the bottom row, with reverse next to fourth and fifth.<!-- F-WP06-001 --> Every forward gear is synchronized, meaning the gearbox is built to let the gears mesh smoothly as you shift.<!-- F-WP06-001 -->

### Getting into reverse without grinding

The transmission has a built-in lockout that stops you from shifting straight from fifth gear into reverse.<!-- F-WP06-002 --> To shift into reverse without the gears grinding, come to a full stop and push the clutch pedal all the way down. Then either pause a few seconds before moving the lever into reverse, or shift briefly into a forward gear first.<!-- F-WP06-002 --> Either action gives the input shaft, the spinning part inside the gearbox that reverse gear must line up with, time to stop turning before reverse engages.<!-- F-WP06-002 -->

### Shifting for good fuel economy

The owner's manual gives recommended shift points for normal acceleration. Shift from first to second around 15 mph (24 km/h), and second to third around 27 mph (43 km/h).<!-- F-WP06-003 --> Shift from third to fourth around 39 mph (62 km/h), and fourth to fifth around 53 mph (85 km/h).<!-- F-WP06-003 --> As a general rule, drive in the highest gear that still lets the engine run and speed up smoothly. That gives you the best fuel economy and the lowest emissions.<!-- F-WP06-003 -->

### Starting off on a hill

Honda's owner's manual for this generation of Fit does not describe a dedicated hill-start procedure.<!-- F-WP06-009 --> This car also does not have a Hill Start Assist feature, which is a system that appears on some later Honda models.<!-- F-WP06-009 --> The manual's page on parking does cover parking on a hill. Turn your wheels, use first gear if you are facing uphill or reverse if you are facing downhill, and set the parking brake.<!-- F-WP06-009 --> It does not, however, describe how to pull away from a stop on an uphill grade.<!-- F-WP06-009 -->

Because of that gap, the hill-start routine below is a general three-pedal technique common to manual-transmission cars, built from this car's own documented clutch and parking-brake behavior. It is good general practice, not a Honda-published Fit procedure.

## Step by step

### Drive a manual transmission for the first time

**Safety warnings, before you touch a pedal:**
- Always practice first in an empty parking lot or a very quiet street, not in traffic.
- Always fasten your seatbelt and adjust your seat and mirrors before you start the engine.
- Always press the clutch pedal all the way to the floor before turning the key to start the engine; the starter will not engage otherwise.
- Do not rev the engine hard or ride the clutch pedal partly pressed. This wears the clutch out faster and can damage it.
- Always come to a full stop before shifting into reverse.
- Do not try to shift straight from fifth gear into reverse. The transmission has a lockout and needs a short pause, or a pass through a forward gear, first.<!-- F-WP06-002 -->

**Steps:**
1. Adjust your seat, mirrors, and seatbelt before starting the engine.
2. Push the clutch pedal all the way to the floor. The starter only works with the clutch down.
3. Turn the key to start the engine, then shift the lever into first gear.
4. Keep the brake pedal or parking brake applied while you slowly let the clutch pedal rise.
5. Feel for the "friction point," the moment the engine note changes and the car wants to creep forward.
6. Gently press the gas pedal while you keep easing the clutch pedal up.
7. Once the car is moving, finish releasing the clutch pedal and keep your foot off it.
8. Shift up around 15 mph into second, 27 mph into third, 39 mph into fourth, and 53 mph into fifth.<!-- F-WP06-003 -->
9. To shift up, press the clutch fully down, move the lever to the next gear, then ease the clutch back up.
10. To slow down, press the clutch down fully just before the engine would stall, then brake normally.
11. Once stopped, shift to neutral and take your foot off the clutch until you are ready to move again.

**How you know it worked:** you can pull away smoothly and shift up and down through the gears. You can also come to a full stop without the engine stalling or the car jerking.

### Start on a hill (manual transmission)

**Safety warnings, before you try this:**
- Always practice this first in an empty parking lot or a quiet street with a gentle slope, not on a busy or steep hill.
- Always keep your right foot ready to move to the brake pedal in case the car starts to roll backward.
- Do not practice on a hill with another car stopped close behind you until you are confident you will not roll back.
- Always turn on your hazard lights if you stall on a hill and are blocking traffic, then try again.
- Do not rest your foot on the clutch pedal once you are moving; this wears the clutch out faster.

**Steps:**
1. Come to a full stop on the hill using the brake pedal.
2. Pull the parking brake up firmly with your right hand.
3. Push the clutch pedal all the way to the floor.
4. Shift the lever into first gear.
5. Move your right foot from the brake pedal to the gas pedal.
6. Press the gas gently while slowly letting the clutch pedal rise, until you feel the engine pull against the car.
7. When you feel the car want to move forward, release the parking brake fully.
8. Keep easing the clutch pedal up while giving a little more gas, so the engine does not bog down or stall.
9. Once the car is moving smoothly forward, finish releasing the clutch pedal all the way.

**How you know it worked:** the car moves forward up the hill smoothly, does not roll backward at any point, and the engine does not stall.

## What can go wrong
- **Letting the clutch pedal up too fast.** This stalls the engine, meaning it shuts off because it cannot keep running against the sudden load.
- **Riding the clutch.** Holding the clutch pedal partway up while driving wears the clutch out faster.
- **Giving too little gas while releasing the clutch.** This also stalls the engine, especially on a hill.
- **Releasing the parking brake before the clutch has caught, on a hill start.** This lets the car roll backward.
- **Forgetting to fully release the parking brake once moving.** This can overheat the rear brakes.
- **Looking down at the shift lever instead of watching the road.** Learn the shift pattern by feel.
- **Trying to shift straight from fifth gear to reverse.** Pause first, or pass through a forward gear, instead.<!-- F-WP06-002 -->
