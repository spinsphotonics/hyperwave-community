# Chapter 50: Suspension — Springs, Coilovers, Sway Bars, and Alignment

## What you will learn

- What a spring, a coilover, and a sway bar each actually do
- 9 real suspension products confirmed to fit your GD3, and how each was checked
- 7 real products that looked right but were rejected, and exactly why
- How to run that same fitment check yourself before you ever buy a part
- Street and track alignment targets, and why they are not the same numbers

## The short version

A lowering spring drops your ride height and changes how stiff your suspension feels, and a coilover is a fully adjustable spring-and-shock unit. A sway bar reduces how much your car leans in a corner. This chapter lists 9 real products confirmed, from each product's own listing text, to fit your 2007-2008 GD3 Fit. It also lists 7 look-alike products this book rejected, because they were actually built for the different 2009-2014 GE8 Fit. Learning to spot that difference yourself is one of the most useful skills a Fit modder can build.

## The full explanation

### What these parts actually do

A **spring** is the coiled metal part that holds your car's weight up and compresses when the wheel hits a bump. A shorter, stiffer aftermarket spring usually lowers your ride height and reduces how much the car leans and dives. A **coilover** combines a spring around a shock absorber, called a **damper**, into one adjustable unit. Most coilovers let you set ride height independent of how hard the spring is preloaded, and many also let you adjust damping, meaning how quickly the shock compresses and rebounds.

A **sway bar**, also called an anti-roll bar, is a metal bar that connects the left and right suspension. It twists slightly in a corner to resist body lean. A thicker sway bar generally means a stiffer, less-leaning car.

Three more terms matter for the alignment section later in this chapter. **Camber** is how much a wheel tilts inward or outward at the top, viewed from the front of the car, measured in degrees. **Caster** is how much the steering axis tilts, viewed from the side. **Toe** is whether the wheels point slightly toward each other or away from each other, viewed from above.

### The single most important rule in this chapter

Chapter 47 warned you that a part built for the 2009-2014 Fit, chassis code GE8, does not automatically fit your 2007-2008 Fit, chassis code GD3, even when it looks identical. This chapter puts that warning into practice with real parts, real listings, and real mistakes this book's own research caught.

Here is how you run the same check yourself. Read the product listing's own text, not only its headline. Look for the words "GD3," "2007-2008," or "07-08," or for two separate fitment lines, one for 2007 and one for 2008. A generic claim like "fits Honda Fit" is not enough on its own, since that same wording can quietly cover the GE8 too. Watch for three specific traps. A trusted brand can sell a near-identical part for the GE8 under a similar name. A part number can look right while sitting on a different generation's page. And a Japanese-market production-date window can fail to line up with the US 2007-2008 model years, even when the chassis code looks correct.

Row 3 is worth reading twice. A listing can say "2008" in its title and still not be for your car. That happens when the same listing also tags itself with a trim or region code, like "RS," that Honda never sold as a US 2008 base Fit. Row 7 shows an even sneakier version of the same trap: the chassis code is right, but the production-date window quietly excludes your car's actual build year. Read every date and code on a listing, not only the ones that jump out at you.

### The same part, three different listings

One more case study drives the lesson home. The retailer Heeltoe Automotive sells the identical SPC 81260 camber bolt on three separate product pages. One page is tagged "07-08 Honda Fit" for your GD3. Another is tagged "09-14 Honda Fit" for the GE8, and a third is tagged "15-20 Honda Fit" for the newer GK generation. <!-- F-WP16-021 --> A buyer who searches only by the bare part number, 81260, could click into the wrong page and never notice. <!-- F-WP16-021 --> Always confirm you are on the generation-matched page, not only the correct part number.

### Sway bars for the rear

The Progress Technology 22mm rear sway bar, part 62.1060, is listed for "07-08 Honda Fit," and its own body copy adds "Compatible with GD Honda Fit." <!-- F-WP16-016 --> The Ultra Racing 16mm rear sway bar is listed for "Honda Fit (GD) 2001-2008," a thinner alternative to the Progress bar above. <!-- F-WP16-017 -->

One listing deserves a caution instead of a recommendation. A Whiteline sway bar link is sold under a web address that says "rear sway bar link." The same page's own title and body text, read directly, say "Front Sway Bar Link" throughout instead. <!-- F-WP16-018 --> Because the page disagrees with itself about which end of the car this part is for, this book does not use it as a confirmed rear-bar part. <!-- F-WP16-018 --> As a bonus, a Cusco 24mm bar listed for "Honda Fit-Jazz GD1/GD3" was also found this pass. It is explicitly a front sway bar, not a rear one, so skip it if you are specifically shopping for a rear bar. <!-- F-WP16-019 -->

### Camber and alignment adjustment parts

The SPC EZCam 14mm camber bolt, part 81260, gives roughly plus or minus 1.75 degrees of front camber adjustment when both bolts on a strut are replaced. <!-- F-WP16-020 --> It installs by swapping out one or both of your strut's existing pinch bolts for this eccentric, off-center bolt. Buy it from the page tagged "07-08 Honda Fit," not the "09-14" or "15-20" pages selling the identical part number. <!-- F-WP16-020 -->

Honed Developments, a shop focused specifically on the GD3, sells a strut-top part as part of its published GD3 suspension recipe. This part increases both front caster and front camber beyond what a camber bolt alone provides, though no numeric adjustment figure for it was confirmed this pass. <!-- F-WP16-022 --> Your Fit's rear suspension is a **torsion-beam axle**, a solid rear beam with no built-in camber or toe adjustment from the factory. <!-- F-WP16-023 --> Honed Developments also sells a rear alignment shim kit, listed for "GD & GK Jazz/Fit," that adds camber and toe adjustment where none exists from the factory. <!-- F-WP16-023 -->

### Alignment specs: street versus track

These two alignment targets come from different sources and different goals, so this book labels each one clearly rather than blending them into a single number.

One GD3-focused suspension recipe publishes a **track or performance setup**. Up front: camber -3.5 to -4.5 degrees, caster +2 to +2.5 degrees, and toe 0 degrees. <!-- F-WP16-024 --> In the rear: camber -2 to -2.5 degrees, and toe +2 to 0 (in). <!-- F-WP16-024 --> This target came from a single source, so treat it as a strong starting point for a dedicated track car, not a universal rule. <!-- F-WP16-024 -->

GD3 owners report a gentler **street or daily-driving setup** instead: roughly -1.25 to -2.0 degrees of front camber, which balances comfortable driving with acceptable tire wear. <!-- F-WP16-025 --> Toe is kept as close to 0 as the front and rear allow. <!-- F-WP16-025 --> Owners report that rotating your tires regularly helps manage the extra wear that negative camber causes on a street-driven car. <!-- F-WP16-025 --> Notice how much less aggressive this is than the track numbers above; a track alignment on a daily driver chews through tires fast.

One number this book could not confirm is your Fit's original factory alignment spec. We could not confirm this for the 2008 Fit's factory alignment; check your owner's manual or a dealer. <!-- F-WP16-026 --> A source found this pass gave factory numbers alongside a tire fitment and pressure spec. That pressure spec conflicts with this book's own verified owner's-manual data elsewhere in this book. <!-- F-WP16-026 --> This book's research could not confirm which generation or market that source actually describes. <!-- F-WP16-026 --> Rather than guess, this book flags the conflict and sends you to your own door-jamb sticker and factory manual instead.
