# Chapter 29: Spark Plugs

## What you will learn
- What a spark plug does, and which plugs fit your 2008 Fit's engine
- The correct gap and torque for a new spark plug
- How to replace all four spark plugs yourself, step by step
- Why valve clearance, a small engine gap checked at the same visits, is worth knowing about
- What can go wrong during a plug swap, and how to avoid it

## The short version

A spark plug ignites the fuel and air mixture inside your engine's cylinder. Your Fit takes an NGK IZFR6K-13 plug, or the equivalent Denso SKJ20DR-M13.<!-- F-WP09-036 --> The gap, the small space the spark jumps across, should be 1.2 to 1.3 millimeters (0.042 to 0.051 inches) on a new plug.<!-- F-WP09-037 --> Each plug tightens to about 13 pound-feet (18 newton-meters) of torque, a measure of twisting force.<!-- F-WP09-038 --> Replacing all four takes about 40 minutes the first time.<!-- P-WP11-13 -->

## The full explanation

### What spark plugs do, and when yours need changing

Every time a cylinder in your engine fires, a spark plug creates the spark that ignites the fuel and air inside it. Your Fit's owner's manual treats spark plug replacement as a Maintenance Minder sub item. This means the dashboard's Maintenance Minder computer decides when it is due, rather than a fixed mileage printed in the manual.<!-- F-WP09-009 --> No exact number is printed for your specific car.<!-- F-WP09-009 --> Some general guidance for Honda's iridium-style plugs, the kind used on this engine, points to roughly 100,000 miles. This figure is not specific to the 2008 Fit. We could not confirm it as an exact number for your car, so check your owner's manual and dashboard display instead.<!-- F-WP09-039 -->

### Which plugs, what gap, what torque

Your Fit's Specifications table lists two matching plug part numbers: NGK IZFR6K-13 and Denso SKJ20DR-M13. Either one is correct; they are made to the same specification.<!-- F-WP09-036 -->

A new plug's gap, the small air space between the plug's electrodes where the spark jumps across, should measure 1.2 to 1.3 millimeters, or 0.042 to 0.051 inches.<!-- F-WP09-037 --> Most new plugs ship pre-gapped correctly from the factory. Use a gap gauge to check this measurement, but do not force-adjust a pre-gapped plug unless it is clearly wrong.

Torque means how tight a bolt or plug is turned, measured in force applied at a distance. Each spark plug tightens to about 18 newton-meters, which is 1.8 kilogram-force-meters or 13 pound-feet.<!-- F-WP09-038 --> A torque wrench, a tool that stops or signals once you reach a set tightness, keeps you from cracking the cylinder head's soft aluminum threads by overtightening.

### Valve clearance: a related check at the same visits

Valve clearance is the small gap that lets your engine's valves open and close correctly as they heat up and cool down. Your Fit's manual has the technician check this clearance at the same Maintenance Minder visits, labeled A, B, 1, 2, or 3, where other service happens.<!-- F-WP09-010 --> It only gets adjusted if the engine sounds noisy; there is no separate fixed mileage number for adjusting it.<!-- F-WP09-010 --> Check this on a cold engine, meaning the cylinder head is below 100 degrees Fahrenheit (38 degrees Celsius). The correct clearance is 0.15 to 0.19 millimeters (0.006 to 0.007 inches) on the intake valves.<!-- F-WP09-045 --> The correct clearance is 0.26 to 0.30 millimeters (0.010 to 0.012 inches) on the exhaust valves.<!-- F-WP09-045 --> We could not confirm a do-it-yourself procedure for adjusting valve clearance on this engine from this book's sources. This job is usually left to a technician, so check your owner's manual or a qualified shop if your engine is noisy.

## Step by step

### Replace the spark plugs

**Safety warnings, before you touch a coil or a plug:**
- Always let the engine cool completely before starting. The cylinder head and exhaust stay hot long after the engine is off.
- Do not drop anything into an open spark plug hole. A dropped bolt or tool can badly damage the engine.
- Always work on one plug at a time so you never mix up which coil and boot go where.
- Do not overtighten a plug. A torque wrench prevents cracking the cylinder head threads.

**Steps:**
1. Let the engine cool completely.
2. Open the hood and remove the plastic under-cowl or engine cover panel to reach the coils.
3. Unplug the electrical connector from the first ignition coil.
4. Remove the bolt holding that ignition coil, and lift the coil straight up and out.
5. Look inside the plug hole and clear away any loose dirt with a rag or brush.
6. Fit the spark plug socket over the old plug and turn it counterclockwise to remove it.
7. Inspect the old plug's tip for unusual color or heavy wear.
8. Check the new plug's gap against the specification on its box, but do not force-adjust a pre-gapped plug.
9. Apply a small amount of anti-seize compound to the new plug's threads.
10. Thread the new plug in by hand until it stops, to avoid cross-threading.
11. Tighten the plug the rest of the way with a torque wrench to about 13 pound-feet (18 newton-meters).<!-- F-WP09-038 -->
12. Reinstall that ignition coil, its bolt, and its electrical connector.
13. Repeat steps 3 through 12 for each remaining cylinder.
14. Reinstall the under-cowl or engine cover panel.
15. Start the engine and listen for smooth, even running with no misfire.

**How you know it worked:** the engine starts easily, idles smoothly with no shaking or misfire, and the check engine light does not come on after a short drive.

## What can go wrong

- **Threading a plug in with the socket instead of starting it by hand.** This risks cross-threading the soft aluminum cylinder head.
- **Skipping the torque wrench and guessing "snug."** This can under-tighten or over-tighten the plug.
- **Mixing up which coil connector plugs into which coil.** Work on one cylinder at a time to avoid this.
- **Dropping a bolt into the plug hole while the coil is off.** Keep loose tools and hardware well clear of open plug holes.
- **Assuming your engine's plug interval matches general online guidance.** Your Fit's own Maintenance Minder display is the only source that actually tracks your car's driving.<!-- F-WP09-009 -->
