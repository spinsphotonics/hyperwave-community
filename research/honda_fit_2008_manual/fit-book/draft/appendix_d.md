# Appendix D: Fuse and relay maps

Your 2008 Fit has three fuse boxes: one inside the car, and two in the engine compartment. This appendix lists every fuse position in all three boxes, with its amperage and the circuit it protects, straight from the owner's manual. It also tells you honestly where the manual does not give you a relay map, instead of making one up.

## Fuse box locations

| Fuse box | Location | How to open it | Fact ID |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interior (passenger compartment) fuse box | Behind the driver's coin tray, on the dashboard on the driver's side | Turn the coin tray's dial counterclockwise, then pull the tray toward you to remove it | F-WP21-002 |
| Primary under-hood fuse box | Engine compartment, driver's side | Push the tabs on the fuse box cover to release the lid | F-WP21-003 |
| Secondary fuse box | On the positive terminal of the battery, in the engine compartment | This box holds a single 80 A fuse that protects the battery circuit | F-WP21-004 |

<!-- F-WP21-001 -->
Your Fit has three fuse boxes in total, not two. Many cars have only one box under the dash and one under the hood. Your Fit adds a third, very small fuse box mounted directly on the battery's positive terminal.

Position No. 27 is your accessory power socket (cigar lighter) fuse — the one to check first if a phone charger or other plug-in accessory stops working. Position No. 18 is listed twice in the manual because it is an equipment-dependent slot: on a car with a Tire Pressure Monitoring System, it is a 7.5 A TPMS fuse; on a Canadian-market car, it is a 10 A daytime running light fuse instead. A US base-trim 2008 Fit without TPMS and without Canadian daytime running lights would not use either circuit at that position, so treat position 18 as reserved rather than assuming one fixed value.

## Checking and replacing fuses

<!-- F-WP21-007, F-WP21-008 -->
**Always** replace a blown fuse with one of the exact same amperage rating shown in the tables above. Replacing a fuse with a higher-rated one greatly increases the chance of damaging your electrical system. If you do not have the correct-rated fuse on hand, the manual says to install a lower-rated one instead of a higher one, and to put in the correctly-rated fuse as soon as you can — a fuse with a lower rating may blow again on its own, which by itself does not mean something else is wrong.

**Do not** keep replacing a fuse that blows again in a short time with another fuse of the same rating. The manual says this points to a serious electrical problem. Leave the blown fuse in that circuit and have the car checked by a qualified technician instead.

<!-- F-WP21-006 -->
To check whether a fuse is blown: (1) turn the ignition switch to LOCK (0) and make sure the headlights and other accessories are off; (2) remove the cover from the fuse box; (3) for the large fuses in the under-hood boxes, look through the top of the fuse at the wire inside; (4) for the smaller fuses in the primary under-hood box and all fuses in the interior box, pull each one out with the fuse puller; (5) look for a blown wire inside — a blown fuse's wire is broken or melted. Check the fuse listed for the affected device first, but check all the fuses before you decide a blown fuse is the cause.

<!-- F-WP21-005 -->
A fuse puller is stored in the interior fuse box. Use it on the smaller fuses in the interior box and the smaller fuses in the primary under-hood box. The larger under-hood fuses are checked by looking through the top of the fuse, and removed with a Phillips-head screwdriver instead of the puller.

<!-- F-WP21-009 -->
If you pull the radio fuse, or if the battery is disconnected, the audio system disables itself as an anti-theft feature. The next time you turn the radio on, "ENTER CODE" appears in the frequency display, and you must enter the audio system's 5-digit code using the radio's preset buttons to turn it back on. That code should be on a radio code card that came with the vehicle. You get 10 tries; after 10 failed attempts, you must leave the radio powered on for 1 hour before trying again. If the code card is lost, a dealer can look up the code using the audio system's serial number.
