# Chapter 9: The Magic Seat and Cargo Tricks

## What you will learn
- What the Magic Seat is and why the Fit's cargo space is unusual for such a small car
- How to fold the rear seat flat for cargo (Utility Mode)
- How to flip the rear seat cushions up for tall items (Tall Mode)
- How to make one long flat surface for lumber or surfboards (Long Mode)
- How to recline the front seats flat to rest (Refresh Mode)

## The short version
The Magic Seat is Honda's name for the Fit's 60/40-split rear seat, split so each side folds on its own <!-- F-WP05-028 -->. It can fold four different ways beyond normal seating, called Utility, Tall, Long, and Refresh mode <!-- F-WP05-028 -->. Each mode takes a few minutes and no tools, but each one also has real safety rules you must follow before you start.

## The full explanation
Honda built the Magic Seat as a 60/40-split rear seat, meaning the two sides split unevenly so you can fold one side and leave a passenger seated on the other <!-- F-WP05-028 -->. Honda describes five total seating and cargo setups: the standard five-passenger mode, plus refresh mode, tall object mode, long object mode, and utility mode <!-- F-WP05-028 -->. This chapter covers the four non-standard modes as step-by-step procedures.

With every seat up in standard mode, the Fit holds 90.1 cubic feet of passenger space and 21.3 cubic feet of cargo space behind the back seat <!-- F-WP05-029 -->. Fold the rear seat-backs down flat for Utility Mode, and cargo space grows to 56.8 cubic feet <!-- F-WP05-030 -->.

Two basic seat motions make up all four modes. First, a rear seat cushion can fold up and lock against the seat-back, which is the base move behind Tall Mode <!-- F-WP05-031 -->. Second, a rear seat-back can fold down flat, which is the base move behind Utility Mode and part of Long Mode <!-- F-WP05-032 -->. Long Mode and Refresh Mode add more steps on top of these two basic motions.

## Step by step

### Utility Mode: fold the rear seat down flat for cargo
Utility Mode gives you a flat load floor from the tailgate forward, for boxes, groceries, or bikes <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always remove any items from the rear seat before you fold it.
- Always make sure nothing sits on the floor before you fold the seat-back down.
- Always keep cargo below the bottom of the windows, so it does not block the side curtain airbags (airbags built into the roof rail above the windows).
- Always tie down loose cargo so it cannot fly forward during hard braking.
- Do not put heavy items directly on a folded seat-back.
- Do not let a passenger sit on a rear seat you have folded down; return each side fully upright, locked, and belted first.

**Steps:**
1. Decide which side of the 60/40 rear seat you are folding: driver's side, passenger's side, or both <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.
2. Remove everything from that rear seat.
3. Move the matching front seat as far forward as it will go <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.
4. Lock the rear outer head restraint (the padded support behind your head) on that side in its lowest position <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.
5. If you are folding the driver's side, also set the rear-center head restraint to its lowest position <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.
6. If you are folding the driver's side, pull the latch plate to release the center seat belt from its anchor, and let the belt retract into the ceiling holder; store the buckle there too <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.
7. Pull up the release lever on the outside of that seat-back <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.
8. Fold the seat-back forward until it lies flat <!-- F-WP05-032 -->.
9. Repeat steps 2 through 8 on the other side if you need the full-width flat floor.
10. Load your cargo, keeping it below window height and tied down.

**How you know it worked:** The seat-back lies flat and level, forming a flat load floor from the tailgate forward. It does not spring back up when you let go.

### Tall Mode: flip the rear cushions up for tall items
Tall Mode opens floor space in front of the seat-back, for items too tall to fit under a normal seat <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always remove any items from the rear seat before you fold the cushion up.
- Always make sure the cushion locks firmly against the seat-back before you load anything against it.
- Do not stack items higher than the top of the seat-backs; they can block your rear view and be thrown around the car in a crash.
- Do not place cargo where it blocks your view through the rear window.

**Steps:**
1. Remove everything from the rear seat cushion you are flipping up.
2. Pull up on the rear seat cushion <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.
3. Fold the seat leg down as the cushion comes up <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.
4. Push the seat cushion firmly against the seat-back until it locks in place <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.
5. Repeat for the other side if you want both cushions up.
6. Load your tall item into the open floor space in front of the seat-back.
7. To return to normal seating later, make sure the floor under the seat is empty first <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.
8. Pull the seat leg up fully <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.
9. Slowly push the seat cushion back down while holding it with both hands <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.
10. Set the seat leg into its floor guide; a latch appears when it seats correctly <!-- F-WP05-031 -->.

**How you know it worked:** The seat cushion stays locked upright against the seat-back on its own. It does not fall back down when you let go.

### Long Mode: carry long items like lumber or surfboards
Owners report that Long Mode combines the Tall and Utility motions to create one flat path from the tailgate to the dashboard <!-- F-WP05-034 -->. We could not confirm an official Honda step list for this exact combined mode on the 2008 GD3; check your owner's manual before loading anything long or heavy <!-- F-WP05-034 -->.

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always keep at least one working seat belt and seating position free for every person riding in the car; do not use more seats than are actually available once you reconfigure them.
- Always tie down long cargo. An unsecured long item can fly forward with great force during hard braking, which can seriously injure or kill someone in the front seat.
- Do not let cargo extend into the space where an airbag needs to open.
- Do not drive with cargo blocking your view through the rear window or a side mirror.
- We could not fully confirm these exact steps against the 2008 GD3 owner's manual. Follow your own owner's manual's cargo-loading warnings if they differ from this book <!-- F-WP05-034 -->.

**Steps (community-reported):**
1. Fold the passenger-side rear seat cushion up, as in Tall Mode steps 1 through 4, or fold that seat-back down flat, as in Utility Mode, whichever gives you the flattest path to your item <!-- F-WP05-034 -->.
2. Move the front passenger seat as far forward as it will go <!-- F-WP05-034 -->.
3. Recline the front passenger seat-back forward until it rests against the folded rear seat, forming one flat surface from the tailgate to the dashboard on that side <!-- F-WP05-034 -->.
4. Leave the driver's-side rear seat upright if you still need to carry a rear passenger <!-- F-WP05-034 -->.
5. Load the long item, such as lumber, a surfboard, or a ladder, along the flattened path.
6. Tie the item down using the cargo area's tie-down anchors so it cannot slide or shift.

**How you know it worked:** The item lies flat and secured along one continuous surface from the tailgate to the dashboard. It does not shift when you push on it by hand.

### Refresh Mode: recline the front seats flat to rest
Refresh Mode reclines the front seats flat, level with the rear cushions, for resting when the car is parked <!-- F-WP05-033 -->. This is not a driving position.

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always park the car, set the parking brake, and turn off the engine before you recline the seats this far.
- Always reinstall both front head restraints and lock both seats securely upright again before anyone drives or rides in the car.
- Do not drive the car, or let anyone ride in a moving car, with the front seats in this reclined position; seat belts are not designed to protect a reclined occupant (a person in the seat).
- Do not let a child use this mode unsupervised; the fold-down lever and mechanism could pinch fingers.

**Steps:**
1. Park the car, set the parking brake, and turn off the engine.
2. Recline the rear seat-backs as far back as they will go <!-- F-WP05-033 -->.
3. Remove both front head restraints and store them somewhere secure so they do not roll around <!-- F-WP05-033 -->.
4. Using the seat slide lever, move both front seat cushions as far forward as they will go <!-- F-WP05-033 -->.
5. Pull up the seat-back angle adjustment lever on each front seat <!-- F-WP05-033 -->.
6. Pivot each front seat-back backward until it is level with the rear seat cushions, creating one large cushioned area <!-- F-WP05-033 -->.
7. To return to the normal driving position, hold each seat-back with your hand first.
8. Pivot each seat-back back upright, controlling the motion so it does not come up too fast.
9. Reinstall both head restraints <!-- F-WP05-033 -->.
10. Lock both seats securely before anyone drives or rides in the car.

**How you know it worked:** The front seat-backs lie flat and level with the rear seat cushions, forming one continuous cushioned surface. Each seat-back stays down on its own without you holding it.

## What can go wrong

### Utility Mode
- Forgetting to move the front seat forward first stops the folded seat-back from lying flat.
- Leaving the head restraint up blocks the seat-back from folding flush.
- Skipping the center seat belt's anchor release on the driver's side can strain or damage that belt.
- Not checking the passenger airbag-off indicator after you restore the seat can leave you unsure if the front passenger's weight sensor still reads correctly.

### Tall Mode
- Not pushing the cushion firmly enough against the seat-back means it is not truly locked, and it can fall during driving.
- Forgetting to fold the seat leg down can leave the mechanism loose.
- Loading an item so tall that it blocks the rear window can also interfere with the seat-backs later, when you want to return the seat to normal.

### Long Mode
- Forgetting to move the front passenger seat forward before reclining it leaves a gap instead of a flat surface.
- Not tying the item down lets it slide during braking or turns.
- Loading an item so long that it sticks out past the rear bumper without a proper reflective flag or marker is unsafe, and may be illegal in some states.

### Refresh Mode
- Forgetting to remove the head restraints first stops the seat-back from lying fully flat.
- Letting go of the seat-back too quickly when you return it upright is risky; it can spring up faster than expected, so control it by hand.
- Forgetting to relock the seats and reinstall the head restraints before driving again leaves the car unsafe for the next trip.
