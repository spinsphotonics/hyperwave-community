# Chapter 11: First Drive for a New Driver

## What you will learn
- What is different about starting off in a manual Fit versus an automatic Fit
- The gear positions on the base-trim automatic transmission, and what each one does
- A short first-drive routine for each transmission type
- Where to find the full manual-transmission practice routine in this book

## The short version
A manual Fit needs your left foot on the clutch pedal before it will move. An automatic Fit only needs you to pick the right gear lever position and press the gas. Both transmissions reward a slow, gentle first drive in an empty parking lot.

## The full explanation

This chapter gives you a first look at driving your Fit, split by transmission type. If you do not know which one your car has, look at the floor. A clutch pedal on the far left, next to the brake and gas, means you have a manual. Only two pedals means you have an automatic.

### If your Fit has a manual transmission

A manual transmission asks you to pick every gear yourself using a lever and a clutch pedal. The clutch is the pedal that disconnects the engine from the wheels so you can change gears without grinding them.

The Fit's manual gearbox uses a standard five-speed "H" shift pattern.<!-- F-WP06-001 --> First and third gear sit in the top row of the pattern, and second, fourth, and reverse sit in the bottom row, with reverse next to fourth and fifth.<!-- F-WP06-001 --> The gearbox is synchronized in every forward gear, which means the gears are built to mesh smoothly as you shift.<!-- F-WP06-001 -->

This chapter's first-drive routine for a manual is short on purpose. Chapter 12 walks through the full sequence, step by step, with the exact clutch and gas technique to use, so read that chapter before your first real practice session.

### If your Fit has an automatic transmission

The base-trim automatic Fit has a full shift gate, meaning it offers more lever positions than just "Drive."<!-- F-WP06-004 --> The positions are Park, Reverse, Neutral, Drive, a position marked "D" with a small "3" (called D3), Second, and First.<!-- F-WP06-004 --> The Sport-model Fit has a shorter gate with paddle shifters instead, but the base trim you are learning on has all seven positions.<!-- F-WP06-004 -->

Here is what each position after Park, Reverse, and Neutral actually does:

- **Drive (D):** your normal, everyday driving position. The transmission picks a suitable gear on its own, from first through fifth, based on your speed and how hard you are pressing the gas.<!-- F-WP06-005 --> When the engine is cold, it may shift up at a higher engine speed than usual, to help the engine warm up faster.<!-- F-WP06-005 -->
- **D3:** works like Drive, but the transmission stays in first, second, or third gear and will not shift into fourth or fifth.<!-- F-WP06-006 --> Honda intends this position to give you engine braking on a steep downhill, and to stop the transmission from constantly shifting between third and fourth in stop-and-go traffic.<!-- F-WP06-006 --> Engine braking means using the drag of the engine, instead of only the brakes, to slow the car down.
- **Second (2):** locks the transmission in second gear, so it will not drop to first when you stop.<!-- F-WP06-007 --> The manual recommends this position for climbing a grade with more power, and for extra engine braking on a steep downhill.<!-- F-WP06-007 --> It also helps for starting out on a slippery surface or in deep snow, and for reducing wheel spin.<!-- F-WP06-007 -->
- **First (1):** locks the transmission in first gear.<!-- F-WP06-008 --> By moving the lever among First, Second, D3, and Drive as conditions call for it, you can shift the automatic somewhat like a manual, just without a clutch pedal.<!-- F-WP06-008 -->

For an ordinary first drive, you will spend almost all of your time in Drive. Save Second, First, and D3 for hills, snow, or heavy traffic, and remember to move the lever back to Drive once you no longer need them.

Note for parents and new drivers: this chapter's driving sources cover the gear lever positions themselves. They do not cover the exact steps for starting the engine and pulling out of a parking space on an automatic. Chapter 6 of this book, on starting and stopping the car, covers the engine-start sequence in full.

## Step by step

**Manual transmission — short version.** Full safety warnings and the full 11-step routine are in Chapter 12. In brief: fasten your seatbelt and adjust your seat and mirrors, then press the clutch fully to the floor and start the engine. Shift into first gear, then slowly release the clutch while pressing the gas gently until the car moves.<!-- F-WP06-001 --> Go to Chapter 12 before you try this for the first time.

**Automatic transmission — first drive.**
1. Fasten your seatbelt and adjust your seat and mirrors before you shift out of Park.
2. Press the brake pedal, and move the shift lever from Park to Drive.<!-- F-WP06-005 -->
3. Release the parking brake.
4. Slowly release the brake pedal, then press the gas pedal gently.
5. Let the transmission pick its own gears as your speed changes; you do not need to shift it yourself in Drive.<!-- F-WP06-005 -->
6. If you are climbing a long, steep hill and want more power, or descending a steep hill and want more engine braking, move the lever to D3.<!-- F-WP06-006 -->
7. Move the lever back to Drive once you are back on a normal road.

## What can go wrong
- **Manual:** letting the clutch pedal up too fast causes the engine to stall, which means it shuts off because it cannot keep running against the load. Chapter 12 covers this in detail.
- **Automatic — forgetting to shift back to Drive:** if you leave the lever in Second, First, or D3 after the hill or snow is behind you, the transmission stays stuck in those lower gears.<!-- F-WP06-006 --><!-- F-WP06-007 --><!-- F-WP06-008 --> It will not use all five gears until you move the lever back.<!-- F-WP06-006 --> Get in the habit of checking the lever position once conditions are back to normal.
- **Automatic — confusing D3 with Drive:** D3 feels almost identical to Drive at low speed, since both use first through third gear in that range. The difference only shows up above the speed where Drive would normally shift into fourth or fifth.<!-- F-WP06-006 -->
