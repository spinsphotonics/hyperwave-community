# Chapter 10: Fuel

## What you will learn
- What fuel grade your Fit needs
- How big the fuel tank is, and roughly how far it will take you
- What the low-fuel light means, and what happens if you ignore it
- How to fill the tank correctly and safely
- What the "CHECK FUEL CAP" message means and how to clear it

## The short version
Your 2008 Fit needs 87 AKI (pump octane) regular unleaded gasoline or higher <!-- F-WP05-035 -->. The tank holds 10.8 US gallons (41 liters) <!-- F-WP05-036 -->. Fill it outdoors, away from any flame or spark, and never try to add fuel again after the pump nozzle clicks off <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.

## The full explanation

### Fuel grade
Use 87 AKI (pump octane, the number posted on the gas station pump) unleaded gasoline or higher <!-- F-WP05-035 -->. Using a lower-octane fuel can cause a heavy, persistent metallic knocking sound in the engine, and that knock can damage the engine over time <!-- F-WP05-035 -->. Honda also recommends gasoline with detergent additives (chemicals that help keep the engine clean) <!-- F-WP05-035 -->. Where you can find it, choose gasoline without manganese-based additives such as MMT <!-- F-WP05-035 -->. Those additives can trigger the malfunction indicator lamp, the dashboard warning light also called the "check engine" light <!-- F-WP05-035 -->. Your Fit is designed for gasoline blended with up to 10% ethanol or up to 15% MTBE (a fuel additive; short for methyl tertiary-butyl ether) by volume, but never gasoline containing methanol <!-- F-WP05-035 -->.

### Tank size and range
The fuel tank holds 10.8 US gallons, or 41 liters <!-- F-WP05-036 -->. The owner's manual and this book do not give an exact miles-per-gallon figure in this chapter's sources. Your real-world range will depend on how and where you drive, so use your fuel gauge, not a mental estimate, to judge how far you can go.

### The low-fuel light
The low fuel indicator (a dashboard light) turns on when about 1.7 US gallons (6.3 liters) remain in the tank, before the needle reaches E <!-- F-WP05-037 -->. A small extra reserve remains even once the needle reaches E, but treat that reserve as a safety margin, not fuel to drive on normally <!-- F-WP05-037 -->. Running the tank out of fuel can make the engine misfire and can damage the catalytic converter (a part that cleans your exhaust) <!-- F-WP05-037 -->. Avoid driving with an extremely low fuel level <!-- F-WP05-037 -->.

### The fuel door and cap
The fuel fill door opens from outside the car; pull its notched edge to open it, since this trim has no separate release lever inside the car <!-- F-WP05-038 -->. The fuel fill cap stays tethered to the filler neck, so you cannot drop or lose it <!-- F-WP05-038 -->. You may hear a hissing sound as you remove the cap; that is normal tank pressure escaping <!-- F-WP05-038 -->. The cap's tether fits into a slit on the fuel fill door, so it can hang there while you refuel <!-- F-WP05-038 -->. Screw the cap back on until it clicks at least once; an improperly tightened cap can trigger the malfunction indicator lamp and a "CHECK FUEL CAP" message <!-- F-WP05-038 -->.

### The "CHECK FUEL CAP" message
If the fuel fill cap is loose or missing, a "CHECK FUEL CAP" message scrolls across the instrument panel's information display the next time you start the engine <!-- F-WP05-039 -->. You can press the trip-meter select/reset knob to clear the message from the display early <!-- F-WP05-039 -->. It comes back every time you restart the engine until you fix the cap <!-- F-WP05-039 -->. If the system keeps detecting a loose or missing cap, the malfunction indicator lamp also turns on <!-- F-WP05-039 -->. To fix it, turn the engine off, check that the cap is installed, and tighten it until it clicks at least once <!-- F-WP05-039 -->. The malfunction indicator lamp will not turn off right away; it can take several days of normal driving after you fix the cap <!-- F-WP05-039 -->. If it stays on longer than that, have a dealer inspect the car <!-- F-WP05-039 -->.

## Step by step: Refuel the car

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Gasoline is highly flammable and explosive. You can be burned or seriously injured while handling it <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
- Always turn off the engine before you start fueling <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
- Always keep heat, sparks, and open flame away from the fuel fill area <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
- Always handle fuel outdoors only, never in an enclosed space <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
- Always wipe up any spilled fuel right away <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
- Do not try to "top off" the tank once the pump nozzle clicks off on its own; leave room for the fuel to expand as its temperature changes <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.

**Steps:**
1. Park with the driver's side of the car closest to the fuel pump.
2. Turn off the engine <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
3. From outside the car, open the fuel fill door by pulling its notched edge <!-- F-WP05-038 -->.
4. Remove the fuel fill cap slowly; a hissing sound as tank pressure escapes is normal <!-- F-WP05-038 -->.
5. Fit the cap's tether into the slit on the fuel fill door, so the cap hangs there while you fuel <!-- F-WP05-038 -->.
6. Insert the pump nozzle and select 87 AKI regular unleaded fuel or higher <!-- F-WP05-035 -->.
7. Fill until the nozzle clicks off automatically, then stop; do not try to add more <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
8. If the nozzle clicks off repeatedly before the tank feels full, try a different pump; this can mean a problem with the fuel vapor recovery system <!-- F-WP05-040 -->. A dealer visit may help if it keeps happening <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
9. Screw the fuel fill cap back on until it clicks at least once <!-- F-WP05-038 -->.
10. Close the fuel fill door.

**How you know it worked:** The pump handles cleanly, the cap clicks at least once when you tighten it, and the fuel gauge needle moves up. No "CHECK FUEL CAP" message or malfunction indicator lamp appears on your next drive.

## What can go wrong
- Not tightening the fuel cap until it clicks can trigger a "CHECK FUEL CAP" message and the malfunction indicator lamp <!-- F-WP05-039 -->. This can take several days of driving to clear, even after you fix the cap <!-- F-WP05-039 -->.
- Trying to "top off" the tank past the automatic click-off risks a spill, and leaves no room for the fuel to expand <!-- F-WP05-040 -->.
- Forgetting to close the fuel fill door before you drive away leaves it open and exposed.
- Driving with the fuel gauge near empty risks a misfire and can damage the catalytic converter <!-- F-WP05-037 -->.
