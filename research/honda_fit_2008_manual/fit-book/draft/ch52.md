# Chapter 52: Intake, Header, and Exhaust

## What you will learn
- What an intake, a header, and an exhaust system each do on your Fit's engine.
- The real dyno numbers researchers found for each part on this exact car.
- Why a manufacturer's own claim is weaker proof than two separate independent tests.
- Why no clean, isolated dyno number exists for an exhaust system alone.
- How these parts perform together, instead of one at a time.

## The short version
An intake, a header, and an exhaust each free up a small amount of power on your L15A1 engine <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. Every real dyno number found for these parts on this car is modest, in the single digits <!-- F-WP17-003 -->. No clean, isolated dyno test for an exhaust system alone was found for this car <!-- F-WP17-008 -->.

## The full explanation

### Words you need first
A dynamometer, or dyno, is a machine that measures how much power an engine makes <!-- background -->. Some dynos measure power at the wheels <!-- background -->. That number is written as whp <!-- background -->. Wheel numbers are usually lower than crankshaft numbers <!-- background -->. Power gets lost on the way to the wheels <!-- background -->. A cold air intake replaces the factory air box and tubing <!-- background -->. It feeds air into your engine <!-- background -->. A header replaces the factory exhaust manifold <!-- background -->. That is the metal piece that collects exhaust gas right off the engine <!-- background -->. A cat-back exhaust replaces the pipe and muffler after the catalytic converter <!-- background -->. The catalytic converter is the part that cleans exhaust gas <!-- background -->. An axle-back exhaust replaces only the section behind the rear axle <!-- background -->. It is a smaller piece than a full cat-back <!-- background -->. A test pipe, sometimes called a catless downpipe, is a straight pipe <!-- background -->. It replaces the catalytic converter itself <!-- background -->.

### Intake gains
Researchers found dyno numbers for three intake products on the L15A1 <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. Injen's SP1511 cold air intake is built specifically for the 2007-2008 Fit <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. Injen advertises a gain of up to 7 hp, plus up to 8 lb-ft of extra torque <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. That same figure appears on a second retailer's page <!-- F-WP17-002 -->. Both pages trace back to the same original Injen claim <!-- F-WP17-002 -->. They are not two separately run tests <!-- F-WP17-002 -->. This book reports the number as Injen's own claim, not as two independent tests <!-- F-WP17-002 -->.

A different, independent video test used a Dynapack dyno on a car identified as a GD3 <!-- F-WP17-003 -->. An HPS Performance cold air intake gained a reported 5.9 whp at 5,685 rpm <!-- F-WP17-003 -->. It also gained 7.6 lb-ft of torque at 3,655 rpm <!-- F-WP17-003 -->. An HPS Performance short-ram intake pulls air from inside the engine bay instead of outside <!-- F-WP17-004 -->. It gained a reported 7.1 whp at 5,645 rpm and 6.4 lb-ft at 5,590 rpm <!-- F-WP17-004 -->. Three different intake products, tested two different ways, all landed in the same narrow range <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. Every reported gain is roughly 5 to 8 hp, and roughly 6 to 8 lb-ft <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. No source found claims a double-digit horsepower gain from an intake alone <!-- F-WP17-001 -->.

### Header gains
Only one number-based dyno claim for a header alone was found <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. It came from a test on an otherwise completely stock GD3 Fit <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. A fitfreak.net forum thread reported a gain of "5+HP" <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. Owners report that figure; it was not independently re-tested by this research <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. That single number sits below this book's normal bar of two separate sources <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. Treat it as a rough hint, not a settled fact <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. A second header claim of "+9 hp" turned up during research <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. It named a header built for the 2009-2014 Fit <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. That is a different car, with a different engine, so it does not count here <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. Header products built specifically for the 2007-2008 GD3 Fit are still sold today <!-- F-WP17-006 -->. No published dyno number is attached to that current product's own listing <!-- F-WP17-006 -->.

### Exhaust gains, and the honest gap
Stop and remember Chapter 47 before you read this section. A test pipe removes your car's catalytic converter, the part that cleans exhaust gas. Removing it is exactly the kind of change the Clean Air Act calls a defeat device. Federal law makes that illegal on a car driven on public roads, with real fines attached. <!-- F-WP15-001 --> A test pipe belongs on a car built only for closed-course track or competition use. It does not belong on a car you also drive to school or work, unless a specific part carries a CARB EO that covers your exact car.

Community discussion describes a test pipe alone as "usually" adding about 3 to 5 whp <!-- F-WP17-007 -->. One owner reported a header, a test pipe, and a cat-back exhaust installed together <!-- F-WP17-007 -->. That combination raised the car from 92 whp to 100 whp <!-- F-WP17-007 -->. That is an 8 whp gain for all three parts combined, not the test pipe alone <!-- F-WP17-007 -->.

Here is the honest gap in this research. No clean, isolated dyno number for a cat-back or axle-back exhaust alone was found for this exact car <!-- F-WP17-008 -->. Every full-exhaust dyno result found was reported together with a header, a test pipe, or an engine tune <!-- F-WP17-008 -->. There is no way to know what the exhaust piece alone contributed <!-- F-WP17-008 -->. Rather than guess at a number, this book leaves it unconfirmed <!-- F-WP17-008 -->. Real cat-back and axle-back exhaust products built for the 2007-2008 Fit do exist today <!-- F-WP17-009 -->. A'PEXi and HKS both sell one <!-- F-WP17-009,F-WP17-010 -->. Neither brand's own product page publishes a specific dyno figure <!-- F-WP17-009 -->. Both use only general marketing language instead <!-- F-WP17-010 -->.

### What happens when you combine all of them
Two community reports show what these parts can do together <!-- F-WP17-011 -->. Neither report isolates any single part's own share of the gain <!-- F-WP17-012 -->. One 2008 Fit owner started with a stock rating near 107 hp <!-- F-WP17-011 -->. He reported reaching about 115 whp <!-- F-WP17-011 -->. He got there by adding an intake, a header, a catless downpipe, a full exhaust, and an engine tune together <!-- F-WP17-011 -->. A second build reported 115.46 whp from a header, a downpipe, an axle-back muffler, and a tune <!-- F-WP17-012 -->. A third build, with an intake, a header, and a downpipe, reached 101 whp with a different tune <!-- F-WP17-012 -->. These are real reports from real owners <!-- F-WP17-012 -->. Treat them as "several parts together," not proof of what any one part adds by itself <!-- F-WP17-012 -->.

### Putting it together
An intake, a header, and an exhaust are each real, modest upgrades on the L15A1 <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. None of them is a magic power boost <!-- F-WP17-001 -->. Every isolated number found for these parts is small, in the single digits <!-- F-WP17-003 -->. The header claim rests on one source, not two, so treat it carefully <!-- F-WP17-005 -->. No isolated exhaust-only number exists at all <!-- F-WP17-008 -->. This book will not invent one <!-- F-WP17-008 -->. If you want a real, noticeable gain, expect it to come from combining several parts, plus a tune, not any single part alone <!-- F-WP17-011 -->.
