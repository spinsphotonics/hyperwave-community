# Chapter 14: Highway, Rain, Snow, Heat, and Night

## What you will learn
- Why driving fast on the highway costs you fuel economy
- What kind of tires the owner's manual recommends for winter driving
- The rules for using tire chains on a Fit
- Which weather and lighting conditions this chapter's sources do not cover

## The short version
Speed above about 45 to 50 mph costs you fuel economy fast, because wind resistance rises sharply. For winter, "M+S" or all-season tires cover most conditions, and real snow tires or approved cable-type chains, on the front only, help further. The sources for this book do not cover rain, extreme heat, or night driving for this specific car.

## The full explanation

### Highway speed

Aerodynamic drag, the resistance of the air pushing back against the car, rises sharply above 45 mph (75 km/h).<!-- F-WP06-012 --> The EPA's general driving guidance says gas mileage usually drops quickly above 50 mph. Each 5 mph you drive over 50 mph acts like paying about $0.29 more per gallon, based on EPA's national average fuel price at the time that figure was published.<!-- F-WP06-013 --> This EPA guidance is written for gasoline cars in general, not for the Fit specifically, but it applies to any gasoline car including the Fit.<!-- F-WP06-013 --> Roof racks, carriers, and trailers add extra drag on top of that.<!-- F-WP06-012 -->

### Winter tires and traction

Tires marked "M+S," which stands for mud and snow, or marked "All Season" on the sidewall, are suitable for most winter driving.<!-- F-WP06-014 --> For the best traction in snow or ice, the owner's manual recommends installing dedicated snow tires or tire chains, and notes these may be legally required in some places.<!-- F-WP06-014 --> If you mount snow tires, they must be radial tires of the same size and load range as your originals. They must go on all four wheels, not only the drive wheels. Dry-road traction may be lower than with your original tires.<!-- F-WP06-014 --> Ask your tire dealer for a maximum speed recommendation for the snow tires you choose.<!-- F-WP06-014 -->

### Tire chains

Tire chains are allowed on the Fit, but with limits specific to this car's tight tire clearance.<!-- F-WP06-015 --> Mount chains only on the front tires, never the rear.<!-- F-WP06-015 --> Only SAE Class "S" cable-type traction devices with rubber chain tensioners are allowed. The manual says not to use metal link-style chains.<!-- F-WP06-015 --> Install chains only when conditions or local law require them, and follow the chain maker's instructions. Mount them as tight as possible and drive slowly. Check that they are not contacting the brake lines or suspension, and stop right away if you hear contact.<!-- F-WP06-015 --> Remove the chains as soon as the road is clear.<!-- F-WP06-015 -->

### Rain, heat, and night driving

This book's research did not find Honda-specific guidance for the 2008 Fit on driving in rain, in extreme heat, or at night. We could not confirm this for the 2008 Fit. Check your owner's manual, and until you find more specific guidance, use general safe-driving practice instead. Slow down in rain, since wet roads reduce traction. Watch your temperature gauge and coolant level more closely in extreme heat. Chapter 16 covers overheating in detail. Use your headlights and reduce speed at night, when your view of the road is shorter.
