# Chapter 62: After the track

## What you will learn

- What to do to your car right after you finish your last session
- Why you should avoid the parking brake right after hard driving
- What to check before you drive home
- What to check once you get home, for a fuller post-event inspection

## The short version

Take a slow cool-down lap at the end of your last session, instead of braking hard and parking right away. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> Once the car has cooled, recheck your tire pressure, tread, and brake pedal feel before you drive home. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> At home, check for fresh leaks, fluid levels, pad wear, tire wear, and lug torque before you put the car back into everyday use. <!-- F-WP20-015 -->

## The full explanation

### Right after your last session

Drive one slower, gentle lap at the end of your last session, instead of braking hard and parking the car right away. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> This cool-down lap lets air move through the brakes, engine, and transmission, bringing their temperatures down gradually rather than all at once. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> Avoid using the parking brake, also called the hand brake, right after a hot session. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> A very hot brake rotor, clamped tight by the parking brake, can warp or damage the pad or rotor as it cools. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> Let the car cool with the parking brake off instead. <!-- F-WP20-014 -->

### Before you drive home

Once the car and its tires have cooled, recheck your tire pressure and tread condition before you leave for home. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> Pressures measured right after a hot session read higher than your correct driving pressure, so wait until the tires cool before trusting the number. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> A hard session can also reveal cuts, exposed cords, or uneven wear that were not there that morning. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> Check your brakes too: the pedal should still feel firm, not soft or noticeably longer in travel than it felt that morning. <!-- F-WP20-014 --> Brake fluid can boil or fade under sustained hard track use, and a spongy or long pedal is a sign that has happened. <!-- F-WP20-014 -->

### The fuller inspection, once you get home

Look under the car and around the engine bay, on clean, dry ground, for any fresh drips of oil, coolant, or brake fluid that were not there before the event. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Check that your engine oil, coolant, and brake fluid are all still at the correct levels. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> A hard day of high-RPM driving and heavy braking uses up and stresses fluids faster than ordinary street driving does. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Check the visible brake pad thickness at each wheel, and look for fresh cracks, heavy discoloration, or fluid residue near the calipers. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Pad wear is one of the fastest-consumed items on a track day, so this check matters even if the car seemed fine on the drive home. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Inspect all four tires for uneven wear, cuts, or a change in the tread surface. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Re-torque every wheel's lug nuts once the car and wheels are fully cool. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Track driving's repeated heating and cooling, combined with harder cornering loads, can work fasteners loose faster than normal driving does. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Finally, walk around the car and listen for anything that came loose — in the engine bay, under the car, or inside. <!-- F-WP20-015 --> Check trim panels, undertray fasteners, and exhaust hangers before you put the car back into everyday use. <!-- F-WP20-015 -->
