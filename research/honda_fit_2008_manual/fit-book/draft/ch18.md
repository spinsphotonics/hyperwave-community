## The short version
A recall is a free, safety-related repair that the maker must offer because a part can fail in a dangerous way. Your 2008 Fit has 8 confirmed recalls, and you should check every one against your car's own VIN. You can check for free at nhtsa.gov/recalls using your car's 17-character VIN. Do this today, and check again every so often, not only once when you buy the car.

## The full explanation

### What a recall is
A **recall** is a safety fix that a car maker must offer for free, because a part on the car can fail in a way that could hurt someone <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, or **NHTSA**, is the US government agency that tracks recalls <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. Every recall gets a **campaign number**, which is a code that identifies that exact recall <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. A **TSB**, or Technical Service Bulletin, is different from a recall <!-- F-WP02-008 -->. A TSB is a repair instruction the maker sends to dealers for a known problem that is not considered a safety defect <!-- F-WP02-008 -->. A TSB is not free unless it is still covered by your warranty <!-- F-WP02-008 -->.

### The 8 confirmed recalls for your 2008 Fit
NHTSA's own database lists 8 recall campaigns for the 2007-2008 Honda Fit <!-- F-WP02-027 -->. Before you drive a used 2008 Fit, or even your own car if you have not checked lately, confirm every one of these shows as completed, not open <!-- F-WP08-011 -->.

| Campaign number | Date recall began | Component | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10V033000 | March 19, 2010 | Power window master switch (driver's door) — water can enter through an open window and damage the switch, which can overheat or smoke <!-- F-WP02-001 --> | Dealer inspects the switch and installs a water cover. Dealer replaces the switch if it is already damaged. Free <!-- F-WP02-001 --> |
| 13V260000 | Parts ready October 18, 2013 | Power window master switch (driver's door) — a repeat of the same defect, because the 2010 fix did not fully solve it <!-- F-WP02-002 --> | Dealer replaces the power window master switch with an updated part. Free <!-- F-WP02-002 --> |
| 10V624000 | January 24, 2011 | Headlight low-beam wiring harness and combination switch — a bad connection can make low-beam headlights stop working <!-- F-WP02-003 --> | Dealer inspects the low-beam terminal and repairs it as needed. Free <!-- F-WP02-003 --> |
| 07V549000 | December 12, 2007 | Frontal airbag Occupant Detection System (ODS) wire harness — road salt tracked onto the floor can corrode this wire under the carpet. This one only applies if your car was ever sold or registered in one of 21 listed salt-belt states, or Washington, DC <!-- F-WP02-027 --> | Dealer inspects the wire harness and replaces it if needed. Free <!-- F-WP02-027 --> |
| 16V344000 | June 27, 2016 | Passenger frontal airbag inflator, made by a supplier named Takata — this covers both original and replacement inflators, and it can rupture and throw metal fragments <!-- F-WP02-028 --> | Dealer replaces the passenger airbag inflator. Free <!-- F-WP02-028 --> |
| 17V029000 | March 2017 | Passenger frontal airbag inflator — a later expansion of the same Takata defect as 16V344000 above <!-- F-WP02-029 --> | Dealer replaces the front passenger airbag inflator. Free <!-- F-WP02-029 --> |
| 19V501000 | On or about August 12, 2019 | Passenger frontal airbag inflator — this one only applies if the inflator had already been swapped once under an earlier Takata campaign <!-- F-WP02-004 --> | Dealer replaces the passenger airbag inflator again. Free <!-- F-WP02-004 --> |
| 20V770000 | NHTSA notified December 10, 2020 | Drive shaft — a coating defect can let road salt corrode the shaft until it breaks. This one only applies to manual-transmission 2008 Fits <!-- F-WP02-005 --> | Dealer inspects the drive shaft and replaces it if necessary. Free <!-- F-WP02-005 --> |

The ODS wire harness fix (07V549000) uses two related sensor systems, called ODS and OPDS <!-- F-WP02-027 -->. Together they detect a child seat or an out-of-position rider in the front passenger seat, so the airbag can be turned off for that seat when it should be <!-- F-WP02-027 -->. **Takata** was a company that made a type of airbag inflator that can explode over time as its propellant breaks down <!-- F-WP02-028 -->. That is why three separate campaigns above deal with a Takata passenger inflator <!-- F-WP02-013 -->. **Salt-belt states** are states that salt their roads heavily in winter, which speeds up rust on the underside of a car <!-- F-WP02-027 -->.

### Two look-alike recalls that do NOT apply to your car
It is easy to search for "Honda Fit recall" and find a campaign that looks like a match but is not <!-- F-WP02-006 -->. The 2009 Fit is a different car underneath a similar name <!-- F-WP02-006 -->. Campaign 18V268000 covers a passenger airbag installation error, but its own list of affected years is 2007 and 2009 through 2013 — 2008 is not on that list <!-- F-WP02-006 -->. Campaign 19V182000 covers a driver-side airbag inflator, but it names only the 2007 Fit, not the 2008 Fit <!-- F-WP02-007 -->. Your car's passenger-side inflator is still covered, through the three campaigns in the table above <!-- F-WP02-007 -->. This is why you should always check your own car's VIN, instead of only searching by year and model <!-- F-WP08-008 -->.

### One service bulletin worth knowing about
Honda Service Bulletin 14-058 extended the warranty on the **electric power steering (EPS)** system to 10 years, or 150,000 miles, whichever came first <!-- F-WP02-008 -->. It applies to 2007-2008 Fits with a stored trouble code called a **DTC**, which stands for diagnostic trouble code <!-- F-WP02-008 -->. The specific codes are DTC 32-09 or DTC 61-04 <!-- F-WP02-008 -->. The symptom is steering that feels heavier than normal, especially when the car is not moving <!-- F-WP02-008 -->. By 2026 a typical 2008 Fit is past both limits of that warranty, so this repair is now an out-of-pocket cost, not a free warranty claim <!-- F-WP08-012 -->. A second bulletin, 13-021, covers a rocker arm oil pressure switch <!-- F-WP02-009 -->. It can trigger the **MIL**, or Malfunction Indicator Lamp, which is the dashboard light most people call the check engine light <!-- F-WP02-009 -->. It applies to 2007-2011 Fits and is fixed by replacing the switch and its seal <!-- F-WP02-009 -->.

### What other 2008 Fit owners have reported to NHTSA
NHTSA keeps a public database of consumer complaints <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. As of this research, it held 217 total complaint records for the 2008 Fit <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. A single complaint can be tagged with more than one category, so the counts below add up to more than 217 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Here are the top categories, in order, with the number of complaints in each: air bags, 57 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Structure, 43 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Power train, 24 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Engine, 23 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Electrical system, 18 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Exterior lighting, 17 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Vehicle speed control, 14 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Brake-related complaints combined, 22 <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Air bags being the top category matches the recall list above <!-- F-WP02-011 -->. Three of your car's eight recalls involve airbags <!-- F-WP02-011 -->.

### How to check your own car's VIN, right now
Go to **nhtsa.gov/recalls** and use the "Search by YMM or VIN" option <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. Type in your car's full 17-character VIN and click Search <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. The VIN is printed on a small plate at the lower left corner of the windshield, visible from outside the car <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. It is also on your registration card and often your insurance card <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. NHTSA also lets you search by license plate and state instead <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. If your car has no open recalls, the tool says "0 unrepaired recalls associated with this VIN" <!-- F-WP08-008 -->. A search by year, make, and model instead of VIN gives you general recall and complaint information, not your specific car's repair status <!-- F-WP08-008 -->. Note that this VIN search shows unrepaired-recall status "for certain manufacturers," so it may not show every kind of record for every maker <!-- F-WP08-008 -->. American Honda's own owner site, owners.honda.com, also offers a VIN-based recall check, and Honda's customer service line, 1-800-999-1009, is another way to ask about recall or repair status <!-- F-WP08-009 -->. Using both the NHTSA tool and Honda's own site is a good habit, since a brand-new campaign can show up on one before the other <!-- F-WP08-009 -->. The exact page behavior on Honda's own site was not independently confirmed this pass, so treat it as a helpful backup, not your only check <!-- F-WP08-009 -->.
