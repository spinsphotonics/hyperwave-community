# Chapter 49: Tires and Wheels — Sizes, Offset, and Weight

## What you will learn

- What the numbers in a tire size and a wheel offset actually mean
- Which wheel and tire sizes owners report fitting a GD3 without rubbing
- Why a bigger wheel is not automatically a better wheel
- What autocross class a stock Fit runs in, and how tire choice connects to it

## The short version

Fitting a tire and wheel to your Fit depends on diameter, width, and offset working together, not any single number. Owners of first-generation Fits report that a 195/50R15 tire on a 15x6.5-to-7-inch wheel fits without rubbing. Bigger wheels and wider tires get progressively tighter, and they also add weight your engine has to turn, which can work against your handling goal instead of helping it.

## The full explanation

### Reading a tire size, and what "offset" means

A tire size like 195/50R15 has three parts. The 195 is the tire's width in millimeters. The 50 is the **aspect ratio**, the tire's sidewall height as a percentage of that width. The R15 means it is a radial tire built for a 15-inch wheel diameter.

**Offset** is a separate number, measured in millimeters, that describes how far the wheel's mounting face sits from its true centerline. A higher offset number pulls the tire in toward the engine and suspension. A lower offset number pushes the tire out toward the fender. Offset, tire width, and wheel diameter all work together to decide whether a tire **rubs**. A tire rubs when it touches the fender, a suspension part, or a body panel while turning or going over a bump.

### Sizes owners report fitting without rubbing

Owners report that 195/50R15 on a 15x6.5-to-7-inch wheel, at +40 to +42mm offset, is the combination that fits a stock-height GD3 without rubbing. <!-- F-WP16-001 --> A 205/45R16 tire on a 16x7-inch wheel is described as more marginal. It only works at a minimum offset of +42mm, and some owners report rear rubbing, especially with passengers in the back seat. <!-- F-WP16-001 -->

Owners also report on a 205/50R15 tire, one size wider than the 195/50R15 baseline. <!-- F-WP16-002 --> The consensus from those reports is that a 205/50R15 fits without severe rubbing. It runs tighter than the baseline size, though, and it may need the fender lip rolled back or a lower ride height to stay fully clear at full steering lock. <!-- F-WP16-002 -->

One owner who autocrossed a GD3 for four to five seasons reports a staggered setup. Up front sit 225/45R15 tires on 15x7.5-inch wheels, and the rear runs narrower 205/50R15 tires. <!-- F-WP16-003 --> A **staggered** setup means the front and rear tires are different sizes on purpose. This particular combination is reported by only one source, so treat it as one experienced owner's setup, not a confirmed community consensus. <!-- F-WP16-003 -->

### Weight: why bigger is not always better

A wheel and tire are part of your car's **unsprung weight**, the mass that your springs do not support, since it sits below them and moves with the road surface. Heavier unsprung weight generally makes a suspension react slower and feel less precise, even if the tire itself grips well. This is one reason the autocross setup above pairs a wider front tire with a narrower, lighter rear wheel. The wider front tire adds grip up front, without adding weight to the rear too. <!-- F-WP16-003 -->

Every size owners report above stays inside the 15-inch wheel diameter family. This matters because your Fit's Sport trim already used a 15-inch factory wheel. Staying at 15 inches keeps your speedometer and anti-lock brake system reading closer to correct than jumping straight to a 16-inch or 17-inch wheel would. <!-- F-WP16-006 -->

### Autocross tire sizes and classing

**Autocross** is a timed event where you drive one car at a time through a course marked with cones, usually in a parking lot. A stock, unmodified 2008 Fit runs in **H Street (HS)** class under SCCA's national Solo rules, since the rulebook's "Fit" entry does not carry a model-year restriction. <!-- F-WP16-004 --> Once you add aftermarket wheels, tires, and other Street Touring-eligible parts, your Fit typically moves into **Street Touring FWD (STF)** class instead. STF allows a wider range of parts than H Street does. <!-- F-WP16-005 -->

Combining every source found for this chapter, two sizes come up most often for GD3 autocross: 195/50R15 and 205/50R15, both usually run on 15x7-to-7.5-inch wheels. <!-- F-WP16-006 --> Chapter 58 covers autocross classing and event prep in full detail; this chapter only connects your tire choice to that bigger picture.

### A buying tip before you spend

Every size above is community-reported, meaning real owners shared it online, not a guarantee written by Honda or a tire manufacturer. Your own car's ride height, past accident history, and even factory tolerances can shift what actually clears. Before you buy a full set, ask the seller or a shop whether they have fit that exact size to a GD3 before. Check clearance on your own car at full steering lock before you commit.
