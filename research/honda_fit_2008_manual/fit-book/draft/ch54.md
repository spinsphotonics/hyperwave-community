# Chapter 54: Power Adders

## What you will learn
- What a turbocharger and a supercharger are, and how they differ from a header or an exhaust.
- The three real, GD3-specific turbo or supercharger kits researchers found.
- Why all three of those kits are hard, or impossible, to buy new today.
- What real reliability problems owners and builders report.
- What else you would likely need to change if you add real boost: clutch, fuel system, and more.

## The short version
Exactly three real, brand-named turbo or supercharger kits were ever built specifically for your GD3 Fit's L15A1 engine <!-- F-WP18-001 -->. All three are hard to buy new today; two are fully discontinued, and the third is small-batch and often out of stock <!-- F-WP18-002 -->. Adding real boost is reported to also demand a stronger clutch and a bigger fuel system, not only the turbo or supercharger kit itself <!-- F-WP18-019 -->.

## The full explanation

### Words you need first
A turbocharger is a small turbine that uses your engine's own exhaust gas to force extra air into the engine <!-- background -->. A supercharger does the same job, but it is spun by a belt connected to the engine, not by exhaust gas <!-- background -->. Both are ways to force more air in, so the engine can burn more fuel and make more power <!-- background -->. This forced air is called boost, and it is measured in psi, or pounds per square inch <!-- background -->. An intercooler is a small radiator that cools the forced air down before it enters the engine <!-- background -->. Cooler air is denser and safer for the engine <!-- background -->. A CARB EO is a California approval number that lets a part be sold as legal for street use in every state <!-- background -->. A clutch is the part that connects your engine to the transmission on a manual car <!-- background -->. A flywheel is a heavy spinning disc the clutch grabs onto <!-- background -->.

### The three real kits
KraftWerks made a bolt-on supercharger kit built specifically for the manual-transmission 2007-2008 US Fit <!-- F-WP18-001 -->. It was also sold under the related brand name Jackson Racing <!-- F-WP18-001 -->. The kit used a Rotrex C15-60 supercharger, plus Hondata engine management and an intercooler, included in the box <!-- F-WP18-001 -->. It was never built for the automatic-transmission Fit <!-- F-WP18-001 -->. Neither KraftWerks' own current shop nor Jackson Racing's own current shop lists a Fit kit today <!-- F-WP18-002 -->. This kit is discontinued <!-- F-WP18-002 -->. Used kits can sometimes be found on sites like eBay instead <!-- F-WP18-002 -->.

Two different power figures were reported for this kit, from two different points in its life <!-- F-WP18-003 -->. An early write-up, at the kit's first announcement, reported 121 whp for the "base kit" <!-- F-WP18-003 -->. A later write-up described a "High-Boost" version, reporting 164 whp and 132 lb-ft of torque at 10 psi of boost <!-- F-WP18-003 -->. Both numbers are reported by enthusiast media, not independently re-tested by this research <!-- F-WP18-003 -->.

This kit's retail price was reported at $4,495 plus shipping, from a single source <!-- F-WP18-004 -->. Reliability reports mostly cover the same Rotrex supercharger design used across other Honda models, not the Fit specifically <!-- F-WP18-004 -->. Owners of those other cars report belt and tensioner problems <!-- F-WP18-004 -->. One report describes a tensioner bolt snapping and the belt coming off during highway driving <!-- F-WP18-004 -->. Another describes belt edge wear from rubbing on the pulley <!-- F-WP18-004 -->. KraftWerks itself states it added an automatic belt tensioner specifically for the Fit kit, and reports no belt issues on it <!-- F-WP18-004 -->. That is the manufacturer's own claim, though, not an independent owner report <!-- F-WP18-004 -->.

HKS made a bolt-on turbo kit named specifically for "2007 and 2008 model year Honda Fits with a 1.5L engine" <!-- F-WP18-005 -->. This is confirmed directly by California's own CARB Executive Order record, D-186-27 <!-- F-WP18-005 -->. The kit used a Garrett T25 turbocharger, a front-mount intercooler, and HKS's own F-Con IS engine management, which plugs into the OBD-II port <!-- F-WP18-005 -->. Maximum boost was set at 6.0 psi per that same official record <!-- F-WP18-005 -->. HKS described the kit as "legal in all 50 states," based on that CARB approval <!-- F-WP18-005 -->. HKS reported roughly a 30 percent gain in peak horsepower <!-- F-WP18-006 -->. That kit is also reported discontinued today, with only used or old-stock units left to find <!-- F-WP18-006 -->.

Unequal Fabrication, a small aftermarket parts maker, currently lists a "Honda Fit GD3 (07-08) Turbo Kit" at $2,300 <!-- F-WP18-007 -->. The listing describes a bolt-on kit that keeps your air conditioning, heat, and fog lights working <!-- F-WP18-007 -->. The seller states this kit can "safely make 170hp on the stock internals with a healthy clutch" <!-- F-WP18-007 -->. That is a manufacturer claim, not an independent test <!-- F-WP18-007 -->. Separate tuning software, such as the Hondata FlashPro covered in Chapter 53, is required and not included in the price <!-- F-WP18-007 -->. The listing states the kit is "FOR OFF ROAD USE" <!-- F-WP18-007 -->. At the time of this research, the kit was shown as "Out of Stock," built to order with a variable wait time <!-- F-WP18-007 -->.

### A warning about generic listings
Unbranded turbo kits also show up for sale on sites like eBay, labeled for the "GD3" or "L15A" <!-- F-WP18-008 -->. One such listing claims a huge 485 horsepower gain <!-- F-WP18-008 -->. That number is far above every other figure found in this research and is not backed up anywhere <!-- F-WP18-008 -->. This book does not use that number, and you should not trust it either <!-- F-WP18-008 -->. A GD3-specific community thread, from experienced builders, gives a more realistic picture instead <!-- F-WP18-009 -->. They describe the stock L15A1 as able to hold about 8 psi of boost safely <!-- F-WP18-009 -->. They suggest a realistic target of roughly 160 to 200 whp on stock internals <!-- F-WP18-009 -->. One named builder blew his engine at 220 whp on a custom turbo setup <!-- F-WP18-009 -->. He then rebuilt it with a larger bore to reach about 260 whp <!-- F-WP18-009 -->. This is a single community thread, so treat it as a reported estimate, not a lab-tested fact <!-- F-WP18-009 -->.

One well-written article about a turbocharged Fit was found and read in full, then set aside <!-- F-WP18-010 -->. The car in that article used the L15A7 engine, which is the newer 2009-2014 Fit's engine, not yours <!-- F-WP18-010 -->. It was excluded from this chapter entirely, so no number from it appears anywhere above <!-- F-WP18-010 -->.

### What else you would likely need
Owners report that the stock clutch can slip once real boost is added <!-- F-WP18-020 -->. Exedy sells Sport Clutch kits built specifically for the 2007-2008 Fit in two strength levels <!-- F-WP18-019 -->. The Stage 1 kit is rated for 128 lb-ft of torque to the wheels, and costs about $454 <!-- F-WP18-019 -->. The Stage 2 kit is rated for 160 lb-ft <!-- F-WP18-019 -->. For a daily-driven, non-boosted car, though, community advice says you should not need to upgrade the stock clutch at all <!-- F-WP18-020 -->. Some race-style clutches are reported to feel "too grabby" for comfortable daily driving <!-- F-WP18-020 -->. Save the clutch upgrade for if, and only if, you actually add boost <!-- F-WP18-020 -->.

Fuel system upgrades are also reported as necessary once you add real power <!-- F-WP18-021 -->. For the KraftWerks supercharger kit specifically, owners report upgrading to larger 410cc fuel injectors at 10 psi of boost <!-- F-WP18-021 -->. Changing injector size always requires a matching ECU retune, to avoid running dangerously lean <!-- F-WP18-021 -->. For custom turbo builds, owners report that aftermarket fuel pumps, such as a Walbro or Deatschwerks pump, are commonly used instead of the stock pump <!-- F-WP18-021 -->.

No source found in this research shows the stock axles failing specifically because of added horsepower <!-- F-WP18-022 -->. This is left honestly unverified, rather than guessed at <!-- F-WP18-022 -->.

### Putting it together
Three real turbo or supercharger kits were built for your exact car, and all three are hard to buy new today <!-- F-WP18-002 -->. Reported power gains for these kits range from about 121 whp up to about 260 whp on a heavily built engine <!-- F-WP18-009 -->. Reliability problems are real and reported, mostly around belts, tensioners, and the need for a stronger clutch and fuel system <!-- F-WP18-004 -->. Treat any huge, unsupported power claim you find online, like the 485 horsepower eBay listing, with real suspicion <!-- F-WP18-008 -->.
