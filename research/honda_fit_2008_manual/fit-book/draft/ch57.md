# Chapter 57: Seats, harnesses, roll bars

## What you will learn

- What SCCA autocross rules require for seat belts and helmets in a stock Fit
- Why a stock Fit does not need a roll bar or harness for autocross
- What NASA requires for seat belts and roll bars at a track day
- Why NASA's roll bar rule depends on your car's body style, not your speed
- What happens once you move up to wheel-to-wheel racing

## The short version

A stock, closed-body Fit does not need a roll bar or an aftermarket harness for autocross or a typical track day. <!-- F-WP19-003, F-WP19-010 --> Both SCCA and NASA only require a roll bar once a car is either heavily modified or has an open top, like a convertible. <!-- F-WP19-004, F-WP19-010 --> Neither rulebook ties the roll bar requirement to how fast you drive. <!-- F-WP19-010, F-WP19-013 -->

## The full explanation

### Two organizations, two kinds of event

This chapter covers rules from two organizations. SCCA, the Sports Car Club of America, runs autocross events, called Solo. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> NASA, the National Auto Sport Association, runs High Performance Driving Events, known as HPDE, along with Time Trial and full wheel-to-wheel racing. <!-- F-WP19-007 --> An HPDE is a track day where you drive your own car on a real racetrack. An instructor rides along at first, to help you build skill rather than to race other cars. <!-- F-WP19-007 -->

A roll bar is a metal hoop or frame built into the car to protect your head in a rollover crash. <!-- F-WP19-003 --> A roll cage is a fuller, more complete version of the same idea, with multiple bars protecting more of the cabin. <!-- F-WP19-011 --> A harness is a set of racing seat belts with more attachment points than a factory belt, usually four, five, six, or seven points instead of the factory's three. <!-- F-WP19-001 -->

### SCCA autocross: what a stock Fit needs

For autocross, SCCA's current rulebook requires a lap belt in every car. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> A lap belt is the strap that crosses your hips; a shoulder belt is the strap that crosses your chest. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> Using a shoulder belt or a harness is strongly recommended, but it is not required in a closed car like your Fit. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> Your Fit's factory-installed belt system already meets this rule. <!-- F-WP19-001 -->

Here is a rule worth remembering carefully: SCCA only allows a non-factory harness in an open car. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> An open car means a convertible, or a car with its targa top or T-tops removed. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> Even then, the car also needs a roll structure that meets the rulebook's Appendix C standard. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> The top of that structure must sit at or above the top of your helmet. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> Your Fit is a closed hardtop with no factory open-top version, so this rule does not open a path to installing a racing harness in it. <!-- F-WP19-001 --> The rulebook does not let you swap a racing harness into a closed car at all. <!-- F-WP19-001 -->

Some autocrossers install a harness bar, a bar that lets you mount alternate belts, without a full roll cage. <!-- F-WP19-006 --> A harness bar is only a belt-mounting fixture. It is not a certified roll structure by itself. <!-- F-WP19-006 --> So installing a harness bar alone does not satisfy the roll-structure requirement above, and does not open the door to using a harness in a closed car either. <!-- F-WP19-006 -->

Helmets are a firmer rule. Every competitor, in every class, needs a helmet meeting one of a specific list of Snell, SFI, or FIA safety standards. <!-- F-WP19-002 --> Snell, SFI, and FIA are organizations that test and certify helmets for motorsport use, and none of them offer an exception based on speed or class. <!-- F-WP19-002 --> The chin strap must be fastened securely. <!-- F-WP19-002 --> Most cars with a normal-size windshield, like your Fit, do not need a full-face helmet, but open-wheel cars and karts do. <!-- F-WP19-002 -->

### SCCA autocross: when a roll bar becomes mandatory

A roll bar or cage is not required for a closed car running in SCCA's Street, Street Touring, Street Prepared, or Street Modified categories. <!-- F-WP19-003 --> These are the categories a base, lightly-modified Fit would run in; see Chapter 58 for exactly which class. <!-- F-WP19-003 --> A roll bar only becomes mandatory in specific, heavily-modified classes: A Modified, B Modified, C Modified, F Modified, open cars in the Prepared category, D Modified, and E Modified. <!-- F-WP19-004 --> These "Modified" and "Prepared" classes are SCCA's most heavily-built, purpose-made-for-racing categories, well beyond a car with bolt-on street parts. <!-- F-WP19-004 --> Building a car into one of these classes is a major step beyond the stock or lightly-modified Fit this book focuses on. <!-- F-WP19-004 -->

If a roll bar is added to a car in one of the more modified categories, SCCA's Appendix C sets rules for it. <!-- F-WP19-005 --> The top of the bar cannot sit below the top of the driver's helmet. <!-- F-WP19-005 --> The rulebook also suggests it extend at least 3.0 inches (76.2 mm) above the helmet. <!-- F-WP19-005 --> It must use seamless steel tubing, not a bolted-together post design, and must attach to the car's structure at two points, forming a hoop shape. <!-- F-WP19-005 -->

### NASA HPDE and Time Trial: what a stock Fit needs

For an HPDE or Time Trial day, NASA requires modern, good-condition factory seat belts, or a DOT-approved restraint system. <!-- F-WP19-007 --> DOT stands for the U.S. Department of Transportation, which sets safety standards for belts sold for street cars. <!-- F-WP19-007 --> A lap belt alone, with no shoulder strap, is not allowed. <!-- F-WP19-007 --> Your Fit's stock three-point belt already meets this rule, with no changes needed. <!-- F-WP19-007 -->

NASA also requires a helmet meeting Snell SA2010 or newer, or an equivalent standard, for both the driver and any passenger. <!-- F-WP19-009 --> Eye protection like goggles or a face shield is recommended, but is not a hard requirement the way the helmet is. <!-- F-WP19-009 -->

### The roll bar rule that surprises a lot of people

Here is the most important, and most misunderstood, rule in this chapter. NASA's roll bar requirement for HPDE and Time Trial depends entirely on whether your car is open or closed. <!-- F-WP19-010 --> It does not depend on how fast you drive, or which run group you are in. <!-- F-WP19-010 --> NASA's rulebook states that open cars, meaning convertibles or open T-tops, should have a roll bar installed. <!-- F-WP19-010 --> Nothing in NASA's rules ties a roll bar requirement to a driver's lap time or top speed. <!-- F-WP19-010 --> Your base 2008 Fit is a closed hardtop with no factory open-top version. It does not need a roll bar for any NASA HPDE or Time Trial run group, no matter how fast you get. <!-- F-WP19-010 -->

Many drivers assume that going faster, or moving into a higher run group, eventually triggers a roll bar requirement. <!-- F-WP19-013 --> That assumption is wrong for both organizations covered in this chapter. <!-- F-WP19-013 --> The real trigger is always event type and car body style, never a specific speed or lap time. <!-- F-WP19-013 -->

There is one thing that does change your equipment needs on a closed car: adding a hard-shell, fixed-back racing seat. <!-- F-WP19-008 --> NASA's rulebook says stock belts should not be worn with that kind of seat, so a car with one needs a harness meeting a specific certification standard instead. <!-- F-WP19-008 --> This does not apply to your Fit's stock seats. It only matters if you later install an aftermarket racing seat. <!-- F-WP19-008 -->

### Wheel-to-wheel racing: the one place equipment is always mandatory

Once you move beyond HPDE and Time Trial into wheel-to-wheel Competition — racing where more than one car is on the track at once, with finishing positions — the rules change completely. <!-- F-WP19-011 --> A full roll cage is required in every car, with no exception for car type or speed. <!-- F-WP19-011 --> The cage must meet detailed construction standards. It needs a continuous main hoop close to the roof, and a diagonal brace. <!-- F-WP19-011 --> Padding must cover anywhere your head or limbs could hit it. <!-- F-WP19-011 --> The cage also needs a minimum of six mounting points to the car's floor or rocker panels. <!-- F-WP19-011 --> A 5-, 6-, or 7-point harness, certified to a specific SFI or FIA standard, is also required, and that certification carries a hard expiration date with no exceptions. <!-- F-WP19-012 -->

Building a Fit to this level is a large step past autocross or an HPDE day, and beyond the scope of a first project car. <!-- F-WP19-011 --> If you ever consider it, treat this chapter as only a starting point, and work from the full current rulebook. <!-- F-WP19-011 -->
