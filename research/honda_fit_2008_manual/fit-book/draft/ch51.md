## The short version

A track-oriented brake pad, a high-boiling-point brake fluid, and stainless steel brake lines all work together to give you a firmer, more consistent pedal under repeated hard braking. This chapter presents real manufacturer data on two pad compounds and two fluid types, all confirmed to fit or apply to your GD3. Fresh, correct fluid matters as much as any pad choice, since fluid that boils turns your brake pedal soft right when you need it most.

### Brake fluid: DOT numbers and boiling points

**Brake fluid** transfers the force from your foot on the pedal to the pads or shoes at each wheel, through a sealed hydraulic system. Brake fluid absorbs a small amount of water over time, and hot brakes can heat that fluid enough to boil it. Fluid that boils turns to gas bubbles, which compress instead of pushing the pads, and your pedal goes soft or sinks to the floor.

A **DOT rating** is a US safety standard that sets a minimum boiling point for a brake fluid. **Dry boiling point** is the boiling point of fresh, unused fluid. **Wet boiling point** is the boiling point after the fluid has absorbed a set amount of water, closer to how fluid behaves after real-world use.

| Fluid | Dry boiling point | Wet boiling point |
|---|---|---|
| FMVSS 116 DOT 4, regulatory minimum | 446°F (230°C) | 311°F (155°C) |
| FMVSS 116 DOT 5.1 (non-silicone), regulatory minimum | 500°F (260°C) | 356°F (180°C) |
| Motul DOT 5.1, actual product data | 522°F (272°C) | 365°F (185°C) |
| Motul RBF 600 Factory Line, a racing DOT 4 fluid | 594°F (312°C) | 399°F (204°C) |

<!-- F-WP16-029 -->

Read that table carefully, because it teaches an important lesson. The racing-grade DOT 4 fluid at the bottom has a higher boiling point than the plain DOT 5.1 fluid above it. This is true even though 5.1 is technically the "higher" number. <!-- F-WP16-029 --> A DOT rating is a minimum standard, not a guarantee that a higher-numbered fluid always beats a lower-numbered one. <!-- F-WP16-029 --> When you shop for a track-capable fluid, compare actual boiling points on the data sheet, not only the DOT number printed on the bottle.

### Stainless steel brake lines

A **stainless steel brake line** replaces the factory rubber brake hose with a braided steel outer layer over a sealed inner tube. Rubber hoses can swell slightly under hard, repeated braking, which can make the pedal feel soft even with good pads and fluid; a stainless line resists that swelling.

Three stainless line kits were confirmed for the GD3 this pass. A StopTech kit, part 950.40017, is listed as a front kit for "Honda Fit 2007-2008." The listing found this pass marks it discontinued at that retailer, so treat it as a lead to chase down, not a guaranteed current purchase. <!-- F-WP16-031 --> A Fastline Performance kit carries the chassis code directly in its own part number, FLP-BLKIT-GD3, in addition to a "2007-08 Honda Fit" title. <!-- F-WP16-032 --> A Techna-Fit kit, part HN-2600, is listed for "2007-2008 Honda Fit" by two independent retailers. <!-- F-WP16-033 -->

Notice that the Fastline part number is the strongest fitment confirmation of the three. The manufacturer built the chassis code into the part number itself, not only into marketing text on a web page. <!-- F-WP16-032 -->
