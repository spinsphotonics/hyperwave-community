# Chapter 32: Lights and Bulbs, Wipers, Fuses

## What you will learn
- Every bulb size and type in your 2008 Fit, from headlights to the dome light
- How to replace a headlight bulb, and tail, brake, or turn signal bulbs
- What size wiper blades your Fit uses, and how to replace them
- How to find and replace a blown fuse, and why you should never use the wrong amperage
- What can go wrong during each of these jobs

## The short version

Your Fit uses a mix of halogen bulbs, meaning bulbs with a heated filament inside a gas-filled glass bulb.<!-- F-WP09-048 --> It also uses small wedge-style bulbs for everything from turn signals to the dome light.<!-- F-WP09-058 --> Replacing most exterior bulbs takes about 15 minutes and basic tools.<!-- P-WP11-18, P-WP11-19 --> Wiper blades should be checked every 6 months and replaced at the first sign of trouble.<!-- F-WP09-047 --> A blown fuse, a small safety device that breaks an electrical circuit, is one of the easiest and safest repairs in your whole car.<!-- P-WP11-21 -->

### Wiper blades

Check your wiper blades' condition at least every 6 months, per your owner's manual, and replace any blade at the first sign of cracking, hardening, or streaking.<!-- F-WP09-047 --> Your front wiper blades are two different sizes: 24 inches on the driver's side, and 16 inches on the passenger's side.<!-- F-WP09-047 --> Your rear wiper blade, on the tailgate, is 14 inches.<!-- F-WP09-047 -->

### Fuses

A fuse is a small safety device that breaks an electrical circuit on purpose if too much current tries to flow through it. This protects the wiring behind it from damage or fire. Your Fit stores some spare fuses. Amperage rating, meaning the current level a fuse position is designed to carry, is printed on the fuse box's own lid diagram and in your owner's manual.<!-- P-WP11-21 --> Never substitute a fuse with a higher amperage rating than the one that was there originally. This raises the risk of damaging your electrical system.<!-- P-WP11-21 -->

## Step by step

### Replace a headlight bulb

**Safety warnings, before you touch a bulb:**
- Do not touch the glass part of a halogen bulb with bare skin. Oil from your fingers can make it overheat and shatter when lit. Handle it by its base only.
- Always clean the glass with denatured alcohol and a clean cloth before installing it, if you accidentally touch it.
- Always let the engine cool if it was recently running, since you will reach in near the engine bay.

**Steps:**
1. To reach the driver's side bulb, start the engine, turn the wheel fully right, then turn the engine off.
2. To reach the passenger's side bulb, instead turn the wheel fully left.
3. Use a flat-tip screwdriver to remove the holding clip from the inner fender.
4. Pull the inner fender cover back to expose the back of the headlight.
5. Remove the electrical connector from the bulb by pulling it straight back.
6. Remove the rubber weather seal by pulling on its tab.
7. Unclip the end of the hold-down wire from its slot.
8. Pivot the hold-down wire out of the way and remove the old bulb.
9. Install the new bulb, holding it by its base, making sure its tabs sit in their slots.
10. Pivot the hold-down wire back into place and clip its end into the slot.
11. Check the wire's installation through the inspection window on the headlight assembly.
12. Install the rubber weather seal back over the back of the headlight assembly.
13. Push the electrical connector onto the new bulb until it is securely connected.
14. Reinstall the inner fender cover and the holding clip, pressing the clip's center to lock it.
15. Turn on the headlights to confirm the new bulb works.

**How you know it worked:** the headlight comes on and looks similarly bright and evenly aimed as the other side, and the inner fender cover and clip are back in place.

### Replace tail light and brake light bulbs

**Safety warnings, before you pry the cover:**
- Do not force the trim tool or screwdriver against painted body panels without a cloth underneath. You can scratch the paint.
- Always confirm which of the bulbs in the assembly is actually burned out before you disturb the others.

**Steps for the rear light assembly, on the tailgate side:**
1. Open the tailgate.
2. Place a cloth on the edge of the light assembly cover to protect the paint.
3. Pry the cover off carefully at the notch on its middle edge with a flat-tip screwdriver.
4. Identify which bulb is out: stop/taillight, back-up light, turn signal, or taillight.
5. Turn that bulb's socket one-quarter turn counterclockwise and remove it from the assembly.
6. Pull the burned-out bulb straight out of its socket.
7. Push the new bulb straight into the socket until it bottoms out.
8. Turn on the lights, or press the brake pedal for a stop/taillight bulb, to confirm it works.
9. Turn the socket clockwise back into the light assembly until it locks.
10. Reinstall the light assembly cover.

**Steps for the high-mount brake light, on the rear window:**
11. Open the tailgate.
12. Remove the light assembly cover by pushing in the tabs on both sides and pulling it off.
13. Turn the socket one-quarter turn counterclockwise and remove it from the assembly.
14. Pull the old bulb straight out of its socket.
15. Push the new bulb straight into the socket until it bottoms out.
16. Put the socket back into the light assembly and turn it clockwise to lock it.
17. Press the brake pedal to confirm the new bulb lights up.
18. Push the cover back onto the light assembly until it locks in place.

**How you know it worked:** the specific light you replaced turns on when tested, and both covers are fully seated and locked.

### Replace the wiper blades

**Safety warnings, before you raise a wiper arm:**
- Do not open the hood while the wiper arms are raised off the glass. The hood can hit and damage the arms.
- Always handle a raised wiper arm carefully. Do not let it snap down onto bare glass without a blade attached.

**Steps for the front wiper blades:**
1. Raise the wiper arms off the windshield, lifting the driver's side first, then the passenger's side.
2. Press and hold the lock tab on the blade assembly.
3. Slide the blade assembly toward the lock tab until it releases from the wiper arm.
4. Grasp the tabbed end of the old blade and pull firmly until it comes out of its holder.
5. If the new blade has no plastic or metal reinforcement strip, move the strip from the old blade into the new one's edge slots.
6. Make sure the new blade's rubber tabs fit into each notch of the reinforcement strip.
7. Slide the new blade into the wiper arm until it is engaged along its full length.
8. Lower the wiper arm back down onto the windshield.
9. Repeat steps 2 through 8 for the other front wiper arm.

**Steps for the rear wiper blade, if equipped:**
10. Raise the rear wiper arm off the rear glass.
11. Slide the old blade out of the wiper arm.
12. Move the reinforcement strip to the new blade if it does not already have one.
13. Slide the new blade assembly onto the wiper arm until it locks in place.
14. Lower the rear wiper arm back down against the glass.

**How you know it worked:** all wiper arms sit flat against the glass with the new blades locked in place. A test run of the wipers wipes cleanly, with no streaks, chatter, or missed spots.

### Replace a fuse

**Safety warnings, before you open a fuse box:**
- Always turn the ignition switch to LOCK (0) and turn off the headlights and every accessory before checking or changing fuses.
- Do not install a fuse with a higher rating than the circuit calls for. This greatly increases the risk of damaging the electrical system.
- Do not ignore a fuse that blows again right away with a same-rated replacement. That means there is a real electrical problem, and the vehicle should be checked by a qualified technician.

**Steps:**
1. Turn the ignition switch to the LOCK (0) position.
2. Make sure the headlights and all other accessories are off.
3. Figure out which box holds the fuse: primary under-hood, on the driver's side, interior, behind the coin tray, or secondary, on the battery.
4. Remove the cover from that fuse box.
5. Find the suspect fuse using the chart on the fuse box lid or in the manual.
6. Pull a small fuse straight out using the fuse puller from the interior box.
7. For a large fuse in an under-hood box, remove it with a Phillips screwdriver instead.
8. Look through the top of the fuse at the thin wire inside. A blown fuse shows a broken or melted wire.
9. If you are not sure which one is blown, check every fuse for that circuit, not only the first one.
10. Push in a new fuse of the exact same amperage rating.
11. Turn the ignition back on and test whether the device now works.
12. Reinstall the fuse box cover.

**How you know it worked:** the device that stopped working, such as a light, wiper, or power window, now works normally, and the new fuse does not blow again right away.

## What can go wrong

- **Buying a bulb without checking its cross-reference against your old bulb's markings.** A headlight sold as "H4" is often the same bulb as an HB2, but confirm this before you install it.<!-- F-WP09-048 -->
- **Touching a new halogen bulb's glass with bare fingers.** Oil from your skin can make it overheat and shatter when lit.
- **Guessing which rear bulb is out instead of testing each function first.** Test the brakes, turn signal, and reverse gear before you open any cover.
- **Prying a light assembly cover without a cloth underneath.** This can scratch the paint around it.
- **Forgetting to transfer the reinforcement strip into a wiper blade that ships without one.** The new blade will not sit correctly on the glass without it.
- **Letting a raised, unblocked wiper arm snap down onto bare glass.** Always support or lower an arm carefully.
- **Installing a higher-rated fuse to make it stop blowing.** This risks damaging the wiring or causing a fire.<!-- P-WP11-21 -->
- **Checking only one fuse and missing that a different one in the same circuit is the actual problem.** If your device still does not work, check every fuse on that circuit.
- **Forgetting that removing the radio fuse can lock the radio.** You may need your 5-digit code to unlock it again.
