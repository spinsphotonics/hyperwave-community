# Chapter 36: Won't Start, Won't Crank, or Cranks But Won't Start

## What you will learn
- The difference between "won't crank" and "cranks but won't start"
- A 9-step flowchart to work through when your Fit will not start
- What the immobilizer light tells you, and what to do if it blinks
- When the problem is likely the battery, and when it is likely something else
- When to try something yourself, and when to call for a tow

## The short version
Cranking means the starter is spinning the engine over; starting means the engine actually fires and keeps running on its own. If the dashboard is completely dark, check the battery first. If the dash lights up but the engine will not crank, check your clutch pedal or shift position and the immobilizer light next. If the engine cranks normally but will not fire, think about flooding, fuel, or ignition.

## The full explanation

### Two different problems that feel similar

"My car won't start" can mean two very different things, and telling them apart is the fastest way to find the cause.

**Won't crank** means when you turn the key to START, nothing happens, or you hear only a click or a slow grinding sound. The starter, the motor that spins the engine to get it running, is not turning the engine over at a normal speed.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Cranks but won't start** means the starter spins the engine at a normal speed, but the engine never fires up and keeps running on its own.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

This chapter's flowchart below asks you a short question at each step, then tells you exactly where to go next depending on your answer. No step sends you down more than 3 possible paths.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

### The immobilizer

Your Fit has an immobilizer, an anti-theft system built into the key. The key has a chip inside it that the car's computer must recognize before the engine will start.<!-- F-WP04-029 --> A key-shaped light on the dashboard shows you what the immobilizer is doing. Step 3 of the flowchart below tells you what to watch for.

## Step by step

### The 9-step flowchart

Work through these steps in order. Each step tells you exactly which step to go to next, based on what you find.

**Step 1.** Turn the key to ON (the second position) without starting the engine. Do the dashboard warning lights and gauges light up normally?
- If **yes**, go to Step 2.
- If **no**, meaning the dashboard is completely dark, go to Step 8.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 2.** Check your starting position. On a manual transmission, is the clutch pedal pressed all the way to the floor? On an automatic, is the shift lever fully in Park or Neutral, with the brake pedal pressed? The starter will not engage at all unless this is correct.<!-- F-WP04-002 --><!-- F-WP04-003 -->
- If this is correct and the car still will not start, go to Step 3.
- If it was **not** correct, fix it and try again. If it now starts, you are done.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 3.** Turn the key to ON and watch the key-shaped immobilizer light. Does it flash, or stay lit, instead of going out normally?
- If **yes**, go to Step 4.
- If **no**, go to Step 5.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 4.** Try your spare key.
- If the spare key **starts** the car, your primary key or its chip is likely damaged. Stop here and have the primary key replaced by a dealer.
- If the spare key **also fails** to start the car, the immobilizer system itself may be at fault. Stop here and see a dealer or a qualified shop.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 5.** Turn the key to START. Does the engine crank, meaning turn over or spin, at a normal speed?
- If **yes**, go to Step 6.
- If it makes **no sound** and does not move at all, go to Step 8.
- If you hear a **single click**, or a slow grinding sound and the engine barely turns, go to Step 8.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 6.** The engine cranks normally but does not fire. Do you smell strong raw gasoline, or did you pump the gas pedal several times before trying to start?
- If **yes**, this may be a flooded engine, meaning too much fuel has reached it. Wait a few minutes without touching the pedal, then try again without pumping the pedal. If it still will not fire, go to Step 7.
- If **no**, go to Step 7.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 7.** Check the fuel gauge. Is the tank empty or very close to empty?
- If **yes**, add fuel and try again.
- If **no**, this points to a fuel-delivery or ignition problem. It could be the fuel pump, a fuse, the spark plugs, or the ignition coils. This needs a scan tool and a shop visit, so stop here and arrange service.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 8.** Check the battery terminals, the metal connection points on top of the battery. Are they clean, tight, and free of white or blue corrosion?
- If **no**, clean and tighten them, then go back to Step 1.
- If **yes**, go to Step 9.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**Step 9.** Jump-start the car using the terminal order already covered in Chapter 16. Connect positive to positive on both batteries first. Then connect negative on the booster battery, then the unpainted metal ground point on your car.<!-- F-WP07-012 --> Jump-starting has real risks, including sparks near battery gases. Follow every safety warning in Chapter 16 before you connect a single cable.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
- Does the car **start**? If yes, drive for at least 20 to 30 minutes to recharge the battery, then have the battery and charging system tested soon.
- If **no**, the starter, the alternator, or a deeper electrical fault is likely. Stop here and arrange a tow to a shop.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->

**How you know it worked:** the engine starts and keeps running on its own. The dashboard lights behave normally, and the immobilizer light goes out after its brief flash at key-ON.

## What can go wrong
- **Skipping Step 2.** Many "won't start" calls turn out to be a clutch pedal not fully pressed, or a shifter not fully in Park. Always check this before assuming a bigger problem.<!-- F-WP04-002 --><!-- F-WP04-003 -->
- **Missing the immobilizer light at Step 3.** A blinking key light means the chip in your key was not recognized. Trying to force a start without checking this wastes time.<!-- F-WP04-029 -->
- **Continuing to pump the gas pedal after a possible flooded engine.** This adds more fuel and makes flooding worse, not better.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->
- **Jumping straight to a jump-start before checking the battery terminals.** Dirty or loose terminals are checked at Step 8. That is an easy first fix that a jump-start alone would not solve.<!-- WP-12 Section G -->
- **Connecting jumper cables in the wrong order, or to the wrong point.** Follow the exact order in Step 9 and in Chapter 16 to avoid sparks near the battery.<!-- F-WP07-012 --><!-- F-WP07-014 -->
