# Chapter 56: Weight reduction and aerodynamics

## What you will learn

- Which parts owners commonly remove from a base 2008 Fit to save weight
- How much weight the spare tire, its cover, and the jack system add up to
- How much weight the air conditioning system adds
- Why SCCA's Street category limits what you can remove
- Why aerodynamic parts are a thin topic for this specific car

## The short version

Removing the spare tire, its cover, and the jack and tools together saves you roughly 30 to 35 pounds. <!-- F-WP19-017 --> Removing the air conditioning system saves about 23.46 pounds more. <!-- F-WP19-018 --> Aerodynamic parts like spoilers and splitters exist for this car, but they rarely help much at Fit speeds, and SCCA's Street category will not let you use them anyway. <!-- F-WP19-024, F-WP19-025 -->

## The full explanation

### Why weight matters

A lighter car turns, stops, and accelerates better with the same engine. <!-- F-WP19-022 --> Removing weight is one of the cheapest ways to make a stock Fit quicker at autocross or a track day. <!-- F-WP19-022 --> Autocross is a timed driving event where you drive one car at a time through a course marked with cones. <!-- F-WP19-024 --> SCCA stands for the Sports Car Club of America. It is a group that runs autocross and racing events and writes the rulebooks for them. <!-- F-WP19-024 -->

### The spare tire system

In this book, the "spare tire system" means the spare tire itself, its cover, and the jack and tools that go with it.

Community-reported figures found for a first-generation Fit like yours put the spare tire itself at about 21 pounds (9,525 g). <!-- F-WP19-014 --> The fabric cover some Fits carry over the spare weighs about 7.76 pounds (3,520 g). <!-- F-WP19-015 --> The jack, wrench, tow hook, and mounting bracket together weigh about 4.91 pounds (2,227 g). <!-- F-WP19-016 --> Add those three together and you get roughly 30 to 35 pounds. <!-- F-WP19-017 --> These numbers come from a single online community source, so treat them as a good estimate, not an exact figure. <!-- F-WP19-017 -->

Removing the spare tire system is easy and reversible. You can put it back before you drive home. But driving without a spare means you have no backup if you get a flat tire on the way to or from the event. Plan around that risk. <!-- F-WP19-017 -->

### The air conditioning system

Owners report that removing the entire air conditioning system — the condenser, compressor, refrigerant lines, brackets, and belt pulley — saves about 23.46 pounds (10,645 g). <!-- F-WP19-018 --> This job also frees up a small amount of engine power, since the compressor no longer drags on the engine's belt. <!-- F-WP19-018 --> This is a bigger job than pulling the spare tire, and it is usually a track-only or Street-Prepared-category modification, not something you do for a single event. <!-- F-WP19-022 -->

### Sound deadening and rear seats

Owners report removing sound-deadening material — the tar-like padding under the carpet that quiets road noise — for further weight savings. <!-- F-WP19-019 --> One community source lists about 11 pounds of sound deadening found so far, but the source itself calls this tally incomplete. <!-- F-WP19-019 --> Sound deadening is a modest weight item on this car compared to the seats or the air conditioning system. <!-- F-WP19-019 -->

Rear seat weight is a different story. We could not confirm this for the 2008 Fit. Check your owner's manual. <!-- F-WP19-020 --> A number exists for the newer, second-generation Fit's rear seat, but that is a different car, so this book will not print it as if it applied to yours. <!-- F-WP19-020 --> The Fit's fold-flat Magic Seat design — Honda's name for rear seats that fold down flat to open up cargo space — makes removing the rear seat an easy, reversible job. <!-- F-WP19-020 --> That is likely why owners still do it, even without a confirmed weight figure. <!-- F-WP19-020 -->

### The realistic number, and the extreme number

One community source describes a full "race trim" build that removes about 534.67 pounds (242,525 g) total. <!-- F-WP19-021 --> That number assumes gutting most of the interior, fitting lighter wheels and tires, a lightweight flywheel and battery, and a dedicated racing seat. <!-- F-WP19-021 --> That is not a realistic goal for a typical weekend autocrosser. <!-- F-WP19-021 -->

A more honest number keeps the car street-legal and Street-category-legal. <!-- F-WP19-022 --> Combining the spare tire system and the air conditioning system saves about 55 to 60 pounds. <!-- F-WP19-022 --> SCCA's Street category rule requires the car to run "as specified by the manufacturer with only standard equipment." <!-- F-WP19-022 --> That means a Street-classed Fit cannot legally remove its rear seats, sound deadening, or air conditioning at all. <!-- F-WP19-022 --> Owners who want to remove more have to move up into Street Prepared, Street Modified, Prepared, or Modified category first. <!-- F-WP19-022 -->

### Aerodynamics: an honest, thin topic

Aerodynamic parts use the shape of moving air to push a car down onto the road, which can help it grip better in fast corners. <!-- F-WP19-025 --> For the base 2008 Fit, this is honestly a thin topic, for three reasons. <!-- F-WP19-025 -->

First, only a few parts are made specifically for this exact chassis. <!-- F-WP19-023 --> Rear spoilers are the most common part sold for a GD3 Fit, and most of them are small, styling-focused pieces rather than parts built to add real downforce. <!-- F-WP19-023 --> Front and side splitters — panels that redirect air under the front of the car — are also sold specifically for the GD3 chassis, by at least two retailers. <!-- F-WP19-023 --> Canards, small wing-like fins added to the front bumper, are the thinnest category. No canard was found molded specifically for this car's bumper shape; the parts sold are generic pieces trimmed to fit. <!-- F-WP19-023 -->

Second, SCCA's Street category will not let you use most of these parts anyway. <!-- F-WP19-024 --> The rulebook does not list splitters, canards, or non-factory wings as allowed Street-category modifications. <!-- F-WP19-024 --> You would need to move up to Street Prepared, Street Modified, Prepared, or Modified category before you could legally add them. <!-- F-WP19-024 -->

Third, aerodynamic parts work best at higher speeds, because the extra grip they create grows with the square of your speed. <!-- F-WP19-025 --> A Fit at typical street or track-day speeds is well below the speed range where a splitter or wing meaningfully outweighs the extra weight and drag it adds. <!-- F-WP19-025 --> Treat a rear spoiler as a styling choice, and a splitter as a niche option for a higher-category or track-only build, not a priority modification for this car. <!-- F-WP19-025 -->

### A general checklist, beyond the specific numbers

Beyond the confirmed figures above, owners of small economy hatchbacks commonly remove other items too. <!-- F-WP19-026 --> These include the factory first-aid or warning-triangle kit, any aftermarket subwoofer or amplifier, and floor mats. <!-- F-WP19-026 --> They also empty the trunk of anything not needed that day. <!-- F-WP19-026 --> These items do not have specific weight figures behind them in this book; they are general community practice, not measured facts. <!-- F-WP19-026 -->
