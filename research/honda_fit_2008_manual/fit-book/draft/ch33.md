# Chapter 33: Keeping It Clean Inside and Out

## What you will learn
- How to wash and wax your 2008 Fit's exterior without damaging the paint or wheels
- Why you should remove the rear antenna before a drive-through car wash
- How to clean your seat belts safely, and why they need to fully air-dry
- How to clean the interior and keep your floor mats from becoming a safety problem

## The full explanation

### Washing and waxing the exterior

A clean, well-waxed exterior protects your Fit's paint from the sun, road salt, and everyday dirt. Wax, or a sealant, is a protective coating you apply on top of clean paint; it helps water bead up and roll off instead of sitting on the surface. Your Fit's manual specifically warns against using a drive-through car wash without first removing the rear roof antenna by hand, since the wash's spinning brushes can damage it.<!-- P-WP11-23 --> Reinstall the antenna once you are done.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->

Your Fit's wheels are aluminum alloy, meaning a lightweight metal mix, and they have a protective clear-coat finish. Avoid harsh wheel cleaners or a stiff brush on them; these can damage that clear-coat.<!-- P-WP11-23 --> Use a car-wash soap made for vehicles, not dish soap, since dish soap can strip away wax.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->

### Cleaning seat belts and the interior

Your seat belts should never be cleaned with bleach, dye, or cleaning solvents.<!-- P-WP11-23 --> If they get dirty, clean them with a soft brush and mild soap and water instead, and let them air-dry fully before you use the car again.<!-- P-WP11-23 --> A damp seat belt that gets rolled back up into its retractor can stay damp for a long time, so give it time to dry completely first.

When you clean the rest of the interior, vacuum the seats, carpet, and floor.<!-- P-WP11-23 --> Wipe hard surfaces, like the dashboard and door panels, with a mild cleaner and a soft cloth.<!-- P-WP11-23 --> Take out your floor mats and clean them separately.<!-- P-WP11-23 --> Once everything is completely dry, put the mats back and re-anchor them into their factory anchor points. A loose mat can slide forward and interfere with the pedals, or with a seat's weight sensor, which is a real safety issue, not only a tidiness one.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->

## Step by step

### Wash and wax the exterior, and clean the interior

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Do not use a drive-through car wash without first unscrewing and removing the rear roof antenna by hand. The brushes can damage it.
- Do not use bleach, dye, or cleaning solvents on the seat belts.
- Always let seat belts air-dry fully before using the vehicle again.
- Do not clean aluminum alloy wheels with harsh chemicals or a stiff brush. It can damage their protective clear-coat.

**Steps for the exterior wash and wax:**
1. If you plan to use a drive-through wash, unscrew and remove the rear antenna by hand first.
2. Rinse the whole car with plain water to knock off loose dirt.
3. Wash a small section at a time with car-wash soap and a wash mitt, then rinse that section.
4. Clean the wheels separately with a mild detergent and a soft brush or sponge, avoiding harsh wheel cleaners on alloy wheels.
5. Dry the car with a clean microfiber towel before water spots form.
6. Apply wax or sealant in small sections, following the product's instructions.
7. Buff each section with a clean, dry microfiber towel.
8. Reinstall the antenna if you removed it.

**Steps for the interior:**
9. Remove the floor mats and shake or vacuum them separately.
10. Vacuum the seats, carpet, and floor, using an attachment to reach corners.
11. Wipe the dashboard, door panels, and other hard surfaces with a mild cleaner and a soft cloth.
12. Wipe the insides of the seat belt loops with a cloth dampened in mild soap water or isopropyl alcohol.
13. If the seat belts themselves are dirty, clean them with a soft brush and mild soap and water, then let them air-dry fully.
14. Make sure carpeting and floor mats are completely dry before you put the floor mats back.
15. Re-anchor the floor mats onto their factory anchor points. A loose mat can interfere with the pedals or a seat sensor.

**How you know it worked:** the exterior is clean with an even, beaded look where waxed, the wheels are clean without dulled or damaged clear-coat, the interior looks and smells clean, and every floor mat is dry and re-anchored in place.

## What can go wrong

- **Washing the car in direct hot sun.** This makes soap and water dry into spots before you can rinse it off. Work in shade if you can.
- **Using a stiff brush or strong wheel cleaner on the aluminum alloy wheels.** This can damage their protective clear-coat finish.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->
- **Skipping the antenna removal before a drive-through wash.** The spinning brushes can bend or break it.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->
- **Cleaning seat belts with bleach or a solvent-based cleaner.** This can weaken the belt webbing itself.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->
- **Putting damp floor mats back down, or not re-anchoring them.** This can interfere with the pedals or a seat's weight sensor, which is a genuine safety concern.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->
- **Using dish soap instead of car-wash soap.** Dish soap can strip away any wax already on the paint.<!-- P-WP11-23 -->
