# Chapter 23: Safety First — Jacking, Jack Stands, Wheel Chocks, Hot Parts, Fluids

## What you will learn

- The one rule that matters most in this whole chapter: never trust a jack alone
- How to jack up your Fit and support it safely on jack stands
- Why hot parts under the hood and under the car can burn you
- How to handle engine oil, brake fluid, and other fluids without hurting yourself or your car
- What can go wrong when someone skips a safety step

## The short version

A jack lifts your car, but a jack can slip or roll off its point without warning. Jack stands, wedges you place under the car once it is up, are what actually holds the car's weight while you work underneath it. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> Every job in Chapters 24 through 27 that lifts the car depends on you doing this one step correctly.

## The full explanation

### Why a jack alone is not enough

A **jack** is a tool that lifts one corner or end of a car. A **jack stand** is a separate metal support you slide underneath once the car is up, so its weight rests on solid steel instead of the jack. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> The owner's manual itself warns that a car can easily roll off a jack and seriously injure anyone underneath it. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> That is why every maintenance job in this book that needs the car off the ground also needs jack stands, not the jack by itself.

Your Fit's trunk holds a small scissor jack, meant only for changing a flat tire on the side of the road. A **floor jack** is a separate, larger tool on wheels, rated to hold much more weight, and it is what you use for jack-stand work in the garage. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> Buy a floor jack and jack stands that are each rated for at least your car's weight. Do this before you start any job in Chapters 24 through 27 that needs the car raised. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->

### Where to lift, and where never to lift

Your owner's manual shows a molded mark on the underbody near each wheel. This is the lift point built for the small scissor jack. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> For jack-stand work, there are also reinforced seams along each rocker panel (the body panel below the doors) and along the front subframe, set well back from the bumper. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> These reinforced seam points come from community reports, not from the owner's manual itself, so check them against your own car and a second source before you rely on them. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->

Never set a jack or a jack stand under the floor pan, the exhaust, a plastic under-cover, or a random point on the frame. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> Any of these can bend, crush, or puncture under the car's weight.

### Hot parts, fluids, and other everyday hazards

Beyond jacking, a few hazards come up again and again in Chapters 24 through 27. An engine, its exhaust, and its brakes all get hot enough to burn skin, so let them cool before you touch anything nearby. <!-- P-WP11-1, P-WP11-6, P-WP11-2 --> Used and fresh engine oil can irritate your skin and eyes, so wear gloves and eye protection whenever you handle it. <!-- P-WP11-1 --> Brake fluid splashes easily, damages paint on contact, and hurts eyes, so eye protection matters there too. <!-- P-WP11-9 --> Brake dust should never be blown around with compressed air or brushed dry; use a brake-parts cleaner spray instead, and never breathe it in. <!-- P-WP11-7, P-WP11-8 -->

Used oil is hazardous to the environment. Never pour it in the trash or on the ground; take it to a recycling center in a sealed container. <!-- P-WP11-1 -->

## Step by step: Jack up the car and set it on jack stands

**Safety warnings, before you touch the jack:**
- Do not ever get any part of your body under a car supported only by a jack. The car can roll off a jack and seriously injure anyone underneath. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
- Always work on a flat, level, hard surface. Never jack up a car on a slope, on soft ground, or on an incline. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
- Always set the parking brake and chock the wheels that will stay on the ground before you start jacking. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
- Always use the vehicle's marked or reinforced jacking points, never the floor pan, exhaust, plastic under-covers, or a random point on the frame. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
- Do not exceed a jack's or a jack stand's rated weight capacity. Check the rating sticker before you buy or borrow one. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
- Always give the car a firm shake test on the stands before working underneath, to confirm it will not slip. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->

**Steps:**
1. Park on a flat, level, solid surface and set the parking brake. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
2. Chock the wheels that will remain on the ground. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
3. Locate the lift point: the owner's manual shows a molded mark on the underbody near each wheel, for the factory scissor jack. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
4. For jack-stand work, also find the reinforced pinch-weld seams on each rocker panel and the front subframe, well back from the bumper. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
5. Treat step 4's points with caution: they are community-reported, not from the owner's manual, so double-check against your own car and a second reference. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
6. Position the floor jack under the chosen point and raise the car slowly. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
7. Stop as soon as the tire lifts a couple of inches and check that the jack is still centered on the point. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
8. Continue raising to the height you need. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
9. Slide a jack stand under a solid point near the jack and adjust it to the right height. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
10. Slowly lower the floor jack until the car's weight rests on the jack stand. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
11. Repeat steps 6 through 10 for the other end or side, if you are lifting the whole car. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
12. Give the car a firm push and shake test before you get near or under it. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
13. Leave the floor jack nearby as a backup, but do not rely on it to hold weight once the stands are set. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
14. When done, reverse the process: raise slightly with the floor jack, remove the stands, then lower slowly and fully. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->

**How you know it worked:** the car sits solidly on the stands with no rocking when pushed. All four (or two) contact points look evenly loaded. The wheels you did not lift stay chocked and on the ground the whole time. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->

## What can go wrong

The most common mistake is placing a jack or a jack stand on the floor pan, a plastic cover, or the exhaust instead of a reinforced point. That mistake can bend the body or crush the part. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> The second most common, and most dangerous, mistake is working under a car held up by the floor jack alone, with no stands underneath. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> Jacking on a slope or soft ground can let the car shift, or let the jack sink into the ground while you work. <!-- P-WP11-22 --> Forgetting to chock the wheels that stay on the ground can let the whole car roll while one corner is in the air. <!-- P-WP11-22 -->
