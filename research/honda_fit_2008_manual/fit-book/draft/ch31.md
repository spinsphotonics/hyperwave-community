## The full explanation

### Timing chain, not timing belt

Many cars need their timing belt, a rubber belt that keeps the engine's valves and pistons moving in sync, replaced on a strict mileage schedule. A skipped or broken timing belt can be a serious, sudden problem. Your Fit's engine does not have one of these belts at all. It uses a timing chain instead, a metal chain that does the same job.<!-- F-WP09-044 --> Your Fit's own maintenance item table lists spark plug replacement and valve clearance inspection as scheduled jobs. It has no line at all for replacing a timing belt or timing chain.<!-- F-WP09-044 --> Honda treats the timing chain as a part that lasts the life of the engine under normal use, with no scheduled replacement interval. It only needs service if a specific problem comes up. That could be unusual noise, chain stretch, meaning the chain has worn and lengthened slightly, or the engine's timing actually skipping.<!-- F-WP09-044 -->

This is good news for your maintenance budget. Unlike a timing belt on many other cars, there is no expensive, scheduled chain replacement waiting for you at some fixed mileage. It is also a reason to pay attention if your engine ever sounds different or runs roughly. A timing chain problem is not something your maintenance schedule will warn you about ahead of time.

### The drive belt

Your Fit has one drive belt, which spins accessories such as the alternator, the part that recharges your battery while the engine runs. Inspecting and adjusting this belt is a Maintenance Minder sub item, meaning the dashboard's own maintenance computer decides when it is due, rather than a fixed mileage printed in your manual.<!-- F-WP09-007 --> This book's sources do not include a do-it-yourself procedure card for inspecting or adjusting this belt, so a technician should check it as part of your regular Maintenance Minder service visits.

Check your owner's manual, or ask your technician to look over your coolant hoses at your next service visit, since this book does not have a confirmed, Fit-specific answer for that particular item.

## What can go wrong

- **Assuming your Fit needs a timing belt change at some mileage, the way many other cars do.** Your engine has a timing chain, not a belt, and Honda gives it no scheduled replacement interval.<!-- F-WP09-044 -->
- **Ignoring an unusual engine noise because "the timing chain doesn't need service."** A timing chain is only serviced when a problem shows up, such as noise, stretch, or a timing skip, so unusual noise is exactly the sign to have it checked.<!-- F-WP09-044 -->
- **Never having the drive belt inspected because the dashboard has not flagged it.** The Maintenance Minder decides the timing, but it still relies on your regular service visits to actually happen.<!-- F-WP09-007 -->
- **Assuming this chapter covers a hose-replacement schedule that this book could not confirm.** Check your owner's manual, or ask your technician, since this chapter is honest about that gap rather than guessing at a number.
