# Chapter 17: Title, Registration, Insurance, Inspection, and Emissions Basics

## What you will learn
- What a **title** is, and why you should never leave it in the glovebox.
- What paperwork you should get when you buy from a dealer.
- What paperwork you should get when you buy from a private person.
- What the federal **Buyers Guide** sticker must tell you at a used car lot.
- Why registration, insurance, and inspection rules are different in every state, and where to check yours.

## The short version
A title is the legal paper that proves who owns a car, and it is the most important paper you will ever get with it. When you buy a car, you should also get a bill of sale and a written record of the mileage. Registration, insurance, and inspection rules are set by each state, so always check your state DMV's website for the exact steps.

## The full explanation

### Buying from a dealer
A **title** is the legal document that proves who owns a car <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. If you buy a used 2008 Fit from a licensed dealer, you should receive five things at the sale <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. First, the title, or proof the dealer can transfer it to you <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. Second, a **bill of sale**, which is the dealer's own sales contract <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. It shows the price, the taxes, and the fees <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. Third, a **Buyers Guide** window sticker, which becomes your copy once you buy the car <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. Fourth, an **odometer disclosure statement**, which is the mileage reading, signed by the seller <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. Federal law requires this statement for most cars under 20 model years old at the time of sale <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. Fifth, a temporary plate, or a finished application to title and register the car <!-- F-WP08-001 -->. This lets you drive it home legally while you wait for permanent plates <!-- F-WP08-001 -->.

### Buying from a private person
Buying from a private seller works a little differently <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. You should still get the title, but the seller must sign it over to you <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. The seller's name on the title must match their name exactly <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. If it does not match, you will need extra paperwork. A chain of title is a paper trail showing each past owner in order. A power of attorney is a signed paper letting one person sign for another <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. You should also get a bill of sale with both people's names, addresses, and signatures <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. It should list the car's year, make, and **VIN** <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. A VIN is the 17-character code that identifies one specific vehicle; you will use it again in Chapter 18 <!-- F-WP08-007 -->. You need a written record of the mileage at the time of sale too <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. Some states also want a release-of-liability form <!-- F-WP08-002 -->. This form tells the DMV that the seller is no longer responsible for the car after the sale <!-- F-WP08-002 -->.

### Why this book cannot give you exact state rules
Every state runs its own registration, insurance, and inspection system <!-- F-WP08-003 -->. Some states require a safety inspection or an emissions test <!-- F-WP08-003 -->. Some states require neither one <!-- F-WP08-003 -->. Some states want a notarized signature on the title, and some do not <!-- F-WP08-003 -->. Because these rules do not match from state to state, and because they can change, this book cannot list every one correctly and keep it current <!-- F-WP08-003 -->. Check your state DMV's website for the fees, deadlines, and inspection rules where you live <!-- F-WP08-003 -->.

### The Buyers Guide sticker
The Federal Trade Commission's Used Car Rule forces dealers to post a Buyers Guide sticker on every used car <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. Private sellers do not have to post one <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. The dealer must put the sticker in the window before you are even allowed to inspect the car for buying <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. The sticker must say whether the dealer offers a warranty <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. If it does, the sticker must state how long the warranty lasts, what share of repair costs the dealer pays, and which systems it covers <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. It must list the car's 14 major systems and the kinds of defects that can happen in them <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. It must suggest asking the dealer if you can get the car inspected by an independent mechanic first <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. It must warn you not to trust spoken promises that are not written down <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. Since a 2018 update, it must also recommend getting a vehicle history report <!-- F-WP08-004 -->. The dealer must hand you this same sticker as your copy when you buy the car <!-- F-WP08-004 -->.
