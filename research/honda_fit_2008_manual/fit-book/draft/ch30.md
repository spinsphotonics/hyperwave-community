# Chapter 30: Coolant, Transmission Fluid, Brake Fluid, Power Steering, and Washer Fluid

## What you will learn
- What coolant type your 2008 Fit needs, and how much it holds
- How to check the coolant level and top it up, and how to do a full drain and fill
- What transmission fluid your car needs, and how to check and change it for your transmission type
- What brake and clutch fluid your Fit uses, and the one fixed rule that always applies
- Why this car has no power steering fluid to check at all
- What washer fluid to use, and what never to put in that reservoir

## The short version

Your Fit uses five separate fluids under the hood, besides engine oil: coolant, transmission fluid, brake fluid, clutch fluid on manual-transmission cars, and washer fluid.<!-- F-WP09-011, F-WP09-008, F-WP09-013, F-WP09-033, F-WP09-035 --> There is no power steering fluid on this car at all, because your Fit steers with an electric motor instead of hydraulic pressure.<!-- F-WP09-034 --> Each fluid has its own correct type; using the wrong one can cause real damage, so this chapter tells you exactly which one goes where.

## The full explanation

### Engine coolant

Coolant is the liquid that flows through your engine and radiator to carry heat away and keep the engine from overheating. Your Fit's owner's manual calls for Honda Long-Life Antifreeze/Coolant Type 2, which comes pre-mixed at 50 percent antifreeze and 50 percent water.<!-- F-WP09-020 --> Never add straight, undiluted antifreeze or plain water on its own.<!-- F-WP09-020 --> If Honda's coolant is not available, another major-brand coolant made for aluminum engines, and free of silicates, can be used temporarily. Flush the system and refill it with Honda coolant as soon as you reasonably can.<!-- F-WP09-020 -->

Replacing the coolant entirely is a Maintenance Minder sub item, so the dashboard display decides when it is due; no fixed mileage number is printed.<!-- F-WP09-011 --> The system's capacity depends on which transmission your car has. After a coolant change, a manual-transmission Fit holds 3.8 liters (1.00 US gallon), and an automatic-transmission Fit holds 3.7 liters (0.98 US gallon).<!-- F-WP09-021, F-WP09-022 --> After a full engine overhaul, the total capacity is 5.4 liters (1.43 US gallons) for a manual, or 5.3 liters (1.40 US gallons) for an automatic.<!-- F-WP09-023, F-WP09-024 --> The separate reserve tank, the see-through plastic tank you check day to day, holds 0.4 liters (0.11 US gallon) on its own.<!-- F-WP09-025 -->

### Transmission fluid

Transmission fluid keeps your gearbox's internal parts lubricated and, on an automatic, also carries the hydraulic pressure that shifts gears. Replacing it is a Maintenance Minder sub item on both transmission types, so the dashboard decides normal timing.<!-- F-WP09-008 --> Two fixed exceptions apply only to automatic-transmission Fits. If you regularly drive at low speed in mountains, change the fluid at 60,000 miles (100,000 kilometers), then every 30,000 miles (48,000 kilometers) after that.<!-- F-WP09-008 --> If you tow the car behind a motorhome, change it every 2 years or 30,000 miles (48,000 kilometers), whichever comes first.<!-- F-WP09-008 -->

If you have a manual transmission, your Fit uses Honda Manual Transmission Fluid (MTF).<!-- F-WP09-026 --> If it is not available, an SAE 10W-30 or 10W-40 motor oil carrying the API Certification Seal for gasoline engines works as a temporary substitute. Switch back to Honda MTF soon, since plain motor oil lacks the additives your gearbox needs and can make shifting stiffer over time.<!-- F-WP09-026 --> The **differential** is the gearbox part that splits engine power between your front wheels. A change takes about 1.6 US quarts (1.5 liters); the total amount, including the differential, is 1.7 US quarts (1.6 liters).<!-- F-WP09-027, F-WP09-028 -->

If you have an automatic transmission, your Fit uses Honda Genuine ATF-Z1 only. Mixing in another brand's automatic transmission fluid (ATF) can wear down your transmission, and Honda's own warranty does not cover damage caused that way.<!-- F-WP09-029 --> A drain-and-refill takes about 2.5 US quarts (2.4 liters); the total system capacity, only reached during a full teardown, is about 6.3 US quarts (6.0 liters).<!-- F-WP09-030, F-WP09-031 --> One note for buying fluid today: Honda's ATF-Z1 has since been discontinued across the industry and replaced by Honda ATF DW-1 for service fills. Ask your parts store for the replacement if the original is not on the shelf.<!-- F-WP09-029 -->

### Brake fluid and clutch fluid

Brake fluid transmits the force from your foot on the brake pedal to the brakes at each wheel. Your Fit's manual calls for Honda Heavy Duty Brake Fluid DOT 3, or a DOT 3 or DOT 4 fluid from a sealed container as a temporary replacement.<!-- F-WP09-032 --> Never use fluid marked DOT 5; it is not compatible with this car's brake system and can cause serious damage.<!-- F-WP09-032 -->

One rule for brake fluid always applies, no matter what the Maintenance Minder display shows: replace it every 3 years.<!-- F-WP09-013 --> This is one of the small set of fixed-schedule items in your whole maintenance schedule, alongside the annual oil change and the 160,000-mile idle speed check covered in Chapter 21.

If your Fit has a manual transmission, it also uses a hydraulic clutch, meaning fluid pressure, not a cable, disengages the clutch when you press the pedal. This clutch fluid shares the exact same specification as the brake fluid above.<!-- F-WP09-033 --> Check that its level sits between the MIN and MAX marks on the reservoir, which has a gray cap. A low level can point to a leak in the clutch hydraulic system.<!-- F-WP09-033 -->

We could not confirm a step-by-step, do-it-yourself procedure for replacing brake or clutch fluid from this book's sources at this pass. Check Chapter 27 for more on your braking system, and Chapter 40 for a full brake job, or have a qualified shop replace this fluid on schedule.

### Power steering: this car does not have hydraulic fluid to check

Power steering is the system that reduces how much effort it takes to turn your steering wheel. Many older cars use a hydraulic pump and a reservoir of power steering fluid for this. Your 2008 Fit does not. It uses Electric Power-Assisted Rack-and-Pinion Steering (EPS) instead, on every trim, which means an electric motor assists your steering rather than pressurized fluid.<!-- F-WP09-034 --> Your owner's manual's Fluid Locations diagram has no power steering fluid reservoir at all. It lists only engine oil, automatic transmission fluid, brake fluid, clutch fluid on manual-transmission cars, washer fluid, and the coolant reservoir.<!-- F-WP09-034 --> Your dashboard has its own dedicated electric power steering indicator instead, confirming this is an electrical system, not a hydraulic one.<!-- F-WP09-034 --> This also means there is no power steering fluid to check, top up, or ever change on your Fit.

### Washer fluid

Washer fluid sprays onto your windshield when you use the washers, so the wipers have something to clean with instead of dry glass. Your Fit's manual calls for any commercially available windshield washer fluid.<!-- F-WP09-035 --> Never put engine antifreeze, or a vinegar-and-water solution, into this reservoir.<!-- F-WP09-035 --> We could not confirm the exact reservoir capacity for the 2008 Fit from this book's sources. Check your owner's manual for that figure, and top the reservoir up when the level runs low.<!-- F-WP09-035 -->

## Step by step

### Check the coolant level and top it up

**Safety warnings, before you touch the radiator cap:**
- Do not remove the radiator cap while the engine is hot. Hot coolant can spray out and scald you badly. Wait until the engine and radiator are cool.
- Do not add plain water alone, or straight, undiluted antifreeze, to the reserve tank.
- Do not add rust inhibitors or other additives. They may not be compatible with the coolant or engine parts.

**Steps:**
1. Park on level ground and let the engine and radiator cool completely.
2. Open the hood and find the coolant reserve tank, a translucent plastic tank, not the radiator itself.
3. Look at the coolant level against the MIN and MAX marks on the side of the tank.
4. If the level is at or below MIN, remove the reserve tank cap.
5. Pour Honda Long-Life Antifreeze/Coolant Type 2 in slowly, watching the level.
6. Fill until the level reaches the MAX mark. Do not overfill.
7. Put the reserve tank cap back on, and make sure it seats fully.
8. Wipe up any spilled coolant right away. It can damage engine bay parts.
9. Look around the reserve tank and radiator hoses for any obvious wet spots or drips that would explain a low level.

**How you know it worked:** the coolant level sits at or near the MAX mark on the reserve tank, the cap is seated, and you find no active leak underneath.

### Drain and fill the coolant

**Safety warnings, before you raise the car or open the radiator:**
- Do not remove the radiator cap while the engine is hot. Hot coolant can spray out and scald you. Wait until the engine and radiator are cool.
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands or ramps rated for the weight.
- Always relieve pressure slowly. Turn the radiator cap counterclockwise to the first stop and let it hiss before pressing down and removing it.
- Do not add plain water alone as a permanent fix, and do not mix Honda's concentrated coolant with plain water beyond what the label describes.
- Always clean up spilled coolant immediately. It is toxic to pets and wildlife if left in a puddle.

**Steps:**
1. Let the engine and radiator cool completely.
2. Raise the front of the car and support it on jack stands, then remove the splash shield if one is fitted.
3. Relieve pressure at the radiator cap slowly, then remove the cap fully.
4. Place a drain pan under the radiator's drain plug, and loosen it to drain the radiator.
5. Place the drain pan under the drain bolt at the rear of the engine block, and remove it to drain the block.
6. Let all the coolant drain fully from both points.
7. Reinstall the block drain bolt with a new washer, and tighten it securely.
8. Tighten the radiator drain plug securely.
9. Remove, empty, and rinse the coolant reserve tank, then reinstall it.
10. Pour fresh Honda coolant into the radiator, up to the base of the filler neck.
11. Fill the reserve tank to the MAX mark.
12. Install the radiator cap loosely, not fully tight yet.
13. Start the engine and let it run until the radiator fan cycles on and off at least twice, to work air out of the system.
14. Turn off the engine, let it cool, and recheck the radiator and reserve tank levels.
15. Top off as needed, then put the radiator cap on fully and tightly.
16. Start the engine again and check underneath for leaks at both drain points.
17. Lower the car and remove the jack stands.

**How you know it worked:** the reserve tank sits at the MAX mark after a full warm-up-and-cool-down cycle, and there are no leaks at either drain point. The temperature gauge or warning light behaves normally on a test drive.

### Check and change the manual transmission fluid

**Safety warnings, before you raise the car:**
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands or ramps.
- Always check and change the fluid with the transmission at normal operating temperature, and the car on level ground.
- Do not overfill. Fluid should only reach the edge of the bolt hole, not spill out under pressure.
- Always clean up any spilled fluid immediately.

**Steps (level check only):**
1. Drive the car until the transmission reaches normal operating temperature.
2. Park on level ground and shut off the engine.
3. Remove the transmission filler bolt.
4. Carefully feel inside the bolt hole with a clean finger for the fluid level.
5. If fluid does not reach the edge of the hole, add Honda MTF until it starts to run back out.
6. Put a new washer on the filler bolt, and reinstall it, tightened securely.

**Steps (full drain and refill, continuing from a cool transmission):**
7. Raise the car and support it on jack stands.
8. Place the drain pan under the transmission's drain bolt.
9. Remove the drain bolt and let the old fluid fully drain.
10. Reinstall the drain bolt with a new washer, tightened securely.
11. Lower the car back to level ground.
12. Remove the filler bolt and add fresh Honda MTF through a pump or long funnel, until it reaches the edge of the hole.
13. Reinstall the filler bolt with a new washer, tightened securely.
14. Check for leaks at both bolts after a short drive.

**How you know it worked:** fluid sits right at the edge of the filler bolt hole, neither dripping out under pressure nor clearly low. There are no leaks at either bolt after driving, and shifting feels normal, not stiff or grindy.

### Check and change the automatic transmission fluid

**Safety warnings, before you raise the car:**
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands or ramps.
- Always check and fill the level with the engine at normal operating temperature, and the car on level ground.
- Do not fill above the dipstick's upper mark. Overfilling can damage the transmission.
- Always clean up any spilled fluid immediately.

**Steps (level check only):**
1. Park on level ground and start the engine, letting it run until the radiator fan comes on, then shut it off.
2. Wait about 60 seconds, but no longer than 90 seconds, before checking.
3. Remove the dipstick, the yellow-looped fluid gauge, and wipe it clean with a rag.
4. Insert the dipstick fully back into the transmission.
5. Remove it again, and read the fluid level against the upper and lower marks.
6. If the level is below the lower mark, add Honda ATF-Z1 through the dipstick tube until it is between the marks.
7. Reinsert the dipstick fully and securely.

**Steps (full drain and refill, continuing after the level check):**
8. Raise the car and support it on jack stands.
9. Place the drain pan under the transmission's drain plug.
10. Remove the drain plug and let the old fluid fully drain.
11. Reinstall the drain plug with a new washer, tightened securely.
12. Lower the car back to level ground.
13. Add fresh Honda ATF-Z1 through the dipstick tube using a pump or long funnel.
14. Recheck the level using steps 1 through 5 above, topping off as needed.
15. Check for leaks at the drain plug after a short drive.

**How you know it worked:** the dipstick reads between the upper and lower marks after the correct warm-up-and-wait routine, and there are no leaks at the drain plug. The car shifts smoothly, with no delay or harsh engagement.

## What can go wrong

- **Topping off coolant with plain water instead of the correct pre-mixed type.** This dilutes the mixture and can reduce its protection against freezing or overheating.<!-- F-WP09-020 -->
- **Opening a hot radiator cap and getting scalded by escaping steam and coolant.** Always let the system cool first.
- **Not letting the engine run through a full warm-up cycle after a coolant drain and fill.** This can leave a trapped air pocket that causes overheating later.
- **Using plain motor oil in a manual transmission long-term instead of switching back to Honda MTF.** Motor oil lacks the additives your gearbox needs, and shifting can get stiffer over time.<!-- F-WP09-026 -->
- **Using any automatic transmission fluid other than Honda ATF-Z1, or its replacement, ATF DW-1.** Mixing fluids can wear down your transmission, and the damage may not be covered under warranty.<!-- F-WP09-029 -->
- **Checking automatic transmission fluid cold, or without waiting the 60- to 90-second window after shutdown.** This gives an inaccurate reading.
- **Assuming your Fit has power steering fluid to check because an older car you know did.** This car steers electrically. There is no fluid at all.<!-- F-WP09-034 -->
- **Skipping the 3-year brake fluid change because the dashboard display never mentioned it.** This is a fixed rule, separate from the Maintenance Minder.<!-- F-WP09-013 -->
- **Pouring antifreeze or a vinegar solution into the washer fluid reservoir.** Use only commercial washer fluid.<!-- F-WP09-035 -->
