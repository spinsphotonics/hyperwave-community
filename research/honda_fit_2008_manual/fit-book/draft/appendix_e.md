# Appendix E: Tire and wheel fitment table

This appendix gathers the numbers you need to buy tires or wheels that actually fit your 2008 Fit base trim. A few terms are defined here since they show up together for the first time. **Lug pattern** (also called bolt pattern or PCD) describes how many wheel bolts there are and the diameter of the circle they sit on — a wheel with the wrong lug pattern will not bolt onto your car at all. **Center bore** is the size of the round hole in the middle of the wheel that centers it on the hub; a wheel with too large a center bore needs a plastic ring (a hub-centric ring) to sit properly, and one with too small a bore will not fit over the hub at all. **Offset** describes whether the wheel's mounting surface sits toward the inside or outside of the wheel compared to its centerline, measured in millimeters; changing offset changes how far the tire sticks out from (or tucks into) the fender. **Cold tire pressure** means the pressure measured before you have driven the car, while the tires are still at outside-air temperature — driving heats up the air inside a tire and raises the reading, so checking "cold" gives you a consistent, accurate number.

| Largest tire that fits without rubbing (community-reported) | 195/50R15, on a 15x6.5 or 15x7 wheel with a +40 to +42 mm offset, is the size two independent online communities agree "fits without rubbing" at stock ride height. A wider 205/45R16 (on a 16x7 wheel) is reported to work only with a high offset (+42 mm minimum), and even then some rubbing was reported at the rear, especially with passengers in the back seat — treat 205/45R16 as a borderline size, not a clean fit. Note on the Fit Sport's numbers, for comparison only: the 2008 Fit Sport uses a different factory tire size, P195/55R15 84H, and a different factory wheel, 15 x 6JJ — both larger than the base trim's numbers above. These Sport-trim numbers are not this book's subject car but are included here so you do not confuse a Sport parts listing for a base-trim one.
