# Chapter 26: Tires — Pressure, Rotation, Wear, Replacement, the Spare

## What you will learn

- Your Fit's correct tire pressure, tire size, and wheel size
- How to check and set tire pressure the right way
- How to rotate your tires safely, and the torque your wheel nuts need
- What to look for when checking tires for wear
- Where to find the flat-tire and spare-tire change procedure in this book

## The short version

Your Fit's base trim uses 32 psi (220 kPa) in every tire, front and rear, and the compact spare needs 60 psi (420 kPa). <!-- F-WP10-010 --> Checking pressure takes about 10 minutes and needs only a gauge. <!-- P-WP11-4 --> Rotating all four tires needs jack stands and a torque wrench, and takes about 45 minutes. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->

## The full explanation

### Your Fit's tire and wheel numbers

Your base-trim Fit's factory tire size is P175/65R14 81S, mounted on a 14-inch by 5.5J steel wheel with a full wheel cover. <!-- F-WP10-011, F-WP10-012 --> The wheel bolts on a 4x100 mm pattern, meaning four lug nuts arranged around a circle 100 mm across. <!-- F-WP10-013 --> The wheel's center bore, the hole in the middle that centers the wheel on the hub, measures 56.1 mm. <!-- F-WP10-014 --> Offset means how far the wheel's mounting face sits from its centerline. The factory wheel's offset is ET45, within a reported range of 43 to 47 mm for this tire and wheel combination. <!-- F-WP10-015 -->

### Recommended pressure

Both base and Sport trims use the same cold tire pressure: 32 psi (220 kPa, 2.2 kgf/cm²), front and rear. <!-- F-WP10-010 --> The compact spare tire needs a higher pressure, 60 psi (420 kPa, 4.2 kgf/cm²), since it is a smaller, temporary tire. <!-- F-WP10-010 --> "Cold" means the tire has been parked for at least 3 hours, or driven less than 1 mile (1.6 km). <!-- P-WP11-4 --> A hot tire reads 4 to 6 psi higher than its true pressure, and this can fool you into underfilling it. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->

### Rotating your tires

Rotating tires means moving each wheel to a new position on the car, so all four tires wear more evenly. This is a Maintenance Minder sub item, with no fixed mileage number printed in the owner's manual; the Minder decides the timing. <!-- F-WP09-004 --> Whichever pattern you use, torque every wheel nut to 80 lbf·ft (108 N·m) once the car is back on the ground. <!-- F-WP10-001 -->

### Checking for wear

While you check pressure or rotate tires, look each tire over for bulges, cuts, or objects stuck in the tread. <!-- P-WP11-4 --> These sources do not give a specific tread-depth number for deciding when a tire needs replacing. We could not confirm a tread-wear replacement number for the 2008 Fit from this chapter's sources. Check your tire's own wear indicators, small raised bars molded into the tread, or ask a tire shop.

### The spare tire

Your Fit carries a compact spare tire for roadside use. Chapter 16 covers the full step-by-step procedure for changing a flat tire and using that spare, including the jacking steps, so this chapter does not repeat it. Come back to Chapter 16 if you have a flat on the road; use this chapter for routine pressure checks and rotations instead.

## Step by step

### Check and set your tire pressure

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always check pressure when the tires are cold: parked 3+ hours, or driven less than 1 mile (1.6 km). Hot tires read 4-6 psi high and will fool you into underfilling them. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
- Do not let air out of a hot tire to try to match the cold-pressure number; you will underinflate it. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
- Always check the spare tire too, not just the four road tires. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->

**Steps:**
1. Find the recommended pressures on the driver's doorjamb sticker, or use 32 psi front and rear as a cold-pressure fallback. <!-- F-WP10-010 -->
2. Remove the valve cap from the first tire and set it somewhere you won't lose it. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
3. Press the tire gauge firmly onto the valve stem until the hiss stops and read the number. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
4. Compare the reading to the recommended pressure for that tire. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
5. If the pressure is low, add air in short bursts, checking often, until it matches the number. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
6. If the pressure is too high, press the small pin inside the valve to release a little air, then recheck. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
7. Reinstall the valve cap. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
8. Repeat steps 2 through 7 for the other three tires. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->
9. Check the compact spare the same way; it should be 60 psi (420 kPa). <!-- F-WP10-010, P-WP11-4 -->
10. While you are down there, look each tire over for bulges, cuts, or objects stuck in the tread. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->

**How you know it worked:** every tire, including the spare, reads within about 1 psi of its recommended cold pressure, and no tire shows a bulge, cut, or embedded object. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->

### Rotate the tires

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use four jack stands. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
- Always loosen every wheel nut a bit before you raise that corner off the ground. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
- Always chock the wheels that stay on the ground before you start jacking. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
- Do not fully tighten wheel nuts with the wheel hanging free; snug them, lower the car, then torque them on the ground. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->

**Steps:**
1. Park on level, solid ground and set the parking brake. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
2. Chock the wheels you are not lifting. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
3. Loosen each wheel nut about a half turn while all four tires are still on the ground. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
4. Raise one corner and support it on a jack stand; remove that wheel. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
5. Repeat step 4 for the other three corners so all four wheels are off. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
6. Move each wheel to its new position, front-to-back and side-to-side per the rotation chart, unless your tires are directional. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
7. If your tires are marked directional, only swap front-to-back on the same side, never side-to-side. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
8. Put each wheel back on its new hub and thread the nuts on by hand. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
9. Snug the nuts in a crisscross (star) pattern with the wrench, one corner at a time. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
10. Lower the car until the tire touches the ground but still has some jack support. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
11. Torque each wheel nut to 80 lbf·ft (108 N·m) in the crisscross pattern. <!-- F-WP10-001, P-WP11-5 -->
12. Lower the car fully and remove the jack stands. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
13. Check and set the pressure in all four tires once they are all back on the ground. <!-- P-WP11-4, P-WP11-5 -->

**How you know it worked:** every wheel nut is torqued to 80 lbf·ft. No wheel wobbles when you drive at low speed. The tires are now in their new rotation positions. <!-- F-WP10-001, P-WP11-5 -->

## What can go wrong

Checking pressure right after driving gives a falsely high reading. <!-- P-WP11-4 --> Using a different gauge each time, without noticing it reads differently from your own gauge, causes confusion over time. <!-- P-WP11-4 --> Forgetting the spare tire is easy, and it slowly loses pressure over months even if it is never used. <!-- P-WP11-4 --> Ignoring a low-tire-pressure warning light and skipping the physical check anyway defeats the point of having the light. <!-- P-WP11-4 -->

During rotation, trying to fully torque a wheel nut while the wheel still hangs free in the air can leave it improperly seated. <!-- P-WP11-5 --> Skipping the crisscross tightening pattern can seat the wheel unevenly. <!-- P-WP11-5 --> Rotating directional tires side-to-side puts the tread pattern running backward. <!-- P-WP11-5 --> Forgetting to re-torque wheel nuts after the first 50 to 100 miles of driving on a re-used wheel is another common miss. <!-- P-WP11-5 -->
