# Chapter 16: When Something Goes Wrong on the Road

## What you will learn
- What each red or important warning light means, and what to do about it
- How to change a flat tire safely, step by step
- How to jump-start a dead battery, step by step
- How to handle an overheating engine, step by step
- The rules for towing this car, and what the front towing hook is really for
- Why this chapter cannot give you a Honda-specific accident checklist, and what to do instead

## The short version
Pull over safely, turn on your hazard lights, and read the warning light before you decide what to do next. A flashing or steady red brake light, a low oil pressure light, or a stuck-on high temperature light all mean stop and deal with it, not keep driving. Keep the compact spare's speed limit and the flat-tow distance and speed limits in mind, since both are lower than normal driving speed.

## The full explanation

### Warning lights you may see on the road

The owner's manual only gives an explicit color label, "(Red)," to one indicator: the Parking Brake and Brake System Indicator.<!-- F-WP07-018 --> It comes on whenever you turn the ignition to ON, as a parking-brake reminder, and stays on until the parking brake is fully released.<!-- F-WP07-018 --> A beeper also sounds if you drive with the parking brake partly applied.<!-- F-WP07-018 --> If it stays on after you release the parking brake, or comes on while you are driving, something is wrong. The brake fluid level is probably low, or there is a brake system problem.<!-- F-WP07-018 --> Press the brake pedal lightly to check if it feels normal. If it feels normal, check the brake fluid level at your next stop. If the pedal does not feel normal, meaning it goes down much farther or needs much harder pressure, slow down by downshifting and pull over immediately.<!-- F-WP07-018 --> Do not keep driving; have the vehicle towed and repaired.<!-- F-WP07-018 --> If the ABS indicator, the light for the anti-lock brake system, comes on together with this brake indicator, have the vehicle inspected by a dealer right away.<!-- F-WP07-018 -->

The Low Oil Pressure Indicator should never come on while the engine is running.<!-- F-WP07-019 --> If it flashes or stays on, oil pressure has dropped very low. Pull over safely, shut off the engine, and turn on your hazard lights. Let the vehicle sit a minute, then open the hood and check the oil level, adding oil to the full mark on the dipstick if needed.<!-- F-WP07-019 --> Then restart the engine and watch the indicator. If it does not go out within 10 seconds, shut the engine off again; there is a mechanical problem that needs repair before you keep driving.<!-- F-WP07-019 --> This indicator is commonly described as a red, oil-can-shaped light, but that color could not be confirmed for the 2008 Fit specifically.<!-- F-WP07-019 -->

The Charging System Indicator means, if it comes on brightly while the engine is running, that the battery is not being charged.<!-- F-WP07-020 --> Immediately turn off all electrical accessories, and avoid using power windows and similar loads. Keep the engine running, since starting it again would drain the battery further, and go to a service station or garage for help.<!-- F-WP07-020 --> Owners report that this light is a red battery-shaped symbol, though the owner's manual text itself does not name a color for it.<!-- F-WP07-020 -->

The Malfunction Indicator Lamp, also called the check-engine light, means one of the engine's emissions-control systems may have a problem.<!-- F-WP07-021 --> The car may feel unchanged, but fuel economy can drop and emissions can rise. Continued driving may cause serious damage, so have the vehicle checked by a dealer as soon as possible if it comes on repeatedly.<!-- F-WP07-021 --> If it comes on right after you refuel, check for a loose or missing fuel cap first. The manual may also show a "CHECK FUEL CAP" message. Tightening the cap can take several days of driving to clear the light.<!-- F-WP07-021 --> This indicator does not demand pulling over right away, the way the brake, oil-pressure, or overheating indicators do, but the manual still says not to keep driving on it indefinitely.<!-- F-WP07-021 --> We could not confirm this indicator's color for the 2008 Fit specifically; the manual does not label it at all.<!-- F-WP07-021 --> Do not assume the check-engine light is a red light. Based on the manual's own wording, only the brake system indicator is confirmed red; the check-engine light is never given a color in the manual.<!-- F-WP07-022 -->

### Hazard lights and reflectors

The hazard warning button, also called the four-way flashers, is on the instrument panel.<!-- F-WP07-023 --> Pressing it flashes all four exterior turn signals and both instrument-panel turn indicators at the same time.<!-- F-WP07-023 --> Use the hazard lights if you need to park in a dangerous spot near heavy traffic, or if your vehicle is disabled.<!-- F-WP07-023 --> Every roadside procedure in this chapter, flat tire, overheating, and low oil pressure, tells you to turn on the hazard lights as an early step.<!-- F-WP07-023 -->

We could not confirm this for the 2008 Fit. A full read of the owner's manual found no mention anywhere of a warning triangle, reflective triangle, road flare, or roadside reflector. It is not listed as standard equipment or as a recommendation.<!-- F-WP07-024 --> Check your owner's manual, and consider carrying a reflective triangle or flares as extra roadside protection even though the Fit's manual does not require or mention one.<!-- F-WP07-024 -->

### Towing this car, and the towing hook

Honda names two approved ways to tow this vehicle for ordinary, non-emergency situations. Flat-bed equipment, where the whole car is loaded onto the back of a truck, is what the manual calls the best way to transport the vehicle.<!-- F-WP07-025 --> Wheel-lift equipment, where the tow truck's two arms lift the front tires off the ground while the rear tires stay down, is called an acceptable way to tow the vehicle.<!-- F-WP07-025 --> The manual says to call a professional towing service, and never to tow the car with just a rope or chain, since that is very dangerous.<!-- F-WP07-025 -->

The owner's manual does not offer flat towing, meaning all four wheels rolling on the ground, as a supported method for either transmission.<!-- F-WP07-026 --> It only allows front-wheels-on-the-ground towing as a damage-only contingency, when flatbed or wheel-lift equipment cannot be used.<!-- F-WP07-026 --> This method is capped at 50 miles (80 km) and 35 mph (55 km/h) maximum.<!-- F-WP07-029 --> For a manual transmission, release the parking brake, shift to neutral, and leave the ignition switch in the ACCESSORY position so the steering wheel does not lock.<!-- F-WP07-027 --> For an automatic transmission, release the parking brake, start the engine, shift to D, and hold for 5 seconds.<!-- F-WP07-028 --> Then shift to N, let the engine idle for 3 minutes, and turn it off. Leave the ignition switch in ACCESSORY.<!-- F-WP07-028 --> If you cannot shift the transmission or cannot start the engine, the vehicle must be transported with the front wheels off the ground instead. The front-wheels-down method does not apply in that case.<!-- F-WP07-028 --> Do not lift or tow the vehicle by the bumpers; they cannot support the vehicle's weight and will be seriously damaged.<!-- F-WP07-030 --> Always confirm the steering wheel turns freely, with the ignition in ACCESSORY, before towing begins.<!-- F-WP07-030 -->

The car also carries a single detachable towing hook and its extension tool, stored in the tool kit in the cargo area.<!-- F-WP07-031 --> It mounts into a bolt hole behind a cover panel in the front bumper.<!-- F-WP07-031 --> The manual frames this hook as a "get unstuck" tool. It is meant for freeing the car from sand, mud, or snow over very short distances, or for a towing service to use for straight, flat-ground pulling only.<!-- F-WP07-032 --> It is not for loading the car onto a flatbed, and must not be used as a cargo tie-down.<!-- F-WP07-032 --> Do not tow at an angle with it, and call a towing service for anything beyond a very short pull.<!-- F-WP07-032 -->

### Accidents

We could not confirm this for the 2008 Fit. None of this book's research sources describe a Honda-specific accident checklist, meaning a list of what to do right after a collision. If you are ever in an accident, use general safe practice instead. Check for injuries first, and move to a safe spot off the road if the car can still move. Turn on your hazard lights, call emergency services if anyone is hurt, and exchange insurance and contact information with anyone else involved. Consult your state's driver's handbook or local authorities for the exact steps required where you live, since accident-reporting rules vary by state.

## Step by step

### Change a flat tire

**Safety warnings, before you touch the jack:**
- Always stop far away from traffic lanes, on firm, level, non-slippery ground, before you start.
- Always turn on the hazard warning lights before you get out of the car.
- Always get every passenger out of the car before you change the tire.
- Do not get under the car at any time. It can roll off the jack and crush you.
- Always follow the jacking steps in order. Do not skip the jacking-point check.
- Always handle wheel nuts and the brake hub carefully; they can be hot from driving.
- Never exceed 50 mph (80 km/h) on the compact spare tire. It is a temporary tire only.<!-- F-WP07-004 -->

**Steps:**
1. Pull well off the road, onto firm, level, non-slippery ground, away from traffic.
2. Turn on the hazard warning lights.
3. Put the transmission in Park (automatic) or reverse (manual). Set the parking brake.
4. Turn the ignition off. Have everyone get out of the car and stand away from traffic.
5. Open the tailgate and lift the cargo floor lid using its strap. Hook the strap to the head restraint leg to hold it open.<!-- F-WP07-002 -->
6. Take the tool kit out of the center of the spare tire.<!-- F-WP07-007 -->
7. Turn the jack's end bracket counterclockwise to loosen it, then lift the jack straight out.<!-- F-WP07-006 -->
8. Unscrew the wing bolt and lift the spare tire out of its well.<!-- F-WP07-002 -->
9. Loosen each wheel nut on the flat tire one half turn with the wheel nut wrench. Do not remove them yet.
10. On base-trim cars, pry off the wheel cover using the flat tip of the extension.<!-- F-WP07-010 -->
11. Find the jacking point nearest the flat tire. Look for the mark molded into the body underside.<!-- F-WP07-008 -->
12. Set the jack under that jacking point. Turn the end bracket clockwise until the jack meets the point.
13. Check that the jacking point's tab is resting inside the jack's notch before you raise the car.<!-- F-WP07-008 -->
14. Use the extension with the wheel nut wrench to raise the car until the flat tire is off the ground.
15. Remove the loosened wheel nuts, then pull the flat tire off. Set it down with the outside face up.
16. Wipe dirt off the wheel hub and the spare's mounting surface with a clean cloth.
17. Put the spare tire onto the hub. Thread the wheel nuts on finger-tight in a crisscross pattern.
18. Lower the car to the ground with the jack, then remove the jack.
19. Tighten the wheel nuts the rest of the way in the same crisscross pattern, to 80 lbf·ft (108 N·m).<!-- F-WP07-009 -->
20. Place the flat tire face down in the spare tire well and secure it with the wing bolt.
21. Store the jack and place the tool kit back in the center of the flat tire.<!-- F-WP07-007 -->
22. Store the wheel cover or center cap in the cargo area so it does not get scratched.
23. Unhook the strap, lower the cargo floor lid, and close the tailgate.
24. Drive to a service shop soon to have the wheel nut torque checked and the flat tire repaired.<!-- F-WP07-005 -->

**How you know it worked:** the spare tire sits flat on the ground, all wheel nuts are snug and torqued, and the car sits level. It drives straight with no pulling, wobble, or noise below 50 mph.<!-- F-WP07-004 -->

### Jump start the car

**Safety warnings, before you connect a cable:**
- Always keep sparks, open flames, and smoking materials away from the battery. It can explode.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
- Do not jump-start a battery with slushy or frozen electrolyte, the liquid inside the battery. Let it thaw first.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
- Do not connect the second jumper cable to the dead battery's negative terminal or to any other engine part. Use the grounding point, called "the stay" in the manual, instead.<!-- F-WP07-012 -->
- Always turn off all electrical accessories on both vehicles before connecting cables.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
- Do not try to push- or pull-start a car with an automatic transmission. It will not work.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
- Always keep loose jumper cable ends away from each other and from metal until fully disconnected.<!-- F-WP07-013 -->

**Steps:**
1. Open the hood and check the physical condition of your battery before doing anything else.<!-- F-WP07-011 -->
2. In cold weather, check that the battery's electrolyte is not slushy or frozen.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
3. Turn off the heater, A/C, audio system, lights, and every other electrical accessory.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
4. Put the transmission in neutral (manual) or Park (automatic), and set the parking brake.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
5. If you are using another vehicle, have someone start it and run it at a fast idle.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
6. Connect one jumper cable to the positive (+) terminal on your dead battery.<!-- F-WP07-012 -->
7. Connect the other end of that cable to the positive (+) terminal on the booster battery.<!-- F-WP07-012 -->
8. Connect the second jumper cable to the negative (-) terminal on the booster battery.<!-- F-WP07-012 -->
9. Connect the other end of the second cable to the grounding stay on your car, not the battery.<!-- F-WP07-012 -->
10. Start your engine. If cranking is slow, recheck that every cable clamp has a solid metal contact.
11. Once your car is running, disconnect the negative cable from your car first, then from the booster battery.<!-- F-WP07-013 -->
12. Disconnect the positive cable from your car, then from the booster battery.<!-- F-WP07-013 -->
13. Keep the loose cable ends apart and away from any metal until both cables are fully removed.<!-- F-WP07-013 -->

**How you know it worked:** the engine starts and keeps running on its own once both cables are disconnected, and the charging system indicator is off.

### The car is overheating

**Safety warnings, before you open the hood:**
- Do not open the hood if you see steam or spray coming from under it. Wait until it stops.<!-- F-WP07-017 -->
- Steam and spray from an overheated engine can scald you badly. Stay clear until things cool.<!-- F-WP07-017 -->
- Always let the engine and radiator cool down before removing the radiator cap.<!-- F-WP07-017 -->
- Do not remove the radiator cap while the engine is hot. Hot coolant, the liquid that keeps the engine's temperature down, can spray out and scald you.<!-- F-WP07-017 -->
- Do not keep driving with the high temperature indicator on. It can cause serious engine damage.<!-- F-WP07-015 -->

**Steps:**
1. Safely pull to the side of the road, away from traffic.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
2. Shift to neutral (manual) or Park (automatic), and set the parking brake.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
3. Turn off all accessories and turn on the hazard warning lights.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
4. If you see steam or spray from under the hood, turn off the engine right away.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
5. Wait until no more steam or spray is visible before you open the hood.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
6. If there is no steam or spray, you may leave the engine running and watch the indicator.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
7. If the indicator goes off within a few minutes, wait for it, then continue driving.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
8. If the indicator stays on, turn off the engine and let it sit.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
9. Look for an obvious coolant leak, such as a split hose. Everything is still very hot.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
10. If you find a leak, do not drive again until it is repaired.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
11. If you see no leak, check the coolant level in the radiator reserve tank.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
12. Add coolant to the reserve tank if the level is below the MIN mark.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
13. If the reserve tank was empty, let the engine and radiator cool fully before going further.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
14. Using gloves or a heavy cloth, turn the radiator cap counterclockwise to the first stop only.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
15. Let the pressure release fully, then push down on the cap and turn it off.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
16. Start the engine and set the temperature dial to maximum heat.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
17. Add coolant to the radiator up to the base of the filler neck.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
18. If you have no coolant mix on hand, plain water is an acceptable emergency substitute.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
19. Put the radiator cap back on tightly, then run the engine and watch the indicator.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
20. If the indicator blinks or comes on again, stop driving. The engine needs repair.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
21. If the temperature stays normal, top the reserve tank to MAX and close its cap tightly.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
22. Have the cooling system drained and refilled with the correct coolant mixture soon.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->

**How you know it worked:** the high temperature indicator stays off during normal driving, the reserve tank sits between MIN and MAX, and you see no new coolant leaks.

## What can go wrong
- **Flat tire — loosening wheel nuts after the car is already jacked up.** The wheel just spins. Loosen them a half turn first, while the tire is still on the ground.
- **Flat tire — setting the jack on bodywork instead of the marked jacking point.** Check for the molded mark before you raise the car.<!-- F-WP07-008 -->
- **Flat tire — driving the spare above 50 mph, or for a long distance, instead of repairing the flat promptly.** The compact spare is a temporary tire only.<!-- F-WP07-004 -->
- **Flat tire — skipping the shop visit to have wheel nut torque re-checked.** The manual calls for this after any fresh tightening.<!-- F-WP07-009 -->
- **Jump start — connecting the last cable to the dead battery's negative terminal instead of a ground point.** This raises the risk of sparks near battery gases.<!-- F-WP07-012 -->
- **Jump start — leaving accessories on while connecting cables, or trying to push-start an automatic.** Neither works safely.<!-- F-WP07-014 -->
- **Jump start — letting stray cable ends touch metal while still connected on one end.** Keep the ends apart until both cables are fully off.<!-- F-WP07-013 -->
- **Overheating — opening the hood while steam is still visible, or removing the radiator cap on a hot engine.** Both can cause serious scalding.<!-- F-WP07-017 -->
- **Overheating — continuing to drive after the indicator stays on, instead of pulling over.** This can cause serious engine damage.<!-- F-WP07-015 -->
- **Overheating — forgetting to have the correct coolant mixture put back in, after adding plain water in an emergency.** Plain water is a stopgap, not a permanent fix.<!-- F-WP07-016 -->
- **Towing — using the front towing hook for anything beyond a short, straight, flat-ground pull.** It is a "get unstuck" tool, not a general towing point, and must not be used to load the car onto a flatbed.<!-- F-WP07-032 -->
- **Towing an automatic without the D-then-N cycle.** Skipping the 5-second Drive step, the 3-minute idle in Neutral, and letting the engine cool before shifting can damage the transmission.<!-- F-WP07-028 -->
