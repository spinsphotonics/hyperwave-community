# Chapter 61: Driver skills

## What you will learn

- Where to look with your eyes while driving on track
- Why smooth steering and pedal inputs make you faster, not just safer
- What trail braking means and why drivers use it
- How these skills connect to what you already do behind the wheel

## The short version

Look far ahead, toward the path you want, not at a hazard or the track's edge. <!-- F-WP20-011 --> Smooth, gentle steering and pedal inputs keep more grip in your tires than sudden, jerky ones do. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> Trail braking means letting off the brake gradually as you steer into a corner, instead of coming off it all at once beforehand. <!-- F-WP20-013 -->

## The full explanation

### Where to look

A well-known professional driving coach's free instructional material teaches vision as one of the very first skills to learn, ahead of lines or braking technique. <!-- F-WP20-011 --> Train your eyes to look far down the track, not just at the pavement right in front of your car. <!-- F-WP20-011 --> Point your eyes toward the path you want the car to take. <!-- F-WP20-011 --> Avoid fixing your gaze on a hazard or the track's outer edge, since your eyes tend to pull the car toward wherever they are aimed. <!-- F-WP20-011 --> Turning your head to see into a corner, instead of keeping it pointed straight ahead, helps you spot that path earlier. <!-- F-WP20-011 --> Treat this as a constant scanning habit. Always pick up your next reference point further down the track, rather than glancing once and then forgetting about it. <!-- F-WP20-011 --> Your overall awareness of the drive shapes how well you react. That means what you see, what you feel through the seat as the car grips or slides, and what you hear from the engine and tires. <!-- F-WP20-011 -->

### Why smoothness matters

The same source teaches keeping your hands near the 9-and-3 o'clock position on the wheel, like the hands of a clock, through most corners on most tracks. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> A car that needs less wheel movement tends to be smoother and more predictable to drive. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> Keeping your hand movements small is directly tied to how smooth, and ultimately how fast, you can drive. <!-- F-WP20-012 -->

Here is why smoothness matters so much: weight transfer. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> Weight transfer is the way a car's weight shifts from one end, or one side, to the other as you brake, speed up, or turn. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> Braking shifts weight toward the front tires. Speeding up shifts it toward the rear. Turning shifts it toward the outside tires. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> A sudden, jerky input causes a bigger, more sudden weight shift than the same input made gradually. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> A tire's grip does not grow at the same rate as the extra load it carries. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> That is why a car actually loses more overall grip from a jerky input than a smooth one. <!-- F-WP20-012 --> That is the real reason smooth driving is fast driving, not only safer driving. <!-- F-WP20-012 -->

### Trail braking, explained simply

Trail braking means gradually letting off the brake pedal with your foot as you steer into a corner, instead of coming completely off the brake before you begin turning. <!-- F-WP20-013 --> This trade swaps some of the brake's stopping force for turning force, right as the corner asks for it. <!-- F-WP20-013 --> Braking keeps extra weight, and therefore extra grip, on your front tires right when you are also asking them to change the car's direction. <!-- F-WP20-013 --> Coming off the brake gradually, instead of all at once, helps keep the front tires closer to their best grip as you turn in. <!-- F-WP20-013 --> Many quick drivers pay less attention to their braking point, and more attention to exactly when they finish coming off the brake. <!-- F-WP20-013 --> A smooth, late release keeps the car balanced, and keeps your eyes up on the corner instead of down at your feet. <!-- F-WP20-013 -->

### Putting it together

These three skills work as a set, not on their own. <!-- F-WP20-011, F-WP20-012, F-WP20-013 --> Looking far ahead gives you the time you need to make your steering and pedal inputs smooth, instead of rushed. <!-- F-WP20-011, F-WP20-012 --> Smooth inputs keep your tires gripping their best, which is exactly what trail braking is trying to use as you enter a corner. <!-- F-WP20-012, F-WP20-013 --> None of these skills require a faster car. They only take practice, and they work just as well in a stock Fit as in any other car. <!-- F-WP20-011, F-WP20-012, F-WP20-013 -->
