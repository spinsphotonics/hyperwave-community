# Chapter 37: Noises, Vibrations, Smells, and Leaks: Lookup Tables

## What you will learn
- How to use four lookup tables to narrow down a noise, a vibration, a smell, or a leak
- What each table's urgency rating means for how soon you should act
- How to tell a "Fit-specific" finding from general automotive knowledge, at a glance, in each table
- Where this book's research found a gap, meaning no Fit-specific source at all

## The short version
These four tables sort common car problems by when they happen, how fast you were going, what they smell like, or what color they are. Each row also names an urgency level, from Low to High, so you know how soon to act. A "Fit" tag means real 2007-2008 Fit owners reported this pattern; a "General" tag means the row is true of gasoline cars broadly.

## The full explanation

### How to read these tables

Every row below has a short **Source** tag:
- **Fit** — Owners report that this specific pattern shows up on real 2007-2008 Honda Fits.
- **General** — This is true of gasoline cars broadly, and did not need, or have, a Fit-specific report.<!-- WP-12 Sections C-F: "Grounded in" distinction -->

Use Chapter 34's method before you open these tables: note when it happens, where you notice it, your speed, and any color or smell.<!-- WP-12 Sections C, D, E, F --> Then find the closest matching row below.

Urgency levels mean:
- **Low** — Usually only annoying, not dangerous. Fix it when convenient.
- **Medium** — Worth checking soon. It can get worse or more expensive if ignored.
- **Medium-high** — Get it checked promptly. Waiting has a real cost.
- **High** — This can hurt you, seriously damage the car, or both. Act now.<!-- WP-12 Sections C-F: Urgency column -->

### Table 2: Vibrations, by speed range

| Speed range | Vibration felt where | Likely cause | Urgency | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idle or stopped | Through the seat or floor, not the steering wheel | A worn engine or transmission mount, or a rough idle from a misfire or vacuum leak code | Medium | Fit<!-- F-WP02-016 --><!-- F-WP12-002 --><!-- F-WP12-016 --><!-- F-WP12-017 --> |
| Low speed, under about 20 mph, goes away above that | Shimmy in the steering wheel | A tire out of round, a bent wheel, or a stuck brake caliper part dragging | Medium | General<!-- WP-12 Section D: low-speed shimmy --> |
| Mid-to-highway speed, roughly 45 to 65 mph, through the steering wheel | A shake that gets stronger with speed | A wheel or tire out of balance, meaning its weight is not spread evenly, or a bent rim | Medium-high — have wheels balanced and inspected soon; can speed up tire and bearing wear | Fit + General<!-- WP-12 Section D: mid-highway shake --> |
| Highway speed, roughly 55 to 65 mph, felt through the whole car, automatic transmission only | Shudder, jerk, or hesitation, not a steady vibration | Owners report a split opinion: it could be the automatic transmission itself, or worn ignition coils, valve clearance, or spark plugs presenting as a shudder | Medium-high — get it scanned for codes; do not assume it is only the transmission without a check, since the ignition-related cause is cheaper to fix | Fit<!-- F-WP02-019 --> |
| Any speed, a growl or vibration that grows with speed, sometimes louder in one turning direction | Vibration paired with a grinding or humming noise | A worn wheel bearing, the part that lets the wheel spin smoothly | Medium-high — a bearing that fails completely can cause a wheel to lock up or separate | General<!-- WP-12 Section D: wheel bearing --> |
| Braking, any speed | Pulsation felt in the brake pedal | A warped or worn brake rotor | Medium — schedule a brake inspection; not usually an immediate emergency, but it will worsen | General<!-- WP-12 Section D: brake pulsation --> |

### Table 4: Leaks, by color and location

Two things about this car matter for reading this table. First, the 2008 Fit uses electric power steering, not a hydraulic pump, so there is no separate red or pink power-steering fluid puddle to worry about.<!-- F-WP02-008 --> Any red fluid you see is almost certainly automatic transmission fluid instead, on automatic-transmission cars.<!-- WP-12 Section F --> Second, on a manual transmission, the clutch and the brakes share the same fluid reservoir and fluid type. A clear-to-amber leak near the driver's side firewall could point to either system.<!-- F-WP02-018 -->

| Location | Color | Likely fluid or cause | Urgency | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under the front of the car, roughly under the engine | Amber to dark brown, slightly oily | Engine oil | Medium-high — check the dipstick; do not run the engine low on oil | General<!-- WP-12 Section F: engine oil --> |
| Under the front, closer to the center or passenger side, thin and slippery | Red or pink | Automatic transmission fluid; this car has no hydraulic power-steering fluid to confuse it with | Medium-high — have the transmission checked; low fluid can cause the highway shudder symptom in Table 2 | General<!-- F-WP02-008 --><!-- F-WP02-019 --> |
| Under the front, sweet-smelling | Green, yellow-green, orange, pink, or blue, depending on which coolant was last used | Coolant | Medium-high — do not keep driving if it is also overheating; top up and have the leak found and fixed | General + Fit<!-- WP-12 Section F: coolant --> |
| Under the middle of the car, near the driver's side firewall | Clear to light amber, slippery, not sweet-smelling | Brake fluid or clutch fluid, since they share one reservoir on a manual-transmission Fit | High — brake fluid loss is a safety issue; stop driving and have it inspected right away if the pedal feels low or soft, or a brake warning light is on | General + Fit<!-- F-WP02-018 --> |
| Under the middle-front area | Thin, dark, smells strongly of gear oil | Manual transmission fluid | Medium — top up or have it checked; low fluid can cause hard shifting or gear noise | General<!-- WP-12 Section F: MTF --> |
| Under the middle of the car | Clear, odorless, only in hot or humid weather with the A/C running | Air conditioning condensate water | None — this is normal | General<!-- WP-12 Section F: A/C condensation --> |
| Under the rear of the car | Any fluid color | This book's research found no Fit-specific pattern for rear-of-car leaks; the fuel tank and rear brake and axle parts are the main candidates | High if it smells like gasoline; Medium otherwise, pending identification | General (gap: no Fit-specific source found)<!-- WP-12 Section F: rear leaks, flagged gap --> |

## What can go wrong
- **Treating any table as a final diagnosis.** These tables narrow things down. A shop visit or a code check may still be needed to confirm the exact cause.
- **Ignoring a High-urgency row because the car "seems fine."** Brake fluid loss, a burning-plastic smell, and a flashing check engine light are all High for a reason.
- **Mixing up red transmission fluid with power-steering fluid.** This car has no hydraulic power-steering fluid, so red or pink almost always means automatic transmission fluid instead.<!-- F-WP02-008 -->
- **Assuming the rear-of-car leak row is settled.** This book's research found no Fit-specific source for that row, and says so honestly rather than guessing.<!-- WP-12 Section F: rear leaks gap -->
- **Skipping Chapter 34's four questions before opening these tables.** When, where, at what speed, and what color or smell, make these tables far faster to use.
