# Chapter 25: Air Filter and Cabin Filter

## What you will learn

- What the engine air filter and the cabin air filter each do, and why they are different
- The exact part numbers Honda used for your Fit
- When to replace each filter
- How to replace both filters, step by step, with no jacking or fluids involved
- Common mistakes that reduce airflow instead of improving it

## The short version

Your Fit has two separate air filters. The engine air filter cleans air before it reaches the engine, and the cabin air filter cleans air coming through your heater and air conditioner. <!-- F-WP09-005, F-WP09-006 --> Both jobs are simple, need no tools beyond a screwdriver, and take well under 20 minutes combined. <!-- P-WP11-2, P-WP11-3 -->

## The full explanation

### Two filters, two jobs

The **engine air filter** sits in a black plastic housing in the engine bay and cleans the air the engine breathes before that air reaches the fuel injection system. <!-- P-WP11-2 --> Its part number is Honda 17220-PWA-505, and it fits the 2007-2008 Fit; this number replaced an older part, 17220-PWA-J10. <!-- F-WP09-040 -->

The **cabin air filter**, also called the dust and pollen filter, cleans the air that comes through your heater and air conditioner vents before it reaches you. <!-- F-WP09-006 --> Its part number is Honda 80291-SAA-J01, and it fits the 2007-2008 Fit; this number has since been superseded by 80291-SAA-316. <!-- F-WP09-042 --> This filter is fitted on models with air conditioning, per the owner's manual. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->

### When to replace each one

Both filters follow the Maintenance Minder's own timing under normal driving, with no fixed mileage number printed in the owner's manual. <!-- F-WP09-005, F-WP09-006 --> Two exceptions apply. If you drive mostly in dusty conditions, replace the engine air filter every 15,000 miles (24,000 km). <!-- F-WP09-005 --> If you drive mostly in urban areas with heavy soot from industry or diesel traffic, replace the cabin filter every 15,000 miles (24,000 km). <!-- F-WP09-006 --> Replace it sooner if airflow from your heater or air conditioner feels weaker than usual. <!-- F-WP09-006 -->

## Step by step

### Replace the engine air filter

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always let the engine cool before opening the hood if it has just been running. Parts near the filter box can be hot. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
- Do not start the engine with the air filter housing open or the element removed. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->

**Steps:**
1. Open the hood and find the air filter housing, a black plastic box on the driver's side of the engine bay. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
2. Release the clips holding the two halves of the housing together (there are usually four). <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
3. Lift the top half of the housing up and out of the way. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
4. Note which way the old filter element sits before you remove it. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
5. Lift out the old, dirty filter element. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
6. Wipe out any dust or leaves from inside the housing with a dry rag. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
7. Place the new filter element in the same orientation as the old one. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
8. Close the top half of the housing and press down until every clip snaps shut. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->
9. Check that all clips are fully closed before starting the engine. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->

**How you know it worked:** the housing halves sit flush together, every clip is snapped closed, and the engine starts and idles normally. <!-- P-WP11-2 -->

### Replace the cabin (dust and pollen) filter

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Do not force the glove box past its stops. You can break the hinge or the stop tabs. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->

**Steps:**
1. Open the glove box fully. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
2. Squeeze both sides of the glove box inward to release the stop tabs. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
3. Let the glove box swing down out of the way. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
4. Find the filter tray in the housing behind the glove box opening. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
5. Pinch the two clips on the tray's left and right sides and pull the tray out. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
6. Slide the old filter out of the tray. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
7. Look at the arrow printed on the new filter's frame. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
8. Slide the new filter into the tray with the arrow pointing down. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
9. Push the tray back into the housing until both clips click. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
10. Swing the glove box back up and press it closed. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->

**How you know it worked:** the glove box closes and latches normally, and air from the vents feels stronger than before if the old filter was clogged. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->

## What can go wrong

Forcing a stiff clip on the engine air filter housing can crack the plastic. <!-- P-WP11-2 --> Installing the new engine air filter upside down or backward, or leaving one clip unlatched, lets unfiltered air leak past the filter instead of through it. <!-- P-WP11-2 --> On the cabin filter, installing it with the arrow pointing the wrong way can reduce airflow instead of improving it. <!-- P-WP11-3 --> Not seating both tray clips lets the tray rattle or fall loose later. <!-- P-WP11-3 -->
