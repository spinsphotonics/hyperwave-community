# Chapter 43: Starter, alternator, ignition switch

## What you will learn

- What the starter, the alternator, and the ignition switch each do
- How to replace the starter yourself, step by step
- What a shop will do to replace the alternator, and why this book sends that job to a shop
- What a shop will do to replace the ignition switch, and why there are really two different jobs hiding under that one name
- What can go wrong on each job, including battery-cable safety

## The short version

The starter spins your engine to life when you turn the key or press the start button. The alternator charges your battery and powers your electrical system while the engine runs. The ignition switch is the part the key turns to control power to the starter and the rest of the car. <!-- P-WP14-9 --> <!-- P-WP14-10 --> <!-- P-WP14-11 --> The starter is a doable weekend job. The alternator and the ignition switch both go to a shop in this book, for two different reasons explained below. <!-- P-WP14-9 --> <!-- P-WP14-10 --> <!-- P-WP14-11 -->

## The full explanation

On your Fit, the starter sits low on the driver's side of the engine. This book could not find a plain, factory-style page describing how to remove and install the starter on this car. The steps below instead come from community writeups for this exact car, and reaching the starter's top mounting bolt is widely reported as the hardest single part of the job. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->

The alternator on your Fit sits in a spot that requires removing the intake manifold, the part that feeds air into the engine, before you can even reach the alternator. <!-- P-WP14-10 --> That is a bigger job than a simple bolt-off swap, and it raises the chance of a vacuum leak or a gasket problem if it is not done carefully. This book sends the alternator job to a shop for that reason.

The ignition switch is really two different jobs hiding under one name. One job replaces only the electrical switch behind the steering column covers, which is a moderate job for a confident do-it-yourselfer with basic tools. The other job replaces the whole lock cylinder, the mechanical part the key barrel turns inside. That job needs drilling out security shear bolts (bolts made to snap off flush once tightened, so a thief cannot unbolt the lock) and, on many Hondas, reprogramming the immobilizer system to accept a new key. <!-- P-WP14-11 --> The immobilizer is the security system that only lets the engine start with a key it recognizes; Chapter 7 covers its dashboard light. A "hard to turn" key can be caused by either problem, and choosing the wrong repair wastes time and money. This book sends the whole ignition switch job to a shop so it can be diagnosed correctly first. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->

This car has no ignition-switch recall on record; this is wear-and-tear repair work, not a safety recall. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->

## Step by step

### Replace the starter

Always disconnect the negative battery terminal first, then the positive terminal, before you start this job. <!-- P-WP14-9 --> Always wear eye protection. <!-- P-WP14-9 --> Never let a wrench touch both a positive battery or starter terminal and any grounded metal at the same time; this can cause a dangerous short and a burn. <!-- P-WP14-9 --> Never work under a car held up only by a jack; always use jack stands. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->

1. Disconnect the negative battery terminal, then the positive terminal. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
2. Remove the two 10 mm bolts holding the wiring harness to the intake manifold. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
3. Remove the 10 mm bolt for the dipstick tube bracket. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
4. Remove the 10 mm bolt on the starter that holds a nearby hose bracket. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
5. Detach the radiator hose holder from the starter bracket. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
6. Disconnect the electrical connectors from the starter's "S" (signal) and "B" (battery) terminals. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
7. Remove the starter's mounting bolts. Use a long extension and a swivel joint on the hard-to-reach top bolt. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
8. Guide the starter out from below the car, or through the engine bay, whichever your access allows. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
9. Compare the new starter to the old one to confirm it matches before you install it. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
10. Guide the new starter into place, and install its mounting bolts. We could not confirm an exact Honda torque number for this pass; follow your new starter's own instructions. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
11. Reconnect the electrical connectors to the "S" and "B" terminals. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
12. Reattach the radiator hose holder to the starter bracket. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
13. Reinstall the dipstick tube bracket bolt and the two wiring harness bolts at the intake manifold. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
14. Reconnect the positive battery terminal, then the negative terminal. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->
15. Start the car to confirm the new starter cranks the engine smoothly. <!-- P-WP14-9 -->

## What the shop will do: alternator

Even if a shop does this job, know what to ask about, before you read the summary below. Always disconnect the battery before this job; the alternator's output terminal stays electrically live even with the key off. <!-- P-WP14-10 --> Always wear eye protection if you help with any part of this job. <!-- P-WP14-10 --> Never skip a post-repair check of the charging voltage before you consider the job done. <!-- P-WP14-10 -->

- Disconnect the battery, following the proper terminal disconnection order. <!-- P-WP14-10 -->
- Remove the drive belt, then remove the intake manifold to reach the alternator. <!-- P-WP14-10 -->
- Disconnect the alternator's electrical connector and its positive cable, then unbolt and remove the alternator. <!-- P-WP14-10 -->
- Install the new alternator, and torque its mounting bolts to 17 lbf-ft (24 N-m). <!-- P-WP14-10 -->
- Reconnect the alternator's connector and positive cable. Make sure the cable's ring terminal has its crimped side facing away from the alternator, and torque the cable's terminal bolt to 6 lbf-ft (8 N-m). <!-- P-WP14-10 -->
- Reinstall the intake manifold, using a new gasket if the old one is damaged, reinstall the drive belt, and reconnect the battery. <!-- P-WP14-10 -->
- Start the engine and confirm the charging system light goes out, and the battery charges normally. <!-- P-WP14-10 -->

## What the shop will do: ignition switch

Even if a shop does this job, know what to ask about, before you read the summary below. Always disconnect the battery before any steering-column work, since the airbag system's wiring routes near the column. <!-- P-WP14-11 --> Never let anyone try to bypass the immobilizer system instead of properly reprogramming a new key; a bypassed immobilizer is a theft and safety risk. <!-- P-WP14-11 --> Always confirm with the shop that the airbag wiring near the column was not disturbed, or was properly reconnected if it was. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->

- Diagnose whether the problem is the electrical switch, meaning the key turns but the car does not respond correctly. Or it could be the mechanical lock cylinder, meaning the key will not turn or turns roughly. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->
- If it is the electrical switch only: remove the plastic covers around the steering column, unplug and unbolt the old switch, and install the new one. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->
- If it is the lock cylinder or the full ignition lock housing: remove the steering column covers, and drill out the security shear bolts holding the housing. Remove the old housing and install a new assembly; this part of the job is reported at around 2 hours of labor. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->
- If a new lock cylinder or an immobilizer-linked part is installed, program the new key or keys to the immobilizer system so the engine will start. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->
- Test that the key turns smoothly through LOCK, ACCESSORY, ON, and START, and that the car starts reliably afterward. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->

## What can go wrong

On the starter job, connecting the battery terminals in the wrong order is the most common mistake; always take the negative terminal off first and put it back on last. <!-- P-WP14-9 --> Forcing the starter's top mounting bolt without a swivel joint can strip the bolt or its bracket. Mixing up the "S" and "B" terminal connectors can keep the new starter from working correctly. <!-- P-WP14-9 --> On the alternator job, reconnecting the positive cable with its ring terminal facing the wrong way is a common error. So is skipping a post-job charging-voltage check; either one can leave a problem you will not notice until the battery goes flat later. <!-- P-WP14-10 --> On the ignition switch, buying and installing the wrong sub-repair wastes money. This can mean the electrical switch instead of the lock cylinder, or the other way around, if the shop has not diagnosed the real problem first. Forgetting to reprogram the immobilizer for a new key can leave the car unable to start even after a correct mechanical repair. <!-- P-WP14-11 -->
