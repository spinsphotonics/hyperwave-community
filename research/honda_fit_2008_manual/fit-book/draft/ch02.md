# Chapter 2: Identify Your Exact Car

## What you will learn

- How to read your car's VIN, the Vehicle Identification Number that names your exact car.
- Where to find the metal plates and stickers that identify your Fit.
- Exactly what your base Fit has, and what it does not have compared with a Fit Sport.
- What Honda knows, and does not know for sure, about your car's paint color code.

## The short version

Your car's VIN is a 17-character code that identifies your exact Fit. <!-- F-WP01-031 --> You can find that VIN on a plate at the top of your dashboard, on a label inside the driver's doorjamb, and stamped into the engine compartment. <!-- F-WP01-032 --> A base Fit and a Fit Sport look similar, but the base trim is missing several features the Sport has, such as alloy wheels, fog lights, and cruise control. <!-- F-WP01-027 -->

## The full explanation

### Reading your VIN

A VIN, short for Vehicle Identification Number, is a 17-character code that no two cars in the world share. Reading a VIN means breaking that code into groups of characters, where each group tells you something different about the car.

For your 2008 base Fit, here is what the groups mean:

| VIN positions | What they tell you | Value for your car |
|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | WMI, the World Manufacturer Identifier | JHM = Honda Motor Co., Ltd., Japan, passenger car <!-- F-WP01-031 --> |
| 4-6 | Platform or chassis code | GD3, the first-generation Fit structure <!-- F-WP01-031 --> |
| 10 | Model year code | The digit "8" marks a 2008 model <!-- F-WP01-031 --> |
| 11 | Assembly plant code | "S" marks the Suzuka, Japan plant <!-- F-WP01-031 --> |
| 12-17 | Serial number | A running production number unique to your car <!-- F-WP01-031 --> |

If you want to decode your own real VIN with full confidence, use the official decoder at the NHTSA (National Highway Traffic Safety Administration) website rather than counting characters by hand. Hand-counting is a helpful way to understand the pattern, but the government tool checks the whole code, including a check digit that confirms the VIN is valid.

### Where to find the VIN on your car

Honda puts your VIN in three places. The easiest one to check is a metal plate fastened to the top of the dashboard. You can see this plate by looking through the windshield from outside, on the driver's side. <!-- F-WP01-032 --> The VIN also appears on the certification label inside the driver's doorjamb, printed both as text and as a barcode, and it is stamped into the engine compartment's bulkhead, the wall that separates the engine bay from the passenger cabin. <!-- F-WP01-032 -->

### The sticker that tells you your tire information

That same doorjamb area holds a second important label. This one lists the tire sizes and cold tire pressures Honda recommends for your car. <!-- F-WP01-033 --> "Cold" means the tires have not been driven on recently, since driving heats up the air inside a tire and raises its pressure reading. Check this label, not a number you remember or a tire shop's default setting, when you set your tire pressure.

### What base got, and what base did not get

Honda sold your 2008 Fit as one of two US trims: the plain "Fit," which this book calls base, and the "Fit Sport." <!-- F-WP01-026 --> Both trims came standard with air conditioning, power windows, power door locks, power mirrors, and the 60/40 split "Magic Seat" rear seat system, which lets you fold or flip the rear seats into several different cargo configurations. <!-- F-WP01-027 --> The base Fit's factory tires were sized P175/65R14, mounted on 14-inch steel wheels with full wheel covers; the Fit Sport instead used a larger, wider tire, P195/55R15, on alloy wheels. <!-- F-WP01-027 -->

Compared with the Fit Sport, the base Fit did not come with any of the following:

- Paddle shifters mounted on the steering wheel, available only on Sport models with the automatic transmission.
- An underbody aero kit and a rear roofline spoiler.
- Fog lights.
- A remote keyless entry system with a security system.
- Cruise control.
- 15-inch aluminum alloy wheels.
- The upgraded 200-watt, six-speaker audio system with an auxiliary audio jack for plugging in an outside music player; base cars instead came with a four-speaker AM/FM/CD system.

<!-- F-WP01-027 -->

One research note worth passing along: a Canadian-market Honda specification page describes a similar "base" trim as including an auxiliary audio jack, which conflicts with the US information above. This may reflect a real difference between the US and Canadian versions of the same trim, but it could not be confirmed either way. If your factory radio has an auxiliary jack and everything else here matches your car, that is not necessarily a mistake in this book. <!-- F-WP01-027 -->

### Paint colors

Honda's own announcement for the 2008 Fit states that two colors were new that year: Tidewater Blue Metallic and Blackberry Pearl. <!-- F-WP01-030 --> A parts and pricing website lists seven total exterior colors for the 2008 Fit: Milano Red, Storm Silver Metallic, Tidewater Blue Metallic, Vivid Blue Pearl, Blackberry Pearl, Blaze Orange Metallic, and Nighthawk Black Pearl. <!-- F-WP01-030 -->

A paint code is a short factory code that identifies one specific paint color, and body shops use it to mix a matching paint. Some paint codes were found for these colors, but they came from a general Honda color reference that was not confirmed as accurate specifically for the 2008 Fit. <!-- F-WP01-030 --> Do not order paint by a code from this book. Instead, check the paint code sticker on your own driver's doorjamb, the same area that holds your VIN and tire information, and match that code exactly.
