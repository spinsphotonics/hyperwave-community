# Chapter 46: Body: dents, rust, paint touch-up, headlight restoration

## What you will learn

- How to pull out a small, shallow dent
- How to treat surface rust before it becomes a hole
- How to touch up a paint chip or scratch
- How to restore cloudy or yellowed headlight lenses
- When a body repair is too big for your driveway, and needs a shop instead

## The short version

You can fix a small dent, treat early rust, touch up a paint chip, and clear up hazy headlights with basic tools and some patience. <!-- P-WP14-17, P-WP14-18 --> Rust is the one job with a hard line. Treat it early yourself, but once rust eats all the way through the metal, that spot needs a shop, not a driveway fix. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> Headlight haze is different. It comes from sunlight, not rust, and almost anyone can fix it in under an hour. <!-- P-WP14-18 -->

## The full explanation

### Dents, rust, and paint: one small job that grows

This book covers three small cosmetic body jobs together, because you often do them one after another on the same spot. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> First a dent, then any rust the dent exposed, then a paint touch-up to finish it. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->

Chapter 38 already flagged a specific rust spot on first-generation Fits like yours. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> It shows up where the rear quarter panel meets the rear bumper cover. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> It also shows up along a seam where a plastic cover hides the join between the quarter panel, the rocker panel, and the underside of the car. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> That pocket collects road grit and moisture, and it is the first place rust tends to appear. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> This is more of a concern if you live somewhere that salts the roads in winter. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->

Surface rust is rust that sits only on top of the metal, not through it. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> Treating it is a beginner job: you clean it, treat it with a rust converter (a chemical that changes rust into a paintable surface), then prime and paint over it. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> A hole in the metal is a different story. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> Fixing a rust hole means cutting out the bad metal and welding in a new patch, which is not a beginner task. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> Covering a rust hole with filler alone might look fine for a while, but it does not stop the rust spreading underneath. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> A repair shop estimate for early surface or bubble rust treatment on this kind of spot typically runs $150 to $900. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> A real hole patch or panel replacement typically runs $800 to $2,500. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->

For paint, your touch-up color has to match your exact car. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> Your doorjamb certification label carries your car's paint code. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> This book could not independently confirm the exact paint code numbers for every 2008 Fit color. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> Treat any code you find online as a lead to check, not a confirmed fact. <!-- P-WP14-17 --> Always check it against your own doorjamb label first. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->

### Headlight haze

Modern headlight covers are plastic, not glass, and plastic clouds over time from years of sunlight. <!-- P-WP14-18 --> Ultraviolet light, the part of sunlight that also causes sunburn, slowly breaks down the plastic's clear outer layer. <!-- P-WP14-18 --> This happens to nearly every car with plastic headlight covers, not only the Fit. <!-- P-WP14-18 --> So this job uses a general technique, rather than a Fit-specific guide. <!-- P-WP14-18 -->

## Step by step

### Fixing a small dent

What you need: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- A plunger, or a dedicated dent-pulling tool, for a small, shallow dent in a flat panel

Safety warnings: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- Always wear gloves and eye protection when you sand, wire-brush, or use chemical rust products, later in this same job.
- Do not sand through to bare metal over a large area without protecting it with primer right away. Bare metal left exposed starts rusting again quickly.

Steps: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
1. Clean the dented area with soap and water, then dry it.
2. For a shallow dent on a flat panel, use a plunger with some water on the rim, to create suction.
3. Pull outward in short, firm pulls.
4. For a dent a plunger cannot pull, a dedicated dent-puller kit with glue tabs and a slide hammer can help.
5. This kind of kit can fix many minor dents without damaging the paint.
6. Check the repaired area's paint for cracking. A cracked clear coat over a pulled dent may still need a touch-up.

### Treating surface rust

What you need: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- 220 to 600 grit sandpaper, to feather paint edges (sand them into a smooth, gradual taper instead of a hard ridge) and prep the rust spot
- A wire brush, or a drill-mounted rust-removal wheel
- Masking tape and paper, to protect the surrounding paint
- A rust converter or inhibitor product, to treat the rust before you paint over it
- Rust-inhibiting primer

Safety warnings: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- Always wear gloves and eye protection when you sand, wire-brush, or use a chemical rust converter.
- Do not use rust converter or primer in a closed, unventilated space. Work outdoors, or with good airflow.
- Always let each layer — converter, primer, paint, and clear coat — dry or cure fully before you add the next one.

Steps: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
1. Clean the rusted area with soap and water, then dry it fully.
2. Sand or wire-brush the rust down to a sound, unrusted surface. Feather the edges into the good paint around it.
3. Wipe away all the dust, then apply rust converter or inhibitor, following its instructions.
4. Once the converter has cured, apply rust-inhibiting primer over the treated area.
5. If the rust has already progressed to a hole in the metal, stop here.
6. Treat a rust hole as a job for a shop instead. See the note above.

### Touching up paint

What you need: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- Masking tape and paper
- Fine sandpaper, 600 grit or finer
- A small foam applicator or fine artist's brush
- Touch-up paint matched to your car's paint code, found on your doorjamb certification label. Verify any code you find online against your own label first.
- Clear coat, matched to your paint type, for the final layer over the touch-up paint

Safety warnings: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- Always wear gloves and eye protection during sanding.
- Do not skip letting each coat dry fully before you add the next one.

Steps: <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
1. Mask off the area around the chip, scratch, or repaired dent or rust spot with tape and paper.
2. If there is a rough edge, lightly sand the immediate area with fine sandpaper, then wipe it clean.
3. Apply touch-up paint in thin coats with a fine brush or applicator. Let each coat dry before the next, rather than applying one thick coat.
4. Once the paint has cured per its label, apply clear coat in thin coats over the repaired area.
5. Remove the masking tape and paper once everything has cured.
6. Wax the area once the repair is fully cured, blending it into the surrounding panel.

How you know it worked: The dent is flat enough that you cannot see it easily from a normal viewing distance. There is no more active rust bleeding through the paint at the treated spot. The touch-up paint is close enough in color and gloss that it does not stand out up close. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->

### Restoring cloudy or yellowed headlights

What you need: <!-- P-WP14-18 -->
- A headlight restoration kit. Most kits include sandpaper of several grits, a polishing compound, and applicator pads.
- Masking tape, to protect the surrounding paint
- A clean microfiber cloth
- A spray bottle with water, to keep the sandpaper wet, if your kit calls for wet-sanding
- A UV-protectant sealant, if your kit includes one

Safety warnings: <!-- P-WP14-18 -->
- Do not attempt this job in direct, hot sunlight. The plastic, and any chemical products you use, can behave unpredictably in high heat.
- Always mask off the paint around each headlight before you sand or polish, to avoid scratching it.
- Do not use harsh solvents not meant for automotive plastic. They can cloud or crack the lens further.

Steps: <!-- P-WP14-18 -->
1. Wash both headlights with soap and water, then dry them.
2. Mask off the paint and trim around each headlight with tape.
3. Starting with your kit's coarsest sandpaper, wet-sand the lens in one direction, keeping the surface wet the whole time.
4. Rinse the lens and check your progress. The surface will look hazy white during sanding — this is normal and expected.
5. Move to the next-finer grit in your kit, and repeat the wet-sanding, rinsing between grits.
6. Continue through every grit your kit provides, finishing on the finest one.
7. Rinse and dry the lens fully.
8. Apply the polishing compound with an applicator pad, working it in small circles until the lens looks clear.
9. Wipe off any leftover polish residue with a clean microfiber cloth.
10. If your kit includes a UV-protectant sealant, apply it per the kit's instructions, to help slow future yellowing.
11. Remove the masking tape.
12. Repeat steps 2 through 11 on the other headlight.

How you know it worked: Both headlight lenses look clear instead of cloudy or yellowed. A nighttime check shows noticeably brighter, more evenly spread light on the road or a wall, compared to before. <!-- P-WP14-18 -->

## What can go wrong

### Dent, rust, and paint mistakes

- Painting directly over untreated rust. The rust keeps spreading invisibly under the new paint. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- Applying touch-up paint in one thick coat, which causes it to run or look lumpy. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- Skipping the clear coat step, leaving the touch-up looking dull or mismatched in gloss. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->
- Trying to patch a rust hole with filler alone, instead of cutting out the rusted metal and welding in new metal. Filler alone does not fix the underlying problem. <!-- P-WP14-17 -->

### Headlight restoration mistakes

- Skipping a grit step in the sanding sequence, leaving fine scratches the polish cannot remove. <!-- P-WP14-18 -->
- Letting the sandpaper run dry, which can gouge the plastic instead of smoothing it. <!-- P-WP14-18 -->
- Forgetting to mask the surrounding paint, and scratching it with sandpaper or polish. <!-- P-WP14-18 -->
- Skipping the UV-protectant sealant step, if your kit includes one, and having the haze return sooner than expected. <!-- P-WP14-18 -->
