# Chapter 27: Brakes — Inspection, Pads, Rotors, Fluid

## What you will learn

- Why your Fit's front and rear brakes are not the same type
- How to inspect your front brake pads without removing the wheel
- How to replace front brake pads, and how to replace rear brake shoes
- How to bleed your brakes and what fluid to use
- Safety warnings that apply to every brake job in this chapter

## The short version

Your 2008 Fit's front brakes are disc brakes, and its rear brakes are drum brakes, not disc. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> This matters because the rear job uses shoes and springs instead of pads and a caliper, and it is a different repair. Every brake job in this chapter needs jack stands, eye protection, and patience with springs under tension.

## The full explanation

### Front disc brakes, rear drum brakes

A **disc brake** uses a flat metal disc, called a **rotor**, that spins with the wheel. A **caliper** is a clamp that squeezes flat pads against both sides of the rotor to slow it down. <!-- P-WP11-6 --> A **drum brake** works differently. A hollow metal drum spins with the wheel, and curved brake **shoes** press outward against the inside of the drum to slow it down. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> Inside the drum, a small threaded part called the **self-adjuster** (or just the **adjuster**) takes up slack as the shoes wear down. This keeps the brake working correctly without you adjusting it by hand every time. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->

Honda's own owner's manual states plainly that your Fit's front brakes are disc, and its rear brakes are drum. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> This applies across the 2008 Fit lineup, with no separate statement carving out a different design for base trim. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> Do not assume your rear brakes have pads and a caliper like the front; they do not.

### Brake fluid

Your brake system, and your clutch system if you drive a manual, both use Honda Heavy Duty Brake Fluid DOT 3. <!-- F-WP09-032 --> A DOT 3 or DOT 4 fluid from a sealed container works as a temporary substitute. <!-- F-WP09-032 --> Never use DOT 5 brake fluid; it is not compatible with your Fit's braking system and can cause serious damage. <!-- F-WP09-032 --> On a manual-transmission Fit, the clutch pedal is operated by hydraulic fluid from the same specification, held in its own reservoir with a gray cap. <!-- F-WP09-033 --> Replace your brake fluid every 3 years, no matter what the Maintenance Minder display shows. <!-- F-WP09-013 -->

### Torque and bolt sizes: what is confirmed, and what is not

Your wheel nuts, front or rear, torque to 80 lbf·ft (108 N·m) whenever a wheel comes off for brake work. <!-- F-WP10-001 --> Owners report a front caliper bracket-to-knuckle bolt torque of around 80 lbf·ft. <!-- F-WP10-005 --> This number comes from two community sources discussing a Fit Sport, not a confirmed base-trim source, so treat it as a starting point, not a certainty. <!-- F-WP10-005 --> No confirmed number exists yet for a rear caliper bracket bolt on this car. <!-- F-WP10-006 --> Since your Fit's rear brakes are drum, not disc, there is no rear caliper to begin with. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->

## Step by step

### Inspect the front brake pads through the wheel

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always let the brakes cool before you get close to the wheel if you just drove. The caliper and rotor can be very hot. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
- Do not touch the wheel or brake parts with bare hands right after driving. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->

**Steps:**
1. Park on level ground and set the parking brake. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
2. Turn the steering wheel toward you so you can see through the wheel spokes at that corner. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
3. Shine the flashlight through the spokes at the caliper, the metal bracket that squeezes the pads onto the rotor. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
4. Look for the pad material pressed against the flat, shiny rotor surface on each side of the caliper. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
5. Estimate the pad's thickness; a healthy pad is clearly thicker than a thin metal backing plate. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
6. Compare the inside and outside pad if you can see both; they should wear about evenly. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
7. Repeat steps 2 through 6 on the other front wheel. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->
8. Listen for a metallic screeching sound the next time you brake gently; the manual says this warns you the pads need replacing. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->

**How you know it worked:** you can see both pads on each front wheel and estimate their thickness. Or, you learn you cannot see enough, and you need to remove the wheel or have a shop check them. <!-- P-WP11-6 -->

### Replace the front brake pads

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always wear eye protection. Brake dust and springs under tension can hurt your eyes. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
- Do not breathe brake dust; never use compressed air or a dry brush on brake parts, per the manual's own maintenance-safety caution. Use brake-parts cleaner instead. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
- Always work on one side at a time and leave the other wheel on the ground so you have a reference for how parts go back together. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
- Do not let the caliper hang by its brake hose; support or hang it so the hose has no weight on it. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
- Always pump the brake pedal several times before driving, until it feels firm, before you rely on the brakes again. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->

**Steps:**
1. Loosen the front wheel nuts, then raise and support the car on jack stands. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
2. Remove the wheel and set it aside. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
3. Look at how the old pads and springs sit before you remove anything, or take a photo. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
4. Remove the caliper slide-pin bolts and lift the caliper off the bracket. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
5. Support the caliper with a hook or bungee so it does not hang by the hose. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
6. Pull the old pads out of the bracket. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
7. Use the C-clamp against the old pad to push the caliper piston back into its bore. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
8. Clean the bracket and slide pins with a wire brush and brake-parts cleaner. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
9. Apply a thin coat of brake grease to the pad backing and slide pins. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
10. Place the new pads into the bracket. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
11. Slide the caliper back over the new pads and reinstall the slide-pin bolts. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
12. Reinstall the wheel and snug the nuts by hand. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
13. Lower the car, then torque the wheel nuts to 80 lbf·ft (108 N·m) in a crisscross pattern. <!-- F-WP10-001, P-WP11-7 -->
14. Repeat steps 1 through 13 on the other front wheel. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
15. Pump the brake pedal several times with the engine off until it feels firm before driving. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
16. Check the brake fluid reservoir level and top it off if needed. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->
17. Drive slowly at first and test the brakes gently before returning to normal driving. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->

**How you know it worked:** the brake pedal feels firm, not soft or spongy. The car stops evenly with no pulling to one side. There is no grinding or squealing under light braking. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->

### Replace the rear brake shoes (drum brakes)

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always wear eye protection. Springs are under strong tension and can snap free unexpectedly. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
- Do not breathe brake dust; never use compressed air or a dry brush on brake parts. Use brake-parts cleaner instead. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
- Do not work under a car held up only by a jack. Use jack stands. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
- Always work on one side at a time, using the untouched side as your reference for how everything goes back together. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
- Do not get your fingers between a compressed spring and the backing plate. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
- Always keep grease off the brake shoe linings and the drum's inner surface; grease on the linings ruins braking. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
- Always press the brake pedal several times after reassembly, before driving, to seat the shoes and set the self-adjuster. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->

**Steps:**
1. Loosen the rear wheel nuts, then raise and support the car on jack stands. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
2. Remove the wheel and set it aside. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
3. Release the parking brake, then pull the drum straight off the hub (tap gently if it is stuck). <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
4. Take a photo of the spring and lever layout before touching anything. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
5. Unhook the lower return spring with your spring tool or pliers. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
6. Unhook the upper return spring the same way. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
7. Remove the hold-down springs and pins that pin each shoe to the backing plate. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
8. Lift out the old shoes together with the adjuster and parking-brake link still attached. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
9. Transfer the adjuster, parking-brake lever, and springs onto the new shoes in the same layout as the photo. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
10. Apply a thin dab of high-temperature grease to the backing plate's contact pads and the parking-brake lever pivot, keeping grease off the linings. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
11. Set the new shoes onto the backing plate and reinstall the hold-down pins and springs. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
12. Hook the lower return spring back into place. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
13. Use your C-clamps or spring pliers to compress the shoes enough to hook the upper return spring. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
14. Check that the shoes sit fully in their bosses (the raised mounting pads that hold each shoe in place) and the adjuster turns freely. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
15. Slide the drum back onto the hub. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
16. Reinstall the wheel and snug the nuts by hand. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
17. Lower the car, then torque the wheel nuts to 80 lbf·ft (108 N·m) in a crisscross pattern. <!-- F-WP10-001, P-WP11-8 -->
18. Repeat steps 1 through 17 on the other rear wheel. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
19. Press the brake pedal firmly several times with the engine off until the pedal feels normal. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
20. Adjust the parking brake if it now feels loose. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->
21. Drive slowly at first and test the brakes gently before returning to normal driving. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->

**How you know it worked:** the brake pedal feels firm, and the parking brake holds the car on a hill. Both rear wheels turn freely with light drag when the car is off the ground. There is no grinding or dragging noise while driving. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->

### Bleed the brakes

**Safety warnings, before you start:**
- Always wear eye protection. Brake fluid can splash and it damages paint and eyes. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
- Do not let the brake fluid reservoir run dry during bleeding, or you will draw air back into the lines and have to start over. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
- Do not reuse fluid that comes out of the bleeder; only use fresh fluid from a sealed container. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
- Always keep brake fluid off painted surfaces; wipe up any spill immediately. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
- Do not drive the car until the pedal feels firm and consistent, not soft or sinking. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->

**Steps:**
1. Remove old fluid from the reservoir with the baster, leaving a little so it does not run dry. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
2. Fill the reservoir with fresh Honda Heavy Duty Brake Fluid DOT 3. <!-- F-WP09-032, P-WP11-9 -->
3. Slide clear tubing over the bleeder screw at the first wheel and route the other end into your catch bottle. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
4. Have your helper press the brake pedal down and hold it. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
5. Open the bleeder screw briefly to let fluid and air escape, then close it before the pedal is released. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
6. Have your helper release the pedal only after the bleeder screw is closed. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
7. Repeat steps 4 through 6 until no more air bubbles appear in the tubing. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
8. Check the reservoir level and top it off before moving to the next wheel. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
9. Move to the next wheel and repeat steps 3 through 8. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
10. Recheck the reservoir level after all four wheels and fill to the MAX mark. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
11. Press the brake pedal several times and confirm it feels firm and consistent, not soft. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->

**How you know it worked:** the pedal feels firm on the first press, not soft or sinking to the floor. No air bubbles appear in the tubing on a final check at each wheel. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->

## What can go wrong

On front pads, pushing the piston back without first opening the brake fluid reservoir cap slightly can push fluid out or damage a seal. <!-- P-WP11-7 --> Forgetting to support the caliper can stretch or kink the brake hose. <!-- P-WP11-7 --> Skipping the pedal-pumping step before your first drive can mean the pedal goes to the floor instead of feeling firm. <!-- P-WP11-7 -->

On rear drum shoes, losing track of how the springs and adjuster were arranged is exactly why a before photo matters so much. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> Getting grease on the shoe linings can cause the brakes to grab or fade. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> Forcing the return spring on without a tool can make it snap loose unexpectedly. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> Not resetting the self-adjuster can leave the parking brake or pedal feeling loose. <!-- P-WP11-8 --> Several independent do-it-yourself writeups single out the main return spring as the one step that trips up first-timers. If you are not comfortable with springs under tension, this is a reasonable job to hand to a shop. <!-- P-WP11-8 -->

On bleeding, letting the reservoir run empty partway through draws in fresh air, undoing your work. <!-- P-WP11-9 --> Opening the bleeder screw too far can let it come out, and over-tightening it can snap it. <!-- P-WP11-9 --> Skipping the final firm-pedal check and driving with air still in a line is the most dangerous mistake in this whole chapter. <!-- P-WP11-9 -->
