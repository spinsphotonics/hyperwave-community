# Chapter 42: Cooling system: thermostat, radiator, water pump

## What you will learn

- What the thermostat, radiator, and water pump each do to keep your engine cool
- How to replace the thermostat
- How to replace the radiator
- How to replace the water pump, and why it is easier on this car than on many others
- The safety rules for working on a hot cooling system, and what can go wrong

## The short version

Your engine's cooling system moves coolant, a special liquid, through the engine to carry heat away. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> The thermostat controls when that coolant starts flowing to the radiator, the radiator gets rid of the heat, and the water pump pushes the coolant around the whole loop. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> <!-- P-WP14-7 --> <!-- P-WP14-8 --> All three jobs need the engine completely cool first, since a hot system can spray scalding coolant when opened. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> <!-- P-WP14-7 --> <!-- P-WP14-8 -->

## The full explanation

The thermostat is a small valve in the cooling system. It stays shut while the engine is cold, so the engine warms up faster. Once the engine reaches its normal operating temperature, the thermostat opens, and coolant can flow to the radiator. On your Fit, the thermostat sits inside a housing that Honda's own service information calls the "water outlet." <!-- P-WP14-6 -->

The radiator is a large, flat, finned unit at the front of the car. It takes hot coolant from the engine and lets outside air pass through it, cooling the coolant before it flows back to the engine. <!-- P-WP14-7 --> If your Fit has an automatic transmission, the radiator also carries a set of hose connections that cool the automatic transmission fluid. You need a radiator that matches your transmission type. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->

The water pump is the part that physically pushes coolant through the whole system. On many cars, the water pump is driven by the timing chain or timing belt deep inside the engine, which makes it a hard job. On your Fit, the water pump pulley is driven by the same external drive belt the Maintenance Minder already checks. You can reach it from the right front wheel well. <!-- P-WP14-8 --> That makes the water pump a meaningfully easier job on this car than on many others.

For all three jobs, check Appendix B for the correct coolant type and the amount your Fit needs after a refill. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> <!-- P-WP14-7 --> <!-- P-WP14-8 --> Coolant is toxic. Keep spills away from pets and children, since it can taste sweet to animals. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->

## Step by step

### Replace the thermostat

Always let the engine cool completely before you open the cooling system; a hot system can spray scalding coolant. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> Never open the radiator cap or the thermostat housing while the engine is warm or running. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> Always wear eye protection when you drain or refill coolant. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> Never let coolant sit on your skin for long, and never leave a spill where a pet or child could reach it. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> Never run the engine until you have refilled the system and bled the air out of it. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->

1. Make sure the engine is completely cool before you start. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
2. Remove the air cleaner assembly to reach the thermostat housing area. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
3. Drain the engine coolant into a clean drain pan. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
4. Disconnect the upper radiator hose and the water bypass hose from the housing. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
5. Disconnect the coolant temperature sensor's connector, then remove the housing from the cylinder head. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
6. Lift the old thermostat out of the housing, and note which side faces the engine. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
7. Remove all old gasket material from the housing's mating surfaces, its bolts, and the bolt holes, then clean and dry every surface. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
8. Fit a new sealing ring to the coolant temperature sensor, and set the sensor aside for now. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
9. Apply Honda liquid gasket, part number 08717-0004, 08718-0003, 08718-0004, or 08718-0009, to the housing's mating surface, following the bead pattern on the tube. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
10. Install the housing quickly. Some Honda liquid gaskets start to skin over in as little as 4 to 5 minutes. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
11. Reconnect the coolant temperature sensor and its connector, then reconnect the upper radiator hose and the water bypass hose. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
12. Reinstall the air cleaner assembly. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
13. Wait at least 30 minutes before you refill the system with coolant. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
14. Do not run the engine for at least 3 hours after you install the housing, so the gasket can cure. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
15. Refill the radiator with the correct coolant and bleed the air out, following your car's own fill procedure. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
16. Clean up any spilled coolant right away. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->
17. Start the engine, let it reach normal operating temperature, and check for leaks around the housing. <!-- P-WP14-6 -->

### Replace the radiator

Always let the engine cool completely before you drain coolant or disconnect hoses. <!-- P-WP14-7 --> Never open the radiator cap while the engine is warm or running. <!-- P-WP14-7 --> Always wear eye protection when draining or refilling coolant. <!-- P-WP14-7 --> Never work under a car held up only by a jack; always use jack stands. <!-- P-WP14-7 --> Always keep your hands clear of the electric radiator fan; it can switch on even with the engine off, if the coolant is still hot. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->

1. Make sure the engine is completely cool before you start. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
2. Raise the car and support it on jack stands, if you need to reach the lower hose and drain plug. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
3. Drain the engine coolant into a clean drain pan. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
4. Disconnect the coolant temperature sensor connector near the radiator, and unclip its harness clamp. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
5. If your car has air conditioning, disconnect the A/C compressor clutch connector and move its harness clamps out of the way. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
6. Disconnect the lower radiator hose. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
7. On an automatic transmission car, disconnect the automatic transmission fluid cooler hoses, and plug both the hose and the line to keep dirt out. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
8. Lower the car if it is raised, and remove the coolant reservoir. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
9. Disconnect the radiator fan motor connector, and the air conditioning condenser fan motor connector if equipped, then unclip their harness clamps. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
10. Disconnect the upper radiator hose, then remove the radiator's upper mounting brackets. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
11. Lift the radiator up and out. If your car has air conditioning, remove the condenser fan shroud first. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
12. Remove the fan shroud, radiator cap, coolant temperature sensor, and drain plug from the old radiator. Reuse the ones that are still good, or transfer them by your new radiator's instructions. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
13. Fit new O-rings and assemble the fan shroud, cap, sensor, and drain plug onto the new radiator. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
14. Set the new radiator in place, making sure its lower rubber cushions seat firmly. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
15. Reinstall the upper mounting brackets, then reconnect the upper radiator hose. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
16. Reconnect the fan motor connectors and their harness clamps, then reinstall the coolant reservoir. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
17. Raise the car again if needed, and reconnect the lower radiator hose. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
18. On an automatic, remove the plugs and reconnect the transmission fluid cooler hoses. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
19. Reconnect the coolant temperature sensor connector, and the air conditioning compressor clutch connector if equipped, with their harness clamps. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
20. Lower the car. Refill the radiator with the correct coolant, and bleed the air out per your fill procedure. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
21. Clean up any spilled coolant right away. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->
22. Start the engine, let it reach normal operating temperature, and check every connection for leaks. <!-- P-WP14-7 -->

### Replace the water pump

Always let the engine cool completely before you drain coolant. <!-- P-WP14-8 --> Never open the cooling system while the engine is warm or running. <!-- P-WP14-8 --> Always wear eye protection when draining or refilling coolant. <!-- P-WP14-8 --> Never work under a car held up only by a jack; always use jack stands. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->

1. Make sure the engine is completely cool before you start. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
2. Drain the engine coolant into a clean drain pan. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
3. Raise the car and support it on jack stands, then remove the right front wheel. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
4. Remove the splash shield, the plastic panel that covers the underside of the wheel well. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
5. Loosen the water pump pulley's mounting bolts, then remove the drive belt. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
6. Remove the water pump pulley's mounting bolts and the pulley itself. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
7. Remove the water pump's mounting bolts, and lift the old pump away from the engine block. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
8. Clean the old gasket material off the mounting surface. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
9. Fit the new pump with a new gasket or O-ring, following its instructions. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
10. Install the pump's mounting bolts and torque them per the new pump's instructions. We could not confirm an exact Honda torque number for this pass; check Appendix C for any later confirmation. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
11. Install the water pump pulley, and loosely fit its mounting bolts. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
12. Install the drive belt, then tighten the pulley's mounting bolts fully. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
13. Install the splash shield and the right front wheel, then lower the car. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
14. Refill the radiator with the correct coolant, and bleed the air out per your fill procedure. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
15. Clean up any spilled coolant right away. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
16. Start the engine, let it reach normal operating temperature, and check around the pump for leaks. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->

## What can go wrong

Opening any part of the cooling system while the engine is still warm can spray scalding coolant on your skin. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> <!-- P-WP14-7 --> <!-- P-WP14-8 --> On the thermostat job, missing the liquid gasket's short install window lets it skin over before the housing is bolted down. Installing the thermostat backwards can keep the engine from warming up correctly, so check the old part's orientation before you throw it away. <!-- P-WP14-6 --> On the radiator job, forgetting to plug a disconnected transmission fluid cooler line lets dirt into the transmission's cooling circuit. Skipping the bleed-air step afterward can cause a false overheating reading on a later drive. <!-- P-WP14-7 --> On the water pump, do not mistake a small, normal amount of weeping at the pump's bleed holes for proof the old pump had failed. A healthy pump can show a trace of this. Judge a water pump by whether its pulley turns freely or by an active leak, not by a trace of weeping alone. <!-- P-WP14-8 -->
