# Appendix G: Glossary

This glossary defines every technical term used in this book, in one sentence each, at a level a new driver can follow. Terms are alphabetized. Where the dashboard or the owner's manual uses an abbreviation, such as "MIL" or "TPMS," the full name is given first.

**ABS (Anti-lock Brake System)**: A safety system that pumps the brakes very fast on its own so your wheels don't lock up and skid when you brake hard.

**Accessory position**: The ignition key position that powers things like the radio and the power outlet without starting the engine.

**AKI (octane rating)**: The number on a gas pump's label that tells you how well that fuel resists engine knocking; the 2008 Fit needs at least 87 AKI.

**Alloy wheels**: Wheels made from a lightweight metal blend instead of plain steel; the 2008 Fit's base trim has steel wheels with covers instead.

**Auto-down window**: A power window feature where one firm push on the switch lowers the whole window by itself, without you holding the switch down.

**Autocross**: A timed driving event, usually held in a large parking lot or empty lot, where drivers race one at a time against the clock through a course marked out with cones.

**Automatic transmission**: A transmission that changes gears for you on its own, so there's no clutch pedal to press.

**Auxiliary (aux) jack**: A small audio plug on some car stereos that lets you plug in an outside music player; the US base 2008 Fit does not have one.

**Axle-back exhaust**: A replacement exhaust part that swaps out only the last section, from about the rear axle back to the tailpipe, usually just to change the sound.

**Bolt pattern (lug pattern)**: The number and spacing of the bolt holes on a wheel, which must match the studs on the car for the wheel to bolt on correctly.

**Boost**: Extra air pressure that a turbocharger or supercharger pushes into the engine, above normal air pressure, to help the engine make more power.

**Bore and stroke**: Two engine measurements — how wide each cylinder is (bore) and how far the piston travels inside it (stroke).

**Break-in period**: The first several hundred miles of driving a new car, when the maker asks you to drive gently so engine parts can settle in properly.

**Camber**: How much a tire tilts inward or outward at the top when you look at the car from the front.

**Campaign number**: The specific ID number NHTSA assigns to one safety recall, used to look that exact recall up online.

**Canard**: A small, wing-shaped piece bolted to the front bumper that is meant to press the front of a fast-moving car down slightly.

**CARB EO number**: A number the California Air Resources Board gives to an aftermarket part after testing, showing that part can legally be used on a car driven on public roads in California.

**Cargo volume**: How much space is available for luggage or gear, usually measured in cubic feet.

**Caster**: The tilt of the steering axis when viewed from the side of the car, which affects how the steering wheel returns to center and how stable the car feels driving straight.

**Cat-back exhaust**: A replacement exhaust system that starts right after the catalytic converter and runs back to the tailpipe, usually installed to change the exhaust sound or let gases flow out easier.

**Catalytic converter**: A part in the exhaust system that cleans harmful gases out of the exhaust before they leave the car.

**Center bore**: The hole in the middle of a wheel that fits around a matching part on the car's hub to help keep the wheel centered.

**Certification label**: A sticker on the driver's doorjamb that lists your VIN, along with the recommended tire sizes and tire pressures.

**Charging system**: The parts, mainly the alternator, that keep the car battery topped up while the engine is running.

**Chassis code**: A short factory code, like GD3, that identifies exactly which body and underlying platform a specific car was built on.

**Check Fuel Cap message**: A message that appears on the dashboard display when the gas cap is loose or missing.

**Childproof lock**: A small lever on a rear door that stops that door from being opened using the inside handle.

**Clutch (aftermarket)**: A stronger replacement clutch, built to grip harder than the factory clutch so it can handle more engine power without slipping.

**Coilover**: A shock absorber and spring built together as one adjustable unit, often letting an owner change the car's ride height and how stiff it feels.

**Cold air intake**: A replacement air-intake system meant to pull in cooler outside air instead of the warmer air already inside the engine bay, which can add a small amount of power.

**Combination switch**: The single lever on the steering column that also controls the turn signals and headlight high/low beams.

**Compact spare tire**: A small, lightweight temporary tire (sometimes called a "donut") meant only for short trips at reduced speed until the real tire is fixed.

**Complaint (NHTSA)**: A report a vehicle owner files with the government describing a problem with their car, whether or not it turns into a recall.

**Compression ratio**: A number describing how much an engine squeezes the air-fuel mixture before it ignites it.

**Consumer notice**: The letter a manufacturer mails to a car's registered owner explaining a recall and what to do about it.

**Cool-down lap**: One slow, gentle lap driven at the end of a track session so the brakes, engine, and transmission cool down gradually instead of being parked while still very hot.

**Cruise control**: A feature that holds the car at a steady speed on its own, so the driver doesn't need to keep a foot on the gas; not included on the 2008 Fit's base trim.

**Curb weight**: How much the empty car weighs with a full tank of gas but no passengers or cargo.

**D3 (gear position)**: An automatic-transmission setting that works like Drive but keeps the transmission from shifting into 4th or 5th gear, useful for engine braking downhill.

**Daytime Running Lights (DRL)**: Lights that turn on by themselves whenever the engine is running, making the car easier to see in daylight; not fitted on the US 2008 Fit.

**Destination charge**: A flat fee added to a new car's price to cover shipping it from the factory to the dealer.

**Diagnostic Trouble Code (DTC)**: A short code the car's computer stores when it detects a problem, which a mechanic reads with a scan tool.

**Displacement**: The total volume swept by all of an engine's cylinders, usually given in liters, which roughly shows how big the engine is.

**Doorjamb**: The frame area you see when a door is open; the certification label and VIN sticker are usually placed there.

**DOT brake fluid rating**: A number (like DOT 3, DOT 4, or DOT 5.1) that a federal safety standard uses to set a brake fluid's minimum boiling point; a higher DOT number does not always mean a higher boiling point for every specific product.

**Downpipe (test pipe)**: The section of exhaust pipe right after the turbocharger or catalytic converter; a "test pipe" is a downpipe with the catalytic converter removed, usually meant only for track use.

**Dyno (dynamometer)**: A machine that measures how much power an engine or a car's wheels actually produce.

**ECU (Engine Control Unit)**: The car's onboard computer that decides things like how much fuel to add and when to fire the spark plugs, based on readings from its sensors.

**Electric Power Steering (EPS)**: A power-steering system that uses an electric motor, instead of hydraulic fluid pressure, to help you turn the steering wheel.

**Electrolyte**: The liquid inside a car battery that lets it store and release electricity; in very cold weather it can thicken or freeze.

**Electronic Brake Distribution (EBD)**: A system that automatically balances how much braking force goes to the front wheels versus the rear wheels.

**Emissions control system**: The group of parts that work together to reduce pollution coming out of the engine and exhaust.

**Engine swap**: Removing a car's original engine and installing a different one, usually to get more power or better reliability.

**EPA (Environmental Protection Agency)**: The US government agency that tests and publishes the official fuel-economy (MPG) numbers for new cars.

**Fast idle**: Running an engine a little faster than normal idle speed, often done to a booster vehicle's engine during a jump start.

**FIA (Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile)**: An international motorsports organization whose safety standards, such as harness and helmet certifications, are often required at racing and track events.

**Final drive ratio**: An extra gear ratio built into the transmission that affects how the engine's speed relates to the wheels' speed in every gear.

**Flat-tow**: Towing a car with all four wheels rolling on the ground behind another vehicle; the 2008 Fit's owner's manual does not support this method for either transmission.

**Flatbed towing**: Towing a car by loading all four wheels onto the back of a truck, so none of the car's wheels touch the road.

**Flywheel**: A heavy spinning disc attached to the engine that smooths out its power pulses; a lighter aftermarket flywheel lets the engine change speed faster.

**Fog lights**: Extra, low-mounted lights that help light the road edge in fog; standard on the Fit Sport trim only, not on base.

**Friction point**: The spot while slowly releasing a clutch pedal where the engine and transmission start to connect, and the car begins to move.

**Fuel vapor recovery system**: A system that catches gasoline vapor at the pump instead of letting it escape into the air; a fault in it can make the pump nozzle click off early and repeatedly.

**Gear ratio**: A number describing how many times the engine turns for every one turn of the wheels in a given gear; smaller numbers generally mean higher top speed, larger numbers mean more pulling power.

**Ground clearance**: The distance between the lowest point of the car's underside and the ground.

**Ground point ("the stay")**: A metal point in the engine bay, away from the battery itself, used as the final connection spot when jump-starting a car to reduce spark risk.

**H Street (HS)**: An entry-level SCCA autocross class, meant for cars that are still mostly stock, with only limited changes like tires and shocks allowed.

**Harness (racing harness)**: A multi-point seat belt system, with straps over both shoulders and between the legs, used in place of a factory 3-point belt for extra crash protection.

**Harness bar**: A bar bolted across the car behind the front seats, used to anchor a racing harness's shoulder straps.

**Hazard warning lights**: All four turn signals flashing together at once, used when parked in a dangerous spot or when a car is disabled.

**Head restraint**: The padded part above a seatback that supports your head and helps prevent whiplash in a crash; often just called a headrest.

**Header**: A set of pipes that connects each engine cylinder to the rest of the exhaust system; a performance header is shaped to let exhaust gases flow out more easily than the factory manifold does.

**High beam**: The brighter, longer-reaching headlight setting meant for dark roads with no oncoming traffic nearby.

**Hood release**: The handle inside the car, usually under the dashboard, that pops the hood partway open.

**Horsepower**: A measurement of how much power an engine can produce.

**HPDE (High Performance Driving Event)**: A track day where drivers practice driving skills at speed with instruction, rather than racing wheel-to-wheel against other cars.

**i-VTEC / VTEC**: Honda's system for adjusting how and when engine valves open, which helps the engine make good power and stay efficient across a range of speeds.

**IIHS (Insurance Institute for Highway Safety)**: An independent group, funded by insurance companies, that crash-tests vehicles and rates how well they protect the people inside.

**Immobilizer**: An anti-theft system that keeps the engine from starting unless it recognizes the electronic chip built into your key.

**Intercooler**: A small radiator-like part that cools down air after a turbocharger or supercharger has heated and pressurized it, before that air enters the engine.

**Investigation (NHTSA)**: A formal government review into a possible safety defect, which may or may not lead to an official recall.

**Jacking point**: A specific spot marked on the underside of a car's body where it is safe to place a jack.

**Jump start**: Starting a car with a dead battery by temporarily connecting jumper cables to a charged battery in another vehicle or a portable booster pack.

**Keyless entry**: A remote fob that lets you lock or unlock the doors by pressing a button instead of using the physical key; not included on the base 2008 US Fit.

**Long Mode**: One of the Fit's Magic Seat cargo layouts, used for sliding long items like lumber or a surfboard through to the front of the car.

**Low beam**: The normal, everyday headlight setting used whenever other traffic is nearby.

**Magic Seat**: Honda's name for the Fit's flexible 60/40-split rear seat, which can fold flat or flip up into several different cargo and passenger layouts.

**Maintenance Minder**: A dashboard system that tracks the car's condition over time and tells you, with a wrench-shaped light and a code, when a specific service is due.

**Malfunction Indicator Lamp (MIL)**: The dashboard light — often called the "check engine light" — that comes on when the emissions-control system detects a problem.

**MMT**: A manganese-based fuel additive that Honda recommends avoiding where possible because it can cause the check-engine light to come on.

**Moderate overlap front test**: A crash test where only part of the front of the car strikes a barrier, similar to many real-world head-on crashes that aren't perfectly centered.

**Motor mount**: A rubber-and-metal part that holds the engine and transmission in place and absorbs vibration; a worn one can cause a clunk when shifting gears.

**MPG (miles per gallon)**: A measurement of how far a car can travel using one gallon of fuel.

**MSRP (Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price)**: The price a car's maker suggests dealers charge, before tax, fees, and any negotiating.

**MTBE**: A fuel additive some gasoline blends contain; the Fit's manual allows fuel with up to 15% MTBE by volume.

**NHTSA (National Highway Traffic Safety Administration)**: The US government agency that manages vehicle recalls, safety ratings, and owner complaint records.

**Occupant Detection System (ODS)**: Sensors that check the front passenger seat for a person, a child seat, or nothing at all, to help decide whether the airbag should go off.

**Occupant Position Detection System (OPDS)**: Sensors that check exactly where a front passenger is sitting, so the airbag can be adjusted or turned off if someone is sitting too close to it.

**Odometer**: The gauge that shows the total distance a car has been driven over its whole life.

**OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) part**: A replacement part made by, or to the same standard as, the part the car originally came with from the factory.

**Overlap crash test**: A type of crash test that hits only part of a vehicle's front end, rather than the whole width, to better match many real-world crashes.

**Paddle shifters**: Levers mounted behind the steering wheel that let a driver manually bump an automatic transmission up or down a gear; found on the Fit Sport's automatic only, not on base.

**Parasitic draw**: A small amount of electrical current a parked, switched-off car still uses; too much of it drains the battery even when the car is left alone.

**Parking brake**: A separate brake, set by hand, that holds a parked car in place; also called an emergency brake.

**Piggyback (tuning device)**: A small aftermarket box that changes the signals going to the factory ECU without replacing it, often used to adjust the fuel mixture.

**Powertrain**: The engine, transmission, and connected parts that work together to move the car.

**Readiness codes**: Internal checks the emissions computer runs to confirm all its own monitoring systems have finished testing; some states check these during an emissions test.

**Recall (safety recall)**: A free, manufacturer-run repair program ordered after a safety-related defect is found in a group of vehicles.

**Redline**: The highest engine speed considered safe to reach; going past it risks damaging the engine.

**Refresh Mode**: A Magic Seat layout where the front seats recline flat to rest, usable only while the car is parked.

**Reverse lockout**: A mechanism in a manual transmission that stops the driver from shifting straight from the highest gear into reverse without pausing.

**Ride height**: How high the bottom of the car sits above the ground; lowering it usually helps handling but reduces ground clearance.

**Roll bar**: A metal bar or hoop mounted behind the seats to help keep the roof from collapsing onto the people inside during a rollover crash.

**Roll cage**: A full frame of metal tubing built inside a car to protect the people inside during a serious crash; more extensive than a simple roll bar.

**Rollover rating**: A safety score estimating how likely a specific vehicle is to roll over in a single-vehicle crash.

**RPM (revolutions per minute)**: How fast the engine is spinning, shown on the tachometer.

**SAE Class S**: A size and type classification for tire chains; the Fit's manual only allows this cable-type chain, and only on the front tires.

**Salt-belt states**: A group of US states where winter road salt is heavily used, which some recalls (like rust or corrosion-related ones) apply to instead of the whole country.

**Security system**: An anti-theft alarm system, standard on the Fit Sport trim only, not on the 2008 US base Fit.

**SFI (SFI Foundation)**: A US organization that tests and certifies motorsports safety equipment, such as racing harnesses and helmets, to a set standard.

**Shift interlock (shift lock)**: A safety mechanism that keeps an automatic transmission's shift lever locked in Park unless the brake pedal is pressed.

**Shift lever**: The lever a driver moves to choose a gear, in either a manual or an automatic transmission.

**Side airbag**: An airbag that deploys out of the seat or door area to protect against a side-impact crash.

**Splitter**: A flat panel added to the front or sides of a car's bumper, meant to help manage airflow underneath the car.

**Spoiler**: A wing-shaped piece mounted on the trunk or hatch; on a light, slower car this is mostly for looks, though on faster cars it can help push the rear tires down.

**Spring rate**: A number describing how stiff a spring is, based on how much force it takes to compress it a set distance; a higher number means a stiffer, firmer ride.

**SRS (Supplemental Restraint System)**: The umbrella term for a car's airbags and their related crash sensors, working alongside the seat belts.

**Stainless brake lines**: Brake hoses wrapped in a woven stainless-steel sleeve instead of plain rubber, which resist expanding under hard, repeated braking.

**Standalone engine management**: A complete aftermarket computer that fully replaces the factory ECU, instead of working alongside it.

**Street Prepared**: An SCCA autocross class that allows bigger changes than Street Touring, such as race-only tires, engine tuning, and body modifications, and is no longer necessarily street-legal.

**Street Touring**: An SCCA autocross class that allows more modifications than Street classes, like aftermarket suspension, brakes, and wheels, while the car must still stay street-legal.

**Supercharger**: A device, driven by a belt from the engine, that forces extra air into the engine to help it make more power.

**Sway bar**: A suspension part connecting the left and right wheels that reduces how much the car's body leans in turns.

**Synchronized transmission**: A manual transmission design that lets gears mesh smoothly during a normal shift, without extra tricks like a double-clutch.

**Tachometer**: The gauge that shows how fast the engine is spinning, in RPM.

**Takata airbag inflator**: A now-recalled type of airbag part, made by the Takata company, that can explode and send metal fragments into the car after long exposure to heat and humidity.

**Tall Mode**: A Magic Seat layout where the rear seat cushions flip up, opening floor space for tall items.

**Tech inspection**: A safety check of a car, done before a track day or race, that looks at things like tires, brakes, fluids, and seat belts.

**Technical Service Bulletin (TSB)**: A repair instruction a manufacturer sends to its own dealers about a known problem; it is not a safety recall and is not always a free repair.

**Time Trial**: A track event where each car is timed individually on an open track, instead of racing wheel-to-wheel against other cars at the same time.

**Tire chain**: A traction device wrapped around a tire to improve grip on snow or ice.

**Toe**: How much a car's tires point inward or outward, viewed from above, compared to pointing straight ahead.

**Torque**: A twisting force; for a car this means both the pulling power an engine produces and the exact tightness a bolt or nut must be tightened to.

**Torsion beam**: A simple, solid rear suspension design, used on the Fit, where a single beam connects the two rear wheels instead of letting them move fully independently.

**TPMS (Tire Pressure Monitoring System)**: A system that watches tire pressure and warns the driver on the dashboard if a tire gets significantly low.

**Trail braking**: Easing off the brake pedal gradually while turning into a corner, instead of releasing the brake fully before starting to turn.

**Tread wear indicator**: A raised bar built into a tire's grooves that becomes visible once the tire is worn down and needs replacing.

**Trim level**: A specific version of a car model, such as "base" or "Sport," that comes with its own defined set of standard features.

**Trip meter**: A mileage counter, separate from the odometer, that a driver can reset to track the distance of a single trip.

**Turbocharger**: A device powered by the engine's own exhaust gases that forces extra air into the engine to help it make more power.

**Utility Mode**: A Magic Seat layout where the rear seatback(s) fold down flat to make a larger, flat cargo floor.

**VDS (Vehicle Descriptor Section)**: The middle part of a VIN that encodes details like the car's platform, engine, and body type.

**VIN (Vehicle Identification Number)**: A unique 17-character code stamped on every car that identifies its maker, features, and production details.

**Warranty extension**: A manufacturer's decision to cover a specific repair for longer than the original warranty period, usually issued after a known problem turns up.

**Wheel nut**: One of the nuts that holds a wheel onto the car; these must be tightened to an exact torque spec, not just "as tight as possible" (also commonly called a lug nut).

**Wheel offset**: How far a wheel's mounting surface sits from the exact center-line of the wheel, which affects how far the tire sticks out from or tucks under the car.

**Wheel-lift towing**: Towing a car by lifting only its front (or rear) wheels off the ground on a tow truck's frame, while the other wheels stay on the road.

**Wheelbase**: The distance between the centers of the front and rear wheels.

**Whp (wheel horsepower)**: Horsepower measured at the wheels, after some power is lost through the drivetrain, instead of measured at the engine itself.

**Wing bolt**: A bolt with flat, wing-shaped tabs that can be loosened or tightened by hand; used to hold the 2008 Fit's spare tire in its well.

**WMI (World Manufacturer Identifier)**: The first three characters of a VIN, which identify the company and country that built the vehicle.

**Zone A/B/C recall**: A way NHTSA and automakers roll out some large recalls in stages by region (zone) instead of notifying every owner nationwide at once, often based on local climate or humidity risk.
