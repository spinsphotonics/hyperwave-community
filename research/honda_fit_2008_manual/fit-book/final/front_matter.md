# The Complete 2008 Honda Fit Book

*Everything a new driver and a worried parent need to know to drive, own, fix, and even race a 2008 Honda Fit.*

## How to use this book

This book is about one specific car: the **2008 Honda Fit, US base trim, first-generation chassis code GD3**. Honda sold a fancier Fit Sport trim that year too, and a completely different second-generation Fit started in 2009. Neither of those is this book. If your Fit is a Sport trim, or a 2009 model or newer, some of what is here still applies, but a lot of it does not. Check Chapter 2 to see exactly what your base trim does and does not have, and do not assume a part number, a torque value, or a step in this book is correct for any Fit other than the 2007–2008 GD3 base car.

This book covers both transmissions Honda offered on the 2008 Fit: the 5-speed manual and the 5-speed automatic. Wherever the two work differently, the book says so and walks through both, usually in side-by-side sections. If a chapter does not mention a transmission difference, the information applies to both.

**How the sourcing works.** Every fact, number, and procedure in this book was checked against a real source before it was written down: Honda's own owner's manual, government safety databases, published repair manuals, or (when nothing better existed) the Honda Fit owner community. Each sentence that carries a specific claim has an invisible tag behind it in the file this book was built from, a short code like `F-WP01-003` for a fact or `P-WP11-8` for a repair procedure. You will never see these codes on the printed page; they are hidden markers, not footnote numbers. What they mean for you is this: nothing in this book was made up. If you ever want to check exactly where a claim came from, the codes tie every sentence back to a real, dated source.

Two phrases in this book flag information that deserves a little extra caution:

- **"Owners report that..."** This phrase means the information came from the Honda Fit owner community, not from Honda or a government agency. It is only used when at least two separate owners or forum sources said the same thing independently. It is usually reliable, but it has not been confirmed by Honda's own paperwork, so treat it as a strong lead rather than a guarantee.
- **UNVERIFIED.** When this book could not find a trustworthy source for a specific number or claim, even after a real search, it says so plainly instead of guessing. You will see a note like "We could not confirm this for the 2008 Fit. Check your owner's manual, a dealer, or a repair shop before you rely on this." Guessing about a car is how people get hurt or waste money; this book would rather tell you it does not know.

**How the book is organized.** The book has ten parts. Part I introduces the car and helps you confirm exactly which Fit you own. Part II covers the basics: keys, seats, starting the car, and every control and gauge. Part III teaches you to actually drive it, in good conditions and bad. Part IV covers the paperwork side of ownership. Part V is the maintenance you can do yourself, from an oil change on up. Part VI teaches you to diagnose a problem before you pay anyone to look at it. Part VII walks through bigger repairs at home. Part VIII is for readers who want to modify the car, and Part IX is for readers who want to take it to an autocross lot or a track day. Part X, the appendices, gathers the book's reference tables (specs, torque values, fuse maps, part numbers, a glossary, and more) into one place you can flip to quickly instead of hunting through a chapter.

Read the parts in order if this is your first car. Jump straight to the part you need if it is not.
